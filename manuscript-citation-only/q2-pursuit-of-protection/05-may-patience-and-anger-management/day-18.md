# Day 18 — Road Rage and the Real Target

Traffic on the interstate had been stop-and-go for twenty minutes, and the guy in the pickup ahead of me kept riding his brakes for no reason I could see — no exit close, no merge, nothing. When he did it a fourth time I laid on the horn longer than I needed to and said something out loud in the car that I wouldn't have said in front of my kids.

He hadn't cut me off. He hadn't done anything except drive a little too cautiously in front of a man who was already late. I knew that, even in the moment. What I hadn't let myself ask was why a stranger's brake lights had the power to make my chest go tight and my hands grip the wheel like it owed me something.

The honest answer came later, once I was parked in my own driveway and still fuming. Forty minutes earlier, a client had told me — calmly, reasonably — that they were moving the account to someone else. I hadn't processed that. I hadn't even let myself feel it. I carried it into the car instead and handed it to the first slow-moving pickup truck I found, because a truck is easier to be angry at than a lost account and what it might say about whether I'm good enough at my job.

Here's the question worth asking before the horn, not after: is this really about the man in front of me, or is he just standing where my anger can reach him? Most days the honest answer is the second one. The brake lights are just the target of convenience, not the offense.

Proverbs gives the alternative, built on insight rather than gritted teeth. Proverbs 19:11 says a man's good sense is what makes him slow to anger, and that there's real honor in letting an offense pass rather than holding onto it. Overlooking means recognizing that this particular offense — a cautious driver, a slow checkout line, a kid's spilled cereal — isn't carrying the weight I'm putting on it, not pretending nothing happened, and choosing not to unload onto it what it didn't cause.

Good sense, the verse says — not good luck, not a naturally easy temperament. Sense. A decision to notice where the heat is coming from before it goes somewhere it doesn't belong.

**Today:** The next time something small sets you off faster or harder than it deserves, stop and ask yourself out loud: "What is this really about?" Name the actual source, even just to yourself, before you decide the driver — or the coworker, or your kid — deserves what you're about to give them.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've taken things out on people who had nothing to do with what was bothering me, and called it their fault instead of mine. Help me find the real source of my anger before I hand it to someone who didn't earn it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
