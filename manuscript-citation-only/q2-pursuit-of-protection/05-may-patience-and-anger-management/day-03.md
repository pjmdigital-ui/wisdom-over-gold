# Day 3 — The Anger That Isn't About What You Think

Theo's son spilled a full glass of milk across a stack of invoices Theo had spent the evening organizing on the kitchen table. Theo shot up out of his chair like the table had caught fire. He grabbed a towel and threw it down harder than he needed to. He told his eight-year-old, who was already close to tears, to "watch what you're doing for once." The boy went to his room without being told to. Theo stood there holding a soaked towel, breathing harder than the moment called for.

It wasn't really about the milk, and he knew that even while it was happening. That's how it goes sometimes. A man can watch himself do something and still not manage to stop. Two hours earlier, his business partner had called to say a deal they'd both been counting on had fallen through. Theo had privately promised himself this deal would finally put them ahead instead of just even. He hadn't told his wife yet. He hadn't told anyone. He'd come home and started reorganizing invoices because it was a problem small enough to actually solve.

So why did his son's spilled milk get the full weight of a deal falling apart? Ask that honestly and the honest answer stings a little. It's easier to be furious at a kid over a glass of milk than to sit with the fear underneath the real thing, that Theo might not be able to provide the way he'd told himself he would. Milk is a safe target. It can't ask him hard questions back.

Scripture doesn't tell Theo his anger was fake. It doesn't tell him to swallow it whole either. Paul gives the Ephesians something more useful. Feeling angry is not the sin, he says. Carrying it past the end of the day without dealing with it is the problem. It sits there overnight and hardens into something worse. Theo's fear was real. He had the rest of that day to name what scared him and bring it to the one place that could hold it. Instead he brought it to a third grader over three dollars of spilled milk. By the time the sun went down, nothing about the real deal had changed. But something between him and his son had changed.

Most men's worst outbursts aren't really about what set them off. The trigger is just the nearest soft target when something heavier is already sitting on his chest, unnamed and unspoken. Find the real source before sundown and there's a real chance to deal with it honestly. Let the sun go down while you're still telling yourself it was about the milk, and it settles in for the night. Then it finds a new target tomorrow.

**Today:** Before you go to bed tonight, write down what was actually eating at you the last time you overreacted to something small. Then go to whoever caught the blast and say, "That wasn't about you. Here's what it was really about." Let them see the real thing instead of the cover story.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've aimed real fear at people who didn't cause it because they were closer and easier to hit than the actual problem. Help me find the honest name for what's troubling me before I let it spill onto someone who doesn't deserve it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

EPHESIANS 4:26
