# Day 7 — Closing Week One

Look back honestly at the six days behind this one and a pattern probably shows up, even if each moment wore a different face. Maybe it was a meeting where you cut someone off. Maybe it was a bad day you unloaded on your family and called it honesty. Maybe it was milk on the invoices, or an email you almost sent, or an apology you assumed had settled something that hadn't settled at all. Different rooms, different people, mostly the same wire running underneath.

Strip away the specific triggers and the sentence doing the work most days sounds something like this: *my anger proves something true and urgent, and acting on it right away is what a strong man does.* It proves he's serious. It proves he's right. It proves he won't be walked over. That sentence has been running all week, wearing a different outfit each day, and most men never catch it because it never announces itself directly. It just shows up as a reflex that feels like character.

Scripture has been circling this same point from several angles all week, and it lands here with the least patience for excuses. Ecclesiastes 7:9 warns against a quick temper, because anger that goes unchecked doesn't just pass through a foolish man. It takes up residence in him. Not a visit, not a pass-through. A residence. Anger that isn't examined moves in instead of flaring and leaving. It finds a room in a man's chest and starts paying rent, coloring how he reads the next email, the next comment from his wife, the next thing his kid does wrong. A week of unexamined reactions doesn't stay contained to six separate incidents. It becomes a posture.

What changes a man is noticing which single moment made him wince a little harder than the others, not remembering all six days in detail. The reflex he calls strength. The venting he calls honesty. The misdirected blast at an easy target. The false choice between exploding and staying silent. The apology he thought closed an account that was still open. The email he almost sent before checking what the warning light was telling him. One of those is the one worth sitting with.

That's the goal of a week like this. Not perfect self-control by Friday, but one honest sentence a man can finally name instead of just living underneath it without ever looking up to notice it's there.

**Today:** Reread the six days behind this one and pick the single moment that landed closest to home. Write down, in your own words, the false belief it exposed and the true one replacing it. Then read that sentence out loud before you start tomorrow.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, this week has shown me how much of my anger I've never examined, only obeyed. Help me carry into tomorrow not guilt over the days behind me, but one true sentence I can live by. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
