# Day 19 — Anger Online

Warren wasn't a person who picked fights. His wife would have said that about him without hesitation. But at 11:40 on a Tuesday night, phone lit up in a dark bedroom, he was six replies deep into an argument with a stranger in the comments of a parish Facebook post about school reopening plans, and his thumbs were moving faster than his thinking.

The stranger — a screen name, a cartoon avatar, no face — had said something dismissive about parents who'd raised concerns at the last meeting. Warren knew the actual families being talked about. He typed a response that started reasonable and ended somewhere else, sharper than he meant, the kind of sentence built to win rather than to be true. He hit send and felt something close to satisfaction. Then he read the three replies that came back and felt his jaw tighten all over again.

He told himself he was just standing up for people who couldn't defend themselves in the thread. That's a defensible sentence on its own. It doesn't explain a man arguing with a stranger he'll never meet, over a post that will be forgotten by Friday, at a volume he'd never use if the other person were standing in his kitchen.

Proverbs draws the line without raising its voice. Proverbs 17:27 ties real knowledge to a man who holds his words back, and ties real understanding to a man whose temper stays level. Restraint doesn't erase a point worth making — it just means the point comes from a man who has command of himself, rather than from an argument that has taken command of him. A cool spirit can still say something true. It just isn't racing to say it before it's been thought through, and it isn't typing at a volume the man behind the words would never use in person.

That's the tell worth checking, screen glowing in a dark room: would I say this, in this tone, to this person's face, over coffee, with nothing to prove? If the honest answer is no, then what's really driving the reply is the wish to be seen winning, in front of an audience that mostly isn't paying attention and won't remember by morning.

**Today:** Before you respond to anything online today that's raised your temperature — a comment, a group chat, a thread — put the phone down for ten minutes first. When you pick it back up, ask: "Would I say this to his face?" If not, don't send it as typed.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've said things behind a screen that I'd never say to a man in person, and told myself it was standing up for what's right. Give me a cool spirit instead of a quick one, especially when no one's watching but You. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
