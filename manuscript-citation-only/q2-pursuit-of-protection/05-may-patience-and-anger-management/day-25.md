# Day 25 — Cooling Down Without Shutting Down

Griffin and his wife were arguing about a five-thousand-dollar transmission repair neither of them had budgeted for when he felt the old pressure building behind his eyes. He'd read enough, by now, to know not to say something he'd regret. "I need a minute," he told her, and walked out to the garage.

That part he got right. What came after didn't. Griffin didn't come back in twenty minutes, or that evening. He answered her texts about pickup schedules in single words and nothing else for two days. He came to bed after she was asleep and left before she woke. When she finally asked him directly if he was still angry, he said he was fine — flat, closed, a door with the light off behind it.

By the third day she'd stopped asking. That was the part that finally got his attention: not that she was still upset, but that she'd quietly given up expecting an answer.

What was Griffin actually protecting by going silent for two days? Not his marriage — he'd have said that, if you'd pushed him, but it isn't what the evidence shows. Ask him honestly and the real answer surfaces: *as long as I'm not yelling, I'm not the problem here — she can wait until I'm ready.* Withdrawal felt like restraint. It functioned like a punishment with no announced end date.

Scripture does commend leaving a fight before it fully ignites: "The beginning of strife is like letting out water; so quit before the quarrel breaks out" (Proverbs 17:14, RSV-CE). That's real wisdom, and it's exactly what Griffin thought he was practicing. But quitting a quarrel and abandoning a person aren't the same move, even though they can look identical from across a kitchen table. A timeout has a stated return — *I need twenty minutes, I'll be back and we'll finish this.* Stonewalling has no return date, because its real purpose is making the other person feel the cost of having upset you, not cooling down.

The test is whether the silence is heading somewhere, or whether the silence is the whole message.

**Today:** The next time you need distance from a hard conversation, say it out loud instead of just disappearing: *stepping back to cool down isn't the same as shutting someone out — I'm going to come back, and I'm going to name when.* Give an actual time. Then keep it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, teach me the difference between the restraint that protects a conversation and the silence that punishes the person in it. When I need to step back, give me the honesty to say so instead of just disappearing. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
