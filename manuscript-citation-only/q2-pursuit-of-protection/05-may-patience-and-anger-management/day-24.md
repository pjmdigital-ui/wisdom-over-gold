# Day 24 — What Your Anger Costs Your Witness

Gavin had built his electrical contracting business from one truck to nine employees over eleven years, and the standard he demanded on every job was painted on the side of that truck: right the first time. So when he walked the final inspection on a forty-thousand-dollar panel upgrade and found that his newest hire had wired two circuits backward, something in him went hot before he'd even finished reading the panel.

The homeowner was standing four feet away. Gavin's own son, sixteen and spending his first summer on the crew before senior year, was holding the ladder. None of that registered. Gavin tore into the kid in a voice that carried through the open garage and into the driveway — careless, sloppy, do you want to burn this family's house down — until the homeowner took a visible step back toward her kitchen door and the young hire's ears went the color of the wire he'd just been accused of ruining.

Nobody said much on the drive back to the shop. Gavin's son sat in the passenger seat looking at his boots the whole way. Later that week Gavin heard secondhand that the kid had started asking around about openings at another company.

If you'd asked Gavin that afternoon, he'd have told you he was protecting his company's name — that a man who lets sloppy work slide is a man nobody respects. What he wouldn't have named, because he hadn't looked at it yet, is the belief underneath: *if I don't perform outrage right now, nobody will believe I take this seriously.*

Scripture's instructions to a young pastor named Timothy were about who should be trusted to lead a church, but the standard set there wasn't measured in decibels. 1 Timothy 3:2-3 lays out the qualifications for a church leader: a man beyond reproach, faithful to his wife, self-controlled, sensible, respectable, welcoming, able to teach, not given to drink, not prone to violence but gentle, not quarrelsome, not driven by money. Temperate. Gentle. Not quarrelsome. Any man expecting to be followed is held to that same bar, whether the people following him wear his last name or his company shirt.

Gavin's newest hire didn't walk away from that garage doubting his boss's electrical knowledge. He walked away doubting whether it was safe to bring Gavin a mistake — and a man nobody can safely bring a mistake to eventually gets told nothing at all, right up until the mistake is too big to hide.

The son in the passenger seat is the harder cost. He wasn't on that job to learn wire gauges. He was watching to find out what his father does under pressure, and what he saw was pressure getting vented on the one person in the room with the least power to push back.

**Today:** Say it before you need it: *the respect I want from the people under my authority is built by how I handle being right, not by how loud I get about it.* Before you correct anyone today — an employee, a teammate, one of your own kids — decide your tone before you open your mouth.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for the times I've used my authority to vent instead of to lead. Give me the temperance to correct without tearing down, especially in front of the people who are still learning who I am. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 TIMOTHY 3:2-3
