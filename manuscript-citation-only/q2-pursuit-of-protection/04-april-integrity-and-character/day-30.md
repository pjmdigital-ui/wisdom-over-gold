# Day 30 — Ready for May

Eli had a good month, and he knew it. Thirty days of catching himself mid-shortcut and taking the honest road instead — the expense report he corrected, the hard sentence he said out loud at his own kitchen table, the small trust he'd started rebuilding one kept promise at a time. He was proud of it in the clean way a man is allowed to be proud of real work, and driving home from his last men's group meeting of the month, windows down, he let himself feel settled about it. *I've got a handle on this now.*

Six minutes later a pickup cut him off changing lanes without a signal, close enough that Eli hit the brakes harder than he needed to. He leaned on the horn a full three seconds longer than the moment required, and when the truck didn't so much as flinch, he heard himself shout something at the windshield he wouldn't have wanted his daughter to repeat — except she was in the back seat, and she did hear it, and went quiet in the particular way kids go quiet when Dad's voice changes register.

He sat at the next red light with his hands still tight on the wheel, genuinely startled at himself. Thirty days of hard-won honesty, and none of it had touched whatever had just fired off in under two seconds over a lane change that cost him nothing real. The belief hiding under that surprise deserves to be said outright, because a lot of men carry it after a good stretch of growth: *once I've gotten a handle on one part of my character, I've basically arrived — the rest will take care of itself.* Integrity is one fruit among several, and getting good at telling the truth doesn't automatically make a man patient — any more than getting strong in one muscle automatically strengthens the rest of the body. There's no finish line a man crosses once and then coasts past.

St. Paul lists the whole set together, deliberately, in one breath, and the entire list deserves to be read rather than picking a favorite out of it: "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control; against such there is no law" (Galatians 5:22-23, RSV-CE). Faithfulness is in there — the honesty, the kept word, the steadiness this whole month has been building. So is patience, sitting right in the middle of the list, distinct from faithfulness, its own fruit requiring its own cultivation. A man can genuinely grow one and still be almost untouched in another, the way Eli was honest at his own kitchen table and still nearly unrecognizable at a red light six minutes later.

That's an honest way to close a month, not a discouraging one. April asked a real question of you — whether your word could be trusted when no one was checking — and if you're honest, some days you passed and some days you didn't, but the direction was true. You are, by any fair measure, a more integrated man than you were thirty days ago. The people around you can feel that, even the ones who couldn't name what changed.

May is going to ask a different question of the same character: not whether you tell the truth, but whether you can hold your peace when something cuts in front of you — literally or otherwise — without losing what you spent this month building. Patience is the same foundation, tested from a different angle — no softer a test than integrity — and May is easier walked into knowing that already than discovering it at the next red light.

**Today:** Say out loud: *the same character that made me honest this month is going to have to grow patient next month — I'm not finished, I'm continuing.* Then, the next time something small cuts you off today — in traffic, in conversation, in your own house — let it pass without a word, as practice for the month ahead.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for what You built in me this month — the honesty, the kept promises, the small trust rebuilt one true word at a time. I know the fruit isn't finished growing. Start preparing me now for the patience I'm going to need next. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
