# Day 17 — The Promise You Made and Forgot

"After the game, buddy, I promise." You said it in the car, half-listening, because your son wanted to show you a card trick and you had a call in nine minutes. He held on to that word all day. You didn't remember saying it until he brought the deck over that night, already shuffled, already excited, and you had to tell him tomorrow. You were tired, and tomorrow felt true enough in the moment.

It wasn't a lie, exactly. You meant it when you said it. That's the part worth sitting with. You didn't break the promise on purpose. You just used it as a placeholder to end a conversation, a way to buy nine minutes without paying for them. Somewhere along the way you'd started keeping two kinds of promises — the ones you write down because someone with power over you is watching, and the ones you say to a seven-year-old, which apparently don't need to survive contact with your schedule.

Your son didn't file a complaint. He just quietly stopped bringing you the cards.

The belief under that car-seat promise was simple: *a word given to my kid costs me nothing to break, because he has no way to hold me to it.* That's exactly what makes it dangerous. Not that you'd say it out loud, but that you'd never have to. Scripture doesn't grade vows by the power of the person you made them to. Ecclesiastes tells a man not to delay paying whatever he's vowed, because God takes no pleasure in fools. Better never to make the vow at all than to make it and not follow through. The preacher isn't only talking about vows made at an altar. He's naming a pattern. Words spoken carelessly, as if they cost nothing, are exactly the words God holds you to. A promise made to a child who can't enforce it is still a vow. It doesn't get graded on a curve because the other party is small.

This is where a man's character actually gets built or worn down. Not in the moments someone's checking, but in the hundred small yeses he hands out to people who trust him completely and have no leverage to make him keep them. Your kids are building their whole sense of whether words mean anything by watching what happens to yours.

The fix is closing the gap between what you say and what you do until there isn't one. That starts with saying less, not more, so what you do say can be trusted completely.

**Today:** Find your son (or daughter) and say out loud: "I forgot the trick I promised you — I'm going to keep it now." Then do the thing you promised, today, however small. Before you make your next promise to a child this week, pause and ask yourself: "Can I actually pay this?" If the answer isn't yes, don't say it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for the promises I've handed my children like they cost nothing, then let slip because no one made me answer for them. Teach me to guard my word with the same seriousness whether I'm speaking to You, to a client, or to a boy holding a deck of cards. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ECCLESIASTES 5:4-5
