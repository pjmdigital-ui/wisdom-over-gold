# Day 20 — The Body Doesn't Lie

"How was the meeting?" your wife asked, setting the pasta down. "Fine," you said. "No big deal." Your shoulders were up near your ears. Your jaw hadn't unclenched since you walked in the door. You picked up your phone to check something that didn't need checking, eyes down, and your leg started bouncing under the table the way it does when a number in your head won't stop running.

Your ten-year-old looked up from his plate. "Dad, are you mad?"

You weren't mad. You were scared. The meeting had gone worse than fine, and you hadn't figured out yet how to say that out loud, so you'd decided not to. Except you never actually decided that. Your mouth said "fine." Your body had already told the whole table the truth twenty seconds before your son asked the question that made it official.

Ask yourself honestly which one your family trusts more when the two disagree — your words, or the way you're standing while you say them. You already know the answer. Kids especially read posture, jaw, eyes, and breath before they process a single word, and they're rarely wrong about what those signals mean.

Under the "fine" was a belief worth naming directly: *what I say is the honest part, and what my body does is separate — a background detail nobody's really reading.* That belief treats the body as if it doesn't matter to the truth, a costume you can arrange while the real communication happens somewhere else, in the sentence. Scripture won't let you split it that way. St. Paul reminds the Corinthians that their bodies are a home for the Holy Spirit, a gift from God rather than something they own outright. They were bought at a price, and that means honoring God with the body itself, not treating it as separate from the soul inside it. Paul isn't talking about posture at the dinner table. He's making a much bigger claim: your body is not a separate, lesser piece of you that sits outside your integrity. It belongs to God, not to you as an image to manage, and it tells the truth about you whether you've allowed it to or not.

That cuts two ways. Your body was never given to you as decoration — something to arrange for how it reads to a room, a firm handshake and steady eye contact used like a tool to manage what people believe about you. And it was never given to you as a separate department either, free to broadcast the real state of things while your mouth says something else. A temple doesn't have a public-facing wing and a private one. Whatever is actually true in you is going to surface through your body eventually. The only question is whether you'll get ahead of it with honest words, or let your son have to ask.

The fix is closing the gap between what's true and what you say, instead of performing a calm you don't feel, so your body stops having to say it for you.

**Today:** Tonight, notice the next moment your body is telling a truth your mouth isn't. Instead of managing the room, say the real thing out loud: "I'm not fine, actually — today was hard." Say it plainly, once, to whoever's in front of you.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for treating my body as a costume to manage instead of a temple that already belongs to You. Close the gap between what I say and what's true in me, so my family can trust both. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 CORINTHIANS 6:19-20
