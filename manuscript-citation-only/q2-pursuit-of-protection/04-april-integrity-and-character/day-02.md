# Day 2 — The Corner You Cut When You're Tired

It was 11:40 on a Thursday night. I was three hours past the point where I do good work. The client wanted the revised numbers by Friday morning. The model had a piece I hadn't fully checked — assumptions about renewal rates I'd built quickly and never gone back to compare against last year's real numbers.

I remember the exact thought, because I said it to myself almost word for word: *It's close enough. I'll fix it later if it matters. Nobody's going to open this file and check the formula — they're going to look at the summary tab.* I sent it. I closed the laptop. I told myself I'd circle back Monday and tighten it up before anyone relied on it for anything real.

Monday came. I didn't circle back. Three weeks later the client made a hiring decision partly on those numbers. The soft assumption I knew about never got flagged, because nothing ever makes you revisit a corner you already got away with cutting. I found out later it hadn't broken anything. That doesn't mean it had been right.

Here's what I didn't look at closely, at 11:40 that night. I would never have sent that model to a client fresh, on a Tuesday morning, with a full night's sleep behind me. I had a standard. I just believed the standard was optional after a certain hour. My integrity was something I owed people when I had the energy for it, and something I could set aside without a word when I didn't. Tired-me and rested-me were running on two different sets of rules, and I'd never once admitted that out loud.

Proverbs names exactly what that gap costs a man. The one who walks with integrity walks in safety. The one who twists his path to fit the moment will eventually get caught. Notice the word *walks* — not sprints, not performs on the good days. A walk covers the whole distance, tired miles included. A man builds real security by being the same man at 11:40 PM that he is at 9:00 AM. One set of standards, carried the whole way, instead of two different men trading places depending on how worn down he is.

Being tired just strips away the energy you'd normally spend covering for a flaw that was already there. What's left standing when you're too tired to perform is the actual shape of the man. That's exactly why the late-night corner matters more than the daylight ones, not less.

**Today:** Name the specific hour or condition where your standards tend to slip — exhaustion, deadline pressure, being alone with no one checking your work. Write down: "My integrity doesn't get to clock out when I'm tired." Then go back and finish the piece of work you know you left soft, done right, before anyone else touches it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let exhaustion talk me into a lower standard more than once, and called it practical instead of what it was. Steady me in the hours when I'm too tired to perform for anyone, so what's left standing is still honest. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
