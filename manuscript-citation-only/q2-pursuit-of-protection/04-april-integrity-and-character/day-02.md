# Day 2 — The Corner You Cut When You're Tired

It was 11:40 on a Thursday night and I was three hours past the point where I do good work. The client wanted the revised numbers by Friday morning, and the model had a piece I hadn't fully stress-tested — a set of assumptions about renewal rates I'd built quickly and never gone back to check against last year's actuals.

I remember the exact thought, because I said it to myself almost word for word: *It's close enough. I'll fix it later if it matters. Nobody's going to open this file up and audit the formula — they're going to look at the summary tab.* I sent it. I closed the laptop. I told myself I'd circle back Monday and tighten it up before anyone relied on it for anything real.

Monday came. I didn't circle back. Three weeks later the client built a hiring decision partly on those numbers, and the renewal assumption I knew was soft never got flagged, because nothing ever forces you to revisit a corner you got away with cutting. I found out later it hadn't broken anything. That's not the same as it having been right.

Here's what I didn't examine at 11:40 that night: I would never have sent that model to a client fresh, on a Tuesday morning, with a full night's sleep behind me. I had a standard. I just believed the standard was optional after a certain hour — that my integrity was something I owed people when I had the energy for it, and something I could set aside without a word when I didn't. Tired-me and rested-me were operating on two different sets of rules, and I'd never once admitted that out loud.

Proverbs names exactly what that gap costs a man: "He who walks in integrity walks securely, but he who perverts his ways will be found out" (Proverbs 10:9, RSV-CE). Notice the word *walks* — not sprints, not performs on the good days. A walk is the whole distance, the tired miles included. Security is what a man builds by being the same man at 11:40 PM that he is at 9:00 AM — one set of standards, carried the whole way, instead of two men trading places depending on how depleted he is.

Fatigue just strips away the energy you'd normally spend covering for a character flaw that was already there. What's left standing when you're too tired to perform is the actual shape of the man — which is exactly why the late-night corner matters more than the daylight ones, not less.

**Today:** Identify the specific hour or condition where your standards tend to slip — exhaustion, deadline pressure, being alone with no one checking your work. Write down: "My integrity doesn't get to clock out when I'm tired." Then go back and finish the piece of work you know you left soft, properly, before anyone else touches it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let exhaustion talk me into a lower standard more than once, and called it practical instead of what it was. Steady me in the hours when I'm too tired to perform for anyone, so what's left standing is still honest. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
