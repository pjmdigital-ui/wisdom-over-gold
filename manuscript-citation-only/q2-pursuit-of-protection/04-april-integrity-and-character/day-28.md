# Day 28 — Closing Week Four

You're four miles into a Sunday morning run, the week finally behind you, on a trail you've done a hundred times. There's a switchback near the ridge where the marked path bends hard around a stand of trees, and a worn dirt shortcut cuts straight through them instead — thirty seconds faster, a little steeper, a little rougher on the ankles. You've taken the shortcut before. Today your legs are tired and your eyes drift toward it out of habit before you've even decided anything.

You stay on the marked trail instead, and somewhere in that half-second decision, the whole week comes back to you at once — not as one big test, but as four or five small ones strung together. The morning you were afraid of what telling the truth would cost you. The afternoon honesty meant losing time you didn't feel like you had. The ordinary Tuesday nobody was grading, where being trustworthy in something small turned out to matter more than you knew. The dinner table where staying quiet would have been so much easier than saying the true thing out loud.

None of those moments, taken alone, looked like it would have mattered much if you'd swerved. That's the belief that needs catching before it hardens into a habit: *one small departure from the straight path doesn't really move me off course — I'll correct at the next real test.* A man rarely wrecks his character in one dramatic wrong turn. He drifts there, thirty seconds at a time, on a hundred small shortcuts he told himself didn't count.

Proverbs describes the alternative in language that could be written for a man on a trail: keep your eyes fixed straight ahead, watch where your feet are actually landing, and don't drift right or left toward whatever's waiting off the path. Notice what's being guarded: the eyes and the feet, the ordinary, moment-to-moment direction of an ordinary week, not one big decision. Sureness doesn't come from getting the big calls right. It comes from a man who's stopped negotiating with the shortcut, one unremarkable step at a time, until staying on the marked path is just what he does.

Looking back at this week, you didn't get every step right. Nobody does. What you can say honestly is that when the trail bent, more often than not, you bent with it instead of cutting through.

**Today:** Walk back through the last seven days and name one moment you took the shortcut anyway — a corner cut, a truth softened, a promise let slide. Say it out loud: *my eyes are set on the straight path, not the shortcut, even when no one's timing my mile.* Then do the small, corrective version of that moment today — make the call, send the honest message, finish the thing you cut short.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for a week that tested my integrity in more ways than I expected, and for the moments I stayed on the path instead of cutting through it. Where I swerved, set my eyes straight again, and keep my feet sure for whatever this next week asks of me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
