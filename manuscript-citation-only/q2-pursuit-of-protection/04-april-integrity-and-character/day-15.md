# Day 15 — Integrity at the Negotiating Table

Dean drove two hours to look at a used excavator a retiring landscaper was selling out of his own barn. The machine was in better shape than the listing let on — low hours, recent maintenance records, a model that was moving fast right now because of a supply shortage. Comparable units were listing for thirty-four to thirty-seven thousand dollars. The seller, a man named Walt who clearly hadn't priced anything beyond a quick search two years old, had it listed at twenty-two.

Standing in the barn, Dean had a line ready before Walt even opened his mouth to negotiate: "Market's pretty soft on these right now." It was true in a narrow, technical sense, built specifically to leave Walt anchored to a number fifteen thousand dollars below what the machine would fetch anywhere else. Dean had used some version of that line in a dozen negotiations. It had never once felt like lying.

He didn't say it. Walt named his price — twenty-two — and Dean told him, plainly, that comparable units in this condition were going for thirty-four to thirty-seven, and that he'd pay thirty for it today. Walt looked at him for a second like he was waiting for the catch. There wasn't one. Dean drove home eight thousand dollars poorer than he could have been, with an excavator he needed and a receipt that told the truth.

The belief behind Dean's rehearsed line was that a negotiation is a different moral zone than the rest of life, where withholding what you know, or framing true facts to mislead, doesn't count as dishonesty because it's just how the game is played. Every man who's negotiated anything has reached for some version of that belief, because the game genuinely does reward it.

Scripture doesn't grade negotiations on a curve. Proverbs says a rigged scale disgusts the Lord, while an honest weight pleases Him. A balance is rigged the same way whether a man tips it with an outright lie or with a technically-true sentence engineered to produce a false impression. God isn't measuring the cleverness of the deception. He's measuring the weight.

Dean's eight thousand dollars is gone. What isn't gone is the plain fact that the price he paid was the actual value of what he got — which is a kind of peace a discounted excavator was never going to buy him.

**Today:** Before your next negotiation — a deal, a raise, a price on something you're selling — write down any information you know that the other side doesn't, and decide now, before you're in the room, that you'll volunteer it rather than let their ignorance set the price.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've treated negotiating as a place where the normal rules bend, and I want to stop pretending that's true. Let my word carry the same weight at the table as it does everywhere else. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
