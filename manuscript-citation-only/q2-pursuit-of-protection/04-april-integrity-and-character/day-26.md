# Day 26 — The Man Who Can Be Trusted With Little

For two years I was the guy who reconciled the petty cash drawer. Forty-dollar office supply runs, a stack of parking receipts, the occasional twelve-dollar lunch someone forgot to expense correctly. Nobody in that building considered the drawer important. I used to joke about it myself, half-complaining to my wife at dinner that I had an MBA and I was spending Friday afternoons matching gas station receipts to a spreadsheet.

I did it anyway, every week, down to the penny, even the weeks I was buried in bigger things and could have waved it through unreconciled and nobody would have noticed for months. There was a stretch where I was thirteen cents off for three straight weeks and I walked the building asking people if they remembered a coffee run, because thirteen cents unexplained bothered me more than it should have.

My manager never said anything about the drawer directly. What she said, eighteen months later, standing in my office doorway with a folder under her arm, was that she was putting my name forward for the region's largest account — eleven million dollars a year, the kind of relationship that had ended two other managers' careers when it went sideways. I asked her, half-joking, why me over three people with more tenure. She said she'd watched how I handled the small stuff nobody was checking. That was the whole answer.

Jesus tells a version of this exact moment as a parable, and the master's verdict lands on precisely the point I'd missed for two years without knowing it: the master praises the servant as good and faithful precisely because he was faithful with a small amount, and promotes him to responsibility over much more, welcoming him into his own joy. The servant in the story wasn't rewarded for the size of what he managed. He was rewarded for how he managed something small — carefully, honestly, as if it mattered, when almost nobody was watching closely enough to know if it did.

I'd spent two years believing that petty cash was beneath the kind of trust that really counts — that real integrity would show up someday, in some bigger test I was still waiting for. It never occurred to me that the bigger test was already running, disguised as a drawer full of receipts, and that whoever handed out the eleven-million-dollar account was watching the very thing I'd been treating as a formality.

Character gets built in the hundred small transactions before anyone's counting, tested there long before it's ever trusted with anything larger. The moment it finally gets noticed is just when the results become visible.

**Today:** Name one small responsibility you've been treating as beneath your real attention — an expense report, a returned tool, a promise to a kid that seems minor. Say out loud: *I am trustworthy in the small things, not because someone's watching, but because that's who I actually am.* Then handle that one small thing today with the same care you'd give something ten times its size.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for the small things I've handled carelessly while waiting for a bigger test to prove myself. Make me faithful in what's in front of me today, however little it looks. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MATTHEW 25:23
