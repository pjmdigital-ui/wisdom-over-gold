# Day 18 — Money Tells the Truth About You

Tax season, eleven at night, laptop open on the kitchen table. You're going through the Venmo history for the side coaching work you did last year — six grand, give or take, paid by parents who never asked for a receipt and will never get a 1099 from you. Your accountant emailed that morning asking for "all 2025 income, even the cash stuff." You typed a number into the spreadsheet. It was lower than the real one.

Nobody audits a youth sports coach making six thousand dollars on the side. You know that. The thought running through your head is quieter than a risk assessment. It sounds more like: *this money is basically a gift for something I'd have done anyway, not real income anyone needs to know about.* You'd never say that about the number on your W-2. You'd never round that down and call it a rounding error. The real difference is that this money is invisible, and in your head, invisible money apparently plays by different rules.

Here's the part worth noticing. You'd be furious if an employee handled your company's petty cash with this same logic — decided some of it "doesn't really count" because no one's watching the drawer. You'd call that stealing, and you'd be right. The only difference between his hand in your drawer and yours in the government's is which side of the deal you're standing on.

Jesus makes an odd move in the parable of the dishonest steward. He uses money, of all things, as the proving ground for whether a man can be trusted with anything real. He asks who would ever hand over the true riches to a man who can't even be trusted with something as ordinary as money. He's not saying money is what matters. He's saying money is the test. Small, unglamorous, easy to fudge, and it reveals whether you're the kind of man who can be trusted with something bigger. A dollar figure on a spreadsheet at eleven at night is a smaller version of the same character question that shows up later in bigger rooms with bigger stakes.

Money is honest that way. It won't flatter you. What you do with the part nobody's tracking — the cash tip, the untaxed side gig, the receipt you could pad and no one would check — tells you plainly what you actually believe about being watched. If the number changes only when the audience does, the number was never really about the money.

**Today:** Go back to that spreadsheet, that invoice, that number you rounded in your favor because no one was checking. Correct it. Say to yourself as you do it: "What I do with money no one's tracking is still Him tracking." Then send your accountant the real figure.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for treating the money no one else sees as money that doesn't matter to You. Give me the same honesty with the dollar nobody's watching that I'd insist on from anyone I trusted with mine. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

LUKE 16:11
