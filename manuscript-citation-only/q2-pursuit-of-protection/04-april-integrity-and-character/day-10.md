# Day 10 — The Silence That Speaks

Owen was on a video call with the client when Dale, the VP of sales, said the six-week delay had been "a legal review issue on our end." That wasn't true. The delay was Owen's engineering team missing two sprints in a row, and everyone on the call from Owen's company knew it. Dale glanced at the small square of Owen's face on the screen, the kind of glance that expects a nod.

Owen didn't nod. He also didn't correct Dale out loud in the moment — that would come later, in the room, not on a recorded call with a client watching. But when the client, still looking for confirmation, asked directly, "Owen, does that match what you saw on your end?" the pause in the room stretched half a second too long to be nothing.

In that half-second, Owen came close to doing something that never announces itself as lying: letting someone else's lie stand because staying quiet felt like the smaller sin. Nobody would have pushed if he'd let it pass. Instead he said, plainly, "The delay was on our engineering side. We fell behind on two sprints." No apology tour, no defense of Dale, no elaborate story. One true sentence and nothing else.

What Owen almost did was a lie by omission — no technical definition required — letting his silence answer the question a false yes would have answered. That's the belief worth naming plainly, because it's a comfortable one: *silence isn't really lying, so it doesn't count.* It lets a man protect himself from conflict while still telling himself he never said anything false.

Jesus doesn't leave room for that distinction. "Let what you say be simply 'Yes' or 'No'; anything more than this comes from evil" (Matthew 5:37, RSV-CE). He's not just condemning false words — He's naming the evasive space around them, the qualifiers and unnecessary silences men use to avoid committing to the truth without technically lying. A yes that isn't really yes, or a silence standing in for a no a man won't say out loud, comes from the same place a lie does.

Owen's one sentence cost him something. Dale didn't speak to him for the rest of the day. But the client trusted the next thing Owen told them, and so did Owen, walking out of that room.

**Today:** Think of the conversation this week where staying silent would let someone else's version of the truth stand unchallenged. Practice saying the plain sentence out loud, alone, before you have to say it in the room: state exactly what's true, nothing added, nothing left for silence to imply.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let silence say things for me that weren't true, because speaking up felt harder than staying quiet. Give me the courage to let my yes be yes and my no be no, even when a client or a boss is waiting for something easier. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
