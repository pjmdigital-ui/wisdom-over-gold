# Day 7 — Closing Week One

Sunday night. Bills paid, calendar cleared for the week ahead. I sat at my desk longer than I needed to. I wasn't doing anything in particular. I was just going back over the last several days, the way you check your pockets before you leave the house, patting down for something you half-remember losing.

An expense line I'd padded without a second thought. A late-night shortcut I'd taken on a client model, telling myself I'd fix it later and never did. A small lie to my daughter that turned out to be a lesson she'd learned better than I meant to teach it. A justification I'd caught myself building before I'd even decided to do the thing it was defending. On their own, none of them felt like much. I could explain away any single one of them in a sentence if you asked me directly.

Laid next to each other, though, they stopped looking like separate incidents. They looked like a pattern. The same move, worn four different shapes, showing up wherever the situation offered me a shortcut and decent odds that nobody would check.

I'd been telling myself these were unrelated slips, each one too small to say anything about the kind of man I actually am. That was the belief I'd been running on all week without saying it out loud: *these are isolated incidents, and isolated incidents don't add up to a character.* But nothing lives in isolation in one life. The man who cuts the corner at work is the same man who tells the smaller lie at home. There's only one of me making all these calls.

Titus doesn't leave room for a man to split his integrity by area. He tells Titus to be a model of good conduct in every area of life, teaching with an integrity and seriousness that don't shift depending on the setting. In every area — not just the visible half of his life, not just at the office, not just in front of his kids, but as one consistent man wherever he happens to be standing. That phrase rules out the very excuse I'd been leaning on all week: that this compromise over here doesn't touch that part of my life over there.

It does. It's one direction, showing up four times in one week, and I finally sat still long enough to see it as a single thread instead of four separate snags.

**Today:** Pick the one compromise from this week that's still bothering you the most. Go correct it concretely — repay it, retract it, redo it, apologize for it. Then write down: "This is one pattern, not four separate moments, and I get to decide what it adds up to."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, this week showed me a pattern I'd been calling a series of accidents, and I know now it was one direction the whole time. Turn that direction toward You this week, in every part of my life at once, not just the parts people are watching. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

TITUS 2:7
