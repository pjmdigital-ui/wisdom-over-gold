# Day 16 — Telling Your Wife the Whole Truth

The first text from Kayla was ordinary. A question about a project the two of them had worked on together three jobs ago. Aaron answered it and didn't think twice. Over six weeks the texts kept coming, though, drifting from work catch-up to how are you really doing, to a running joke only the two of them were in on. Aaron never sent anything he'd have been embarrassed to read out loud. He also never told Rachel any of it was happening.

He had a reason ready every time the thought crossed his mind to mention it. Rachel had a hard enough month already — her mother's diagnosis, the kids' school schedule falling apart. No reason to hand her something to worry about over nothing. That was the sentence Aaron kept telling himself: *Telling her would only worry her about something that isn't even a real problem — better to protect her from it.* He believed it while he was thinking it.

Six weeks in, Kayla sent something clearly flirty, and Aaron didn't respond for two days, telling himself the silence was the fix. That night he told Rachel everything — the whole six weeks, not just the last message. Not because she asked, not because she'd found anything. She was quiet for a long moment, more hurt by the six weeks he'd kept from her than by anything Kayla had actually written.

"Protecting her" was the belief Aaron used to justify keeping quiet, and it's one of the more convincing ones a man tells himself. It borrows the language of love while doing the opposite of what love requires. He was protecting himself from an uncomfortable conversation, and dressing the decision up as care for Rachel so he wouldn't have to look at it plainly.

St. Paul's instruction to the Ephesians doesn't leave room for that kind of managed disclosure between people who belong to each other. He tells them to put away lying and speak truthfully with one another, because they belong to each other as parts of the same body. A husband and wife are the closest possible fit for that kind of belonging. Not two separate parties deciding what the other needs to know, but one body, where holding back from her is really holding back from himself.

Rachel didn't need protecting from six weeks of harmless-sounding texts. She needed the whole truth on day one, so she could trust the account Aaron gave her of his own life, including the parts that were genuinely nothing.

**Today:** Tell your wife the thing you've been managing rather than sharing — the version of events you've been editing down to avoid a hard conversation. Give her the whole account today, including the part where you decided not to tell her sooner.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've called it protecting her when it was really protecting myself from a conversation I didn't want to have. Give me the courage to give my wife the whole truth, not the edited version. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

EPHESIANS 4:25
