# Day 25 — Integrity When It's Inconvenient

The scrape was small — a thin gray line along the bumper of a sedan two spaces down from Lachlan's rental car, in the far corner of an airport economy lot at 5:40 in the morning. He'd clipped it backing out, barely felt it. When he got out to look, there was no one else in sight. No cameras that he could see. No dent, just a smudge of paint, the kind of mark a lot of owners wouldn't even notice for months.

His flight boarded in fifty-five minutes, and he still had to return the rental, catch the shuttle, and clear security. Leaving a note meant finding paper, finding a pen, writing down his information, and hoping the owner wasn't the type to make it more complicated than it needed to be. Every minute he spent standing in that lot was a minute closer to sprinting through a terminal.

He caught himself already halfway back to his own door before he'd decided anything, and that was the detail that stopped him. Not the scrape, but how fast his body had started making the choice before his conscience got a vote. Since when did doing the right thing come with an asterisk for how busy a man is? He'd never have said out loud that honesty is optional when it's inconvenient. But that's the deal he was about to make without naming it: *the truth still matters, unless telling it costs me something I don't have time for.*

Micah asks a question that cuts straight through that kind of hedge, and it deserves to be heard in full. God has already shown a man what's good, Micah says, and asks nothing more complicated of him than to act justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with his God. Notice what's missing from that summary. No clause exempting a man who's running late, no asterisk for inconvenience. Justice is required, full stop. The same requirement whether it costs you fifty dollars or fifty-five minutes, not a virtue you only get to practice when the timing works out.

Most men don't fail integrity in the big, obvious tests. They fail it in this kind of moment instead — small, private, easy to justify, gone in sixty seconds if they just keep walking. The real question was which version of himself Lachlan was willing to be when being the other version was free and no one would ever know, not whether he made his flight.

He went back. Found a gas receipt in his glovebox, wrote his name and number on the back, tucked it under the wiper, and ran the rest of the way to the shuttle. He missed nothing but a few minutes of margin he didn't need.

**Today:** Say it out loud: *doing what's right doesn't come with a convenience clause — if it's true when it's easy, it's true when it costs me time.* Then look for one place today where honesty is going to cost you a few inconvenient minutes, and do it anyway, on time, without shrinking it down to fit your schedule.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, You don't ask me to do justice only when it's convenient, and I confess how often I've acted as if that were the deal. Give me the discipline today to let what's right cost me my time without resentment. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MICAH 6:8
