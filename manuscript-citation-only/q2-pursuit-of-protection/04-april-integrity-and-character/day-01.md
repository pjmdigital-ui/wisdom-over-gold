# Day 1 — The Lie of "No One Will Know"

Derek did his expense report on Sunday nights, kitchen table, laptop open, the rest of the house asleep. Tonight it was the Denver trip — three days, one closed deal, a stack of receipts he was matching against the company portal one line at a time.

He got to Tuesday night. He'd eaten alone at the airport, a burger at the gate bar, wolfing it down between a delayed flight and a missed connection. Nobody from the client had been anywhere near him. But the form had a field for it, and the field paid better than "personal meal" did, so he typed "Client Dinner — Discussed Q3 Renewal" and bumped the total up eleven dollars to round out the tip he never actually left.

It took him four seconds. He didn't think of it as lying. He thought of it as filling out a form the way forms get filled out — everybody pads a little, the company builds it into the budget, nobody's ever going to pull this specific line and ask him to produce a receipt for a conversation that never happened. He hit submit and felt nothing. That was the part worth noticing, if he'd been paying attention: it didn't even register as a decision.

The belief running underneath that four seconds was simple, and Derek would have denied holding it if you'd asked him directly: *if no one sees it, it doesn't cost anything.* Not the company's ledger, not his own character, not his standing before God — none of it, because none of it was being watched. He'd never once said this sentence to himself. He'd just lived by it, the way a man can hold a belief his whole life without ever hearing himself say it out loud.

Scripture answers that belief without raising its voice. Hebrews 4:13 puts it plainly: nothing in all creation is hidden from God — everything about a man is exposed and fully known to the One he actually answers to, not eventually, not if it happens to surface, but right now, already. There was no moment in that transaction that went unseen. Derek just assumed the only eyes that counted were the ones with a company badge.

This is where most men's integrity actually gets decided — not in the visible tests, the ones with witnesses and consequences, but in the eleven-dollar rounding errors nobody will ever audit. Get honest in the room with no windows and you've found out who you really are. Stay dishonest there and you've built a second man, a private one, who runs the real numbers behind closed doors while the public one keeps up appearances. That second man doesn't stay contained to expense reports. He shows up everywhere eventually, because he's the one you actually are.

**Today:** Go back to one small thing this week you shaded because you were sure no one would check — a report, a text, a half-true excuse. Correct it, even if the correction costs you something small. Then say out loud: "Nothing is hidden from God, so nothing is actually free."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've told myself plenty of things don't count because no one was watching, and I know now that isn't true — You were watching every time. Give me the courage to be as honest in the dark as I am in the light. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
