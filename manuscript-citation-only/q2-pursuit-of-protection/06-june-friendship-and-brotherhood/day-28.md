# Day 28 — Closing Week Four

Trent almost skipped this week. Not the reading — he'd kept up with that — but the parts that asked him to actually do something. Week four had been the hardest stretch of the month by a wide margin, and looking back at his notes now, he could see exactly where he'd stalled. He'd read the entry on surface-level friendships and thought of three men immediately, then closed the notebook without writing their names down. He'd read the one on competition and felt something sour turn over in his stomach about a friend's new truck, and hadn't wanted to look at it directly. The day on being truly known, he'd skimmed. The day on betrayal, he'd sat with longer than any entry all month, because it wasn't hypothetical for him.

That's worth naming plainly before the week closes: obstacles showed up this week that hadn't shown up in weeks one through three, and they were harder because they were about the friendships already in Trent's life — the shallow ones he'd have to either deepen or admit were shallow, the envy he'd rather not examine, the wound from a friend who'd hurt him, and the fear that opening up to a new one would just hand out a second knife.

Scripture doesn't offer a shortcut around any of that. What it offers instead is a posture to hold while the harder repairs happen. 1 Peter 4:8-10 puts love for one another ahead of everything else, because that love is what covers over so much wrongdoing, and pairs it with hospitality freely given and gifts freely used for each other's good, as stewards of what God has entrusted. Above all. Not after the friendship feels safe again, not once the competition has settled down, not conditional on the other man deserving it first. The love comes first, and it's the thing that makes the covering, the hospitality, and the honest use of what you've been given even possible.

Trent's obstacle this week was a pattern of looking away from exactly the friendships that needed the most attention, because those were also the ones with the most at stake. That's common, and it's worth saying so directly: the deepest work in brotherhood rarely happens with a new acquaintance. It happens with the man you've already known for years, where the history is complicated and the risk of the conversation going badly is real.

Nobody finishes a week like this with every friendship repaired; the bar was only ever whether Trent stopped looking away — and by Friday, he had. He'd written the three names down. He'd texted the friend with the truck something honest about his own envy instead of pretending it wasn't there. Small movements, not a finished project. But real ones.

**Today:** Go back through this week's entries and pick the one you skimmed past fastest or resisted hardest — that resistance is information. Say out loud: *The friendship I've been avoiding is exactly the one that needs love first, not once it's earned.* Then take the smallest honest step toward that one man today.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, show me where I looked away this week instead of loving the men in front of me. Give me the steadiness to hold unfailing love for them even where it costs something. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 PETER 4:8-10
