# Day 24 — Afraid of Being Truly Known

Vance had a men's group he showed up to most Thursday mornings, coffee in a paper cup, same six guys, same booth at the diner. He talked about work. He talked about the Cowboys. Once, carefully, he mentioned that the third quarter had been tight at the shop. What he never mentioned was the two nights a week he lay awake running numbers that didn't work, or the argument with his wife that had ended with her sleeping in the guest room, or the fact that he hadn't been to confession in eleven months because he didn't trust himself to say the actual thing out loud, even in a box built for exactly that.

He liked these men. He would have called any of them for a ride at midnight. But there was a door he kept shut around all six of them at once, and he'd shut it so long ago he'd stopped noticing it as a decision.

Ask Vance why, and he wouldn't say it in these words, but it's there under the silence: *If they saw all of it, they'd think less of me — better to be liked for the parts I can control than known and found wanting.* It's a quiet bargain. Approval now, in exchange for never finding out whether he'd still be wanted with the door open.

Scripture describes friendship among believers in a specific way that Vance's arrangement doesn't match. 1 John 1:7 ties real fellowship directly to walking in the light, the way God himself is in the light — that's where the cleansing Christ's sacrifice provides actually reaches a man. Fellowship — real fellowship, not the diner-booth kind — happens in the light, not around it. The verse doesn't describe walking in the light and then also trying to look respectable. It says the light itself is where the cleansing and the connection both happen, at the same time, because they're the same movement. Staying half in shadow keeps a friendship from ever being as real as it could be.

Here's what Vance's bargain actually costs him: it works. Nobody at that table thinks less of him, because nobody at that table knows enough to think anything. He's safe and he's also, in the only sense that matters, still alone. The fear that drives the hiding — *they'd think less of me* — never gets tested, because he never gives them the chance to prove it wrong. A man can keep that fear alive indefinitely just by never walking into the light long enough to find out it was never true, or that the men who matter would have stayed anyway.

The men worth having in your life were given to you, if you have them, to walk in the light next to, not assembled to admire you — which only works if you're actually standing in it instead of managing the entrance.

**Today:** Choose one man from your circle and tell him one true thing you've been managing instead of sharing — the real number, the real fight, the real fear. Say to yourself first, out loud, before you do it: *Being fully known is not the risk. Staying hidden is the risk I've already been paying for.* Then make the call.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I confess how much easier it is to be liked than known. Give me the courage to walk into the light with the men You've put in my life, and give me a friend who receives me there without flinching. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 JOHN 1:7
