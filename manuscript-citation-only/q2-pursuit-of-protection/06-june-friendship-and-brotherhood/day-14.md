# Day 14 — Closing Week Two

Two weeks ago, this month opened with a man staring at a spreadsheet he hadn't shown another living soul: a business slipping toward real trouble, and a decision, made without even discussing it, that no one needed to know until he'd fixed it himself. The belief underneath that decision was never spoken out loud, but it ran the whole show: a man who needs help hasn't really got it handled, so handle it, alone, until it's fixed or it isn't.

This week gave that belief five straight counterexamples, and they're worth holding side by side before the month moves on. Jonathan risked his own claim to the throne rather than let David face Saul's court without an ally. St. Paul, chained in a Roman cell awaiting execution, still wrote asking for Luke and Mark by name. St. Ambrose gave years of unglamorous, patient preaching to a restless young man who wasn't even sure he wanted what Ambrose was offering. St. Augustine, once formed, spent the rest of his life staying inside community rather than treating his own conversion as a finish line. Sts. Basil and Gregory pushed each other toward holiness for decades, through disagreement and separation and reunion, until history couldn't separate one man's story from the other's.

None of them handled it alone. Not one. And in every case, history remembers them partly through the other man standing next to them in the record, more than for any self-reliance of their own.

Scripture named this at the very start of the month, and it's worth returning to now with the weight of two weeks of evidence behind it instead of just an opening claim. Ecclesiastes 4:9-10, the passage about two men doing better together than one alone, because the one who falls has someone there to lift him back up, while the man with no one nearby is left in real trouble. That verse is a warning about what happens to a man with no one positioned to lift him when he falls. It's not a statement about efficiency. Every man falls eventually, whether it's a business, a marriage, or his own character giving way under pressure he never told anyone he was carrying.

The man from two weeks ago was exposed for hiding a struggling business, not weak for having one. That's the same way every man is exposed the moment he decides his trouble is his alone to carry. Jonathan, Paul, Ambrose, Augustine, Basil, and Gregory succeeded, in part, because they refused to fall alone, not because they were stronger than that man.

**Today:** Think back to whatever you've been carrying alone since this month started. Say plainly: "I am not weaker for needing someone positioned to lift me. I am exposed without him." Name one person you can actually put in that position, and tell him what you've been carrying.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, these two weeks have shown me how many good and faithful men refused to walk alone, and how much of my own instinct still runs the other way. Give me the courage to let someone stand close enough to catch me when I fall. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ECCLESIASTES 4:9-10
