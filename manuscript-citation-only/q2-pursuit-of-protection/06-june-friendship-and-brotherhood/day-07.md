# Day 7 — Closing Week One

Sunday night, before the week resets, is a good time to look back at what you've actually read this week, not just what you meant to do with it. A man hiding a real number from everyone who could have helped him. A driveway at eleven at night and a contact list with no one worth calling. A father at a fence who hasn't made a real friend in fourteen years. A parking lot conversation dodged with the word "good." A waiting room chair and an emergency contact field left blank. Different men, different scenes, but pull back far enough and it's the same shape traced six different ways.

None of those men set out to be isolated. That's worth sitting with before the week closes. Not one of them woke up and decided to cut himself off from other men. Each one made a series of small, reasonable-sounding calls: I'll handle this myself for now, I don't want to be a burden, there's no time to build a new friendship, it's easier to say "good" than explain. Each small call felt justified in the moment it was made. Isolation rarely arrives as a decision. It arrives as a pattern of exits that each looked defensible on their own.

Scripture has a name for that pattern, and it isn't flattering. Proverbs 18:1 describes a man who cuts himself off from others as one hunting for excuses to override good sense itself. These are reasons that sound like wisdom but work like exits. Busy is one. Handling it myself is one. I don't want to burden him is one. Every one of them can be true in a narrow sense and still be pulling a man away from the very judgment he needs other men around to keep sharp. A man who's cut off doesn't usually know he's cut off. He just knows he's had a reasonable week of reasonable decisions.

This is the question worth asking before the week is over: what excuse have you been using? Not the general shape of it, the actual sentence. The one you'd say out loud if someone asked why you hadn't called, hadn't told the truth, hadn't followed up. Find that sentence, because it's the thing standing between you and everything you've read the last six days.

**Today:** Go back through this week and find the one action step you read but didn't do, the call, the honest answer, the follow-up text. Do it today, before the week actually ends. If more than one applies, pick the one you're most tempted to keep skipping.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, this week has shown me excuses I've been calling wisdom for years, reasons that sounded sound and were really just exits from the men You placed around me. Break through whatever sentence I've been hiding behind. Give me one real act of brotherhood before this week closes, not just an intention to start one later. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
