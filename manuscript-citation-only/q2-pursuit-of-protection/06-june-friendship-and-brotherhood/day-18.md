# Day 18 — Giving Counsel Without Playing God

Douglas had been meeting with Ezra for coffee every other Thursday for almost a year, ever since Ezra started at the parish men's group fresh out of a broken engagement. Douglas liked the role. He liked being the guy with more mileage, the one who'd already made the mistakes Ezra was about to make and could see them coming from a mile off.

So when Ezra mentioned he was thinking about taking a job two states away, more money, a real title, but far from the family and the parish community he'd finally started to put down roots in, Douglas didn't ask questions. He told him not to take it. He laid out the whole case, twenty years of hindsight packed into fifteen minutes, and expected Ezra to see the sense in it the way Douglas already had.

Ezra went quiet on the drive home and didn't text for two weeks.

The belief working in Douglas was closer to if I've earned the right to speak into his life, I've earned the right to decide for him than to any bad intent. Those are two different rights, and mixing them up turns a mentor into a small god standing over another man's decisions instead of a brother standing beside them. Ezra needed Douglas to help him see the decision clearly enough to make it himself, not to make the call for him.

Scripture is specific about what counsel is for. Proverbs 11:14 says a people without guidance comes to ruin, while safety is found in having many counselors. Safety, not certainty. Counselors surround a decision with wisdom from more angles than one man can see alone. They don't replace the man's own judgment, and the verse never says the counselor gets the final word. A council advises. A king decides. Without ever putting it to a vote, Douglas had appointed himself both.

The harder discipline for a man like Douglas isn't finding something wise to say. He's got plenty of that. It's asking a real question and then sitting in the silence while Ezra works out his own answer, even when Douglas is fairly sure he already knows what that answer should be. Real mentoring hands the weight of the decision back to the other man instead of carrying it for him. That's slower. It's also the only version of counsel that actually builds a man instead of just borrowing Douglas's judgment for a season and handing it back unused the moment Douglas isn't in the room.

Douglas called Ezra back, not to walk anything back, but to ask the question he should have led with: what did Ezra actually want, once the noise of the offer settled down.

**Today:** If you're mentoring or advising another man, replace your next piece of unsolicited advice with a question that hands the decision back to him, something like "what's the version of this you'd choose if you weren't worried what I'd think?" Then say to yourself before you speak again: "My job is to widen what he can see, not to make the choice for him."

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for the times I've mistaken my opinion for Your authority over another man's life. Teach me to counsel like a brother and not a judge. Give me the patience to ask instead of decide. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
