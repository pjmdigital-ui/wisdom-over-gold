# Day 22 — Surface-Level Friendship

Shane counted them up one Saturday morning on the way to the course: eleven men he golfed with regularly, four he grabbed beers with after work most Fridays, a group text with nine guys from the parish men's group that fired off a joke or two every day. By any normal measure, Shane had a full social calendar and a wide circle. When his marriage started cracking that spring, he realized he didn't have a single one of them he could call and actually tell.

He ran through the list in his head before deciding to tell no one. The golf guys talked handicaps and business. The Friday crew talked sports and complained about work. The group text was jokes, prayer requests phrased vaguely enough to stay comfortable, and nothing else. Eleven, four, and nine men, and Shane was carrying the worst season of his marriage completely alone in a life that looked, from the outside, like it had no room left for loneliness.

The belief hiding underneath a full calendar like Shane's is easy to miss precisely because the calendar looks like proof against it: if I have enough friends around me, I don't need any of them to actually know me. Volume stands in for depth. A man can fill every evening of his week with company and still be completely unknown by everyone in the room, because unknown is exactly how he's kept things, comfortable, light, never past the layer where something real might get exposed.

Jesus draws the line between that kind of company and real friendship in one sentence to His disciples, in John 15:15. He tells them He no longer calls them servants, because a servant isn't let in on what his master is doing. He calls them friends instead, because He has made known to them everything He has heard from the Father. The mark of friendship, in His own definition, is being let in, being told what's actually going on, not kept at the distance a servant is kept at. Christ didn't just spend time with the apostles. He told them what He knew. Surface friendship keeps everyone at servant distance: pleasant, present, told nothing that matters.

Widening a circle was never going to fix what Shane had. He didn't need a twelfth golf partner. He needed to take one of the eleven and risk turning him into something closer than a golf partner, which meant being the one to go first, since none of them were going to volunteer to go deeper than the group had ever agreed to go. That's uncomfortable in a way that adding a new contact never is. It's also the only door out of a full calendar and an empty bench.

**Today:** Pick one man from your surface-level circle, the golf group, the work friends, the group text, and tell him one true thing you've never said to anyone in that group. Say it plainly to yourself first: "A wide circle isn't the same as a friend who knows me, and I'm the one who has to make the first one real."

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, show me where I've mistaken a full calendar for real friendship. Give me the courage to be known by at least one man instead of merely surrounded by many. Teach me to go first, the way Christ went first with His own friends. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

JOHN 15:15
