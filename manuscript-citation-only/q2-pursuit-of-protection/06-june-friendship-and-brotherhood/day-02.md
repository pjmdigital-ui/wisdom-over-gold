# Day 2 — The Myth of the Self-Made Man

Dominic's landscaping company ran four trucks. He had a payroll he could no longer meet without dipping into his own savings. He'd been doing exactly that for eleven straight weeks, telling himself each Friday it would be the last time. A big commercial contract had fallen through back in March. He never replaced it. The number in his head at 3 a.m. kept climbing. Nobody outside his accountant knew it existed.

At the Saturday coffee meetup his church men's group ran, someone always asked how business was going. "Growing fast, good problems," Dominic would say, and mean it as a joke that covered up a fact. He'd built the company from a single mower in the back of his truck twelve years earlier. The story he told about himself, and told well, was that he'd done it without owing anyone. That story had become the whole way he saw himself as a man. Admitting the truck payments were late would mean admitting the story was cracking. And that felt the same as admitting he'd never really been capable at all.

So he kept moving money nobody knew was moving. He kept answering "growing fast" to men who would have helped him in an afternoon if he'd told them the real number. Underneath all of it was a simple belief he'd never once examined: if I built this alone, I have to fix it alone, or everyone will find out I was never as capable as I let them believe.

Scripture doesn't accept that kind of math. Ecclesiastes 4:9-10 makes the case plainly. Two men working together get more out of their labor than one man alone, because when one of them stumbles, the other is right there to lift him back up. A man who falls with no one nearby to help him is in real trouble. Notice the verse doesn't promise the fall won't happen. It assumes it will. The only question it cares about is whether there's a hand nearby when it does.

The self-made man is a story men tell after the danger has passed. They edit out every hand that pulled them up along the way. Nobody actually builds anything alone, not a company, not a marriage, not a life of faith. Dominic hadn't built his business alone either. He'd had a bank, a mentor who cosigned his first loan, and a wife who worked nights for two years so he could reinvest everything. He just stopped counting those the moment counting them got inconvenient for the story.

The real cost of guarding that story wasn't only financial. It was the eleven weeks he spent alone with a number no one else knew, falling by himself because he'd convinced himself the lifting-up part was for other men, not him.

**Today:** Call the one man who would actually help you if he knew the real situation, a mentor, an old friend, your own father. Tell him the actual number, whatever kind it is. Say the sentence out loud as you do it: I was never meant to carry this alone, and needing you doesn't undo what I've built.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I have let a story about being self-made keep me from the very hands You placed nearby to lift me. Forgive the pride that made hiding feel safer than asking. Give me one honest conversation today, and the humility to let it change something. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ECCLESIASTES 4:9-10
