# Day 1 — As for Me and My House

Jonah sat at the fold-out table in the parish hall with seven other men. Coffee going cold. The deacon went around the table asking a simple question: "What does the spiritual life of your house actually look like this week?" Most guys gave the answers Jonah would have given. Mass on Sunday. Grace before dinner, when everybody happened to be home. A rosary somewhere in the car console. When it got to him, he talked about the mortgage he'd never missed. He talked about the overtime he'd picked up so his wife could stay home with the kids. He talked about how nobody in his house was afraid of him. He felt good saying it. Solid.

The deacon nodded. Then he asked one more question, gently: "Who starts the prayer in your house?" Jonah opened his mouth and nothing came out. He thought about it the whole drive home. His wife said grace most nights. His wife signed the kids up for religious ed. His wife noticed when someone hadn't been to confession in a while. He kept the lights on. He'd been calling that leadership.

*As long as I provide and don't cause problems, I've done my job leading this family.* Jonah had never said that sentence out loud. But he'd been living inside it for years. He used it to measure himself against men who drank too much or walked out on their families. He always came out ahead. Next to that bar, he looked like a leader. He'd just never asked what he was actually leading toward.

Joshua asked Israel that same question at the end of his life. He'd spent decades watching them drift toward whatever god was easiest. He didn't lecture them about it. He let his own household's direction make the point instead. In Joshua 24:15, he tells the tribes to decide, right then, which god they will follow. Then he tells them what his own family has already decided, no matter what the rest of Israel picks. He didn't wait to see which way the room would go. He decided which way his own home would go first.

That was the piece Jonah had missed. A household doesn't just stand still while a man keeps the lights on. It drifts toward whatever actually gets modeled and started inside it. If nobody starts anything, it drifts toward whatever's easiest that day. Jonah's house already had a direction. He'd just never been the one setting it on purpose.

Providing is real. Keeping the peace is real. But neither one answers Joshua's question. A man can do both faithfully for thirty years. He can still never be the one his kids remember starting a prayer. He can still never be the one who asked a hard question about God at dinner, or named what the family actually believes and why. Somebody sets that tone in every house. It doesn't have to be the father. That's exactly the problem.

**Today:** Tonight, before the day ends, say this out loud where your family can hear it: "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." Then do one specific thing to back it up. Start grace yourself. Pray with your kids at bedtime. Or ask your spouse what she needs from you spiritually this week.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've measured my leadership by what I provide and what I avoid, and called that enough. Show me where my house has been drifting simply because I never set a direction on purpose. Give me the courage to lead its faith the way Joshua led his, plainly and out loud. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

JOSHUA 24:15
