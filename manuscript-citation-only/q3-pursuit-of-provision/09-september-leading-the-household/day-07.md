# Day 7 — Closing Week One

Seth sat in his truck in the driveway Sunday night, engine off. He wasn't in a hurry to go inside. It had been an ordinary week. Nothing had gone wrong. Nobody was sick. Nothing had blown up at work. He just felt strangely unsettled sitting there. He ran back through the last seven days and realized he couldn't point to one moment where he'd actually led his family toward anything. He'd shown up. He'd paid what needed paying. He'd kept the peace at dinner, even on the night everyone was tired and short with each other. None of that counted for nothing. But all of it still felt like maintenance, not direction.

He thought about a guy from his men's group. That guy had mentioned, almost offhand, that he prayed with his kids most mornings before school. Just a minute. Hands on their heads, then out the door. Seth had never done that. He'd never realized a minute like that was even available to him. He'd been treating an ordinary Tuesday morning as nothing but a scramble to get everyone out the door on time.

*Keeping things running is the same as leading them* — Seth had never said that out loud, but the way he'd spent the week showed he believed it. A house can run for years on a man who keeps things going and never once sets a direction. It's like a car idling in a driveway for an hour without going anywhere.

Psalm 127:1 landed on Seth right where he needed it, sitting in a driveway feeling like his effort hadn't added up to much. The verse teaches that a house built without the Lord behind it is effort wasted, no matter how hard the builder works. The verse doesn't excuse a man from building at all. It corrects whose house it actually is, and whose direction the builder is supposed to be working toward. A man can work at his household all week — the bills, the schedules, the repairs — and still be building something with no real foundation under it, if none of that work is aimed toward God.

That was the piece missing from Seth's week. Not effort. Direction. He'd kept the house standing. He hadn't once asked, out loud or on purpose, what the Lord wanted him building it toward.

**Today:** Before you go to bed tonight, write down one specific way you'll set a spiritual direction for your household this coming week. Not maintain it. Set it. A morning prayer with your kids. A hard conversation you've been avoiding. A decision you'll finally make instead of letting things drift.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've spent this week keeping my house running without ever asking whether I was building it toward You. Forgive me for mistaking maintenance for leadership. Show me one direction to set this coming week, and give me the follow-through to actually set it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
