# Day 28 — Closing Week Four

Callahan sat down Sunday night to do what he'd started doing most weeks this month. A few lines in a notebook before bed, about whatever the week had actually taught him, not what he'd meant to write. This week's page filled up faster than usual.

Monday had been about handing his kids' faith over to Sunday school and calling it covered. Tuesday had been a real scare. A phone call about his father's heart that turned out fine, but not before Callahan had to decide, in the space of an hour, whether his own fear would run the house or his faith would. Wednesday he'd caught himself teaching patience on Sunday and losing it with his own son on Tuesday. The gap between the two men was embarrassingly obvious once he wrote it down. Thursday had been his daughter's phone under the table, the loophole she'd learned from watching him work it. Friday, the sign of the cross passed down three generations through a grandfather Callahan never met but apparently still lives in his hands. Saturday's game night had gone sideways over a card played out of turn. Callahan had spent the drive home wondering when he'd started running his house like an audit.

Six different mornings, six different failures, one thread underneath all of them: Callahan had been treating his household like something to run. Assign the faith lessons. Manage the crisis. Correct the behavior. Keep the schedule. He hadn't been treating it like something to shepherd, which is a different job entirely.

St. Peter, writing to the elders of the early Church about this exact difference, could just as easily be writing to a father. In 1 Peter 5:2-3, he tells them to shepherd the flock entrusted to them. Not because they're forced to, but because they want to. Not chasing what's in it for them, but eager to serve. Not lording it over the people in their care, but leading by the example they set. Tend, not manage. Willingly, not because someone has to. By example, not by force. Every failure on Callahan's page that week traced back to one of those three words done backward. Faith handed off instead of tended. Patience demanded instead of modeled. Order enforced instead of offered.

A shepherd gets his flock to follow him by walking in front of them toward something worth reaching, day after ordinary day, until following him is just what they do. It doesn't come from being right more often than they are.

Callahan closed the notebook without a clean resolution to any single failure from the week. He had six specific ones and a better name for what he was actually supposed to be doing about all of them.

**Today:** Read back through your own week — the small failures you'd rather not examine — and write one sentence naming where you managed your household instead of shepherding it. Say Peter's phrase out loud as your own aim: *I want to lead by example, not by force.*

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, You have shepherded me my whole life without ever once forcing me toward You. Teach me to lead my own household the same way — by example, not constraint, tending instead of managing. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 PETER 5:2-3
