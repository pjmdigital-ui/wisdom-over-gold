# Day 10 — Steady in the Storm

The phone rang while Ethan was grilling burgers in the backyard. His three kids were running through the sprinkler behind him. His wife's voice on the other end was flat, the way a voice goes flat when someone's trying hard to sound normal. The radiologist wanted more pictures. Then a biopsy. Then, four days later, a callback appointment. They wouldn't have an answer until the following Tuesday.

Ethan hung up and stood at the grill a full minute before he moved again. His first instinct pulled him toward a version of himself with no feelings showing at all — the stone-faced provider who handles bad news by acting like he hasn't heard it. He served the burgers. He answered his daughter's question about the sprinkler timer. He said nothing to his kids about the call. Somewhere in that first minute, he decided that saying nothing, showing nothing, keeping his face exactly where it always sat, was what it meant to hold this family together.

The belief running underneath that decision was simple and mostly unexamined: a man holds his family together by not letting any of them see him rattled. Show them fear, and the whole house starts to shake with it.

Joshua faced a lot more than a callback appointment. An entire people depended on him to lead them into land held by armies with more experience than his own. God didn't tell him to fake calm until the fear passed on its own. In Joshua 1:9, God commands him to be strong and brave, and to refuse to let fear or dread take hold. God grounds that whole command in one promise: wherever Joshua goes, the LORD his God goes with him. The strength in that verse doesn't come from Joshua's own nerve. It comes from that promise of God's presence — the real reason Joshua could set the fear down, not a trick for hiding that he'd ever felt it.

Ethan's silence at the grill was a performance of strength, not the real thing. And performances take everything a man has to keep running for four days straight. His wife needed a husband who could sit with her in the waiting. She didn't need a wall pretending the waiting wasn't happening. His kids didn't need a father who showed no feelings. They needed one whose fear didn't get to run the house, because he'd handed it to something sturdier than his own composure could ever be.

Tuesday's results came back clear. But the four days in between taught Ethan something the good news almost let him skip right past. The steadiness his family actually needed from him was never his to build in the first place. It had been on offer the whole time, from a God who'd already promised to be present before Ethan ever picked up that phone.

**Today:** Name a place in your marriage or fatherhood right now where you've been performing calm instead of leaning on God's presence for real. Tell your wife the truth about how you're actually doing, out loud, tonight: "I'm not the one holding this together. God is, and I get to lean on that instead of faking it."

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for staying present through a week I didn't handle as well as I wanted to. Forgive me for hiding behind a calm face instead of leaning on You where my family could see it. Let my steadiness come from Your presence this week, not my own performance. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

JOSHUA 1:9
