# Day 26 — Turning the Hearts

Micah watched his son kneel next to his own two-year-old before bed. His son walked the boy's small hand through the sign of the cross, the way you do with a toddler who doesn't have the coordination for it yet. The boy paused at his forehead a half-second longer than the motion needed. A small hitch in an otherwise smooth gesture, before he finished the rest of the sign.

Micah had seen that exact hitch before. His own grandfather did it. The same pause at the forehead, unhurried, almost stubborn about it, like he wanted that part of the prayer to land before he let his hand move on. Micah's grandfather died when Micah was twelve. Micah couldn't remember a single conversation about faith with the man. No lesson, no instruction, nothing that would explain how that exact gesture had traveled three generations to a two-year-old who would never meet him. It passed through a middle generation — Micah's own son — who probably didn't know he was passing it on, any more than Micah had known he was carrying it.

Micah had spent years assuming his own influence on his family had a shelf life. He figured whatever good he managed to plant would need him alive and present to keep it growing. He figured that once he was gone, whatever he hadn't gotten to would simply stay undone. Watching his grandson's hand pause at his own forehead undid that assumption in about four seconds.

The prophet Malachi closes the entire Old Testament with a promise about exactly this kind of movement across time. In Malachi 4:6, he prophesies that God will turn fathers' hearts toward their children, and children's hearts back toward their fathers, so the land will be spared a curse. The turning runs in both directions, across more distance than any one father can see from where he's standing. A man plants something real in his children with no guarantee he'll see it bear fruit in his own lifetime, let alone in a grandchild he might never meet.

**On St. Joachim:** Scripture doesn't record a single word Joachim said. Tradition holds him as the father of Mary and grandfather of Jesus, a man whose whole visible legacy is two names he almost certainly never lived to hear spoken the way the world speaks them now. Joachim raised a daughter faithfully enough that she could say yes to an angel with her whole soul already turned toward God. Whatever he did to shape her, he did it without ever knowing the child she would carry. He turned a heart toward God and never saw the harvest. Neither will most fathers. That was never the point.

**Today:** Write down one thing about your own father, grandfather, or another man's example that shaped you — something he likely never knew had landed. Then name one thing you're doing now, unremarkable and probably unnoticed, that you're trusting God to carry forward the same way.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for the faith that reached me through men who never saw what it became. Let what I do today outlast my own sight of it, turning the hearts You've given me even after I'm gone. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MALACHI 4:6
