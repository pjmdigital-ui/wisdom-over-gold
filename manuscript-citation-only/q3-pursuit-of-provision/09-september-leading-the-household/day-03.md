# Day 3 — Headship as Self-Gift

Cyrus had a system, and everyone in the house knew it without him ever explaining it out loud. Sunday afternoons were his. The recliner was his. When he got home from a long week, the kids knew to keep the noise down for the first twenty minutes while he decompressed, and his wife knew not to hand him a decision that required real thought until after dinner. He thought of himself as the head of the household, and in his mind that word came with certain arrangements attached — deference, quiet, first pick.

It took his oldest son saying something offhand — "Dad's the boss, so we just wait him out" — for Cyrus to hear how that arrangement actually sounded from the outside. He'd never once thought of himself as someone the family had to manage around. He'd thought of himself as the one leading. Somewhere along the way those two things had gotten switched in his head without his noticing.

Scripture gives headship a definition, and it isn't the one Cyrus had been living. In Ephesians 5:25, St. Paul tells husbands to love their wives the way Christ loved the Church — by handing Himself over for her. Christ's headship over the Church was the reason He emptied Himself for the people under His care, all the way to the cross, not a position that got Him served. St. Paul hands that same pattern to husbands, not as a suggestion but as the actual shape leadership is supposed to take in a home. Whatever authority a man carries in his household exists to be spent on the people in it, not collected by him.

That reframes more than Sunday afternoons. A man who thinks leading means being accommodated will run his whole house that way without ever calling it that — the last one asked, the first one served, the one whose mood everyone else has to read and work around. A man who understands headship as self-gift asks a different question before he claims anything for himself: who in this house needs something from me right now that I haven't offered yet?

Cyrus didn't need to give up the recliner. He needed to notice how much of his authority in that house had been running one direction — toward himself — instead of outward, toward the people he was supposed to be leading.

**Today:** Find one place this week where you've been letting your household accommodate your comfort by default — your schedule, your mood, your preferences — and deliberately hand that space to someone else instead. Tell your wife or kids directly what you're doing and why.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've called myself the head of this house while arranging it to serve me first. Show me where my leadership has been collecting instead of giving. Teach me to spend whatever authority You've given me on the people You've placed under my care, the way Christ spent Himself on His Church. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

EPHESIANS 5:25
