# Day 3 — Headship as Self-Gift

Cyrus had a system. Everyone in the house knew it, even though he never explained it out loud. Sunday afternoons were his. The recliner was his. When he got home from a long week, the kids knew to keep the noise down for the first twenty minutes while he unwound. His wife knew not to bring him a decision that needed real thought until after dinner. He thought of himself as the head of the household. In his mind, that word came with certain arrangements attached: deference, quiet, first pick.

It took his oldest son saying something offhand for Cyrus to hear how that arrangement actually sounded. "Dad's the boss, so we just wait him out." He'd never once thought of himself as someone the family had to manage around. He'd thought of himself as the one leading. Somewhere along the way, those two things had gotten switched in his head, and he never noticed.

Scripture gives headship a real meaning, and Cyrus hadn't been living it. In Ephesians 5:25, St. Paul tells husbands to love their wives the way Christ loved the Church. Christ loved the Church by giving Himself up for her, all the way to the cross. He led the Church by spending Himself on the people in His care, not by being served by them. St. Paul gives husbands that same pattern as a command, not just an idea to think about. Whatever authority a man carries in his household exists to be spent on the people in it, not collected by him.

That changes more than Sunday afternoons. A man who thinks leading means getting accommodated will run his whole house that way, without ever calling it that. He'll be the last one asked. The first one served. The one whose mood everyone else has to read and work around. A man who understands headship as giving himself asks a different question before he claims anything for himself: who in this house needs something from me right now that I haven't offered yet?

Cyrus didn't need to give up the recliner. He needed to notice how much of his authority in that house had been running one direction: toward himself. It needed to run the other way too, out toward the people he was supposed to be leading.

**Today:** Find one place this week where you've been letting your household work around your comfort by default — your schedule, your mood, your preferences. Hand that space to someone else on purpose instead. Tell your wife or kids directly what you're doing and why.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've called myself the head of this house while arranging it to serve me first. Show me where my leadership has been collecting instead of giving. Teach me to spend whatever authority You've given me on the people You've placed under my care, the way Christ spent Himself on His Church. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

EPHESIANS 5:25
