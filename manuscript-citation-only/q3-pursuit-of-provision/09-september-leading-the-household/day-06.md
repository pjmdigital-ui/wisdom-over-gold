# Day 6 — The Father Who Formed a Saint

Landon had been getting up twenty minutes early most mornings that year. He prayed before the house woke up. Nothing dramatic. Just a chair in the corner of the kitchen, a Bible, a cup of coffee going cold while he sat with it. He never mentioned it to his kids. He figured they didn't notice, or wouldn't care if they did. Then one morning his twelve-year-old wandered in early for a glass of water. She saw him sitting there and said nothing. She just filled her glass and left. Weeks later, she mentioned to her mother, almost in passing, that she liked knowing Dad prayed every morning "even when nobody made him."

Landon hadn't set out to teach her anything. He'd just kept showing up to that chair. He never realized a habit nobody praised or noticed could still be doing real work.

*If it's not visible or dramatic, it's probably not accomplishing much* — that was the assumption Landon had been living by. It's the same one that makes a lot of men save real spiritual effort for moments that feel important, and let ordinary mornings slide. Moses told Israel something that cuts against that assumption. In Deuteronomy 4:9, he tells them to watch themselves closely so they never lose hold of what they've seen of God. He tells them to keep it alive in their hearts for the rest of their lives, and to pass it on to their children and grandchildren on purpose. That instruction calls a man to keep watch over his own soul, day after ordinary day, and let his children see what he's holding onto.

**On St. Louis Martin:** the father of St. Thérèse of Lisieux, canonized alongside his wife, St. Zélie, ran an ordinary household by any outside standard. He rose early to pray. He took his daughters to Mass and adoration without making a show of it. His temperament wasn't naturally patient, but he stayed patient anyway. He stayed faithful to ordinary practices for decades, with no way of knowing what they'd produce. What they produced was a daughter who became one of the most influential saints of the modern Church. She said plainly that her father's example had shaped her more than any sermon. Louis never planned that outcome. He just kept getting up.

Landon's kitchen chair was doing the same thing, just on a much smaller scale. He had no way yet to know what it was building.

**Today:** Name one small, plain practice of faith you've been doing in private — prayer, Mass, confession, a habit nobody's ever praised you for. Let your kids see it happen at least once this week, without turning it into a lesson.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've assumed the parts of my faith nobody notices don't count for much. Show me that You're building something in my children through those repeated, unnoticed moments, whether I ever see the result or not. Give me the patience to keep showing up to the ordinary practices, the way Louis did. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

DEUTERONOMY 4:9
