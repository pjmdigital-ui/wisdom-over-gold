# Day 24 — Doers, Not Hearers Only

Booker ran the men's Sunday morning study at his parish. It was an eight-week series on patience, one he'd more or less picked for himself because it was what he needed to hear most. That week's session had gone well. He'd walked six other men through Proverbs on the tongue. He'd talked about counting to five before responding to your kids. He closed with a line that got a couple of nods: the moment you feel your voice rising is the moment leadership either happens or doesn't.

Tuesday night his son left the garage door open all night. The truck battery was dead Wednesday morning. Booker had a 7:30 call he couldn't move. He found out at 7:15, standing in the driveway in the cold with jumper cables. By the time the engine turned over, he'd said things to a fourteen-year-old that no amount of coffee could walk back. His son didn't argue. He just went quiet, the kind of quiet that meant the words had landed exactly as hard as they sounded.

Driving to his call, still worked up from the fight, Booker ran back through Sunday's lesson, almost against his will. The same words, the same voice, forty-eight hours old and already broken by the man who taught them. He'd taught six men how to lead their households well. He'd apparently taught his own son nothing that survived one dead battery.

The belief underneath had never been said out loud, but it had been running his Sundays for years: *if I know the right answer and can say it clearly to a room, I'm doing my job as the head of this house.* Teaching had become a comfortable stand-in for actually living what he taught. It was a leadership credential he could point to, instead of proof he had to produce at home.

St. James writes to believers who'd made the same trade. They treated hearing the right thing as the same as living the right thing. In James 1:22, he tells them to actually put God's word into practice, not just listen to it. Listening alone, without acting on it, is a way of fooling yourself. That word for self-deception is the ordinary word for reasoning falsely with yourself, the kind of trick a man plays on his own mind rather than one an outsider pulls on him. A man can sit under good teaching, even give good teaching, and still be lying to himself about whether any of it has reached his own kitchen.

Leading a household gets proven at 7:15 on a Wednesday, in a cold driveway, with a kid watching to see if the man who talked about patience two days earlier has any left for him. It doesn't get proven in front of six men on a Sunday who already agree with him. Booker's son wasn't going to remember the study series. He was going to remember the driveway.

**Today:** Go to whoever heard the harder version of you this week — the one your Sunday self would be ashamed of — and say the true belief out loud to them: *Leading this house means living it, not just knowing it.* Then name specifically what you'll do differently the next time it's hard.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for the gap between what I say I believe and what my family actually sees me live. Close that gap in me — not just in what I know, but in what I do the next time I'm tired and tested. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
