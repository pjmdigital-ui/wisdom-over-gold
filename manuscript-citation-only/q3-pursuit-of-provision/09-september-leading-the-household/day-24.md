# Day 24 — Doers, Not Hearers Only

Booker ran the men's Sunday morning study at his parish, an eight-week series on patience he'd more or less chosen for himself because it was the thing he needed to hear most. That week's session had gone well. He'd walked six other men through Proverbs on the tongue, talked about counting to five before responding to your kids, and closed with a line that got a couple of nods: the moment you feel your voice rising is the moment leadership either happens or doesn't.

Tuesday night his son left the garage door open all night. The truck battery was dead Wednesday morning, and Booker had a 7:30 call he couldn't move. He found out at 7:15, standing in the driveway in the cold with jumper cables, and by the time the engine turned over he'd said things to a fourteen-year-old that no amount of coffee walked back afterward. His son didn't argue. He just went quiet in the way that meant the words had landed exactly as hard as they sounded.

Driving to his call, still worked up from the fight, Booker ran back through Sunday's lesson almost against his will — the same words, the same voice, forty-eight hours old and already broken by their own author. He'd taught six men how to lead their households well. He'd apparently taught his own son nothing that survived contact with a dead battery.

The belief underneath had never been said out loud, but it had been running his Sundays for years: *if I know the right answer and can say it clearly to a room, I'm doing my job as the head of this house.* Teaching had become a comfortable substitute for living the thing taught, a leadership credential he could point to instead of evidence he actually had to produce at home.

St. James writes to believers who'd made the same trade, treating right hearing as though it were the same as right living: "But be doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving yourselves" (James 1:22, RSV-CE). The word behind "deceiving" is the ordinary word for reasoning falsely with yourself — not an outside con, a self-administered one. A man can sit under good teaching, even give good teaching, and still be lying to himself about whether any of it has reached his own kitchen.

Leading a household gets proven at 7:15 on a Wednesday, in a cold driveway, with a kid watching to see if the man who talked about patience two days earlier has any left for him — not in front of six men on a Sunday who already agree with him. Booker's son wasn't going to remember the study series. He was going to remember the driveway.

**Today:** Go to whoever heard the harder version of you this week — the one your Sunday self would be ashamed of — and say the true belief out loud to them: *Leading this house means living it, not just knowing it.* Then name specifically what you'll do differently the next time it's hard.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for the gap between what I say I believe and what my family actually sees me live. Close that gap in me — not just in what I know, but in what I do the next time I'm tired and tested. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
