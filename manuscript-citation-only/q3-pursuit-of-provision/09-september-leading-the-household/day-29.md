# Day 29 — Found Trustworthy

Sullivan found the notebook where he'd kept it since the first of July, tucked in a kitchen drawer he had to dig through junk to reach. He hadn't opened it in a few weeks. Reading straight through three months of his own handwriting in one sitting felt different than living them one day at a time.

July's pages were about his hands. He'd started the month believing his job was just the price he paid to afford the rest of his life. By the end, he was treating the actual work in front of him — the calls, the deadlines, the unglamorous parts nobody saw — as the exact place God had put him to be faithful. Somewhere in the middle, he'd stopped chasing the next number to prove something about himself. He'd started building habits that made faithfulness something he did every day, not just by accident.

August's pages turned to money, and they were harder to read back honestly. He'd walked into the month assuming what he earned was simply his — his to spend, save, or hold onto however he saw fit. God's opinion on the matter had been a Sunday topic at best. A month of firstfruits, budgets, a long-overdue conversation with his wife about a balance he'd been hiding, and one big afternoon of giving away something that actually cost him wore that assumption down to almost nothing. The money had never really been his to keep. It had always been his to manage, and it had just felt like his to keep for years.

September's pages, still fresh, were about the house those first two months had been paying for, without his ever asking whether it was actually being led. Faith formation he'd been handing off. A temper that didn't match his Sunday lessons. A rule he'd been enforcing on his kids and breaking himself under the table. A house that ran on correction instead of joy. Different failures than July's or August's, but the same shape underneath: something entrusted to him, treated for a while like something he'd earned the right to run however he pleased.

St. Paul, defending his own ministry to a church quick to rank apostles against each other, names the standard that actually matters. In 1 Corinthians 4:1-2, he says people should think of him and his fellow workers as servants of Christ, stewards entrusted with God's mysteries. He says the one thing required of any steward is to be found trustworthy with what's been placed in his care. Trustworthy. Not clever. Not impressive. Not even, mainly, successful. That's the single word Paul uses for what a steward owes the owner of what's been entrusted to him.

Three months. Three different things entrusted to Sullivan: his hands and his hours, his money, his household. And the same single question sitting underneath all three the whole time, whether he'd noticed it or not: when nobody was checking, was he running what he'd been given like it belonged to God, or like it belonged to him?

Sullivan closed the notebook on a page that wasn't finished, because none of it actually was finished. Provision was going to keep asking that same trustworthy-or-not question for as long as Sullivan kept being handed things to take care of. It wasn't going to wrap up with one final entry that tied everything off. If the last three months were any sign, that was going to be true for the rest of his life.

**Today:** Read back over your own last three months. Use actual notes if you kept any, or your memory if you didn't. Write one sentence naming the single thread connecting how you handled your work, your money, and your household. Say it out loud: *I don't own what's been given to me. I only get to be found trustworthy with it.*

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thank You for three months that showed me how much of my life was never actually mine to begin with — my work, my money, my household, all on loan from You. Find me trustworthy with everything You've entrusted to me, today and every day after. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 CORINTHIANS 4:1-2
