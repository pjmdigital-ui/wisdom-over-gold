# Day 5 — Who Starts the Prayer

Travis's wife flew out for her sister's wedding on a Thursday night, which meant Travis had bedtime solo for the first time in months. He got the kids fed, bathed, and into pajamas, and was halfway out of his youngest's room when she stopped him. "Wait — we didn't pray." He stood in the doorway a second too long. In six years of bedtimes, he genuinely couldn't remember a single night he'd started the prayer himself. His wife always did. He'd never once thought about why that was, or noticed that his kids had filed prayer under "Mom's thing" the same way they'd filed packing lunches or picking out clothes.

He knelt down, said something short and a little clumsy, and felt his daughter watching him the whole time like it was a novelty. It was. That was the part that stayed with him after he turned off the light.

*She's just better at that stuff than I am* — Travis had told himself some version of that sentence for years, letting it excuse him from ever building the muscle himself. It sounded like humility. It functioned as an exit.

The psalmist describes exactly the transmission Travis had been skipping. God "commanded our fathers to teach [his testimony] to their children; that the next generation might know them, the children yet unborn, and arise and tell them to their children, so that they should set their hope in God, and not forget the works of God, but keep his commandments" (Psalm 78:5-7, RSV-CE). The instruction runs father to child, generation to generation, by design — not because mothers can't carry it, but because the psalm assumes both parents will, each modeling it in their own voice. A child who only ever sees one parent pray learns something true but incomplete: that prayer is optional for men, or at least not urgent enough for Dad to bother starting.

Travis's wife hadn't taken over family prayer because he was incapable of it. She'd taken it over because he'd let her, night after night, by simply never stepping in first. The habit he lacked was small and repeatable: being the one who says "let's pray" before someone else does. A kid doesn't need a theology lecture to learn that lesson. She needs to watch it happen, from both parents, often enough that it stops feeling like a novelty and starts feeling like how the house works.

**Today:** Tonight, before your spouse says a word about it, start the prayer yourself — at dinner, at bedtime, whenever your family prays together. Say it out loud in your own voice, even if it's short and plain.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've let prayer in my house become someone else's job by simply never volunteering to start it. Forgive me for the years my kids only heard one parent's voice leading them to You. Make me the kind of father who initiates prayer without being asked, so my children grow up expecting it from me too. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
