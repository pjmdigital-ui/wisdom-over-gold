# Day 5 — Who Starts the Prayer

Travis's wife flew out for her sister's wedding on a Thursday night. That meant Travis had bedtime solo for the first time in months. He got the kids fed, bathed, and into pajamas. He was halfway out of his youngest's room when she stopped him. "Wait — we didn't pray." He stood in the doorway a second too long. In six years of bedtimes, he couldn't remember a single night he'd started the prayer himself. His wife always did. He'd never once asked why. His kids had filed prayer under "Mom's thing," the same way they'd filed packing lunches or picking out clothes.

He knelt down and said something short and a little clumsy. His daughter watched him the whole time like it was something new. It was something new. That was the part that stayed with him after he turned off the light.

*She's just better at that stuff than I am* — Travis had told himself some version of that for years. It let him off the hook from ever building that habit himself. It sounded like humility. It was really just a way out.

The psalmist describes exactly what Travis had been skipping. Psalm 78:5-7 describes God commanding the fathers of Israel to teach His ways to their own children. He wanted each generation still unborn to grow up knowing what God had done. He wanted them to learn to put their hope in Him, and to keep His commandments instead of forgetting them. The instruction runs father to child, generation to generation, on purpose. That's not because mothers can't carry it. It's because the psalm expects both parents to teach it, each in their own voice. A child who only ever sees one parent pray still learns something. But it's incomplete. She learns that prayer is optional for men, or at least not important enough for Dad to start it himself.

Travis's wife hadn't taken over family prayer because he couldn't do it. She'd taken it over because he let her, night after night, by never stepping in first. The habit he lacked was small and repeatable: being the one who says "let's pray" before someone else does. A kid doesn't need a lecture on theology to learn that lesson. She needs to watch it happen, from both parents, often enough that it stops feeling new and starts feeling like just how the house works.

**Today:** Tonight, before your spouse says a word about it, start the prayer yourself — at dinner, at bedtime, whenever your family prays together. Say it out loud in your own voice, even if it's short and plain.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've let prayer in my house become someone else's job by simply never volunteering to start it. Forgive me for the years my kids only heard one parent's voice leading them to You. Make me the kind of father who initiates prayer without being asked, so my children grow up expecting it from me too. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PSALM 78:5-7
