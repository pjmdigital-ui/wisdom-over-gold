# Day 4 — Present in Body, Absent in Spirit

Ian got home at six, the way he did most nights. He kissed his wife, sat down at the table, and spent dinner with his phone face-up next to his plate. He wasn't doing anything urgent. Just checking scores, half-reading an email he'd answer tomorrow anyway. His daughter told a long story about a fight with her friend at recess. Ian nodded in the right places without catching most of it. His son asked him to help build something out of Legos afterward. Ian said "in a minute" three times, and the minute never came.

Nobody in the house would have called Ian a bad father. He came home every night. He didn't work weekends. He'd have called his evenings family time without a second thought. But if his kids had to describe him at six-fifteen on a Tuesday, they wouldn't have described a man who was there. They'd have described a man sitting in the right chair, with his head somewhere else.

*I'm home, so I'm present* — that was the math Ian had been running for years. He treated showing up as the whole measure of being a father. But being in the room and being present in it are two different things.

God says something specific about Abraham right before Sodom's judgment. It comes almost as an aside, but it shows what God actually valued in him. In Genesis 18:19, God explains why He chose Abraham: Abraham would direct his own children and household after him to keep to the Lord's way. God doesn't say it's because Abraham owned land near his children. He doesn't say it's because Abraham provided for them, or was technically present in the same tents. God singles him out because Abraham would actively guide his household. He would direct it, instruct it, and stay close enough to his own kids to actually shape what they believed and how they lived.

That's a different standard than just showing up. Guiding your kids takes real attention. It takes a man who's actually looking at his kids closely enough to notice the fight at recess. It takes a man who can follow the Lego instructions without half-listening, and catch the moment a nine-year-old is testing whether he'll be heard. Ian could keep every single evening at home and still fail that standard completely. Presence gets measured by where his attention actually goes once he's in the room, not by which room he's sitting in.

**Today:** Tonight, put your phone in another room for all of dinner and the hour after it. Give your full attention to whoever's talking to you. Stop what you're doing. Look at them. Respond to what they actually said, not what you assumed they said.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've counted being home as the same thing as being present, and my family has felt the difference even when I haven't noticed it. Forgive me for the evenings I gave them my body and kept my attention somewhere else. Help me charge my household the way You charged Abraham with his — engaged, watching, actually there. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

GENESIS 18:19
