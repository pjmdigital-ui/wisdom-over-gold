# Day 20 — Leading as Partners

Donovan texted his wife from the office on a Tuesday afternoon: *Moved book club to Thursday since Jack has practice Tues now. You've got pickup Tues/Thurs going forward, I'll handle Mon/Wed.* Efficient, clear, decided. He hit send feeling like a man who had the household calendar under control, which he did — he'd looked at everyone's schedule, found the conflict, solved it, and communicated the solution before it could become a problem. What he hadn't done was ask her a single question before deciding how her Tuesdays and Thursdays would work from now on.

She called that night, not angry exactly, but pointed: she had a standing commitment on Thursdays he'd apparently forgotten, and she would have told him that if he'd asked before texting her the new schedule as a done deal. Donovan's first instinct was defensiveness — he'd been trying to help, trying to lead, trying to keep the whole operation running smoothly so she wouldn't have to think about logistics at all. He ran the calendar the way he ran projects at work: gather the facts, decide efficiently, inform the team.

That instinct carried a belief he'd never examined closely: *as the leader of this house, my job is to decide things efficiently and inform everyone else.* It's a management posture, borrowed straight from the office, applied to a marriage where it doesn't fit. A team executes a plan handed down from above. A wife leading a household alongside her husband is something else entirely — and treating her like staff to be informed, however efficiently, demotes her out of the role scripture actually gives her.

St. Paul opens the household instructions in Ephesians with a line easy to skip past on the way to the more famous verses that follow it. In Ephesians 5:21, he tells believers to submit to one another out of reverence for Christ — mutual, not a chain of command running one direction from husband down. Whatever leadership means in the verses that come after this one, it sits inside a mutual submission that comes first and frames everything else. A man who only ever gives direction and never receives it isn't leading the way that verse describes; he's just managing.

Donovan called his wife back and asked what he should have asked that afternoon: what actually worked for her. They landed on a schedule neither of them would have arrived at alone. It took ten more minutes than the text had. It also meant she'd helped build the plan she was expected to live inside, instead of receiving one she'd had no hand in shaping.

**Today:** Before you make the next household decision — calendar, spending, plans for the weekend — ask your wife's view first and let it actually shape the outcome, instead of deciding and then informing her.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for managing my household instead of leading it alongside the woman You gave me to lead it with. Teach me to ask before I decide, and to value her wisdom as much as my own efficiency. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

EPHESIANS 5:21
