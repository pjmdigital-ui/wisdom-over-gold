# Day 30 — As for Me and My House, Again

Harlan stood in the same kitchen on the last night of September where, thirty days earlier, he'd first sat down with this book and read about a leader drawing a line in the sand for his entire household. Thirty days is not that long. It had been long enough to notice things about his own house he'd been walking past for years.

He thought back over specific mornings from the month: the morning he'd realized nobody in the house started family prayer except his wife, and that he'd let that be true by default rather than by decision; the Sunday he taught patience to six other men and lost his own with his son two days later over a dead truck battery; the Friday game night that folded early because he'd walked in from work and corrected a card played out of turn instead of just playing; the moment his daughter mimicked his own hidden phone habit back at him under the dinner table, teaching him more in four seconds than any lecture he'd ever given her.

A household gets shaped by several hundred small Tuesdays, not by a single decision made once under a spotlight — who starts the prayer, who sets the tone when the day's gone sideways, who apologizes first, who's actually present once he's home instead of just occupying a chair at the table.

Joshua stood before the tribes of Israel at the end of his own life and gave them a choice he'd already made for his own house before he ever asked them to make it. In Joshua 24:15, he tells them that if serving the LORD doesn't appeal to them, they need to decide that very day which god they'll serve instead — whatever their ancestors worshiped, or whatever gods belonged to the land they now lived in — and then he commits his own household's answer up front, regardless of what the rest of them choose. Thirty days earlier, that line had read like a thesis statement, something to agree with in principle. Harlan understood it differently now. Joshua was reporting a direction his house had already been headed for years, decision by ordinary decision, long before he ever stood up to say so.

A household follows the direction its man actually sets, whether he means to set one or not. Harlan had spent September learning that the direction gets decided in driveways and at game tables and under dinner tables, far more often than it gets decided in any single defining moment.

Provision, the whole quarter had been arguing, ran wider than a paycheck: hands offered to real work in July, money held open instead of closed in August, and now a household actually led rather than merely funded and left alone in September — three months, one steward, one Owner he was learning to answer to in every part of it.

What Provision built in Harlan this quarter, Posterity is about to ask him to hand off. October turns toward the marriage at the center of that household — the partnership his leadership either serves or overrides — and the months after it turn toward the fatherhood that leadership has to become up close, not just from the head of the table. As for Harlan and his house: the direction is set. What's left is deciding, one more ordinary day at a time, whether he keeps walking it.

**Today:** Say the words out loud in your own kitchen, in front of whoever's home to hear it: "As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD." Then name one ordinary decision tomorrow morning that will actually point your house in that direction.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for a quarter that taught me provision runs through my hands, my money, and now the household those first two are meant to serve — always wider than a paycheck. Let my house follow the direction I set for it because I set it toward You, on purpose, starting tomorrow morning. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

JOSHUA 24:15
