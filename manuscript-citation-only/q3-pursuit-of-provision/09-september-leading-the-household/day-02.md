# Day 2 — The Domestic Church

Ben's daughter climbed into bed one night. She asked why God let her grandmother get sick, if He could have stopped it. Ben sat on the edge of the mattress. The question landed somewhere he wasn't ready for. He gave her something short and vague about mystery and faith. He kissed her forehead and told her Sister Catherine could probably explain it better on Wednesday. She seemed satisfied. He wasn't.

Driving to work the next morning, the conversation kept coming back to him. Ben paid the tuition for religious ed without complaint. He drove the carpool. He signed every form. In his mind, that had always counted as handling his kids' faith. He'd handed it to people who knew more than he did, the same way he handed off oil changes and taxes. *Isn't that what CCD is for?* He'd never once questioned that thinking. Not until his daughter asked him something real, and he passed her off to someone else to answer.

Moses told the Israelites something different as they stood at the edge of the promised land. He didn't make it optional, and he didn't say someone else could do it. In Deuteronomy 6:6-7, he tells them to hold God's words in their own hearts first. Then he tells them to teach those words to their children constantly. At home. Out on the road. At bedtime. At first light. Woven into the normal rhythm of the day, not saved for one lesson a week. That instruction never mentions a teacher, a program, or a building. It's aimed at the man sitting in his own house, walking his own road, putting his own kids to bed.

The Church has always called the home a domestic church. There's a reason for that. It's the first and smallest parish most kids will ever belong to. The man of the house is its only priest by default, ready or not. Sunday school can give a child words and structure. It can't give a child the sight of her own father praying. It can't give her the sight of him wrestling honestly with a hard question, or admitting he doesn't have every answer but believes anyway. That only gets passed down at home, and only a parent actually living there can pass it down.

Ben had been paying for religious ed for years, and that was good. But he had let it become the whole job, not just support for a job that was always his to carry first. The tuition check was easy. The hard part was sitting back down on the edge of her bed and finishing the conversation he'd handed off.

**Today:** The next time one of your kids asks a real question about God, suffering, or faith, don't hand it off to Sunday school, a priest, or "we'll talk about it later." Sit down right then and answer as honestly as you can, even if your answer is "I don't fully know, but here's what I believe and why."

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've handed off the hardest questions in my own house to people who see my kids one hour a week, while I see them every day. Forgive me for treating this home as anything less than the first church my children will ever know. Give me the words and the nerve to teach them Yourself, through me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

DEUTERONOMY 6:6-7
