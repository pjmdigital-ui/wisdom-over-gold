# Day 26 — Walking Securely

Merritt's accountant emailed on a Tuesday asking for receipts to back up the "client entertainment" line on his Schedule C — eleven thousand dollars across the year, mostly meals and a couple of golf outings that were maybe a quarter business and three-quarters just Merritt spending time with people he liked, on his business's dime. He wrote her a partial answer and closed his laptop, the same way he'd closed it the last three times the topic had come close to the surface.

It wasn't outright fraud, he told himself, building the same defense he'd leaned on for two years of small write-offs that ran generous. Every contractor did some version of this. The number was small enough that nobody would ever look twice, and if they did, he had enough of a paper trail to argue his way through it. The belief underneath the defense was simpler than the defense itself: *what's actually true on the books matters less than whether anyone ever checks.*

Scripture states the opposite as a plain, unadorned fact about how the world actually works: "He who walks in integrity walks securely, but he who perverts his ways will be found out" (Proverbs 10:9, RSV-CE). The proverb names an exposure built into the crooked path itself, whether or not an auditor ever comes looking. Merritt had felt it for two years already, in the small flinch every time the accountant's name showed up in his inbox, in the extra beat before he answered a straightforward question about expenses. That flinch was the exposure already starting, with or without an IRS letter to make it official.

The "security" in the first half of the verse describes a man who can open any envelope, answer any question, and sit across from anyone without calculating first what he needs to hide. Merritt hadn't walked that way in two years, and the eleven thousand dollars he might save on his tax bill this year didn't come close to covering what the flinch had already cost him.

He called his accountant back that afternoon, told her the truth about which meals were real business and which weren't, and asked her to help him refile the difference honestly, even though it meant a real check written to the IRS he hadn't planned on. It cost him roughly four thousand dollars. The next email from her office didn't tighten his chest at all.

**Today:** Name the one number, receipt, or shortcut on your books — business or personal — you'd flinch to have someone actually examine. Make the call or send the correction today, even though it costs you something real, and say the true sentence out loud: *I would rather walk securely than protect a number nobody's checking yet.*

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, You already see every number I've been hoping nobody else looks at closely. Give me the courage to walk in integrity even when it costs me money I'd rather keep. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
