# Day 29 — Sell What You Possess

Duncan checked his brokerage app four times during his son's baseball game — a habit he'd started during a rough stretch of market swings two weeks earlier and hadn't managed to break even after the market steadied. His portfolio wasn't in any real danger. He'd built it slowly over fifteen years and didn't need to touch it for another decade at least. But every red number on the screen produced the same small drop in his stomach, and every green number produced the same small lift, as if his actual well-being rose and fell with a decimal point he refreshed between innings.

The following Sunday, his pastor mentioned from the pulpit that the parish's family life center was still short of its goal, and named a modest but specific figure that would close most of the remaining gap — a figure Duncan could write a check for without changing a single thing about his family's life. His first feeling was a flinch, sharp and immediate, not generosity — the same flinch that showed up when the market dipped. The number in his account was going to shrink either way, and some part of him minded a stranger's building project exactly as much as he minded a bad trading day, which told him more about where his security actually lived than any homily could have.

A rich young man asks Christ what he still lacks, having kept the commandments his whole life, and gets an answer aimed at the one place he hadn't checked. In Matthew 19:21, Christ tells him that if he wants to be complete, he should go sell what he owns, give it to the poor, and come follow Him — trading an earthly account for treasure in heaven. Christ doesn't ask every man He meets to sell everything. He asks this one particular man, because this man's wealth was the exact thing standing between him and the answer he came for. The test was whether he could let it go, not the size of what he had to give up.

St. Francis of Assisi heard that same call and took it further than almost anyone since. Born into real money — his father ran a prosperous cloth business — Francis didn't drift into poverty by accident. He chose it on purpose, and kept choosing it for the rest of his life. Years later, as his order grew and other friars pushed to relax the rule so they could own buildings, books, even modest property for stability, Francis resisted to the end. He'd learned that wealth owns a man simply by sitting there long enough for him to start guarding it instead of God.

Duncan's flinch gave an honest readout of what fifteen years of careful saving had become in his own chest — a second altar he checked between innings, not the tool it was meant to be.

**Today:** Write the check, make the gift, or give away the possession you've been gripping tightest — something that costs you a real number, not spare change — and say the true belief out loud: *My security was never supposed to live in this account. It lives in God.*

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, show me exactly where my grip has tightened around what I have instead of opening toward You. Let me hold what You've given me loosely enough to give it away when You ask. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MATTHEW 19:21
