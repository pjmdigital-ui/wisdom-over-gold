# Day 25 — Hastening to Be Rich

Beckett's phone buzzed during his lunch break with a message from a guy he'd played rec-league basketball with for two seasons: screenshots of a trading account up sixty percent in three weeks, a link to a private group, and a line that read "spots close at midnight, wanted you in before the rest of the group fills." The strategy involved leveraged options and a signal service Beckett didn't fully understand, which the message assured him didn't matter — "just follow the calls."

He had eleven thousand dollars sitting in a savings account, most of it earmarked for a kitchen remodel he and his wife had been planning for two years. He did the math on the walk back to his desk: if the sixty percent held even once more, that's not a remodel, that's the whole thing paid off with a cushion left over. He typed "I'm in" and didn't send it, not yet, but he didn't delete it either. The belief running the whole calculation, never once stated plainly, sounded something like this: *the slow way is for men who don't know how to spot a real opportunity when it's in front of them.*

Scripture doesn't leave much room to argue with that belief. Proverbs 28:20 promises rich blessing to the faithful man, and trouble to the one in a hurry to get rich. Faithful — not lucky, not aggressive, not well-connected. The blessing in this proverb belongs to the man who shows up consistently over time, and the second half names a real consequence waiting on the other end of the shortcut, whether or not the shortcut happens to work this once.

Beckett's friend probably wasn't lying about the sixty percent. What the screenshot couldn't show was the account before it, or the ten guys in the group who'd put in the same eleven thousand dollars and watched it go to zero on a signal that didn't hold. Fast money and steady money work in completely different ways. Beckett had spent two years building the remodel fund one paycheck at a time — every automatic transfer, every month he didn't touch it — and now he was weighing whether to gamble it away in one week, borrowing that patience to take a risk he never signed up for.

Two years of quiet, real discipline sat on one side of the decision. One text message from a guy he knew from a rec league sat on the other, dressed up as boldness. It was really just a guess.

**Today:** Delete the draft text and send a different one — decline the opportunity, plainly, without apologizing for it. Then move the eleven thousand dollars somewhere slightly less convenient to reach on impulse, and say the true sentence out loud: *Real freedom comes from faithful patience, not from a signal on someone else's phone.*

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thank You for the discipline it's taken to save what little I have. Keep me from trading years of faithfulness for one impulsive night, no matter how good the numbers look on someone else's screen. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
