# Day 31 — Whose Money Is This, Revisited

Rowan opened a fresh page in his notebook on August 1st and wrote a single line before he'd decided what the rest of the month would look like: *Everything I have is Yours, not mine.* He wrote it because that was the truth the month opened on, in Psalm 24:1, and it seemed like the kind of sentence a man should write down even before he believed it all the way. Thirty-one days of budgets, debt, a hard conversation with his wife, a redirected windfall, a scare with the market, and one long look at what he was actually gripping too tightly — and tonight he opened the notebook back to that first page and read his own handwriting again.

Rowan was the one who'd changed.

On August 1st, that line had read like poetry — true in the way a lot of true things are true, at a comfortable distance from his actual bank account. A month of decisions later, it read like an audit. Everything meant the retirement account he took real pride in. It meant the emergency fund he'd finally built. It meant the truck he'd paid off and the business he'd grown and the raise he'd earned through real, sustained effort nobody handed him. Every one of those things had felt, at some point this month, like the one exception the psalm must not have meant — surely it wasn't talking about that account, the one Rowan had actually worked for.

David wrote the psalm as a king with more claim to ownership than almost anyone alive in his kingdom, and he opened it in Psalm 24:1 by handing all of it back before he asked God for anything — declaring plainly that the earth and everyone in it belong to God alone. It was a clear-eyed statement of fact from a man secure enough in what he had to admit where it came from, not a confession of failure.

That's the difference a month makes when a man lets it. Every single day of August was really asking one plain question, dressed up differently each time: whose is it. A steward and an owner can write the same check, make the same gift, save the same amount — the difference only shows up in what happens when the money is threatened, questioned, or asked for, because an owner defends what's his and a steward simply asks what the actual Owner wants done with it.

Rowan closed the notebook without any illusion that he'd fully arrived at being a steward instead of an owner. Some days this month he'd managed it — the check he wrote without resentment, the number he corrected even though it cost him. Other days the old sentence had run the whole show without him noticing until later. That's likely to keep being true past tonight, in whatever comes next for how he leads what's been placed in his hands — not just the money, but the whole house money was only ever the most countable part of.

He wrote one more line under the first, closing the same page he'd opened the month on: *Still Yours. All of it, still Yours. Still, apparently, my job to remember it and not my right to forget it.*

**Today:** Read Psalm 24:1 out loud, slowly, over whatever you own outright — the account, the truck, the paid-off house — and let the sentence do the work it's meant to do. Then write down one specific way you'll hold something this week as a steward instead of an owner, and do it before the day ends.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, the earth is Yours, and so is everything in my hands I've been tempted to call my own this month. Thank You for thirty-one days that moved that sentence from something I could say to something I'm learning to actually believe. Carry that same trust into however You lead me next. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PSALM 24:1
