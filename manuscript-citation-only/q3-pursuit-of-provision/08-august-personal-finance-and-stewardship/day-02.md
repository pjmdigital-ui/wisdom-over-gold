# Day 2 — Firstfruits, Not Leftovers

Hugh built his family budget the way he built everything else — a spreadsheet with a tab for each category, color-coded, updated every Sunday night. Mortgage, insurance, groceries, the kids' activities, the retirement contribution he'd automated the day he got his first real paycheck and never touched since. Down at the bottom, under a tab labeled "Giving," sat a single cell with the word "TBD" he'd typed three years earlier and never once replaced with a number.

Hugh believed in giving, or would have told you generosity mattered to him — he just hadn't found the month where there was anything real left over to be generous with. Something always came first: the transmission that needed replacing, the orthodontist, the roof. He told himself he'd catch up at year-end bonus time, and some years he did throw a check at the parish before December closed out. Most years the bonus went to the transmission instead, and the "TBD" cell stayed exactly as it was.

*I'll give once everything else is covered* — that was the actual rule Hugh's spreadsheet had been running on since the day he built it, even though he'd never typed it anywhere. Giving sat last in line, behind every other claim on the money, waiting for a surplus that never arrived because surplus isn't how a budget with twelve other priorities ahead of it works.

Solomon writes the order the opposite direction: honor the LORD with what you have, giving Him the first and best of your income before anything else touches it, and — Solomon promises in Proverbs 3:9-10 — the rest follows in abundance, barns filled and vats overflowing. First fruits meant exactly what it sounds like to a farmer — not the harvest weighed and divided after the family had taken its share, but the earliest and best of the crop, set aside before anyone knew yet how the whole season would turn out. It was an act of trust made in advance, not a settlement made in arrears.

That ordering is the whole point, not a technicality. A man who gives from what's left has made every other claim on his money more urgent than God's. A man who gives first is declaring, before he knows how the month will go, that God gets the first word on his money instead of the last one. That promise describes the shape of a trusting life, not a formula for getting rich by tithing early.

Hugh needed to move his "TBD" cell to the top of the spreadsheet and fund it the same day his paycheck landed, ahead of the mortgage, the same automatic way his retirement contribution had worked for years.

**Today:** Set up your giving — to your parish, a ministry, someone in real need — as the first transaction after your next paycheck, not the last. If it has to be automated to actually happen, automate it today.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let my giving wait behind every other bill for years, as if You could only have what nothing else needed first. Teach me to trust You enough to give from the top of what I have, not the bottom. Move my heart to give before I know how the month will turn out. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PROVERBS 3:9-10
