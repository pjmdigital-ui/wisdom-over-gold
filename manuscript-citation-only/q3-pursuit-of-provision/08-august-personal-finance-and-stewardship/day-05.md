# Day 5 — Little by Little

Bryce found the group chat at eleven at night, forwarded by a guy he barely knew from his gym — forty screenshots of a coin that had gone up three hundred percent in nine days, with more upside promised if you got in before some announcement. He had eight hundred dollars sitting in a savings account earmarked for nothing in particular, and by midnight it was sitting in the coin instead, moved with the same two thumbs he used to order takeout.

He checked the price eleven times before falling asleep, and eleven times again before he'd finished his coffee the next morning. It was up four percent. He did the math on what eight hundred could become if it kept climbing at that rate for a month, and felt something close to what he imagined winning a hand at a casino felt like — closer to appetite than relief. He'd been putting a hundred dollars a month into his retirement account for six years, dutifully, boringly, and it had never once made him feel anything close to what four percent overnight had just made him feel.

*Steady saving is for people who don't know how to actually get ahead* — that was the sentence running Bryce's midnight decision, dressed up as smart opportunity-spotting rather than what it actually was: impatience with the slow math of real accumulation, looking for a shortcut around years of ordinary contributions.

Solomon addresses that exact impatience directly in Proverbs 13:11: money grabbed quickly tends to shrink back down just as fast, while money gathered steadily, a little at a time, keeps growing. Two paths, named side by side, with opposite endings. The hasty path doesn't just fail to deliver the promised upside — Solomon says it actively dwindles, shrinks back down, the money that arrived fast tending to leave just as fast because a man who got it without discipline rarely holds it with discipline either. The slow path, the one that looked boring to Bryce at eleven at night, is the one Solomon actually credits with increase.

Six years of a hundred dollars a month is unglamorous. Nobody screenshots it. It doesn't spike four percent overnight and it never will, and that flatness was exactly what made it trustworthy — the same discipline compounding in the background whether Bryce was paying attention to it or not, while the coin needed his attention every hour to feel worth the risk he'd taken.

Bryce pulled his eight hundred back out two days later, down eleven percent, and put what was left back into the account it came from.

**Today:** Look at whatever you're currently chasing for a fast win — a trade, a tip, a scheme a friend forwarded you — and calculate honestly what steady, boring contribution of that same amount would produce in ten years at an ordinary rate of return. Choose the slow number today, in writing.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for chasing the thrill of fast money instead of trusting the slow, steady work of real stewardship. Teach me to find contentment in the ordinary, unglamorous discipline of gathering little by little. Give me patience for the long path instead of appetite for the short one. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
