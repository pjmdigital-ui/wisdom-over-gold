# Day 4 — The Slave of the Lender

Tobias signed for a new phone at the kiosk without really looking at the numbers, the way he'd signed for the couch two Christmases ago, the way he'd signed for the used SUV that still had eleven payments left on it. Eighteen dollars a month, easy, barely worth thinking about next to everything else on the card. He'd stopped opening the full statement months back. He just paid the minimum plus a little extra, whatever felt right that week. The balance had been sitting somewhere around eleven thousand dollars for over a year, moving a little one way or the other depending on the month, never actually shrinking.

His brother-in-law, debt-free by choice and a little smug about it at family dinners, had asked him once what the interest rate was. Tobias didn't know off the top of his head. He'd genuinely never checked. It embarrassed him a little to admit that out loud, but not enough to go find out that night.

*Everybody carries some debt — it's just how life works now* — that was the actual belief underneath Tobias's eleven thousand dollars. He never said it that plainly, but it was doing all the work anyway. Debt had stopped feeling like a real number with a real cost. It had become background noise, the same category as gas prices or the weather — just a fact of modern life instead of a decision with consequences he was living inside every month.

Solomon draws a much sharper line than "how life works now." In Proverbs 22:7 he says the one with wealth rules over the one without it, and the man who borrows makes himself a slave to the one who lent him the money. Slave is the actual word Solomon uses, not inconvenience, not annoyance, not a line item. A debt a man carries casually is still, in plain terms, someone else's claim on his future work. Every dollar of that eleven thousand had already been spent, but the labor to pay it back was still owed, month after month, to whoever held the note — a real master, even if the monthly payment felt too small to notice.

That word doesn't sound like an exaggeration once a man actually does the math Tobias had been avoiding. Interest piling up on a revolving balance for over a year had already cost him more than the couch and the phone combined were worth. He was paying rent on money someone else still owned, not for things he'd already bought and used up months ago.

Freedom, in Solomon's terms, means no lender holds a claim on tomorrow's paycheck before a man earns it, whether or not he has a bill to pay this month.

**Today:** Pull up every balance you're carrying — cards, loans, financing plans — and read the actual interest rate on each one, out loud, today. Write down the total interest you'll pay this year alone if nothing changes. Then pick the smallest balance and pay more than the minimum on it this week.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've treated debt as background noise instead of the real claim on my future that it is. Show me every place I've let a lender's terms shape my freedom without my noticing. Give me the discipline to work my way out from under what I owe. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
