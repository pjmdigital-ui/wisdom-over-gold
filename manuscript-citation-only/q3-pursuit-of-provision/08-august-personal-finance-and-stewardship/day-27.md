# Day 27 — True Riches

Calvin's year-end bonus hit his account on a Friday — sixty-two hundred dollars after tax, more than he'd expected — and by Saturday morning he'd already found the truck rims he'd been eyeing for eight months, on sale, financing available if he wanted to keep more cash in hand. He sat in the driveway with the tab open, picturing how good the truck would look finished out, when his phone buzzed with the parish bulletin email he usually deleted without reading. This week's prayer list included a family whose water heater had failed, whose insurance wasn't going to cover the four-figure repair before winter.

Nobody had asked Calvin for anything. The bulletin wasn't a fundraising pitch, just a prayer request buried in a list of a dozen others. He closed the rims tab, opened it again twenty minutes later, closed it again. The belief running underneath the back-and-forth was plain enough once he named it: *I earned this bonus specifically, which means I get to decide exactly how it gets spent, and helping strangers wasn't part of the deal I made with my employer.*

Christ tells a parable about a dishonest steward who gets commended, oddly, not for his honesty but for his shrewdness with what wasn't his — and then draws a conclusion from it that has nothing to do with dishonesty and everything to do with proportion, in Luke 16:11: if a man can't be trusted with money, this world's small and tainted currency, why would anyone entrust him with the true riches waiting beyond it? The money in a man's hand right now — bonus, paycheck, whatever's sitting in the account — is the "unrighteous mammon" here, the small, temporary test case. What a man does with it, Christ says, is the evidence God examines before handing over anything bigger.

Calvin hadn't thought of the rims decision as a test of anything. It felt like a private transaction between him and his bank account, nobody else's business. Luke's Christ says otherwise: every dollar that passes through a man's hands is auditioning him for larger trust, and a man who can't release six hundred dollars toward a stranger's water heater when it costs him nothing but a set of rims is showing exactly how he'd handle something that actually mattered.

Calvin covered the repair that afternoon, anonymously, through the parish office. He bought the rims three months later, with cash he'd have spent on them anyway, and they looked exactly as good as he'd pictured. The order turned out to matter more than the purchase ever did.

**Today:** Find the "unrighteous mammon" sitting in your account right now — the discretionary money you've already mentally spent on yourself — and redirect a real portion of it toward a genuine need in front of you today. Say the belief out loud first: *What I do with this money is auditioning me for something bigger, not just spending my own reward.*

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thank You for more than I need this month. Show me the need in front of me that I've been too focused on my own want to notice, and make me faithful with the small money before I ask You for anything larger. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

LUKE 16:11
