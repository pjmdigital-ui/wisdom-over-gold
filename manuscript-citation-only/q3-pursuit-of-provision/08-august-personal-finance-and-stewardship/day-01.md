# Day 1 — Whose Money Is This

Davey spread the year-end statements across the kitchen table on a Sunday night. The mortgage was almost paid off. His 401(k) had finally hit a number he'd been chasing since his twenties. The business account had carried the family through a slow spring, and he never missed a payroll. He sat back and let himself feel proud. Eleven years in that business. Thousands of estimates written by hand before he ever hired an office manager. A hundred Saturdays he could have spent somewhere else. He'd built this.

His wife came in with two coffees. She glanced at the numbers spread across the table and mentioned that their parish was still short on its building fund. Had he thought about what they might give this year? Something tightened in Davey's chest before he'd even answered. It wasn't anger. It felt more like a door closing. *I earned every dollar of this myself. What I do with it is mine to decide.* He didn't say it out loud. He didn't have to. That thought had been running the whole conversation underneath.

That sentence sounds reasonable. It even sounds responsible. Nobody handed Davey his business. He built it himself, on nights he could have quit instead. But under that reasonable-sounding sentence sits a much older claim. It's the same claim made in the garden a long time ago, by people who had far less than Davey does: I decide what belongs to me and what I do with it.

Scripture opens the ledger differently, before a man even gets to spend a dollar. Psalm 24:1 says the earth and everything in it belongs to God. That means every person, every possession, the whole world. Not most of it. Not the part left over after a man's hard work claims its share. All of it — including the business Davey built with his own two hands, the skill he built it with, and the health that let him work eleven years of Saturdays in the first place. David wrote that psalm as a king. He could have claimed as much ownership as anyone in his kingdom. Instead, he opened his song by giving all of it back before he sang another word.

That doesn't erase what Davey actually did. God doesn't need Davey to pretend he did nothing. He just wants Davey to see it correctly. A steward and an owner can do the exact same work with the exact same hands. What makes them different is whether the man believes the thing he's managing was ever really his to begin with. An owner spends however he wants and gets angry when anyone questions it. A steward asks a different question before every decision: what would the real Owner want done with this?

That question sits underneath everything this month is going to ask a man to look at. His giving. His debt. How he compares himself to other men. His fear of running out. Get this one thing wrong, and every other money habit inherits the same mistake. Get it right, and everything else becomes a matter of practice, not belief.

**Today:** Write down one thing you own outright — a paid-off truck, a savings account, a business you built. Say out loud: "This was never fully mine. God, You entrusted it to me." Let that sentence guide every money decision you make this month.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've spent years calling what I earned my own, as if my effort were the final word on who it belongs to. Show me every place I've claimed ownership that was always Yours to begin with. Teach me to hold what I have with open hands, as a steward and not an owner. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PSALM 24:1
