# Day 15 — Zacchaeus's Repayment

Rourke and his old business partner split their company nine years ago, and Rourke has known ever since that the final settlement wasn't clean. He'd padded a client invoice in the last few months before the split, small enough that nobody caught it at the time, large enough that he still remembers the exact figure. His former partner never found out. They still see each other occasionally at industry events, shake hands, ask about each other's kids. Every time, Rourke feels the old debt sitting behind the handshake, unpaid and unmentioned.

He has a reason ready whenever the thought surfaces: bringing it up now, nearly a decade later, would only make things awkward for no real benefit. His partner has moved on and done well. Raising it would just unsettle something that's been sitting still for years, and what would it actually fix at this point? That reasoning has worked well enough to keep Rourke quiet for nine years. It has never once made the number in his memory feel any smaller.

Zacchaeus knew that number better than most. As a chief tax collector working for Rome, he'd built his wealth by collecting more than was owed and keeping the difference, a practice so ordinary among tax collectors that nobody in Jericho would have called it remarkable. When Jesus invited himself to Zacchaeus's house, something in Zacchaeus broke open that a lifetime of careful profit never had. He stood up in front of the crowd that despised him and announced, in front of everyone, that he was giving half of everything he owned to the poor, and that anyone he'd cheated would get back four times what he'd taken. Jesus didn't ask him to itemize the wrongs first. Zacchaeus offered the restitution before anyone demanded it, and Jesus answered him without hesitation, declaring that salvation had come to his house that day, because he too was a son of Abraham.

Notice what actually changed the moment for Zacchaeus: fourfold repayment, named out loud, owed to actual people he could still find and pay back — a costly, public act, not a private decision made only in his own heart. Rourke's old debt works the same way. Feeling bad about the padded invoice for nine years hasn't done his former partner any good and hasn't settled anything real. Only an honest conversation and an honest check would do that — the same uncomfortable, specific, nameable act Zacchaeus climbed down out of his tree to make.

**Today:** Name the one financial wrong you've never actually made right — a debt, a shortcut, a number you know isn't clean — and take the first concrete step toward repaying it: write the check, make the call, or set the meeting. Say plainly: "This debt doesn't get smaller by staying silent. It gets smaller by being paid."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, You know the exact number I've been carrying and calling settled when it never actually was. Give me Zacchaeus's courage to name it and repay it instead of managing it quietly in my own head. Let today be the day I stop rationalizing and start making it right. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
