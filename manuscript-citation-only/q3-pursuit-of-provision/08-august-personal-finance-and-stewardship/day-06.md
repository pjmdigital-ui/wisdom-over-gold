# Day 6 — The Rich Fool

Dustin hit a number on his retirement account that week, one he'd been tracking on a sticky note in his desk drawer for years — the number where, by his own math, he and his wife could stop worrying for good. He took a screenshot, closed the laptop, and felt something settle in his chest that he hadn't felt in a long time. Whatever happened now — a bad year at work, a market dip, even losing his job outright — they had enough. He'd built himself a floor nothing could fall through.

He mentioned it to his father that weekend, expecting something like pride back. His father, seventy-four and long retired, just nodded slowly and said, "That's good. Just don't let the number become the thing you're actually trusting." Dustin laughed it off in the moment. Driving home, the comment sat with him longer than he expected it to.

*Once I have enough saved, I'll finally be secure* — that was the sentence Dustin had built his whole financial life around without ever writing it down. The sticky-note number stood in for something closer to salvation than savings.

Christ tells a story about exactly this man. A rich landowner, after a huge harvest, decides to tear down his barns and build bigger ones. He tells himself he can finally sit back and enjoy years of plenty he's now sure he has coming. That night, God speaks directly into the plan, calling the man a fool and asking who will end up with everything he's built, since his life is being demanded of him this very night. Christ closes the story with the plain point in Luke 12:21: that is what happens to a man who stores up treasure for himself while remaining poor toward what actually matters to God.

Scripture nowhere condemns a full barn. The rich fool's mistake was treating the barn as the foundation his soul actually stood on — treating a number, however large, as a kind of security only God can really provide. A barn can be seized, taxed, drained by inflation, or simply outlived by the man who filled it, the way it nearly was that same night.

**On St. John Vianney:** Vianney could have built himself real security. His parish in Ars grew wealthy under his care, drawing pilgrims from across France, and the collection plates showed it. He kept almost none of it. He slept on a bare frame in a cassock he refused to replace, gave away what came to him as fast as it arrived, and funded an orphanage for years on gifts with no financial cushion behind them. Vianney's peace came from a trust that never needed a number to stand on.

Dustin had transferred his trust onto that sticky note without realizing it.

**Today:** Name the number, real or imagined, you've been treating as the point where you'd finally feel secure. Say out loud: "My security is God, not this number." Then give something away this week that the old version of that sentence would never have allowed.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've built a floor out of savings and called it security, when only You can actually hold me up. Forgive me for trusting a number more than I trust You. Teach me to hold what I've saved with an open hand, ready to give even while I plan ahead. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

LUKE 12:19-21
