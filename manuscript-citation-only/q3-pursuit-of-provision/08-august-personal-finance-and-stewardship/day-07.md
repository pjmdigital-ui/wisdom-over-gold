# Day 7 — Closing Week One

Look back over the six days behind this one and count how many different faces the same lie wore. A man at his kitchen table, staring at numbers he'd built with his own hands, forgetting Whose earth those hands were even attached to. A man with a giving line in his budget that stayed empty year after year, waiting on a surplus that was never built to show up last. A man sitting in his own driveway, resenting a life that was actually more than enough, because someone else's boat had just arrived. A man signing for another payment without reading the number, calling it normal because everyone he knew was doing the same thing. A man moving his savings into a coin at eleven at night, chasing in nine days what real patience builds in years. A man hitting a number on a retirement account and mistaking the floor it gave him for the ground his soul actually stood on.

Six men, six different rooms, and the same root underneath every one of them: a conviction that the money was theirs to begin with, to hoard, compare, gamble, or trust in exactly as they saw fit, God's claim on it optional at best. Nobody wakes up one morning and decides to believe that outright. It gets absorbed a paycheck at a time, a purchase at a time, until a man's whole relationship to money runs on a belief he never actually chose.

Solomon draws the line between the two ways that belief can end: "He who trusts in his riches will wither, but the righteous will flourish like a green leaf" (Proverbs 11:28, RSV-CE). Wither is a strange word to use about a man's bank account, until you notice Solomon is talking about the man, not the account. Riches make an unstable foundation precisely because they can be lost, and a man who has built his identity and his security on top of them withers right along with whatever happens to the number. A man rooted somewhere else — in the God who owns the earth and everything in it, as this week opened by saying — keeps his leaves green regardless of what the market or the neighbor's driveway happens to do that year.

Money held loosely is one thing; money trusted in as a man's actual security is another, and Solomon's warning is aimed squarely at the second.

**Today:** Pick one habit from this week you recognized in yourself — comparison, casual debt, chasing fast money, trusting a number over God — and write down the specific belief driving it. Cross it out. Underneath, write what Solomon actually promises the righteous man: *I will flourish like a green leaf, rooted in God, not in what I own.*

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, this week showed me how many ways I've handed my trust to money instead of You. Thank You for the ownership, the contentment, and the real security only You can give. Root me deep enough that whatever happens to my accounts, I stay green. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
