# Day 22 — Building the House in Order

Leo spent most nights that spring browsing listings for a cabin on a lake three hours north, the kind of place he pictured retiring to early, maybe by fifty, if things went well. He and his wife would sit with laptops open after the kids went to bed, scrolling through photos of docks and screened porches, talking about square footage and how many years until they could actually make an offer on something like it. It felt like planning. It felt like the responsible version of a dream.

What the lake-house planning conveniently skipped over was the eighteen thousand dollars still sitting on two credit cards from a rough stretch three years back, the retirement account that had been under-funded for most of a decade while other priorities took the money instead, and the total absence of anything set aside for the kind of emergency that could sideline the whole plan in a single bad month. Leo knew all three facts. He simply didn't let them sit in the same conversation as the cabin, because one conversation felt like momentum and the other felt like a lecture he'd rather skip.

The belief he never said plainly, even to himself, sounded reasonable enough from a distance: *I've put in the work — I've earned the right to start building toward the reward now, even if the foundation underneath it isn't finished.* It's the appealing logic of a man who wants the second half of a project to start before the first half is actually done, because the second half is the part that photographs well.

Scripture puts the two steps in a stubbornly specific order, in Proverbs 24:27. Get your outdoor work done, get the field ready, and only after that build your house. Not at the same time, and not in whichever order feels more inspiring on a given Tuesday night. Field first, house after — the unglamorous, unfinished, currently-necessary work has to actually get done before the comfortable structure gets built on top of it. A house raised before the field is ready is a structure with nothing solid underneath it, waiting for the first hard season to expose the order it was built in.

Leo and his wife didn't stop dreaming about the cabin. They put the listings away for a season and wrote out, in specific numbers, what closing the eighteen thousand dollars in debt and fully funding both retirement and an emergency account would actually take — and how many months that put between now and an honest offer on anything. The lake house moved to its actual place in the sequence, behind the field that still needed tending.

**Today:** Name the reward you've been planning toward — a purchase, a lifestyle upgrade, an early exit from work — and identify honestly whether the foundational work underneath it (debt paid down, savings built, obligations current) is actually finished. If it isn't, write down the specific field that still needs tending before you build.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for wanting to build the comfortable part of my life before I've finished the necessary part. Give me the patience to do the unglamorous work first, and trust that the reward will still be there when the foundation is ready to hold it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
