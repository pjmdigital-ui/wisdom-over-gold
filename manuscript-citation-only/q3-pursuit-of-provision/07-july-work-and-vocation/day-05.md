# Day 5 — Filled for the Work

Glenn had his skill mapped out like a stock portfolio. Fourteen years writing software, the last six of them building interfaces other engineers called clean, and he could tell you exactly what that was worth on the open market down to the percentile — which companies paid best for it, which certifications moved the number, how to phrase it on a resume so a recruiter's eye caught it in the six seconds they'd actually spend reading. He was good at his craft in a way that showed, and he'd built an entire professional identity around treating that gift as leverage. A card to be played. A number to be maximized.

He believed, without ever putting it in a sentence, that his talent was just something he happened to have — useful, marketable, entirely his own to deploy however served him best. Nothing sacred about it. Skill was skill, whether you used it to build a cathedral or a checkout flow; it belonged to the man who had it and the market that would pay for it.

Scripture describes a very different kind of craftsman in the story of the tabernacle. God doesn't just permit skilled work to happen — He personally names the man for it: "See, I have called by name Bez'alel the son of Uri, son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah: and I have filled him with the Spirit of God, with ability and intelligence, with knowledge and all craftsmanship, to devise artistic designs, to work in gold, silver, and bronze, in cutting stones for setting, and in carving wood, for work in every craft" (Exodus 31:2-5, RSV-CE). Filled with the Spirit of God — for metalwork, for stone-cutting, for wood-carving. Not for prophecy. Not for leading Israel out of Egypt. For craftsmanship, named directly as a place the Spirit of God chooses to dwell and work.

That single detail rearranges what Glenn had been calling his own. His fourteen years of skill weren't a resource he'd built alone and now owned outright, free to spend however the market rewarded best. Ability, intelligence, knowledge, craftsmanship — Exodus lists them as things Bezalel was filled with, not things he manufactured out of nothing. A man's gift for clean code, for solving a hard interface problem elegantly, for craftsmanship in any form, comes from somewhere. Scripture says plainly where: the same Spirit that filled a bronze-worker in the wilderness three thousand years ago.

That doesn't make Glenn's paycheck wrong, and it doesn't mean he should stop negotiating well for his family's sake — providing for them is its own act of faith, as this month has already said. But it does mean his skill is a gift he was filled with for a purpose bigger than his own leverage, not only a market asset, and he'd been managing it like a man managing stock he forgot he'd been given rather than one he'd bought.

**Today:** Name the one skill you're best at — the thing people specifically come to you for. Say out loud: *This ability is a gift I was filled with for more than my own advancement, not something I own outright to spend only on myself.* Then use it once today for someone who can't advance your career at all.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thank You for the skill You filled me with long before I ever called it my own. Forgive me for treating it only as leverage for myself. Show me today where You want this gift used for someone who has nothing to offer me back. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
