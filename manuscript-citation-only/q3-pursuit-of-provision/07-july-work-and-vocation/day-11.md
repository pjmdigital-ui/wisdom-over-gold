# Day 11 — When the Business Fails

Perry unlocked Reyes Hardware for the last time on a Tuesday morning. He wasn't there to open it. He was there to pull the inventory tags off shelves he'd stocked for seventeen years, before handing the keys back to the landlord. He'd watched the numbers slide for two years. A national chain opened nine miles up the highway with prices he could never match. The contractors who used to call him by name started ordering online instead. He cut his own pay before he cut anyone else's hours. He refinanced the house once. He still watched the math run out anyway.

Standing in the empty aisle where the fasteners used to be, Perry felt like a man whose worth had just been repossessed along with the shelving.

That's the belief hiding under a failure like this, and it rarely announces itself so plainly: *what I build is what I'm worth — if the business goes, so do I.* A man can spend seventeen years proving himself skilled, generous, and honest, and still walk out believing all of that vanished the moment the doors locked, because he'd let the business become the whole scoreboard.

St. Paul wrote from a position that makes Perry's morning look easy by comparison. He was chained, awaiting trial, depending on a church's gift just to eat, and he refused to let his situation set his worth. In Philippians 4:11-13, he insists he isn't speaking out of need, because he has trained himself to be at peace no matter what he's facing. He's lived through poverty and through plenty, and he's found the same steadiness in both, able to hold up under whatever comes because of the strength Christ supplies him. Paul calls it something learned, something acquired the hard way, through actually being brought low and actually having plenty, not through avoiding either one.

Perry's seventeen years weren't erased by the closing. He taught himself the trade. He kept every promise he made to a supplier, even in the leanest year. He put two kids through school on what that store earned. None of that requires the business to still be open to remain true. What he's being asked to learn now, the part that doesn't come naturally to a man who ties his identity to what he's built, is that his worth was never the store's to hold in the first place.

**Today:** Name one thing you built, earned, or gave that stays true no matter what happens to your current job, business, or income. Say it out loud: "My worth isn't the scoreboard I built. I can be content whether I have little or a lot, because the One who strengthens me isn't the market."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I have let what I build stand in for who I am, and now I don't know who I am without it. Teach me the contentment Paul learned in want and in plenty. Strengthen me to keep going, not because the business survived, but because You have not let go of me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PHILIPPIANS 4:11-13
