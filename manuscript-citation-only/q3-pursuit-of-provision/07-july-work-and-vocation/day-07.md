# Day 7 — Closing Week One

Look back over the last six days and a pattern starts to repeat. A man in a truck before dawn, treating his work as nothing more than the price he paid for a life that mattered somewhere else. A man running a countdown clock at his desk, waiting out his career like a sentence with parole at the end. A man closing deals under one set of rules on Saturday and singing hymns under another set on Sunday, never noticing there was only one of him the whole time. A man on a forklift at two in the morning, quietly sure his hours counted for less than a missionary's. A man treating his own skill as leverage he'd built alone, forgetting who filled him with it in the first place. A man standing in front of a wall no one would ever open, deciding whether his standard depended on being watched.

Six different men, six different jobs, and the same lie running underneath every one of them: work is what a man does until real life starts, and God's interest in it stops somewhere around the parking lot. Nobody sat down and decided to believe that. It arrived the way most false beliefs arrive. It was assumed rather than chosen, picked up from a culture that treats a career as either a grind to escape or a status to chase, with no third option offered.

Scripture offers a third option, and it isn't a hustle-harder answer or an escape-faster one. The Preacher in Ecclesiastes 3:22 looks at everything else a man might chase, and lands on this: there's nothing better for a man than finding real satisfaction in the work right in front of him. No one can show him what happens after he's gone anyway. This is the actual portion God assigned him to live inside. Qoheleth had tried everything a man can chase for meaning — building projects, vineyards, wealth, pleasure, wisdom itself. After running out of alternatives, this is where he lands: enjoy the work you've actually been given, because that, not some imagined future free of it, is where your days are.

That's worth sitting with plainly. It cuts against ambition and against despair at the same time. A man can still grow and still push for more. But he can't keep treating today's actual work as a placeholder for a better life he's still waiting on. The plumber's truck, the desk, the showroom, the forklift, the keyboard, the framed wall — these make up a man's real life, most days, for most men. Ecclesiastes says the wise man learns to receive that as a gift instead of resenting it as a delay.

**Today:** Write down the belief you've been carrying into your work this week without saying it out loud, and cross it out. Underneath it, write the sentence Ecclesiastes actually hands you: *This work is my lot — not my curse, not my delay. God gave it to me to enjoy and to tend.* Say it before you start tomorrow's first task.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, this week showed me how long I've treated my work as a delay before real life instead of the actual portion You've given me to live inside. Thank You for the ordinary jobs, unglamorous hours, and hidden tasks that make up most of my days. Teach me to receive this work as my lot instead of resenting it as my sentence. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
