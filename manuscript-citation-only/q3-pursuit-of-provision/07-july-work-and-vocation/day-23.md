# Day 23 — The Honest Scale

Wade owned a scrap metal yard, and every load that came through got weighed twice. Once on the way in, once after the junk was sorted out. The difference got paid out in cash at the counter. His scale had drifted a little over the years. If he never had it recalibrated, it would round every load down by a few pounds without a single customer noticing. A supplier mentioned it to him casually, almost as a professional courtesy: some yards just leave it be. Nobody checks. It adds up over a year.

Wade thought about it longer than he wanted to admit. Recalibrating cost money and a half-day shutdown. Leaving it uncalibrated cost nothing, and nobody would ever know. Not the retired guy hauling in a truck bed of old gutters. Not the contractor dropping off a demolition load. Not a single one of them owned a scale of their own to check him against. The math worked entirely in Wade's favor, and it was invisible.

The belief sitting underneath that hesitation is the kind men rarely say plainly, because saying it plainly makes it obviously wrong: *a small, unwitnessed advantage isn't really dishonest if it never gets noticed.* Wade wouldn't have picked a customer's pocket directly. He was seriously considering letting a machine do it a few ounces at a time, on the theory that nobody watching makes it not stealing.

Scripture states the standard flatly, aimed at exactly this kind of quiet advantage, with no regard for whether anyone's checking. Proverbs 11:1 says a rigged scale is hateful to the Lord, while an honest weight is something He delights in. That's about as strong as biblical language gets on this topic. What pleases God in the same verse is plain, unadorned honesty in the measure, nothing dressed up as generosity or impressiveness. A scale that tells the same truth whether the man reading it is desperate for the money or won't remember the transaction by lunch.

Wade called the calibration company that afternoon and paid for the half-day shutdown out of his own margin. He didn't tell his customers why the yard was closed Tuesday. He didn't need to. The point was never that anyone would find out. The point was what kind of scale he wanted to be running when nobody was checking.

**On St. Homobonus:** Homobonus was a cloth merchant in twelfth-century Cremona. He was canonized within two years of his death, an almost unheard-of speed for the era, specifically because his fellow citizens testified that his business dealings were as honest as his prayer life was devoted. He's the patron of businessmen and tradesmen precisely because his holiness showed up on the ledger, not just at Mass.

**Today:** Find the one place in your work where an unwitnessed advantage has been quietly working in your favor — a corner cut, a number rounded your way, a detail you've let slide because no one checks. Fix it today, even though fixing it costs you something and no one would have known.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for the advantages I've let slide simply because no one was watching. Make me a man whose scale reads true whether or not anyone is checking, the way Homobonus was. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
