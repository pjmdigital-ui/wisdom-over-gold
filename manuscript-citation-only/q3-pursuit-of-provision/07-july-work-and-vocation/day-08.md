# Day 8 — Faithful in Little

Warren ran a six-person marketing agency out of a converted duplex. At 9:40 on a Thursday night, he was staring at a client deliverable due at 8 a.m. It was a full campaign report that was supposed to go through two rounds of internal review before it left the building. Round one had happened. Round two hadn't, because the account lead who normally caught the small mistakes had left early for a family thing. Nobody else had time to do it properly before the deadline.

Warren looked at the report one more time himself, tired, and decided it was close enough. A few numbers he hadn't triple-checked. A slide he knew was weaker than it should be. Nothing that would blow up the account if it slipped through. He told himself it was a one-time exception, the kind of thing that happens twice a year in an agency this size. Not a real compromise. Not something that said anything about how he actually ran the place. He hit send at 11:15 and went to bed. He told himself the same thing the next three times this exact scenario repeated over the following year, each time just as sure it was the one-time version.

It never occurred to Warren, that night or the next few times, that a business doesn't actually have a category called "the one-time exception." It only has patterns. Every exception either becomes a pattern or it doesn't, and how much a man means it when he calls it a one-time thing never decides which.

Jesus draws a straight, unbroken line between the small compromise and the larger character underneath it. In Luke 16:10, he says whatever a man's character does with a small, unimportant matter is exactly what it will do with something far bigger. Trustworthy in the small things means trustworthy in the large ones. Dishonest in the small things means the same dishonesty will show up when the stakes rise. The verse treats the little thing as a test — a small enough sample to reveal exactly what a man will do with something bigger, because the same character is operating at both scales. A man who skips the review step on a Thursday night is the same man who'll eventually skip something that actually matters. He's just been tested small first.

That's the part Warren had never let himself see clearly. He was choosing, over and over, in rooms nobody else would ever audit, what kind of man he was in the very little things. He wasn't deciding once, in some large dramatic moment, whether to be a dishonest businessman. Jesus says that choice doesn't stay contained to the small stakes it was made under. It compounds. The agency Warren was actually building was made of a hundred small 11:15 p.m. decisions nobody else ever saw, not the polished one on his website.

Faithfulness in a client report nobody scrutinizes closely, and faithfulness in a much larger decision years from now, are the same test, taken at different volumes.

**Today:** Find the one place this week where you were tempted to call something "just this once" — a shortcut, a skipped step, a corner you told yourself didn't count because the stakes were small. Redo it properly today, and name what you're actually practicing: *How I handle the little things is how I'll handle the much. There's no separate, smaller version of my character.*

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've called too many small compromises exceptions when they were really a pattern I was building one decision at a time. Make me faithful in the very little things nobody checks, not just the large ones everyone will see. Let today's small task be handled by the same character I want to have when the stakes are much higher. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

LUKE 16:10
