# Day 17 — Systems, Not Just Goals

Corbin wrote his number on a sticky note and put it on his monitor the first week of the quarter: $400,000. He looked at it every morning, sometimes twice. He also, most mornings, opened his laptop and worked whatever felt most urgent — a hot lead, an angry client, a proposal due in an hour — and called that a full day. By week nine he was forty percent behind pace, and the sticky note hadn't moved an inch closer.

He genuinely wanted the number. He'd said it out loud in the sales meeting, written it down, visualized hitting it more than once in the shower. What he hadn't done was build anything that made hitting it more likely than not — no fixed call volume, no weekly pipeline review, no protected hour for prospecting that didn't get eaten by whatever fire showed up first. Corbin was treating the goal like it was the plan, as if wanting something badly enough was a strategy in itself.

That's the belief worth naming plainly: *if I want this enough, the work will follow.* It sounds like ambition. It's actually a way of skipping the boring part — the same fifteen minutes of prospecting calls done every single day, whether he felt like it or not, whether a fire was burning or not. Proverbs draws the line between those two men without any softening: "The plans of the diligent lead surely to abundance, but every one who is hasty comes only to want" (Proverbs 21:5, RSV-CE). Not the wanting of the diligent — the plans. Corbin had plenty of appetite; what he never had was a process standing between his appetite and Friday.

A goal tells you where you want to end up. It says nothing about Tuesday morning. A system is what you actually do on Tuesday morning, the same way, whether you feel inspired or not — and it's the system, not the sticky note, that decides whether the number ever arrives. Corbin needed a smaller, boring, repeatable goal: calls made before email, pipeline reviewed every Friday at four, no exceptions carved out for a bad week. The number on the monitor was never going to build itself a road. Only the daily plan would.

He tore the sticky note off the monitor that Friday, not because the number stopped mattering, but because staring at it every morning had never once dialed a phone for him. In its place he taped a short checklist: twenty calls before ten a.m., pipeline updated before he left, one hard conversation he'd been avoiding scheduled by Wednesday. Boring, repeatable, unglamorous — and by the following month, closer to the number than any amount of wanting had gotten him.

**Today:** Take one goal you're chasing right now and write down the specific, repeatable daily or weekly action that actually produces it — not the outcome, the process. Put that process on your calendar this week, at a fixed time, before you write the goal down anywhere else.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for chasing numbers while ignoring the daily discipline that actually produces them. Teach me to build the plan and trust You with the pace of the harvest. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
