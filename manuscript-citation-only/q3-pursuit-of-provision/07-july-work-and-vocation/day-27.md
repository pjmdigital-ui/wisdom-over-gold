# Day 27 — Open Rebuke

Palmer co-owns a small machine shop with a partner he's trusted for eleven years. The partner's brother-in-law works the floor, and everyone on the shop floor knows what Palmer has known for six months: the guy shows up late, cuts corners on tolerances, and lets other men cover for him. Palmer hasn't said a word about it to his partner. Every time he's rehearsed the conversation in his head, it ends with the partnership strained over a problem that isn't technically his to raise.

Two weeks ago a shipment went out with a defect traced straight to the brother-in-law's station. The client called, furious. Palmer fixed it himself that night, quietly, and told his partner it was a supplier issue. He told himself he was protecting the relationship. What he was actually protecting was his own comfort, at the cost of the truth his partner needed and didn't have.

Scripture doesn't leave much room to mistake that kind of silence for love. Proverbs 27:5 says that speaking a hard truth openly is worth more than a love that never shows itself. Read that line slowly and it stops sounding like permission to be harsh and starts sounding like an indictment of a specific kind of cowardice — love that stays hidden, that never risks the conversation, isn't actually protecting the person it claims to care about. Hidden love protects the one who's avoiding the discomfort of speaking. Open rebuke, offered honestly, protects the partnership, the shop floor, and eventually the brother-in-law himself, who deserves the truth more than he deserves Palmer's silence covering for him indefinitely.

Palmer's fear isn't unreasonable. Business partnerships have ended over less than a hard conversation about family. But the shipment that went out defective is what eleven years of trust actually looks like under pressure when nobody's willing to say the true thing out loud. Every month Palmer covers instead of speaks, the debt he's not paying gets bigger — to his partner, to the crew who already know and are losing respect for both owners, and to the brother-in-law, who's never once been told plainly what everyone else already sees.

Open rebuke means naming what's true, directly, to the person who needs to hear it, instead of managing around it forever and calling the management peace.

**Today:** Set a specific time this week to tell your partner exactly what you've been avoiding — plainly, without softening it into vague hints. Say the true belief to yourself first: *Telling him the truth protects this partnership. Staying silent only protects my own comfort.*

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, give me the courage to speak what I've been hiding behind the name of keeping the peace. Show me that real love for the people I work with tells the truth instead of managing around it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
