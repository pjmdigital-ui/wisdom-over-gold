# Day 3 — Whatever You Do

Neil closed a deal Saturday afternoon, and he wasn't proud of it. Nothing illegal. Just the usual pressure play — the "this financing offer expires when you walk out that door" line. He'd used it a hundred times before. This time it was aimed at a young couple who clearly needed one more day to think it over. They didn't get it. He hit his number for the month by 4:40 p.m. and drove home feeling flat, the way you feel after winning at something you'd rather not look at too closely.

Sunday morning he sat in the same pew he always sits in. He sang the same hymns and said the same responses, and he felt completely at peace. In his mind, the showroom floor and that pew were two separate rooms with no hallway between them. In one room, a man answers to God. In the other, he answers to his sales manager and his commission sheet. He'd never once asked whether the version of himself standing under the dealership lights answered to the same God as the version kneeling on Sunday.

Would God even recognize the Neil from Saturday afternoon? That's the question Neil never let himself sit with long enough to answer. The honest answer meant admitting there was only one Neil, not two. And the second one had been running loose all week with nobody watching.

St. Paul doesn't leave room for that kind of split accounting. He wrote to a congregation full of ordinary working people, some of them literally enslaved to human masters. In Colossians 3:23-24, he tells them to throw themselves into whatever task is in front of them as if Christ himself were the one they were working for, not the human boss standing over them. The real reward, the inheritance, comes from Christ, because Christ is who they're actually serving. Whatever your task — Paul doesn't carve out an exception for sales, or for any job with a commission attached. Serving Christ instead of men means the customer across the desk isn't the one Neil is ultimately answering to. Neither is the sales manager tracking his numbers on a whiteboard. Christ is the audience for every deal he closes, whether or not His name ever comes up in the room.

That single truth collapses the two rooms Neil had built in his head into one. There's no version of Monday through Saturday that God isn't watching the same way He watches Sunday morning. The showroom floor isn't neutral ground where a man gets to operate by a different code just because no priest is standing there. It's still ground Christ is present on. It's still work being offered up to Him or withheld from Him, deal by deal.

The question worth asking before every deal isn't "will this close." It's "would I be comfortable with Christ standing at my shoulder while I make this pitch." Because He already is.

**Today:** Before your next call or meeting today, say this out loud, even quietly: *I'm not just serving a customer or a manager right now. I'm serving Christ, and He's already in the room.* Let that be true of one specific interaction today — the one you were most tempted to handle by the old rules.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for treating my work as a room You don't walk into. Show me where I've been serving my numbers instead of You, and give me the courage to close deals I could stand behind on Sunday morning as easily as Saturday afternoon. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

COLOSSIANS 3:23-24
