# Day 3 — Whatever You Do

Neil closed a deal Saturday afternoon that he wasn't proud of. Nothing illegal — just the usual pressure play, the "this financing offer expires when you walk out that door" line he'd used a hundred times, aimed at a young couple who clearly needed another day to think it over and didn't get one. He hit his number for the month by 4:40 p.m. and drove home feeling the particular flatness that comes from winning at something you'd rather not examine too closely.

Sunday morning he sat in the same pew he always sits in, sang the same hymns, said the same responses, and felt entirely at peace. The showroom floor and that pew existed in his mind as two separate rooms with no hallway between them — one where a man answers to God, and one where a man answers to his sales manager and his commission sheet. He'd never once thought to ask whether the version of himself standing under the dealership lights was accountable to the same God as the version kneeling on Sunday.

Would God even recognize the Neil from Saturday afternoon? That's the question Neil never let himself sit with long enough to answer — because the honest answer required admitting there was only one Neil, not two, and that the second one had been running loose all week with nobody watching.

St. Paul doesn't leave room for that kind of split accounting. Writing to a congregation full of ordinary working people, some of them literally enslaved to human masters, he tells them in Colossians 3:23-24 to throw themselves into whatever task is in front of them as if Christ himself were the one they were working for, not the human boss standing over them — because the real reward, the inheritance, comes from Christ, who is the one they are actually serving. Whatever your task — Paul doesn't carve out an exception for sales, or for any job with a commission structure attached. Serving Christ rather than men means the customer standing across the desk isn't actually the one Neil is ultimately answering to, and neither is the sales manager tracking his numbers on a whiteboard. Christ is the audience for every deal he closes, whether or not Christ's name ever comes up in the room.

That single sentence collapses the two rooms Neil had built in his head into one. There's no version of Monday through Saturday that God isn't watching the same way He watches Sunday morning. The showroom floor is not neutral ground where a man gets to operate by a different code because no priest is standing there. It's still ground Christ is present on, still work being offered up to Him or withheld from Him, deal by deal.

The question worth asking before every deal isn't "will this close" but "would I be comfortable with Christ standing at my shoulder while I make this pitch" — because He already is.

**Today:** Before your next call or meeting today, say this out loud, even quietly: *I'm not just serving a customer or a manager right now. I'm serving Christ, and He's already in the room.* Let that be true of one specific interaction today — the one you were most tempted to handle by the old rules.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for treating my work as a room You don't walk into. Show me where I've been serving my numbers instead of You, and give me the courage to close deals I could stand behind on Sunday morning as easily as Saturday afternoon. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

COLOSSIANS 3:23-24
