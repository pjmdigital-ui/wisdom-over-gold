# Day 2 — Before the Fall, There Was Work

Andrew kept a countdown running in his head most Tuesdays. Nine years and four months until he hit thirty years at the firm. Then he could take the pension. He sat in his cubicle running reconciliations, half listening to his own thoughts. They kept drifting toward a dock somewhere, a boat, mornings with nothing on the calendar. He thought about his job the way most men in his row thought about theirs. It was a sentence to be served until parole.

He said as much to a younger associate over lunch once, half-joking. It was the kind of thing men say when they've never really thought it through: "We're all just doing time in here until real life starts." The kid laughed. Andrew didn't correct him, because he believed it. In his mind, work was Eden's leftover punishment. It was the thing Adam got stuck with after he ruined everything. It was the curse a man endures so he can afford the parts of life that actually matter.

Genesis tells a different story than the one Andrew had carried around for two decades. Before sin ever entered the picture — before the tree, before the serpent, before any curse was spoken over the ground — Scripture already shows Adam working. Genesis 2:15 has God placing the man in Eden with a job: take care of the garden and watch over it. That's paradise, not punishment. God put man in a perfect world and gave him work to do before anything had gone wrong at all.

The Fall added thorns, weeds, and sweat to work. That friction wasn't there before. But the labor itself was there from the very first chapter of the human story. God handed it to Adam as part of a very good creation. It wasn't tacked on afterward as a penalty. Andrew's countdown clock had the whole thing backward. All along, he'd been handed a small share of the same task God gave the first man in the garden. He wasn't waiting out a curse. He'd spent twenty years wishing away a gift.

**On St. Joseph the Worker:** the Church didn't set aside a feast for Joseph because he preached or performed miracles. He's remembered mostly for a workshop — for wood, tools, and the ordinary labor of a carpenter in a small town nobody expected much from. He raised the Son of God the same way Andrew handles a spreadsheet: showing up, doing the next honest task, day after unremarkable day. Joseph never needed a countdown. He never treated his work like a sentence to survive before something holier began. The workshop was already holy ground, because the man standing in it gave his hands to God without waiting for a better assignment.

Andrew's cubicle is tilled ground he's been given to keep, the same as any garden entrusted to a man to work — just with different tools than Adam had.

**Today:** Notice the next thought that frames your work as a sentence to survive rather than ground to tend. Stop and replace it out loud: *Work is part of the good world God made, not my punishment to escape it. I'm meant to tend it, not flee it.* Then finish the next task in front of you as if it actually mattered — because it does.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for treating the work You gave me as a curse to outlast instead of ground You entrusted to me. Thank You for Joseph's example of an ordinary workshop made holy by an honest man's hands. Teach me to tend what's in front of me today instead of counting down to some easier life. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
