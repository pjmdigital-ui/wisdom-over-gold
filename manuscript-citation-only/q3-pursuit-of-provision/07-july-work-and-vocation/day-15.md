# Day 15 — Home But Not Present

Cole made it home for dinner at 6:15, which is more than he could say most weeks. He sat down at the table with his family while his phone stayed face-down by his plate like a loaded question. His daughter was telling him about a group project when his phone buzzed once against the table. His eyes went to it before he could stop them. Not long, half a second, but she saw it. She started the sentence over from the beginning in a slightly flatter voice, like she already knew how much of him she actually had.

His body was at that table for the full forty minutes. His shoulders stayed angled a few degrees toward the phone the whole time. His knee bounced under the table the way it did when he was three moves deep into a problem at work. Twice he answered a question his wife asked with a word that didn't quite match what she'd said, because part of him was still finishing an email he hadn't actually opened yet.

The belief running the whole dinner never got examined, because Cole would have said, honestly, that he loves his family and would never claim otherwise: *staying on top of this now is what keeps everything from falling apart, and my family will still be here when I'm caught up.* That belief treats presence as something that can be put off without cost, as if a man's body can sit in a chair while his attention rents itself out somewhere else for free.

Scripture describes a man who never even gets the question wrong. He simply never asks it. Ecclesiastes 4:8 pictures someone whose work has no end and whose appetite for more is never satisfied. He never once stops to ask himself who he's actually working so hard for, and why he's denying himself any enjoyment along the way. Scripture calls that whole pattern empty and miserable. That man in the text has no son, no brother, no one to toil for, and toils anyway without end. Cole's version is stranger and sadder. He has the son, the daughter, the wife, sitting six feet away, and toiled through their company anyway, without ever asking who the work was actually supposed to be for.

A man's presence gets measured by where his eyes go when the phone buzzes, and what his family has learned to expect from that half-second. It isn't measured by the hours logged in a chair at his own table. Cole's daughter needed the half-second back.

**Today:** At your next meal with your family, put the phone in another room — not face-down, gone. Notice how many times you reach for where it used to be, and each time, say to yourself: "I am not toiling for an inbox. I am toiling for the people at this table, and they get to see that."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I have let my body sit at the table while my mind kept working somewhere else, and my family has felt the difference even when I hadn't named it. Teach me to ask for whom I'm actually toiling before the day is gone. Make my presence match my address today. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ECCLESIASTES 4:8
