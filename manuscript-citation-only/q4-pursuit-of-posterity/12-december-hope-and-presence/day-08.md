# Day 8 — A Time for Every Matter

Nelson sat at the dinner table with his phone face-down by his plate, buzzing every few minutes, while his daughter Georgia told him a long, winding story about a fight at recess involving three friends, a jump rope, and a rule nobody could agree on. He nodded in the right places. He asked one or two questions that proved he'd caught the plot. But somewhere around the third twist in the story, half his mind was already drafting a reply to a client email he hadn't opened yet, the phone lighting up beside his fork like it was owed something.

*I'll really tune in tonight once the kids are down and things quiet down.* He'd thought some version of that sentence at this table more nights than he wanted to count, treating dinner as something to get through — present enough not to get called out for being distracted, not present enough to actually be there. Georgia finished her story and looked at him for a reaction, and he gave her one, a beat too late, and she seemed to notice.

Scripture describes a world with real, specific times for real, specific things. Ecclesiastes 3:1 opens with the claim that everything has its own fitting time, that nothing in the ordinary run of a life falls outside some moment built for exactly that purpose. Most men read this verse and picture the big seasons: a time to be born, a time to plant, a time to mourn. It's easy to miss that it applies just as much to an ordinary Tuesday dinner as it does to the big turns of a life. This hour, at this table, with this kid mid-sentence about a jump rope dispute, counts as one of those specific times itself — a father's full attention — not just the gap between more important seasons.

Nelson had been living like the real time for presence was coming later: after the workday actually ended, after the inbox actually cleared, after the kids were older and conversations got easier to follow. But "later" for a man with a full inbox never actually arrives with fewer things pulling at him. There will always be another email. There will only be so many nights Georgia tells him about recess before she stops bringing him the story at all.

**Today:** At your next meal with your family, put the phone in another room before you sit down. Let the clearest sign of your presence be that you finish listening to one whole story your kid tells you, start to end, without your mind drafting anything else while they talk.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've been waiting for a quieter hour to really be present with my family instead of recognizing this hour as the one that's actually mine to give. Thank You for making even an ordinary dinner its own appointed time, not a placeholder before a better one. Help me be fully where I am tonight, with nothing else competing for it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ECCLESIASTES 3:1
