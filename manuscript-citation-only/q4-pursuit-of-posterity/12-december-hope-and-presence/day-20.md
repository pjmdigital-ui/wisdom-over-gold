# Day 20 — Be Not Afraid

Todd's father died shoveling snow off his own driveway at fifty-two, and Todd, now forty-eight, had spent the last six months with an occasional tightness in his chest and no appointment on the calendar to talk to anyone about it. He'd also, without ever framing it this way to himself, backed out of the father-son backpacking trip he'd promised his son all summer — too far from a hospital, he told himself, too much could go wrong out there. He'd started declining flights he used to take without a second thought. Todd would never have said it out loud, but the operating assumption underneath six months of canceled plans was simple: if he managed his life carefully enough, he could keep death from getting anywhere near it.

St. Peter wrote to a scattered, genuinely endangered church, and what he tells them isn't a strategy for risk management. In 1 Peter 1:3 he blesses God for, in His great mercy, giving believers new birth into a living hope through Christ's resurrection from the dead. Peter calls this hope living for a specific reason — it's grounded in a resurrection that already happened. The worst outcome Todd had spent six months steering around has already been answered, not merely postponed by however carefully he drives or how many appointments he avoids.

**On St. John Paul II:** In his first homily as pope in 1978, he told a stadium full of people, "Be not afraid. Open wide the doors to Christ" — three years before a gunman shot him four times in St. Peter's Square. He survived, recovered, and later went in person to the prison cell of the man who'd shot him and forgave him face to face. Decades after that, as Parkinson's disease slowly took his voice and his balance in full view of the entire world, he kept appearing in public anyway, visibly trembling, rather than disappear from a role that had become physically difficult to perform. John Paul had more reason than most men to organize his life around fear of suffering or death. He built it around something else instead, in public, for everyone watching to see.

Todd's fear was quieter than a bullet or a diagnosis, which made it easier to let run his calendar unexamined for half a year. It was still fear governing daily choices instead of hope doing it, and the living hope Peter describes doesn't leave room for either man — the pope in the square or the father in the driveway — to keep letting it.

**Today:** Schedule the appointment you've been avoiding this week, today. Then tell your son the trip is back on.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let fear of what happened to my father quietly run my calendar for months now. Remind me that the hope I have in Christ is alive because of a resurrection that already happened, not one still pending on my caution. Give me the courage to live like a man who isn't managing his life around fear. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 PETER 1:3
