# Day 4 — Sufficient for the Day

Stanley woke at 2:14 and didn't get back to sleep. He lay there running numbers that don't actually work out at 2 a.m., or any other hour: what three college tuitions will cost in real dollars a decade from now, whether the retirement account is on pace for a date he can't quite picture, what happens if his father's health takes the turn everyone's been avoiding naming. He worked the math forward year by year until the years ran out ahead of him faster than the math could keep up, and he was still awake.

He never finished the math, because there's no version of 2 a.m. long enough to solve for every year still ahead of a man. What he was actually doing, without calling it this, was trying to earn tomorrow's peace tonight. He was treating every future problem as something he had to handle before he was allowed to rest, as if provision fifteen years out depended on Stanley solving it in the dark tonight instead of God supplying it when it actually arrived.

Jesus speaks to this exact habit directly, and He doesn't soften it. In Matthew 6:34 He tells His disciples not to worry about tomorrow, because tomorrow will bring enough worry of its own — today's trouble is already all a man needs to carry today.

This verse is worth pausing on longer than most, because of where it sits. It comes right after the verse this whole book was built on. January's first entry stood on Matthew 6:33, on the call to put God's kingdom and righteousness ahead of everything else, trusting that whatever else a man needs will fall into place after that. Jesus doesn't stop there and move on. He continues in the very next breath with the line above, and the two were never meant to be separated: seek the kingdom first, and let today's trouble be enough for today. Ordering a life around God is as much about how much of tomorrow a man is allowed to carry tonight as it is about the direction he aims over decades. This book will return to 6:33 one final time, on its very last day — this verse is a deliberate step toward that ending, not a coincidence of the calendar.

Stanley's 2 a.m. math assumes God's provision for year fifteen depends on Stanley finishing the equation tonight. It doesn't. Provision gets measured a day at a time, the way manna did in the desert — enough for today, gathered today, trusted to show up again tomorrow whether or not the whole future got solved in advance.

**Today:** If you wake at 2 a.m. running the numbers again tonight, say this out loud: "Today's trouble was enough for today. I'm giving tomorrow back to God until it's actually tomorrow." Tomorrow morning, write today's date on a sticky note and put it somewhere you'll see it — a plain reminder that today is the only day you're actually responsible for.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've been trying to solve fifteen years of worry in the middle of the night instead of trusting You with tomorrow when tomorrow actually comes. Thank You for making today's trouble enough, not the whole future's. Help me hand back what isn't mine to carry yet, and let me rest tonight in what You've already promised to supply. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
