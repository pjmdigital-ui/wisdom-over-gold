# Day 30 — The Work Begun in You

Wells found the book on the counter where it had sat, dog-eared and rubber-banded, since the second week of January. He hadn't read it cover to cover in months — just the current day's page, most mornings, coffee in one hand. On the last evening of the year, he sat down at the kitchen table and started flipping back through all twelve months at once.

January's pages were about a man who'd assumed he was supposed to sit at the center of his own life. He remembered the exact morning he'd written down what he was chasing and the sentence underneath it, and a forty-day stretch not long after where he'd given something up just to prove to himself he still could. March's doubt had nearly derailed him — a real stretch of questioning whether any of it was true — until he found, to his own surprise, that the doubt hadn't broken his faith so much as tested which parts of it were actually his own.

Spring and early summer had gone after his character. April cost him a version of himself that cared more about looking right than being right. May was the month he finally stopped mistaking a raised voice for strength, after one blowup at his son over something that hadn't deserved it. June handed him something he hadn't known he needed: two other men who knew the parts of his life he usually kept to himself, and didn't walk away once they did.

Summer into fall turned to what he built with his hands and his money. July taught him his job wasn't a sentence to serve out until real life started somewhere else. August cost him an honest conversation with his wife about money he'd been managing alone, and one afternoon of giving away more than felt safe. September asked the hardest question of the three — whether he was actually leading his household or just funding it — and he was still living out the answer.

The last quarter had gone after what he'd leave behind. October rebuilt his marriage from a partnership he managed into one he actually served. November taught him that fatherhood's real weight sits in the ordinary Tuesdays, not the big moments everyone remembers. December, still fresh on the table in front of him, had shown him hope and presence weren't things to wait on until his circumstances finally settled. They'd been available the whole time, in a God who never waited on Wells to get his life in order first.

Twelve months, four pursuits, and Wells was under no illusion he'd arrived anywhere close to finished. He'd lost his temper more than once since May. He'd caught himself managing his marriage again more than once since October. St. Paul, writing to a church he loved without reservation, names exactly the confidence a year like this one should actually produce. In Philippians 1:6 he tells them he's confident of one specific thing: that the God who started a good work in them will keep at it until it's finished, on the day of Christ. Paul's confidence rested in the One who started the work, staying faithful to complete what He'd begun on a timeline longer than a single year — longer, if it comes to that, than any man's whole life on earth — not in the Philippians finishing it themselves.

Wells closed the book without calling any of it finished, because it wasn't. One entry was left, the last one, and for tonight it could wait until morning.

**Today:** Turn back to the very first page you read this year — the actual book if you still have it, or just your memory of that first morning. Write one honest line about how the sentence you were chasing back then has changed. Not because you've arrived. Because you're not the same man who first wrote it down.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for a year that changed more in me than I noticed while I was living it. I'm not asking for finished — I know the work isn't. I'm asking You to keep doing what You started in me, for as long as it takes. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PHILIPPIANS 1:6
