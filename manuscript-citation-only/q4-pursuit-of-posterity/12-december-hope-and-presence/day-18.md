# Day 18 — Those Who Wait for Him

The test went in the bathroom trash can before Kyle even made it downstairs, wrapped in toilet paper the way Maren always wrapped them, which told him everything before she said a word at breakfast. Three years now. Five negative tests he knew about, probably more she hadn't mentioned, two rounds with a fertility specialist whose office they could now find blindfolded, and a running total of money that neither of them said out loud anymore because saying it out loud made the disappointment feel even more expensive. Kyle had started muting friends on his phone one at a time, without telling Maren, every time another pregnancy announcement showed up in his feed like a door closing somewhere down the hall.

*If God actually wanted to answer this prayer, He would have by now. The silence itself is starting to feel like the answer.*

Lamentations is not a book written by a man whose life was going fine. It comes out of a city in ruins, from a writer surrounded by literal rubble, and it still lands here. In Lamentations 3:24-25 the writer tells his own soul that the Lord is enough for him, worth hoping in for that reason alone, and declares that the Lord is good to everyone who waits for Him, to the soul that keeps seeking Him out. It comes from a writer with every reason to say God had gone silent, and it says something Kyle's three years hadn't let him consider: waiting on God is named here as the very place where His goodness is found, not a holding pattern outside it.

That doesn't shorten Kyle and Maren's wait or promise it ends the way they want. What it changes is what the wait means while it's still happening. Three years of silence from God is not, on its own, proof of three years of abandonment. It might be exactly what this verse says it is: a soul still being sought by the God it's seeking, whether or not either of them can feel that most mornings. There's a difference, too, between the waiting Kyle had been doing and the waiting the verse describes. His had mostly meant checking out: muting the friends, going numb before another disappointment could land. The waiting Lamentations describes is active — a soul that keeps seeking even in the silence, not one that quietly stops looking for God at all.

**Today:** Tell your spouse directly, out loud, that you're still hoping — not just carrying the disappointment silently next to her. Then take the one small practical step you've been putting off, as evidence you're still in this and not giving up on it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, three years of waiting and disappointment have made it hard some months to keep hoping instead of just enduring. Let me be a man who waits for You instead of one who quietly writes You off. Be near to Maren in this too; she's carrying a weight I can't fully share. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
