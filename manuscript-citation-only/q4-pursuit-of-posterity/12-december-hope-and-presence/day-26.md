# Day 26 — A Future and a Hope

Camden sat at the kitchen table two nights before the new year with a legal pad and a pen, the way he did most years about this time. Some years he filled three pages — revenue targets, a training plan, a number on the scale he was determined to hit by June — and white-knuckled his way through January and most of February before the list stopped getting checked, without ever officially giving up on it. Other years, worn out from the last time that happened, he skipped the pad altogether and told himself resolutions were for people who hadn't learned better yet. Either way he ended up in roughly the same place by March: convinced next year rode entirely on how sharp his own plan had been, or convinced planning had never really worked for a man like him.

This year the pad sat blank in front of him longer than usual. He couldn't decide if the blankness was wisdom finally showing up or just tiredness wearing wisdom's clothes.

Jeremiah wrote to people with a far better excuse than Camden's for having no hope left in a plan. He was writing to Israelites hauled off into exile in Babylon, a captivity that would run seventy years. Most of the people reading his letter would die there, in a foreign country, with no plan of their own that could shorten the sentence. Into that, God speaks a promise that has nothing to do with the strength of anyone's twelve-month strategy. In Jeremiah 29:11 the Lord tells them He alone knows the plans He has for them — plans meant for their good and not their ruin, meant to hand them back a future worth hoping in. The plans named in that verse belong to God, not to the exiles reading it. Their future was secured by whose hands were actually holding the years, not by how well they'd mapped the next one.

That doesn't make Camden's legal pad worthless. A man still builds honest rhythms into a year, the way careful, thoughtful people always plan: prayer that happens on purpose, time with his family that doesn't get left to whatever's leftover, some real attention to his own health. The difference is where his hope for the year actually rests. Not on the plan surviving intact past February. On the God who's already holding what the plan can only guess at.

**Today:** Instead of writing a long list of resolutions tonight, write three ordinary rhythms you'll build into the coming year — one for your faith, one for your family, one for your health. Put a date on the calendar three months from now to revisit them, not to grade yourself, but to notice what's actually taken root.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You that my hope for the year ahead doesn't rise or fall on how well I plan it. Give me the wisdom to build honest rhythms instead of anxious lists, and the trust to leave the actual future in Your hands, where it's always belonged. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

JEREMIAH 29:11
