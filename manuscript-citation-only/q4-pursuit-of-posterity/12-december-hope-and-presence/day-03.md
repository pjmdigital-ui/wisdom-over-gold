# Day 3 — Be Still

Lawrence has flown out four of the last five weeks — client sites, a conference, a plant walkthrough that ran two days longer than planned. Every night the pattern repeats. He checks into whatever hotel, drops the bag, opens the laptop at the little desk by the window to clear a last round of email before the alarm resets for a 6 a.m. flight. Somewhere in that routine he tells himself the same thing he's told himself since October: once this stretch of travel eases up, he'll get back to something real with God. Real prayer. Real quiet. Not tonight. Tonight there isn't room.

Does a man actually get peace with God once his calendar clears? Lawrence has never asked himself that directly. He's just lived like the answer is yes, treating stillness before God as a project scheduled for whenever the travel finally lets up, a season that, if he's honest, hasn't arrived in over a year and shows no sign of arriving in the next one either.

Scripture doesn't put stillness off into some quiet, unhurried future either. Psalm 46:10 gives its command in the middle of a psalm about nations in uproar, mountains shaking, waters roaring. The world is falling apart in this psalm, not settling down. It tells the reader to stop and know that God is God, lifted high over every nation and the whole earth. This command to be still was written into the middle of chaos on purpose, not for a man with an empty calendar, because that's the only place stillness actually means anything. Stillness before God has to survive real noise to count for something. A calm man in a calm room is simply resting.

Lawrence's hotel room on a Tuesday night, three flights into a five-flight week, is exactly the kind of room this verse was written for. He doesn't need the travel schedule to clear. He needs two minutes, right where he already is, willing to put down the laptop long enough to know God is God before he knows anything else about tomorrow's meetings.

That night he closes the laptop ten minutes earlier than usual. He sits on the edge of the hotel bed, phone face-down, and says nothing for two full minutes — no request, no agenda, just still, the way the psalm asks. It doesn't fix the week, but it's the first time in a month he's let himself stop performing productivity long enough to actually be somewhere.

**Today:** You don't need an empty calendar to be still before God — you need two minutes, right where you already are. Wherever you are tonight, close everything ten minutes early and sit in silence before Him for two full minutes: no phone, no agenda, just still.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've told myself stillness with You is something I'll get to once life slows down, and it never actually slows down. Thank You for being exalted over the noise itself, not just the quiet after it. Meet me right here, in the middle of a full week, and teach me to be still without waiting for permission from my calendar. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PSALM 46:10
