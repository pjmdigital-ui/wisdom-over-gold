# Day 5 — A Peace the World Can't Give

Wilson and his wife, Bridget, had been circling the same disagreement for four days — whether her parents would stay with them for two full weeks over the holidays or find a hotel for part of the visit. Nobody had raised their voice. It was worse than that: a low, polite cold front, careful sentences, a little too much distance at the dinner table, the kind of unresolved tension that doesn't blow up so much as sit in every room of the house at once.

*I can't actually be at peace until this is settled between us.* Wilson hadn't said the sentence out loud, but it was running underneath everything — his short patience with the kids, his distracted prayer before bed, the sense that nothing could really be right in him until the actual disagreement got resolved. He was waiting for the outcome to arrive before he'd let himself feel steady again.

Jesus said something to His closest friends that cuts directly against that assumption, and He said it on the worst night of His life to say it, hours before His arrest. In John 14:27 He tells them He is leaving His own peace with them — a peace nothing like what the world hands out — and tells them not to let their hearts be troubled or afraid. The circumstances in that room were about to get dramatically worse, not better — betrayal, arrest, the cross, all of it still ahead. Jesus doesn't promise peace once the situation resolves. He gives it as a gift already in hand, before anything gets fixed, entirely unlike how the world hands out peace. The world's peace requires the conflict to end first. His doesn't wait for that.

Wilson had been operating on the world's version without ever calling it that — peace as a payout that arrives once the disagreement with Bridget gets settled one way or the other. But he doesn't have to wait for that conversation to resolve before he's present and calm with his kids tonight, or honest in prayer before he sleeps. He can hold both at once: the tension with Bridget, genuinely unresolved, and real peace underneath it, already given, not contingent on either of them winning the argument about her parents' visit.

That evening he sat with his kids at the table without carrying the tension on his face, present in a way he hadn't managed in days. It didn't solve anything with Bridget. It also didn't need to, not that night, in order for him to be at peace anyway.

**Today:** Do one deliberate act of peace today, even before whatever's unresolved with your wife gets settled — full attention with your kids despite the tension, or a short prayer of honest thanks for your marriage exactly as it stands right now, not once it's fixed.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've been waiting for a disagreement to resolve before I'd let myself feel steady, as if Your peace only arrives after mine does. Thank You for giving peace that isn't the world's kind, not dependent on things being settled first. Let me carry both the tension I'm in and Your peace underneath it, honestly, without pretending either one away. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

JOHN 14:27
