# Day 10 — Entertaining Angels Unawares

Anders noticed Dax eating alone in the break room most days — a contractor brought on in October, quiet, no obvious friends on the floor, the kind of guy who microwaved his lunch and sat facing his laptop so nobody would feel obligated to talk to him. Anders meant to ask him to join the regular Tuesday lunch group. He thought about it almost every week. Something always got in the way: a meeting ran long, he was eating at his desk himself that day, or he just forgot by the time eleven-thirty rolled around.

He told himself it wasn't personal. Dax seemed fine on his own. And Anders had a real excuse most weeks — deadlines, a toddler at home who didn't sleep, a life already full enough without adding a guy he barely knew to the mix. Reaching out felt like a project he'd get to once things eased up, the way a man puts off a phone call he keeps telling himself he'll make "this weekend." Weeks went by. Dax kept eating alone, and Anders kept meaning to fix that.

The Letter to the Hebrews doesn't file hospitality under someday. It states it as a standing instruction, present tense, no exception for a busy season. Hebrews 13:2 tells its readers not to let hospitality toward strangers slip, because some who did that without knowing it were entertaining messengers of God. The word neglect matters here. It isn't warning against outright refusal, which is rare and easy to spot in yourself. It's warning against exactly what Anders was doing: not saying no, just never quite getting to yes, letting the ordinary crowd of a full week push a small kindness off the list again and again.

Nobody knows which stranger might be carrying more than they show. That's the plain sense of the verse before it gets read as a clever hook — some who showed hospitality entertained messengers of God without realizing it at the time. But even without that possibility, the math still works in the reader's favor: a fifteen-minute lunch costs Anders almost nothing and might be the one conversation in Dax's week where somebody looked at him instead of past him. Kindness this small doesn't require extra time a busy man doesn't have. It requires a decision made once, not remade every Tuesday at eleven-fifteen when the excuse shows up again.

Anders walked over on Wednesday before he could talk himself out of it again. "You eating with us today?" Dax looked up like he hadn't expected the question, said sure, and closed the laptop. It wasn't a long conversation. It didn't need to be.

**Today:** Think of one person on the margins of your week — a coworker who eats alone, a neighbor who's new, someone at church nobody quite talks to. Invite them into something ordinary today: a meal, a coffee, a ride. Don't wait for a slower week to do it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let real kindness sit on my mental list for weeks without ever acting on it, telling myself I'd get to it once things calmed down. Give me the nerve to close the laptop and make the small offer today instead of putting it off again. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
