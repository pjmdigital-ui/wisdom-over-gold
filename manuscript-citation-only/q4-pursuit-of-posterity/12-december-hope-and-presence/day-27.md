# Day 27 — Renewed Strength

Easton hadn't taken a full day off in six weeks. Work had gone sideways in November — a key hire that didn't work out, a client threatening to walk, the kind of stretch where every problem needs him personally — and on top of it he'd promised his father-in-law he'd finish the basement before Christmas guests arrived. He was up before five most mornings and still moving after eleven most nights. Any time his wife suggested he hand something off — a project at work, a task on the basement list, even just an evening — he had the same answer ready before she finished the sentence: he'd rather push through alone than depend on someone he'd have to explain himself to later if it went wrong.

The night it caught up with him, he was standing on a ladder running drywall screws by himself at 10:40 p.m., dizzy enough that he had to sit down on the basement steps for a few minutes before he trusted his legs again. Nothing dramatic happened. He just sat there in a half-finished room, worn out, and admitted to himself for the first time that his own strength had a limit, and he'd been driving straight toward it for six weeks like it didn't.

Isaiah wrote to a people who'd spent years assuming their own strength, or the strength of whatever nation they could ally with, had to be enough to save them, and God corrects the assumption directly. In Isaiah 40:31 he promises that those who wait for the Lord will find their strength made new — able to soar the way an eagle does, to run a long way without giving out, to keep walking without collapsing. Waiting here is the posture of a man who's stopped assuming the outcome depends entirely on his own reserves, and started drawing on a strength that doesn't run out the way his does — trust with its sleeves rolled up, not a man sitting back and doing nothing.

This was an old lesson Easton had needed to relearn more than once this year: a man's own strength was never going to be enough on its own, no matter how many times pride talked him back onto the ladder alone. Asking for help isn't the failure. Running on empty and calling it discipline is the far more costly mistake.

**Today:** Name one thing you've been white-knuckling instead of asking for help with — at work, at home, or in prayer. Ask a specific person for that specific help today, out loud, before the day ends.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for treating exhaustion as proof of my own strength instead of a warning that I've been running on my own instead of Yours. Teach me to wait on You the way Isaiah describes — not sitting back, but trusting — and give me the humility to ask for help before I run myself into the ground again. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ISAIAH 40:31
