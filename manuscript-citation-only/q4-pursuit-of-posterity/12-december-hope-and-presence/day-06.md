# Day 6 — The Little Way

August spent a whole Saturday on jobs that will never show up in the story of his life. He fixed the running toilet's fill valve. He went through his daughter Wren's spelling list a fourth time because the words weren't sticking. He folded a mountain of laundry that would be dirty again by Wednesday. In the middle of the afternoon, elbow-deep in a laundry basket, he caught himself thinking of the day as maintenance, the space between the things that actually count. Somewhere out ahead of him, he assumed, was a bigger, more visible chapter that would finally add up to something. This wasn't it.

Wren got the same word wrong for the third time in a row — "necessary," missing the double-s pattern again — and August felt his patience thin before he caught it and started over, gently, for the fourth time. It would have been easier, and faster, to just tell her the answer and move on. He didn't. He walked through it again, slow, the way he had the previous three times, with nothing to show for the extra patience except that she'd eventually get it.

St. Paul, near the end of a long passage about the different gifts God gives His people, lands on a ranking that has nothing to do with visibility. In 1 Corinthians 13:13 he names faith, hope, and love as the three things that remain, and puts love above the other two. Paul doesn't put love first because it produces the most impressive results. He puts it first because it's the one that lasts, the one still standing after the visible things fade. A patient fourth pass through a spelling list, given in love, already meets Paul's definition of the greatest thing there is. Size was never the measure.

**On St. Thérèse of Lisieux:** This book has visited Thérèse once before, in a month about doubt, showing how the smallest, most hidden acts still count even when a man's own confidence in their meaning wavers. This time the angle is different. Thérèse's Little Way wasn't a strategy for eventually reaching something bigger. She scrubbed convent floors, put up with a difficult sister in community, and offered ordinary, unglamorous moments to God as they came. She trusted that the present moment in front of her, not some future one, was already the whole point. She wasn't waiting for a stage large enough to matter. She was fully present to the only stage she actually had.

August finished the spelling list the fourth time through, patient, and there was nothing to show for it except that Wren finally landed on "necessary" correctly and grinned like she'd won something. She had.

**Today:** Pick the least glamorous task already on your list today and do it as a deliberate act of love, on purpose, without rushing through it toward something that feels like it counts more.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've been treating my most ordinary days as the wait before a more meaningful one, instead of trusting that love shown right here already matters to You. Thank You for a saint who found greatness in the smallest, most hidden tasks of an unremarkable day. Help me offer today's overlooked work to You as it happens, not as a placeholder for something bigger later. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 CORINTHIANS 13:13
