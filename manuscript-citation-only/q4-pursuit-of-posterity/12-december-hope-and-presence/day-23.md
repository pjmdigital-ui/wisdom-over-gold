# Day 23 — Emmanuel

Fenwick caught himself mid-Our-Father one morning, somewhere around "give us this day," realizing he had no memory of meaning a single word he'd said since "hallowed be Thy name." His mouth had run the whole prayer on autopilot while his mind was already three tasks into the day — the standup meeting at nine, the kid who needed a ride to school, the reply he still owed a client — and this wasn't the first morning that had happened, just the first one he'd actually noticed.

He'd been to Mass every Sunday of his adult life, said grace at dinner, prayed with his kids at bedtime, and could describe every one of those habits without much trouble. What he couldn't describe, if someone had pressed him, was the last time any of it had felt like talking to a person in the room rather than checking a box a good man was supposed to check.

Is anyone actually in the room when I do this, or am I just talking at the ceiling?

That question had been sitting under Fenwick's prayer life for longer than he wanted to admit. Somewhere along the way, God had become less a presence in his kitchen at six in the morning and more a distant executive he reported to on a schedule: real, in charge, but not exactly there. Matthew's Gospel names the claim that undoes that picture, pointing back to the ancient prophecy that a virgin would bear a son who would be called Emmanuel, a name Matthew stops to translate for his readers as God with us. In Matthew 1:23, the name itself carries the whole point. Not a title describing what God does from somewhere else. A location. With us — not watching from a distance, not managing the account from headquarters, but in the room.

Fenwick's autopilot prayers were still true words, still worth saying. They were missing one small thing that would have made them different: some awareness that the words were landing on Someone actually present, rather than being filed away for later review. A man doesn't fix that by trying harder to feel something. He fixes it by remembering, on purpose, what the name Emmanuel is actually claiming before he says another word: not that God is somewhere nearby if needed, but that He's already in the kitchen, already at the table, already present before the first request is even made.

**Today:** Before you pray tonight, say the word "Emmanuel" out loud once. Then, instead of listing requests, sit in silence for two minutes and simply acknowledge that He's already in the room with you.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've been running my prayers on autopilot for longer than I want to admit, talking without much sense that You're actually here. Thank You for coming near enough to earn the name Emmanuel instead of staying distant. Teach me to pray like a man who believes You're in the room, not one reciting words into empty air. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MATTHEW 1:23
