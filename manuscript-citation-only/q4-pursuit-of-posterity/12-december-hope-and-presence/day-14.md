# Day 14 — Closing Week Two

Winslow sat on the porch Sunday night with the last of a cup of coffee going cold, running back over a week that had rearranged how he thought about presence with the people around him. A man mapped out a whole Saturday and still missed his own daughter's recital from four feet away, phone lit under the program in his lap. Another kept meaning to invite a lonely coworker to lunch and kept letting the week swallow the invitation whole. A friend sent grief advice by text instead of driving over and sitting in the silence himself. A nephew loved his dying uncle from a safe, careful distance instead of the room where it actually cost him something. A husband gave his family the flat, leftover version of his words while saving his real attention for people he was trying to impress.

Different men, different Tuesdays, the same thread running under all of it: presence is a decision made against a dozen smaller, more comfortable decisions — to put the phone away, to make the invitation, to drive the forty minutes, to choose better words — not a feeling that shows up on its own once a man finally has enough time, and each small decision adds up to whether the people around him actually experience him as there.

Proverbs names something true about comfort that this week kept bumping into from a different angle. Proverbs 27:9 notes that oil and perfume lift a man's spirits, but a soul genuinely torn apart by trouble needs more than a pleasant scent can give it. Oil and perfume are small, pleasant, easy comforts — a nice gift, a kind word, a card in the mail. They do lift a heart, genuinely. But a soul actually torn by trouble needs more than something pleasant applied from outside. It needs someone willing to sit inside the trouble with it. Every story this week ran into that same gap between the easy comfort a man can offer at arm's length and the harder, better thing he can only give by actually showing up.

None of these men fixed anything permanently by Sunday. Winslow hadn't either, if he was honest — he could already feel this week's clarity starting to fade back into the ordinary pull of a full calendar. But he'd noticed, this time, exactly where the gap tended to open: not in the big obvious failures, but in the small, repeatable choice to stay comfortable instead of getting close enough to actually matter to someone.

**Today:** Pick the one story from this week that hit hardest — the recital, the lonely coworker, the grieving friend, the dying uncle, the careless words at home. Do the smallest real version of it again today, on purpose, before the week's clarity fades into next week's routine.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for a week that showed me more clearly where I settle for easy comfort instead of real presence. Keep that clarity from fading once the week gets busy again. Let me keep choosing to get close instead of staying safely at arm's length. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
