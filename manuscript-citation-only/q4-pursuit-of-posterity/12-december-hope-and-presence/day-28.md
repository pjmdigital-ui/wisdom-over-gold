# Day 28 — Closing Week Four

Reece sat by the fireplace a few nights after Christmas, the tree still lit in the corner and the house finally quiet after three days of relatives coming and going. He'd meant to check his phone for ten minutes and go to bed. Instead he found himself thinking back over the past week almost without deciding to.

It had been a strange seven days to think through. He'd caught himself, more than once, treating his own family's ordinary life — the same job, the same house, the same unremarkable routines — as though it needed to add up to something bigger before it counted as a life well lived. He'd also written a real check to a family he barely knew, instead of the easy gesture he might have made in a different year. He'd sat down with a blank legal pad instead of a frantic list of resolutions he already suspected he'd abandon. He'd asked his brother for help moving furniture instead of throwing his back out proving he didn't need it.

None of those four moments had felt connected while he was living them. Looking back at the whole week together, Reece could see one thread running under all of it: set your attention on what actually lasts, not on what looks impressive in the moment. St. Paul tells a church he's trying to turn from earthly worries toward something sturdier almost exactly that, in Colossians 3:2: fix your thoughts on what's above, not on what belongs only to this earth. Not because earthly things don't matter — Reece still had a mortgage, still had a family to feed — but because a mind trained to measure itself by what's above stops mistaking the visible win for the real one.

Hidden faithfulness, a costly gift, an honest plan instead of an anxious one, a strength borrowed instead of forced. Four different scenes, the same direction underneath every one of them. That was the week.

**Today:** Before tonight ends, name one thing this past week that pulled your attention toward what's above instead of toward what's merely on the surface of your own life. Thank God for it specifically, and ask Him to make next week's default the same.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for a week that kept pointing my attention toward what actually lasts. Forgive me for the moments I still measured my life by what's visible instead of what's true. As this week closes, set my mind on things above, on purpose, starting tomorrow morning. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
