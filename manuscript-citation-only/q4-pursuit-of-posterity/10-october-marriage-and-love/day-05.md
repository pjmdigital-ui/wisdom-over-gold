# Day 5 — Joint Heirs

Quincy's company offered him a promotion that meant moving the family for the third time in six years. He said yes before he'd even finished the phone call, then told Renata about it that night the way he'd tell her about a schedule change, as a decision already made, with logistics attached. Renata had spent two years rebuilding a client base after the last move. She asked him, calmly, whether it had occurred to him to ask her first instead of telling her after.

It hadn't. Without ever thinking it through, Quincy had assumed his career was the load-bearing wall of the family's future, and hers was something built around it: worth protecting when convenient, movable when it wasn't. He'd never said that sentence to himself in those words. He'd just never once, across three moves, treated her work or her tiredness as carrying the same weight as his own.

*My career is what actually provides for this family. Her job, her needs, her limits — those flex around mine, because mine is the one that can't.* Quincy had built years of decisions on that idea without ever putting it into words he'd have to defend out loud.

St. Peter speaks to husbands directly on exactly this point, and his reason has nothing to do with whose income is bigger. In 1 Peter 3:7, he tells husbands to live with their wives thoughtfully, honoring them as people who share equally in the grace of life, so that nothing gets in the way of their own prayers. The idea of a shared inheritance in that verse puts Renata on equal footing with Quincy before God. She inherits the same grace, holds the same worth, no matter whose paycheck happened to be bigger that year. Peter doesn't tell husbands to give in to their wives on everything. He tells them to live with real care, to actually weigh what she's carrying before deciding what he's owed.

The warning in that verse is the part Quincy had never noticed: a husband who won't honor his wife as an equal heir of that grace finds his own prayers blocked. God ties a man's relationship with Him to how he treats the woman he's supposedly leading. Quincy's third move had gone wrong in how the decision got made. He'd treated it like a call about a resource, when it needed to be a decision made alongside someone who inherits everything he inherits.

Quincy didn't need to stop chasing the promotion. He needed to go back to Renata and actually ask the question this time, not inform her, ask her, and mean it enough to hear a no.

**Today:** Identify one decision where you treated your wife's job, needs, or limits as secondary to your own without saying so out loud. Go to her today, name it honestly, and ask, not tell, what she actually needs from you about it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've treated my wife's needs as something that flexes around mine instead of honoring her as an equal heir of Your grace. Forgive me for the decisions I made for both of us as if they were only mine to make. Teach me to live with her the way Peter describes, with real consideration, not convenience. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 PETER 3:7
