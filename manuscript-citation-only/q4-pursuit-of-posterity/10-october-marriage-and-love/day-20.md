# Day 20 — A Hard Season, Faithfully Prayed

Paulina hadn't been herself in almost a year. It started small: sleeping more, canceling plans, saying less at dinner. By month eight Gerald was living with a wife who moved through the house like she was underwater, present but hard to reach even when they were sitting on the same couch. He'd tried everything he knew: cheering her up, giving her space, getting frustrated and immediately regretting it, praying the same short prayer most nights that things would go back to normal. Nothing moved. Some weeks he caught himself resenting the marriage he actually had, missing the one he used to have, wondering how long he was supposed to keep showing up to a relationship that felt, most days, like effort with no return.

*If my prayers aren't fixing this, either I'm praying wrong or God isn't listening,* was the conclusion Gerald kept almost reaching, the kind of thought that talks a tired man out of persistence right when persistence is the only thing left to offer. St. Paul doesn't promise Gerald a fast fix. He tells him what to do with the waiting itself, in Romans 12:12: rejoice in hope, be patient through hardship, and stay constant in prayer. Constant, not occasional, not until it stops feeling like it's working. The verse measures faithfulness by whether a man keeps showing up to prayer, not by whether the situation visibly changes today.

**On St. Rita of Cascia:** Rita spent years married to a husband whose temper made those years genuinely hard, and she met that difficulty the way Paul describes, with prayer that never let up and love that kept choosing him anyway. By the time he died, he had changed, softened by decades of a patience he hadn't earned. Rita's story doesn't tell anyone to stay silent through real danger. The Church that made her a saint also teaches, plainly, that protecting yourself and the people you love is part of faithful living, not a failure of it. If a marriage involves anything resembling abuse or unsafe conduct, getting real help, pastoral or professional, is the loving move, not a lesser one. What Rita does offer a man like Gerald is narrower: proof that persistent, faithful prayer through a genuinely hard season is never wasted effort, even when nothing visible changes for a long time.

Faithful love, for Gerald, ended up meaning more than praying alone in the car before he went inside. It meant finding Paulina a good therapist and driving her to the first appointment himself. It meant telling her, out loud, that he wasn't going anywhere, and meaning it on the nights that were hardest to mean it.

**Today:** Name the specific hard season your marriage, or your spouse, is in right now, and commit to one concrete, constant act of persistence today: a prayer said out loud for her by name, a call made to find real help, or simply staying present through one more hard evening instead of checking out.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've wondered some nights whether my prayers for my wife are doing anything at all. Teach me to be constant in prayer the way Rita was, patient through hardship instead of demanding a fast fix. Where real help is needed beyond prayer, give me the wisdom and the courage to find it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ROMANS 12:12
