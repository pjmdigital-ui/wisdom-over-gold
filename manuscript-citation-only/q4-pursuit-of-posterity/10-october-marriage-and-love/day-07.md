# Day 7 — Closing Week One

Timothy sat on the back steps Sunday evening while Laura finished cleaning up from dinner inside, and he found himself thinking back over the week without meaning to. Nothing dramatic had happened. No blowup, no crisis. He'd just noticed, sitting there, how much of the week he'd spent treating his marriage as something to manage rather than something to honor: the quick corrections, the mental tally of who'd done what, the assumption that if the bills were paid and nobody was fighting, the marriage itself was fine.

He thought about the sentence he'd built his whole week on without ever saying it out loud: as long as nothing's actively wrong, the marriage doesn't need my attention. It had let him coast through six days without once asking what he actually owed Laura beyond keeping the peace and covering his half of the mortgage.

Scripture had been sitting underneath the whole week, patient, waiting for him to notice it. Back in Genesis 2:18, God looks at Adam alone in an otherwise finished world and calls his being alone the one thing not good, then sets out to give him a companion made for him. God named Adam's aloneness itself as the problem worth fixing, before Adam had a single task that needed help getting done. Eve answered a lack of companionship, not a shortage of labor. Timothy had spent a week living next to Laura while still handling large parts of his inner life alone: his stress about work, his frustration with his brother, his own private tally of who owed whom. He'd been married and lonely at the same time, and hadn't felt the weight of that until Sunday night made him sit still long enough to notice.

Covenant instead of a deal. Giving himself instead of keeping score. Marriage held in honor instead of laughed off at lunch. Those were the week's three lessons, and none of them hold up on their own without a man deciding, on purpose, that his wife deserves more from him than logistics and a lack of complaints. Timothy hadn't failed Laura in any way he could point to. He'd just let a week go by without giving her the one thing marriage was built to give a wife from her husband: real companionship, not just living in the same house.

He went inside before she finished the dishes, dried the last few by hand, and told her plainly that he'd been running the week on autopilot and wanted this coming one to look different. She didn't need the theology. She just needed him standing next to her at the sink instead of on the back steps thinking about it alone.

**Today:** Before tonight ends, tell your wife one specific way you drifted into managing the marriage this week instead of actually being present in it. Ask her what she needed from you this week that she didn't get, and listen without defending yourself.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, this week I kept the peace without really giving myself to my wife. Forgive me for treating the absence of conflict as the same thing as a healthy marriage. As this new week begins, teach me to be truly present with her, not just physically near her. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

GENESIS 2:18
