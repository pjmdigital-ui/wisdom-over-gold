# Day 18 — Where You Go, I Will Go

Roger got the call on a Tuesday morning: his position had been eliminated, effective immediately, twelve years in with barely a week's notice. He drove to a coffee shop instead of home, opened his laptop, and started applying to jobs before he'd told a single person — including his wife, Miriam. He told himself he was being efficient, getting ahead of the problem before it became a crisis. By Thursday he still hadn't said anything. He left the house at his normal time each morning and came home at his normal time each night, running errands and sitting in parking lots in between, rehearsing how he'd explain it once he had better news to soften the bad news with.

Miriam found out Friday, not from Roger but from a mutual friend who assumed she already knew. She was hurt and angry that Roger had spent four days carrying the news alone instead of telling her the truth the morning it happened.

*I'm supposed to be the one who handles this, and handling it means she doesn't have to worry about it yet* was the belief running the show, and it sounded protective right up until Miriam named what it actually was: exclusion dressed up as consideration. Roger had spent four days managing his wife's access to a hardship that belonged to both of them.

Ruth made the opposite decision, and she made it out loud, to a mother-in-law with nothing left to offer her: "Entreat me not to leave you or to return from following you; for where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge; your people shall be my people, and your God my God; where you die I will die, and there will I be buried. May the Lord do so to me and more also if even death parts me from you" (Ruth 1:16-17, RSV-CE). Ruth bound herself to Naomi's actual circumstances, empty hands and all, before either of them knew how the story would end.

A marriage covenant runs the same direction. Miriam hadn't married Roger for the version of him with everything under control. She'd married him for the whole of it, hardship included, and every day he spent hiding a hard season from her taught her, without his ever meaning to say it out loud, that the covenant only covered the easy parts.

That night Roger told her everything — the call, the four days, the parking lots. Miriam cried, then made coffee, then sat down with his laptop and started looking at job listings next to him. "Where you go," she said, "I go. You don't get to decide that alone."

**Today:** Tell your wife about one hardship — financial, physical, emotional — you've been managing alone rather than bringing to her. Let her all the way in today, not after you've already fixed it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for the times I've decided a hardship was mine to carry alone instead of ours to face together. Give me the honesty to bring my wife in while things are still hard, not after I've already solved them. Thank You for a marriage built to hold real difficulty, not just the easy days. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
