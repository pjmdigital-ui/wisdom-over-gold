# Day 9 — Before the Sun Goes Down

Asher found out about the overage from the invoice, not from Nora. The contractor's revised quote for the kitchen came in nine thousand dollars over what she'd told him weeks earlier, when he signed off on the cabinets. By the time the email reached him, she'd known for four days and hadn't said a word. He didn't yell. He went quiet instead, answering her at dinner in single words, letting the silence sit at the table like a third person nobody had invited.

By nine o'clock they'd traded nothing but logistics for hours — who was picking up which kid tomorrow, passed back and forth like coworkers splitting a shift. Asher went up first. He got into bed with his back to her side and told himself the reasonable thing: sleep on it, say nothing you'll regret, let tomorrow sort it out. Nora came up twenty minutes later and did the same, careful not to let her feet cross the middle of the mattress.

He was operating on a belief he'd never said out loud: that distance overnight does the same work a conversation would, only without the risk of saying the wrong thing. Morning, he figured, would arrive with the tension already gone, the way weather passes.

St. Paul allowed for the anger itself — Ephesians 4:26 tells believers they can be angry without sinning — but drew a hard boundary around how long it could sit unaddressed: don't let a day end with it still burning. Same day. He gave no room for "once we've both cooled off" or "in the morning, when it's easier." An unresolved grievance left alone overnight doesn't sit still. It keeps working in the dark, building a case, until each of them has finished the argument privately in his or her own head, with nobody there to correct the record.

Asher lay there rehearsing his side of it, sentence by sentence, the way a lawyer preps a closing argument. Three feet away, Nora was doing the same on her side of the bed. Neither of them was any closer to an actual conversation than they'd been at dinner — they were just getting better armed for one.

At 9:40 he rolled over and said her name into the dark. He didn't manage a full apology. He offered four plain words instead: "I don't want this." She didn't turn over right away. But she answered, and they talked until past eleven — tired, clumsy about it, circling back twice to say something more clearly — until the number on the invoice mattered less than the four days of silence that had let it grow into something bigger than it needed to be.

**Today:** If there's a disagreement sitting unresolved in your house right now, don't wait for tomorrow's better mood. Go to your wife tonight and say the plain version of it: "I don't want to go to bed like this — can we finish talking?" Let it be imperfect. Let it happen before the light goes off.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've let silence do the talking too many times, telling myself distance was the same thing as peace. Give me the courage to go to my wife tonight instead of waiting for morning to fix what only honesty can fix. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

EPHESIANS 4:26
