# Day 13 — Set as a Seal

The kids were at Marisol's parents' for the night, the first evening alone Bruno and she had gotten in six weeks, and they sat down on the couch together with the fireplace on and nothing scheduled. She started telling him about a conversation she'd had with her sister that afternoon, something that had actually unsettled her, and Bruno was there — physically, on the same couch, angled toward her — while his phone sat face-up on the cushion between them, lighting up every few minutes with a fantasy football score he glanced at without meaning to.

He didn't leave the room. He didn't say a word that suggested he wasn't listening. But three times in ten minutes he looked down mid-sentence, and Marisol watched him do it, and stopped mid-thought once to ask, "Are you actually here right now?" He said yes. He believed it, too — being in the room felt like enough, as if presence were a location rather than a decision he had to keep making every few minutes.

The Song of Solomon describes a love that refuses to settle for being merely nearby. In Song of Solomon 8:6, the bride asks her beloved to set her like a seal over his heart and his arm, comparing love's strength to death itself and its ardor to a fire nothing can extinguish. A seal marks something as claimed — singular, given entirely to one thing, not shared out among several. The bride in that poem asks for more than shared space in her beloved's attention. She asks to be the whole of it, worn where he can't set it down and forget it's there.

A phone on the cushion between two people is a small thing to measure a marriage against, and that is exactly why it works as a measure. Bruno's body was in the room the entire evening. His attention was checking out and back in every few minutes, a fraction of him always elsewhere, and Marisol could feel the difference between a man who is present and a man who is merely located.

He put the phone face-down in the other room before she'd finished her second sentence about her sister. It felt almost dramatic, turning off a football score to listen to a family story — the exact size of gesture the moment actually needed, because his wife had learned to notice, correctly, when she had all of him and when she had most of him.

**Today:** The next time you sit down with your wife for real conversation, put your phone in another room first, not just face-down beside you. Tell her plainly: "I want you to have all of my attention, not most of it."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've confused being in the room with actually being present, and my wife has felt the difference even when I haven't noticed it myself. Teach me to give her my whole attention the way I've promised her my whole life. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

SONG OF SOLOMON 8:6
