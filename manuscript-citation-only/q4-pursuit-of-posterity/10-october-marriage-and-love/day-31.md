# Day 31 — One Flesh, Revisited

Barnaby still has the paper the priest read from at his and Adele's wedding, folded into the back of an old missal neither of them opens much anymore. It's the passage from Genesis, chapter two, about a man leaving his father and mother to join his wife, the two of them becoming one flesh. He remembers hearing it that day the way most men do: as a beautiful line about the two of them, about the wedding itself, about a doorway he was walking through that morning and would finish walking through by that evening.

Twenty-some years later, the verse means something different to him now. Leaving his father and mother took years, not one walk down an aisle. An argument in his late twenties finally moved his loyalty from his mother's opinion of Adele to his wife's actual voice. Another argument in his forties made him choose Adele's read on a family decision over what his father had always expected of him. Cleaving to Adele got decided again and again too, not just once at the altar. He chose it on a Tuesday when he told Adele the truth about a mistake instead of managing what she'd think of him. He chose it again in a hard year when staying meant something, because for the first time, leaving had felt like a real option. Understanding "one flesh" took the same shape. Thirty-one days spent looking hard at his own marriage this past month showed him it's still being formed in him, thirty-one days that started with a version of this same verse and end with it again.

The belief this whole month set out to take apart was a simple one, and Barnaby had carried it for years without ever putting words to it: marriage is supposed to make him happy, and if it's this hard, either something's gone wrong or he married the wrong woman. What thirty-one days of scripture, failure, apology, and ordinary faithfulness actually showed him was close to the opposite. The difficulty was rarely proof the marriage had failed, and far more often it was the exact place the covenant was being formed, in the apology offered first, in the eyes guarded on a hard week, in the phone put down, in delighting in the wife of his youth one more time.

Genesis 2:24 describes what a covenant of giving yourself fully actually costs and actually builds, and it's about far more than a wedding day. It was spoken once near the beginning of Scripture, and it gets lived out in pieces for as long as two people keep leaving, and cleaving, and becoming one flesh, instead of stopping after the first time they heard it read aloud.

October closes here, but the covenant it described carries on past the month. It's the ground everything else in this quarter stands on. What gets built in a marriage doesn't stay contained inside it, and it becomes the household a man leads, the children he raises, the legacy he leaves behind long after he's stopped being able to measure any of it himself. That's where the next months of Posterity are headed. This one was about the foundation underneath all of it: two people, one flesh, still becoming what they promised the day they said so out loud.

**Today:** Read Genesis 2:24 out loud to your wife tonight, the way it was likely read at your own wedding. Tell her one specific way you've seen "one flesh" become true between you since, not on the wedding day, but in the years after it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thank You for a covenant that costs more and means more than I understood the day I made it. Let the leaving and the cleaving keep happening in me, today and for as long as You give Adele and me together. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
