# Day 3 — Love That Endures

Charles noticed it during a stretch when Claire was working nights to finish a certification, and he was handling bedtime, dinner, and the dog by himself most evenings. They passed each other in the hallway more than they talked. The easy affection they'd had a year earlier, the inside jokes, the reaching for her hand without thinking about it, had gone quiet under how tired they both were. One night, lying awake after she'd fallen asleep mid-sentence describing her day, Charles took an honest look at himself and didn't like the answer. He didn't feel much of anything toward her lately. Not anger. Not longing. Mostly just tired, right alongside her.

He didn't say it to anyone, but the thought sat there for weeks: *if I loved her the way I used to, I'd still feel it, even now. The fact that I don't means something's cooling off, and I should be worried about it.* He'd been judging the health of his marriage by whether the feeling showed up, and by that measure, this season was failing.

St. Paul wrote about love to a church that was fighting over spiritual gifts, not to newlyweds, which is part of why his description has almost nothing to do with feeling. In 1 Corinthians 13:7-8, he describes a love that carries whatever weight comes, keeps trusting, keeps hoping, and simply outlasts everything set against it, a love that never runs out. Paul lists love's marks as things it does under pressure, like carrying, believing, hoping, and lasting, not things it feels while life is easy. He talks about love enduring because he knows seasons will come when the feeling thins out, and something else has to carry the weight instead.

That changes what Charles had actually been measuring. A feeling that rises and falls with too little sleep and a packed schedule tells you how tired two people are. It doesn't tell you much else. The love Paul describes was still fully at work in Charles's house that whole exhausted stretch. It showed up in the dinners he cooked, the bedtime stories he kept reading alone, the fact that he stayed present instead of checking out when checking out would have been easier. Charles had been calling that absence of feeling a cooling marriage. It looked a lot more like love doing exactly what Paul said it does: enduring through a season that didn't have much warmth left over to spend.

Charles didn't need to force a feeling that wasn't there that week. He needed to keep carrying what needed carrying, and trust that the feeling had a way of coming back once the season did. Its being gone for a while had never been the real test of whether he loved Claire.

**Today:** Think of one specific way you've kept showing up for your wife this week even without feeling much toward her in the moment. Name it to yourself as love, not as its absence, and tell her one thing you appreciate about how she's carried this season too.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've measured my love for my wife by how much I feel it instead of how faithfully I've carried it. Thank You for showing me love that endures rather than love that only shows up when it's easy. Keep me steady toward her in the tired seasons, trusting You with the feeling I can't manufacture on my own. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 CORINTHIANS 13:7-8
