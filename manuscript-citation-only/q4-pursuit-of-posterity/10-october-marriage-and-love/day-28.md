# Day 28 — Closing Week Four

Harvey sat on the back steps Sunday night after Meredith had gone up to bed, running back through the week the way he did most Sundays lately. Nothing had gone wrong. They hadn't fought. He'd caught himself, more than once, measuring the marriage by that low a bar — no blowups, no silence at dinner, nobody sleeping angry — and calling it good, the same way he might call a car running fine as long as the check-engine light stayed off.

He thought back over the week's smaller moments instead: telling Meredith three specific things he actually admired in her instead of the usual "love you" on the way out the door; catching himself irritated over a dropped plan and choosing, for once, to see it as one more place their marriage was still being formed rather than one more thing going wrong; pulling up an old photo of the two of them and feeling, for a few real minutes, something closer to what he'd felt looking at her twenty years ago than what he usually let himself feel on a Tuesday.

None of it had been dramatic. All of it had taken more effort than simply not fighting.

*As long as nothing's actively wrong, the marriage is fine* — Harvey had never said the sentence out loud, but it was the standard he'd been running on for longer than he wanted to admit, and it left the actual enjoying of Meredith as something optional, extra credit for a slow week. Ecclesiastes, for all its weariness about the shortness of a man's days, doesn't let him off with mere absence of trouble. In Ecclesiastes 9:9, the Preacher tells him plainly to enjoy his life with the wife he loves, for however many fleeting days he's been given under the sun — that this, and the work alongside it, is the portion actually given to him. The Preacher has just spent nine chapters cataloging how little a man actually controls. He doesn't conclude from that that marriage is one more thing to endure until it's over. He calls it a portion — something given, meant to be enjoyed on purpose, not merely survived without incident.

A marriage with no active problems and no active enjoyment is still coasting, and coasting has a ceiling most men don't notice until they've been under it for years.

**Today:** Plan one specific thing this week that has nothing to do with logistics, chores, or fixing anything — just enjoying your wife's company on purpose. Put it on the calendar before the week fills in around it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for a wife You gave me not to merely manage but to actually enjoy. Forgive me for settling for the absence of conflict and calling it enough. Show me this week one specific way to enjoy her on purpose, and give me the follow-through to keep the plan. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
