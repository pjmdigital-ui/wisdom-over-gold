# Day 11 — The Vow He Made

Julius sat in the garage a little after midnight, engine cold, radio off, turning his wedding ring between two fingers the way he did when he needed something to do with his hands. The business had been losing money for going on three years. He and Simone hadn't fought that night, and that was almost the worse part. They'd just passed each other in the kitchen like housemates on a shared lease, tired in the same house at the same time, saying almost nothing.

He let himself think through the thing he'd been avoiding: what would actually happen if he left. Not an affair, nothing that dramatic, just an honest look at an exit. He told himself the reasonable version of it, the one that sounded almost noble: he'd promised a version of himself who felt a certain way about her twelve years ago, in a tux, at twenty-six, and a man wasn't obligated to keep honoring feelings he no longer had. In his private accounting, the vow had an expiration date nobody had mentioned out loud at the altar.

Scripture doesn't leave that kind of accounting room to work with. Numbers 30:2 lays down the law about a vow made to God: a man who makes a vow to the Lord, or binds himself by an oath, is not to break his word. He's held to everything he said. The weight of the vow doesn't rest on how the man feels the day he's asked to keep it. It rests on the fact that he said it, in front of God, and God takes a man's word seriously even on the days the man doesn't.

A wedding vow is exactly this kind of vow: a word given in front of God, meant to hold no matter how a man feels underneath it on any given day. Julius had made his in front of a priest and forty witnesses, and more to the point, in front of God, and no amount of exhaustion in this season changed what he'd actually said that day. What he wanted was an exit from something hard, and the vow was the one thing standing between him and calling that exit reasonable.

He put the ring back on before he went inside, a small, almost embarrassing move, but he needed his hands to do something honest before the rest of him caught up. He didn't have a plan yet for fixing three years of drift. He had one decision made: the word he'd given at twenty-six still bound the man he was now, whatever season they were in right now.

**Today:** Say your wedding vow out loud today, the actual words if you remember them, or the substance of them if you don't: "for better or worse, until death." Tell yourself plainly: "I gave my word, and my word still holds, whatever season we're in right now."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, some seasons make my promise feel heavier than the day I made it, and I've caught myself looking for a reasonable-sounding way out. Remind me that my word to her was also a word to You, and hold me to it even on the days I'd rather You didn't. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
