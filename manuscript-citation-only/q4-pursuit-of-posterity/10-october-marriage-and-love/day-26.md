# Day 26 — Greater Love

Alistair spent eleven days in the hospital in March after a surgery that had complications nobody had warned him about. Sabine rearranged her entire freelance schedule around the ICU's visiting hours, slept two nights in a chair rather than go home, and never once complained about any of it, not to him, not that he ever heard, not even the night a nurse asked her when she'd last eaten and she couldn't remember.

He came home eventually and healed, and life folded back into its usual shape faster than either of them expected. He never quite went back and thanked her for those eleven days the way they deserved. Real sacrifice, he'd always assumed, looked like a burning building or an organ donation, something dramatic and clear, the kind of thing you'd rise to if the moment ever truly demanded it. What Sabine had actually done, day after ordinary day in a hospital chair, didn't register on that scale, so it never got named as what it was.

Jesus names the actual scale himself, in John 15:13: there is no greater love than a man laying down his life for his friends. He doesn't say greater love looks like a single dramatic scene. Laying down a life happens in pieces long before it ever happens all at once, if it happens all at once at all: a chair instead of a bed, a schedule instead of a career, eleven ordinary days instead of one heroic one.

**On St. Gianna Beretta Molla:** Gianna was a doctor, married with three young children, when a complication in a fourth pregnancy put her own life at serious risk. Doctors offered her options that would have ended the pregnancy to protect her. She refused them, telling her husband plainly to save the child if it came down to a choice between them. She gave birth to a healthy daughter and died a week later. Gianna's greater love took the shape it did because of a marriage already built, for years before the crisis, on treating her family's life as worth more than her own comfort.

Alistair's eleven days in a hospital chair were nowhere near what Gianna gave. They were cut from the same cloth, and he'd been filing them under "what wives do" instead of what they actually were: his wife laying something real down for him, in the plainest, least dramatic way there is.

**Today:** Name one specific thing your wife has laid down for you, time, sleep, a career step, a version of her own life, that you've never actually thanked her for by name. Tell her tonight, specifically, what it cost her and that you saw it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for a wife who has laid things down for me that I let pass without naming. Show me the sacrifices in my own marriage that don't look dramatic enough to notice, and give me the sense to thank her for them today instead of assuming she already knows. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

JOHN 15:13
