# Day 17 — Words Like Sword Thrusts

Every night once the dishes were done, Peter and Corinne ran through the same list at the kitchen table: whose turn to drive carpool, whether the mortgage payment had cleared, what was left on the credit card, who was calling the plumber. It took maybe ten minutes and it worked, in the sense that nothing ever blew up between them. One Thursday, Corinne set her coffee down mid-list and said, "I feel like I only ever get the project-manager version of you anymore. Do you ever miss just talking to me?"

Peter felt the question land somewhere he didn't want it to. "I'm handling everything on my end, Corinne. What more do you want from me?" It came out sharper than he meant it to, aimed less at her question than at whatever the question had touched. Corinne picked her coffee back up and said, "Never mind," and they finished the list.

Peter told himself later that no real damage had been done — no yelling, no name-calling, nothing a stranger walking past the window would have flagged. *As long as I keep this calm, I'm keeping us fine* was the working theory, and it had held for years, in the narrow sense that nothing had ever caught fire. What it had actually taught Corinne was that a real question would cost her something to ask, so she'd learned to ask fewer of them.

Proverbs draws the line where Peter's theory missed it. Proverbs 12:18 warns that careless words can cut like a blade, while the words of the wise bring healing instead. A sword thrust doesn't have to be loud to do damage. A single sharp sentence, delivered evenly and without raising his voice, can close a conversation just as fast as shouting would. Peter had been calling that keeping the peace, when what he'd actually been doing was keeping a wound from ever getting looked at, stopping the bleeding by refusing to let anyone near the cut.

He thought about it on the drive to work the next morning and called Corinne from the parking lot before he went in. "Yesterday — you asked a real question and I answered it like you were criticizing my performance review. I do miss just talking to you. I don't know when we stopped." Corinne was silent for a second, then said, "Since about the time we had a mortgage, probably." They both laughed, and it was the first real exchange either of them had offered the other in days.

**Today:** Tonight, before the logistics list starts, ask your wife one real question you actually want the honest answer to. When she answers, resist the instinct to defend yourself — let your first response be one that heals instead of one that ends the conversation fast.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for the sharp, quick answers I've used to end conversations I didn't want to have. Give me a tongue that heals instead of one that cuts fast and clean. Make me patient enough tonight to hear what my wife is actually trying to say. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
