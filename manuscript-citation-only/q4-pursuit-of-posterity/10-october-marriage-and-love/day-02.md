# Day 2 — A Covenant, Not a Contract

George kept score of his marriage in his head, without ever writing a word of it down. He took out the trash and fixed the leaking faucet, so Margaret owed him a good mood by dinner. He sat through her sister's baby shower, so he was due a free pass on the next Sunday football marathon. When Margaret came home short-tempered from a bad week at work and snapped at him for leaving dishes in the sink, his first thought went straight to a quiet check of whether she'd held up her end lately, and a private verdict that she hadn't, more than any real concern for the week she'd just had.

He noticed himself doing it one night when Margaret apologized for being distant for a few weeks while she cared for her father in the hospital. George's honest first feeling was relief that the scales had balanced back out. He had been crediting and debiting her for over a year without ever telling her the score even existed.

*As long as we're both holding up our end, this works. The day one of us stops, I'm owed something back, or I'm out.* George had never said it that plainly to anyone. But it was the actual rule running underneath every small resentment he'd been keeping track of.

The prophet Malachi caught the men of Israel running this same kind of score against their wives, and he named it for what it was. In Malachi 2:14, he tells them plainly that the Lord himself stands as witness to the covenant they made with the wife of their youth, a woman who is their companion and their wife by that covenant, even as they had broken faith with her. A covenant made her his companion, not someone on the other side of a deal. Malachi doesn't soften what happens when a man starts treating that covenant as something with conditions. Two verses later, he says God hates divorce and warns the men to guard themselves and stay faithful. God is a witness standing over the promise itself, not over whichever spouse happens to be performing better this month.

A contract protects two people from each other, and it falls apart the moment either side stops holding up their part. A covenant works differently. It's a bond made before God that doesn't change its terms based on this week's performance. George's scorekeeping turned Margaret into someone he was measuring against himself, when the vow he'd actually made bound him to her no matter what she was contributing that week. Her bad week was exactly the kind of week the covenant existed to carry her through, not a broken deal she needed to make up for.

George didn't need Margaret to hold up her end better. He needed to stop keeping score at all, and start treating his vow to her as something he owed no matter what, not a deal he'd only agreed to as long as the terms stayed in his favor.

**Today:** Name one specific thing you've been silently keeping score of in your marriage — a chore, a mood, an apology you feel you're owed. Let it go out loud to your wife tonight, without asking for anything back in return.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've been keeping score in my marriage instead of honoring the covenant I made before You. Forgive me for treating my wife like someone who has to earn my patience and my love. Teach me to give myself to her the way You bind Yourself to Your covenants, without conditions attached. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
