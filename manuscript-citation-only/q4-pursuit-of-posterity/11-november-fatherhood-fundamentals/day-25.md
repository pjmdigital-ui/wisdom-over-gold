# Day 25 — Strong and Courageous

Harold spent Saturday morning packing Ingrid's car for the third time. She'd already packed it herself the night before, everything for the two-day drive to her new job in Denver arranged exactly how she wanted it, and Harold had gone back out after she went to bed to rearrange the boxes so the heavier ones sat lower. In the morning he found a reason to pull up her new apartment complex's website and read the fine print on the lease again, then texted her a paragraph about renter's insurance she hadn't asked for. When she finally pulled out of the driveway, he stood in the yard with his phone already in his hand, ready to call in an hour to make sure she'd stopped for gas like he'd suggested.

He couldn't have said, if you'd asked him directly, that any of this was really about renter's insurance. Ingrid is twenty-four. She has managed a budget, a lease, and a difficult boss for two years already without his help. What Harold hasn't said out loud, even to himself, is the quieter question underneath the packing and the texts: if she doesn't need him to check the oil or read the fine print anymore, what is he still for in her life?

Moses faced a version of this at the edge of the promised land, handing off forty years of leadership to a younger man in front of the entire nation he'd led. He didn't cling to the role. He spoke over Joshua exactly what Joshua would need once Moses was no longer there to supply it. In Deuteronomy 31:7-8, he charges Joshua to be strong and courageous, because Joshua is the one who will lead this people into the land the LORD swore to their fathers and help them take possession of it. He tells him that the LORD Himself goes ahead of him, will be with him, and will never fail him or abandon him, so he has no reason to fear or lose heart.

Notice what Moses doesn't do. He doesn't hand Joshua a checklist of things to call him about. He names strength Joshua already has to grow into, and he points him past himself, to the God who goes ahead of both of them. That's the handoff Harold is actually being asked to make, not a withdrawal of love, but a redirection of it, from a father who stays load-bearing to a father who speaks courage over a daughter capable of standing without him and then trusts the same God who raised her to keep going ahead of her.

Harold's worth to Ingrid was never going to be measured by how long she kept needing him the way a ten-year-old needs him. It's measured by whether he built someone strong enough not to.

**Today:** Before you do one more practical thing for your son or daughter today, check something, fix something, remind them of something they already know, stop and say it a different way instead: "You don't need me the way you used to. That's exactly what I was hoping for." Let those words, not one more piece of unsolicited advice, be what you actually hand them.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thank You for however far You've brought Ingrid already, further than I sometimes let myself notice. Help me let go of needing to be needed, and give me the courage to speak strength over her instead of worry. Go before her the way You went before Joshua, and let that be enough for both of us. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

DEUTERONOMY 31:7-8
