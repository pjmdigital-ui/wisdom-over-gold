# Day 22 — Laying Down the Small Things

Byron had just closed his laptop for the night, one more email typed and ready to send, when Otis toddled up in footie pajamas holding a fistful of wooden blocks and said "again, again." It was the third "again" of the evening, the tower they'd built and knocked down twice already. Byron felt the familiar pull toward the phone on the counter, the thought that he'd earned fifteen quiet minutes after a full day, that a three-year-old's tower could wait while he finished what actually needed finishing. He built it with Otis anyway, half-present, thumb still finding his phone screen between blocks.

Vera caught him at it later and didn't say much. She just mentioned that Otis had noticed, kids notice more than men like to think, and asked whether Byron remembered the last time he'd built something with his son without also checking something else at the same time. He didn't, not clearly.

*My real contribution to this family happens at my desk. The blocks are just the downtime I'm being interrupted from.* Byron had never said that sentence to himself, but it explained exactly why "again, again" felt like an interruption instead of an invitation.

Scripture measures love by a much smaller unit than most men expect. First John 3:16 defines love by Christ laying down His life for us, and calls us to lay down our own lives for each other in the same way. Most fathers will never face a moment that calls for literally laying down their life. What they get instead, most nights, is the far smaller version of the same command: laying down the phone, the inbox, the fifteen minutes they wanted for themselves, for a three-year-old asking for one more tower. That small, repeated version is exactly the love the verse describes, the version most men will actually be asked to practice, over and over, for years.

Fatherhood rarely announces its big moments in advance. It shows up mostly as "again, again," a request small enough to wave off a hundred times before a man notices what he's been waving off. The laying-down this verse asks for is a daily rehearsal, small enough to seem forgettable and repeated enough to actually shape a life.

**Today:** The next time your child asks for "one more" of anything, a game, a story, a tower, put the phone down completely and give it to them fully, until they're the one who decides it's done.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've treated the small, daily asks of fatherhood as interruptions to my real life more often than I've noticed. Teach me that laying down a phone or an inbox for my son is its own real act of love. Help me be fully present the next time he asks for one more. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 JOHN 3:16
