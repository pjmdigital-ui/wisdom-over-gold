# Day 7 — Closing Week One

Rhett sat on the back steps Sunday evening while his wife Wren got their daughter Della ready for bed inside. He was thinking back over a week that had quietly changed how he saw being a father. A man comforting a toddler through a thunderstorm learned that presence, not just providing, is what a child remembers as the shape of "Father." A dad who'd never once blessed his son out loud, only corrected him, found out his kid had been keeping track of which voice he actually heard. A father standing arms-crossed in his own living room realized his body had been saying "not yet" to his daughter long before his mouth ever did. A man who turned good news into the next correction learned how fast that habit teaches a kid to stop bringing him anything good at all. Another man learned the difference between being pleased with his son's performance and actually delighting in his son, and that his kid had been getting almost none of the second. And a father who lost his son for eleven minutes at a fairground discovered that his fear, disguised as anger, was love arriving faster than he could sort it out.

Six different men, six different Tuesdays, one thread running under all of it: a child is always reading something off his father, whether the father means to be teaching anything or not. The correcting, the blessing, the crossed arms, the loud singing or its absence. None of it goes unnoticed. Slowly it becomes a kid's working idea of what a father is, and later, without either of them choosing it, what God is.

Proverbs names what that adds up to over the long run, not just in a single week. In Proverbs 20:7, a father who lives with real integrity is promised a blessing that carries down to his children after him. The blessing here comes from adding up small, ordinary days, not one grand gesture. A father who keeps walking in integrity, day after day, hands his children something they never had to be taught in words. They simply grow up standing in it.

Rhett hadn't fixed anything permanently this week. He'd noticed things about himself he hadn't let himself see before, which is a smaller kind of progress but a real one. Integrity that gets handed down doesn't start with one dramatic overhaul. It starts with a man willing to keep noticing.

**Today:** Pick the one moment from this week's themes that landed hardest: presence, blessing, delight, discouragement, or fear disguised as anger. Do the smallest concrete version of it again today, on purpose, before the week's lesson fades into next week's routine.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thank You for a week that showed me more clearly what my kids are actually picking up from how I father them. Keep me walking in integrity even on the days no one's watching closely enough to notice. Let what I hand down to my children come from that steadiness, not from whatever mood I happen to be in. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
