# Day 11 — The Discipline They're Watching

Judah's alarm went off at 5:15, same as every weekday, and the debate that morning wasn't new: three hours of sleep instead of six, a body that ached in a way that made the couch downstairs sound like the only reasonable option. He'd talked himself out of the run before — "one morning off won't matter," true enough on its own — but this particular morning he was standing in the hallway pulling on his shoes when he heard a door open behind him.

Finch, nine, stood there in pajamas, hair flat on one side, watching. "Why do you get up when you're tired?" he asked, not accusing, just curious the way nine-year-olds are curious about things they've clearly been wondering about for a while.

Judah didn't have a clean answer standing there at 5:20 in the morning. He had a half-formed idea he'd never said out loud: that what he did alone before the house woke up didn't really count toward raising his kids, since none of them were around to see it. The real fathering happened later — dinner, homework, tucking them in. The run was just something he did for himself, off the books.

St. Paul didn't leave that kind of split between private and fathering intact. Writing to the Corinthians about running his own race with something to show for it at the end, he said, in 1 Corinthians 9:27, that he disciplines his own body and keeps it under control, so that after preaching to others he doesn't find himself disqualified. Paul isn't talking about vanity or fitness for its own sake. He's talking about the discipline of the body as proof behind the discipline of the message — a man's word to others only carries weight if his own body isn't running the show behind closed doors.

Finch standing in that hallway exposed "off the books" for what it was. His son wasn't in the room for the run itself most mornings, but he was in the room for this — the choosing, the getting up, the deciding not to talk himself back into bed. That is the actual lesson, and it happens whether or not Judah planned on teaching anything that morning.

He laced the second shoe, told Finch he could stand in the driveway and time him if he wanted, and went out the door five minutes later than usual with a nine-year-old holding a stopwatch on the porch. He hadn't changed anything about the run itself. He'd let someone see the choosing, which turned out to be most of what mattered.

**Today:** Do the hard physical thing you'd normally do alone — the workout, the cold shower, the early run — where at least one of your kids can see you choose it. Say out loud, to them or just to yourself: "This is what keeping a promise to my own body looks like."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've kept the discipline of my body separate from the discipline I'm trying to teach my son, as if the two had nothing to do with each other. Let him see the choosing, not just the results, and let it teach him something about his own will that my words alone can't. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 CORINTHIANS 9:27
