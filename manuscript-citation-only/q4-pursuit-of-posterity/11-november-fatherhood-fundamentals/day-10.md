# Day 10 — Managing His Household Well

Marco had two rules that mattered more than the rest in his house: lights out by nine, no phones at the table. With Nell, ten, both held every night without exception. She knew it, and she didn't fight it much anymore. With Reeve, thirteen, both had gone soft sometime over the summer, one late night at a time, until "just finish the episode" and "let me answer this one text" had become the real rule, whatever the chart on the fridge said.

Nell noticed before Marco did. One night at 8:58 she asked, flat, not even upset about it: "How come Reeve gets to stay up and I don't?" Marco didn't have a real answer. He had an explanation: Reeve's older, Reeve's more responsible, Marco was too tired most nights to have the fight. But nothing that would survive being said out loud to a ten-year-old simply asking why the rule meant one thing for her and nothing for her brother.

He'd told himself, without ever quite putting it in words, that as long as he sounded consistent, same tone, same rules posted on the fridge, the actual follow-through didn't have to match for both kids. The rule on paper was doing the work the follow-through was supposed to do.

St. Paul, writing to Timothy about what to look for in a man fit to lead the Church, used a father's own household as the test case. In 1 Timothy 3:4, he says a leader must manage his own household well, with children who respect him and follow his lead in every way. The standard Paul describes is a household actually run according to what the father says, the same expectation applied the same way to every kid in it, so respect for him doesn't depend on which one is asking or how tired he is that night.

Marco's kids read, correctly, what he actually did rather than what the fridge chart said. Reeve learned that pushing past bedtime worked, and Nell learned that the rules only really applied to her. Neither lesson was one he'd meant to teach, and both had been settling into place unnoticed for months before Nell finally said it out loud at the table. The fix is the same rule, enforced the same way, on the nights he doesn't feel like it as much as the nights he does. Not a stricter rule, just an honest one, applied without exception until his kids stop needing to test whether he means it.

**Today:** Pick the one rule in your house you know you enforce inconsistently. Tell your kids tonight, plainly: "I haven't been fair about this. Starting tonight, it's the same rule for everyone, every night." Then hold it that way for a full week.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let my son get away with what I hold my daughter to, and she noticed before I let myself admit it. Give me the backbone to run my house the same way for both of them, even on the tired nights. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 TIMOTHY 3:4
