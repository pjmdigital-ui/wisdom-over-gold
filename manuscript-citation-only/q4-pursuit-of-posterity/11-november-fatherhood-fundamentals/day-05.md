# Day 5 — Rejoicing Over You

Truett's sixteen-year-old son August spent three weeks building a water-filtration project for the regional science fair. He won second place in his category, beating kids from two magnet schools. He found his dad in the crowd afterward, ribbon still in hand, grinning in a way sixteen-year-olds mostly stop letting themselves do in public. Truett shook his hand and told him "nice job," then asked if he'd remembered to email his teacher a thank-you for the extra lab time. August's grin folded up fast. He said "yeah, I'll do it" in the flat voice he used for homework reminders, not victories.

Truett didn't notice it in the moment. He noticed it three days later, replaying the ribbon and the handshake and the reminder about the email. He realized that in sixteen years of fathering this kid, he couldn't remember the last time August had seen him simply happy about him. Not proud in the managing sense. Not satisfied that a task had gone well. Happy the way you're happy watching someone you love do something they love.

Was there a difference between being pleased with his son's performance and actually delighting in his son? Truett had never separated those two questions before. Once he did, he suspected August had been getting a steady diet of the first and almost none of the second.

The prophet Zephaniah gives Israel a picture of God that has nothing to do with a performance review. In Zephaniah 3:17, he describes the LORD living among His people as a strong warrior who saves them, not quietly pleased with them from a distance, but glad over them, calming them with His love, and celebrating over them with loud singing. Not a nod of approval. Not a quiet satisfaction kept to Himself. The prophet describes a God who sings out loud over His people, the way a father might if he let himself. Nothing in the verse ties that joy to a performance metric first. It's simply what God does over the people He loves.

Truett had built a father-son relationship almost entirely out of the authority side of the job: setting the standard, checking the follow-through, keeping August moving in the right direction. All of that mattered. None of it had ever once sounded like loud singing. A son can know his father is proud of what he produces and still wonder, underneath it, whether his father actually likes him.

**Today:** Tell your kid, specifically and out loud, one thing about them that has nothing to do with achievement or output. Just something you genuinely enjoy about who they are. Let your voice show it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've let being pleased with what my kids accomplish stand in for actually delighting in who they are. Thank You for a Word that shows You rejoicing over Your people with loud singing, not quiet approval held in reserve. Teach me to let my son hear that same gladness from me today, plainly, with nothing attached to it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ZEPHANIAH 3:17
