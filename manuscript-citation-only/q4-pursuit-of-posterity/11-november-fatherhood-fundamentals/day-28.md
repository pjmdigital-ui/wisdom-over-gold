# Day 28 — Closing Week Four

Alistair stood near the punch table at Hollis's graduation open house, half-listening to his brother-in-law tell a story about coaching Hollis's Little League team a decade earlier, when the man said something that stopped Alistair mid-sip: "You know what I actually remember about you from those years? You were at every single game. Every one. I coached three other kids on that team whose dads showed up maybe half the time, and I don't think I ever once looked over and you weren't there." Alistair hadn't thought of it as remarkable. He'd thought of it, most Saturdays, as just where he was supposed to be.

He couldn't point to any single sacrifice from those years that felt, at the time, like it was building toward this room full of family and a diploma on the wall. He remembered a lot of small ones instead — leaving work early more times than his boss probably wanted to count, sitting through a rough freshman season where Hollis barely played, having the same conversation about curfew a dozen different ways before it finally stuck, staying present through a stretch two years ago when Hollis pulled away and it would have been easier to let him go. None of it had felt, on any given Tuesday, like legacy. It had felt like Tuesday.

This month has kept circling back to that same discovery from different angles — a father's daily small sacrifices of presence are the actual material his legacy gets built from. Staying patiently engaged through a defiant season counts by that same measure. So does releasing a grown child toward a life that costs the father something personal. Scripture named, from the very start of this quarter's turn toward fatherhood, what all of it was actually for: Psalm 127:3 calls children a heritage from the LORD, a reward, the fruit of the womb itself. October ended on this verse, looking ahead at what a marriage would eventually be asked to produce. Standing at Hollis's open house, Alistair understood it from the other side — an inheritance he'd actually spent eighteen years handing over, one small Saturday at a time.

**Today:** Name one small sacrifice from this past week you didn't think mattered — a game you almost skipped, a hard conversation you almost avoided, a moment you stayed instead of walking away. Thank God for it out loud tonight, specifically, as part of the heritage this psalm describes.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for the ordinary sacrifices this week that didn't feel like much while I was making them. Let me see them the way You do — as the actual heritage I'm building, not as the cost of it. Keep me faithful in the small things I'll never get credit for. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PSALM 127:3
