# Day 26 — The Race Finished

Zachary was three emails deep into a conversation with a college track coach before he told Beckham any of it was happening. He'd found the coach's contact through a recruiting site, put together a highlight reel from meet footage without asking, and started drafting a message about Beckham's mile time before he'd stopped to consider whether Beckham even wanted to run in college. At Friday's meet he stood at the fence with his phone out, timing every lap against the split he needed for the scholarship cutoff, and when Beckham came in two seconds slow, Zachary's face did something he didn't manage to hide before Beckham saw it.

Beckham ran a personal best that night. It didn't feel that way to either of them in the car afterward.

Zachary would say, if pressed, that all of it comes from wanting the best for his son. Underneath that is a quieter accounting he's never said out loud. Beckham's scholarship, Beckham's varsity letter, Beckham's name in the paper, have become the scoreboard Zachary is using to measure whether he's done fatherhood right. If Beckham succeeds visibly enough, the last eighteen years get to count as a win. If he doesn't, Zachary isn't sure what he's left holding.

St. Paul wrote from a jail cell near the end of his life, with no way of knowing how any of the churches he'd planted would turn out, no scoreboard he could point to and call finished. What he does claim, in 2 Timothy 4:7, is entirely his own: that he stayed faithful in the struggle to the very end, ran his course all the way through, and never abandoned what he believed. Paul doesn't say he made Timothy successful, or that the Corinthians turned out well, or that Ephesus stayed faithful after he left. He accounts only for his own struggle, his own course run, his own faith kept, the part that was actually his to run.

That's the race Zachary has stopped watching. Beckham's mile time is Beckham's race, and Beckham will run it whether Zachary is timing it from the fence or not. Zachary's race is the one only he can finish: whether he showed up, told the truth, stayed faithful to his son as a father regardless of what the stopwatch says on any given Friday. A man can raise a son who never gets recruited anywhere and still finish his own race well. He can also raise a son who goes on to college on scholarship and finish his own race badly, if what that son remembers is a father whose face fell over two seconds.

**Today:** Write down one sentence and say it out loud before you see Beckham tonight: "My race is my faithfulness as a father, not his stopwatch." Then ask him about his day without mentioning the meet, the time, or the recruiting emails first. Let him bring it up if he wants to.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for measuring my fatherhood by what shows up on a scoreboard instead of by my own faithfulness to You and to my son. Help me finish my own race well, whatever Beckham's turns out to look like, and let him know he's loved apart from anything he achieves. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

2 TIMOTHY 4:7
