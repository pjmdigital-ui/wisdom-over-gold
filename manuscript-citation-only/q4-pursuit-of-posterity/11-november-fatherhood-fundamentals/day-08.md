# Day 8 — Discipline Without Provoking

Soren had told his son Finn twice not to throw the baseball near the house. The third time, it went through the garage window in a spray of glass that scattered across the driveway. Soren had just walked out of a work call to see it happen. He crossed the yard fast, grabbed the ball off the ground, and laid into Finn in a voice that had nothing measured about it. Grounded for two weeks. Phone taken. A string of "what were you thinking" that wasn't really a question. Finn, ten years old, stood there taking it with his shoulders up around his ears, the same posture he used with a coach who was yelling, not a father.

Soren swept up the glass alone twenty minutes later, replaying his own voice in his head. He had to admit something uncomfortable. The punishment landed the moment his work call got interrupted, not the moment Finn actually did anything wrong. The window had been broken for maybe four seconds before he was already shouting. It was his own frustration finding an easy target, not correction sized to what his son actually needed to learn.

He'd always told himself discipline in his house was about raising Finn right. Standing in the driveway with a dustpan, he had to wonder how much of it was actually about that, and how much was just his own irritation looking for permission to come out.

The letter to the Hebrews draws a sharp line between the two. Hebrews 12:10-11 compares fathers here on earth, who discipline for a short while based on their own judgment in the moment, with God, who disciplines us for our actual good, so we can share in His holiness. It's painful at the time, not pleasant, but it produces a harvest of right living for those who are trained by it later on. Discipline shaped by the mood of the one giving it is the human pattern named here. Against that stands God's discipline, aimed entirely at the good of the one receiving it. It's painful in the moment but bears fruit, because it was never about the parent's own relief.

What Soren did that afternoon was discipline driven by his own mood in the moment. It was fast and loud, sized to how angry he was rather than to what a ten-year-old actually needed to understand about listening and consequences. Finn probably learned something in that driveway. Mostly he learned that his father gets scary when he's stressed, which isn't nothing, but it isn't the lesson about the ball either.

**Today:** The next time your kid does something that needs correcting, wait sixty seconds before you say a word. Use that time to ask yourself whether the discipline you're about to give is sized to what they need, or to how angry you already are. Then discipline from the first answer, not the second.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let my own frustration set the size of the correction more times than I want to count. Thank You for disciplining me for my good and not Your own convenience, even when it costs You patience I don't deserve. Teach me to discipline my son the same way today, for his sake, not for the relief of my own temper. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
