# Day 27 — The Glory of Sons

Poppy wants Gerard to watch her "swim" in the bathtub for the fifth time tonight, kicking both legs against the tile while she waits for him to say something about it. He's sitting on the closed toilet lid with his phone in his hand, half-watching, thumb still moving through a work inbox that will still be there in twenty minutes. "I see you, bug," he says, not looking up in time to actually see the part she wanted him to see. She does it again anyway.

Gerard has been telling himself something like this for the past year, most days without noticing he's telling himself anything at all: that this stretch — bath time, bedtime, the same three books, the same songs — is the maintenance phase of fatherhood, the part to get through before the real relationship with Poppy starts later, once she's old enough to actually talk to him about things that matter. Is a three-year-old's fifth lap across the bathtub actually one of the things that matters? Most nights, exhausted, he'd say no without thinking twice.

Proverbs answers a different question than the one Gerard is asking, and answers it in a way that reframes his whole night. Proverbs 17:6 pairs two ideas — grandchildren as the crowning glory of the old, and fathers as the glory of their sons. Read that second half slowly: it doesn't say a son's glory is his own accomplishments. It says a son's glory — what he'll one day be proud of, what he'll point to — is his father. Whatever Poppy ends up carrying as her sense of what a father is, what he sounds like, whether he actually watches when she asks him to, is being built right now, in a bathtub, on a Tuesday that feels like nothing.

There is no later version of this relationship waiting on the other side of the maintenance phase. This is the relationship. The five laps across the tub, watched or half-watched, are the material Poppy's future glory in her father gets made out of, one unremarkable night at a time. Gerard isn't failing to be present until some easier season arrives. He's either showing up in this one or he isn't.

**Today:** Put your phone in another room for Poppy's bath and bedtime tonight — not on silent on the counter, actually in another room. Watch every single lap she swims, and tell her specifically what you saw, not just that you saw it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thank You for Poppy and for a night I was tempted to rush through instead of live in. Forgive me for treating the ordinary parts of fatherhood as something to get past. Let me actually be there tonight, all the way, so that whatever glory she finds in me someday is earned in nights like this one. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
