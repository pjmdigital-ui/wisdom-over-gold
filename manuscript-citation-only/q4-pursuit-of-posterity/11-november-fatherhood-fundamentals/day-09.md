# Day 9 — Teaching in the Ordinary Moments

Nathaniel picked up Nico from practice on Tuesdays, twenty minutes each way, and most weeks the radio filled every one of those minutes. Nico, nine, had a habit of asking hard questions right when Nathaniel wasn't ready for them — not "how was practice" hard, but "why did Coach's dad die if he prayed about it" hard, dropped between mile markers like it was nothing.

That Tuesday, Nathaniel gave him the answer he always gave: "That's a good one — let's really talk about it Sunday, at family time." He meant it every time he said it. Sunday came, and family time got eaten by errands and a nap and the game on in the background, the way it usually did, and by the next Tuesday Nico wasn't asking anymore.

Nathaniel had built his whole approach to raising his son in the faith around one scheduled hour a week — a workbook, sometimes a decade of the rosary if the timing worked — and treated the other hundred-plus waking hours as just logistics: driving, dinner, homework, with no real bearing on what Nico actually believed about God. The teaching happened at the appointed time. Everything else was just getting through the day.

Moses gave Israel a different picture of how this actually works. Instructing the people on passing down the law to their children, he said: "And you shall teach them to your children, talking of them when you are sitting in your house, and when you are walking by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise" (Deuteronomy 11:19, RSV-CE). Not "when you convene." Sitting in the house. Walking on the road. Lying down. Getting up. The command covers the whole ordinary shape of a day on purpose, because that's where a kid actually lives, and that's where he's actually watching his father.

The car on Tuesday was "walking by the way" as much as anywhere Nathaniel would ever stand and teach on purpose. Nico's question was the real work, handed to him for free, and Nathaniel had waved it off toward a Sunday that never quite arrived. A father doesn't get to choose when the teaching moments show up. He only gets to choose whether he's paying attention when they do.

**Today:** The next time one of your kids asks you something real — about God, about death, about why something happened — don't defer it to a better moment. Pull over, put the phone down, whatever it takes, and answer him right there, even imperfectly.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've been treating the ordinary minutes with my son like they don't count, saving the real conversations for a time that keeps slipping away from us. Help me see the car ride and the dinner table as the sacred ground they actually are. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
