# Day 29 — Abba, Father

Kieran heard himself say it before he registered he'd said it: "Can't you do anything right?" Fintan had knocked over a full glass of milk reaching across the table, and Kieran's voice came out sharp and cold in a way that had nothing to do with a glass of milk. He knew the sentence the second it left his mouth, because it wasn't his. It was his own father's — Roderick's — a line Kieran had heard aimed at himself a hundred times growing up, over things far smaller than a spilled glass, delivered in exactly that tone, like disappointment was the only currency Roderick knew how to spend on his son.

Kieran went out to the garage afterward and sat in his car in the dark for a while, not going anywhere. He'd spent years telling himself he would never become Roderick — never make his kids flinch the way he used to flinch, never let a small mistake become evidence of who a person fundamentally was. And here it was anyway, decades later, coming out of his own mouth over spilled milk. The belief sitting underneath that shame was one he'd never said out loud: that whatever Roderick put into him was permanent, load-bearing, impossible to build over no matter how hard he tried to father differently.

St. Paul names a different inheritance than the one Kieran assumes he's stuck with. In Romans 8:15, he tells the Romans that they didn't receive a spirit that makes them slaves again to fear, but a spirit of adoption as sons, the very spirit that cries out to God as "Abba, Father." Paul isn't describing willpower — a son gritting his teeth to avoid repeating his father's failures. He's describing a different spirit altogether, received rather than earned, that lets a man call God "Abba" the way a small child calls out to a father he trusts completely, with none of the fear Roderick's voice used to put in Kieran's chest.

That's the actual fix for what happened at the dinner table, and it isn't behavioral. Kieran doesn't out-discipline the old pattern by trying harder to remember not to snap. He fathers Fintan out of a different sonship than the one Roderick gave him — out of knowing himself, first, as a son God delights in rather than a disappointment God tolerates. A man who has genuinely received that can afford to be patient with a spilled glass, because his own worth was never riding on being flawless in front of his own father either.

**Today:** Go apologize to Fintan directly — a short, honest sentence, not a speech. Then, separately, spend five quiet minutes praying the Our Father slowly, dwelling on the first word longer than you normally would. Let "Father" mean something safe before you ask for anything else.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thank You for making me Your son, not out of fear but out of love I didn't earn. Break whatever in me still fathers Fintan out of Roderick's fear instead of Your love. Let me know myself first as Yours, so I can love my own son the way You actually love me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ROMANS 8:15
