# Day 30 — As a Father Pities His Children, Again

Maxwell stood in the driveway on the last night of November, watching the porch light catch the frost starting to form on the windshield, waiting for Boone and Eloise to finish arguing about whose turn it was to pick the movie before he went back inside. Thirty days ago he'd started this month assuming fatherhood was mostly a matter of getting the logistics right once the real pressures of work eased up. He ended it understanding something closer to the truth. Boone and Eloise had been forming their sense of what a father is, and by extension what God the Father is, out of ordinary Tuesdays all along, whether Maxwell meant to be teaching them anything or not.

He thought back over the shape of the month. The first week had been about identity before correction, learning to speak blessing over his kids instead of only weighing in when something went wrong, and catching, with real discomfort, how rarely he'd let either of them see him delighted rather than just present. The second week had turned to discipline and teaching, and cost him the recognition that half his frustration with Boone came from his own anger rather than Boone's actual good, and that a promise made distractedly, "in a minute," "after this call," breaks something in a kid whether or not the father remembers making it. The third week had gone further inward: letting Eloise see him apologize instead of managing the moment to avoid admitting fault, actually listening to what she was saying instead of just managing her behavior, praying for each of them by name instead of lumping them together as "the kids" in a hurry to get to amen. The fourth week had asked the hardest question of all, whether he was staying engaged through the parts of fatherhood that don't look like accomplishment, releasing what needed releasing, measuring himself by his own faithfulness rather than either child's achievements, and noticing, finally, that the ordinary exhausted nights were the gift, not the obstacle to it.

None of it resolved fatherhood into something finished. It showed Maxwell, instead, what he'd been building with Boone and Eloise the entire time, one unremarkable evening at a time, whether he'd been paying attention or not.

This month opened on a single claim about who God is, and Maxwell finds himself back at it tonight. Psalm 103:13 says the LORD's compassion for those who fear Him works the same way a father's compassion works toward his own children. He can't control how the Father speaks to Boone and Eloise's hearts as they grow, what they eventually believe about being seen, forgiven, and delighted in by someone bigger than themselves. He can only be the first, closest picture of that they'll ever have, painted in exactly the small moments November spent making him notice: a blessing spoken instead of withheld, an apology offered instead of avoided, a name prayed instead of skipped over. Whatever pity, patience, and delight this verse says God holds for His children, Maxwell has spent this month becoming a smaller, nearer version of it for his own.

November taught Maxwell how much of fatherhood is presence he can actually give, today, without waiting for an easier season. December is going to ask something related but different of him, not what he does, but what he trusts, when the whole point of a day is simply staying present to the people he loves and to the God who's present to him, without needing to fix, produce, or resolve anything at all. Maxwell called the kids in from the cold, still bickering about the movie, and followed them inside.

**Today:** Write one sentence naming what you hope your kids will believe about God the Father, someday, because of how you fathered them. Read it out loud before you go to bed tonight.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for a month that showed me how much my children are learning about You just by watching me. Let whatever I got wrong this November be covered by Your mercy, and let whatever I got right point them past me, to You. Prepare my heart for whatever comes next, the same way You've stayed present with me through this one. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
