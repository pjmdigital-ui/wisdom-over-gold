# Day 6 — The Anxious Search

Kane lost track of his seven-year-old son Rory for eleven minutes at the county fair, and he could later account for every one of them. One second Rory was next to him at the funnel cake stand, arguing about powdered sugar. The next, Kane turned to pay, and the spot where his son had been standing was just a gap in a moving crowd. He said Rory's name at a normal volume first, the way you do when you're sure it's nothing. Then louder. Then he was moving through strangers with his heart doing something he didn't have a word for yet. He scanned shirt colors, checked under picnic tables, and asked a stranger with kids of her own if she'd seen a boy in a green shirt.

He found him by the animal pens, perfectly fine, staring at a goat. Kane grabbed him hard enough that Rory looked startled, and the first thing out of Kane's mouth was anger, sharp and fast: "Don't you ever wander off like that again." His voice came out higher than he meant it, before any relief had a chance to show up first. It took him the whole walk back to the car to understand that the anger had been relief wearing a disguise. It arrived too fast for him to sort out which feeling actually belonged to him.

He'd always assumed a good father stayed mostly calm, steady under pressure, unshaken. Eleven minutes at a fairground taught him something different. The fear itself, the kind that scrambles a grown man's voice and sends him checking under picnic tables, is what fatherhood costs. It gets paid the moment a father realizes how little control he actually has over where his child is.

Scripture gives St. Joseph that exact fear, at a scale Kane's eleven minutes didn't come close to. Joseph and Mary searched Jerusalem for three days before finding the twelve-year-old Jesus in the temple. When they found Him, Mary's words carry the strain of every parent who has ever lost sight of a child. In Luke 2:48, she asks Jesus why He treated them this way, and tells Him plainly that His father and she had been searching for Him, worried sick. Worried sick. The word isn't softened or explained away. Joseph searched three days for a boy who turned out to be exactly where He belonged, safe the whole time. None of that made the search feel less real while he was living inside it.

**On Joseph:** this is Joseph's fourth appearance in these pages, and each one has shown a different side of him. The man who married Mary at real cost to his reputation. The worker who shaped Christ through ordinary labor. The father who fled by night on one warning. This scene shows something rawer than any of those: three days of a father's real terror, with no angel and no dream to shorten the wait. Joseph wasn't spared the fear. He carried it, searched anyway, and kept moving toward his son until he found him.

**Today:** Think of the last time real fear for your child caught you off guard. Name it to yourself honestly as love, not as a loss of control, and tell your child today, plainly, without the edge, how much finding them matters to you.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for letting my fear for my children come out as anger before I let it be what it actually was. Thank You for Joseph's example of searching, worried sick, without giving up until he found his son. Help me carry that same love for my own children today, honestly, without disguising it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
