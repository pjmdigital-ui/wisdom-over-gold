# Day 12 — A Father's Parting Words

Damien stood at the departure gate with Rocco, both of them out of things to say five minutes before boarding started. Eighteen years old, enlisted, wide-eyed about it in a way he wouldn't have admitted a year earlier. Colleen had already said her piece in the car, crying a little, hugging him twice. Damien had planned to say something too. He'd been composing it in his head for a week. But standing there at the gate, everything he'd rehearsed sounded either too small or too much, so he said "call your mother on Sundays" and left it there.

He told himself it was fine. Rocco already knew what Damien thought of him. Eighteen years in the same house had said it, surely, better than anything Damien could manage standing at a gate with a boarding group being called over the speaker. The important things didn't need to be spoken out loud at the last minute. They'd already been said, a thousand times over, without anyone having to say them directly.

Tobit didn't operate on that assumption. Believing himself near death and about to send his son out on a long trip to collect a debt, he called Tobias to him and gave him a deliberate, spoken blessing and instruction, rather than trusting that a lifetime together had already said it all. In Tobit 4:5-6, he tells his son to keep God in mind every day of his life, to turn away from sin and wrongdoing, and to walk uprightly, telling him that a life lived honestly will go well for him. He didn't stop at general affection. He named the specific things he wanted his son to carry, reverence for God, honesty, uprightness, out loud, on purpose, before Tobias walked out the door.

Damien's whole life with Rocco had said plenty, and none of it replaced this moment. A father's blessing spoken at the threshold does something the accumulated years can't do on their own. It hands a son something specific to carry, in his father's actual words, at the exact moment he's stepping into a world that won't be nearly as gentle with him.

Damien caught up to Rocco at the security line. "One more thing," he said, and Rocco turned around. "You know how to work hard, and you know right from wrong. I've watched you your whole life. Hold onto both, wherever they send you. I'm proud of you." Rocco nodded, said "I will," and Damien let him go through the line without needing it to be more than that.

**Today:** Before your son or daughter's next major step out the door, a first drive alone, a first day at a new school, a move, a deployment, write down the one or two specific things you most want them to carry, in your own words, and say them out loud before they go.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've assumed my kids already know what I think of them without my ever saying it plainly. Give me the words and the nerve to bless them out loud before they need to walk out the door without me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

TOBIT 4:5-6
