# Day 15 — Slow to Anger

Enzo had been on a call with a supplier for twenty minutes, voice tight, numbers not adding up the way they needed to for Thursday's shipment. Knox, four, picked that exact moment to knock his cup off the table, grape juice spreading fast across the hardwood, and Enzo's voice came out before he'd decided to raise it: "What is wrong with you? Watch what you're doing!" Loud enough that Knox's face crumpled before the juice had even finished spreading.

Enzo knew, even as he said it, that the juice had nothing to do with the volume of his own voice. The supplier call, the shipment, three other things stacked up behind it — Knox had simply been standing there when the pressure finally found an exit. Renata came around the corner fast, saw Knox crying and the juice on the floor, and gave Enzo a look he understood without her needing to say anything.

He told himself, walking off to get a towel, that a sharp reaction now saved him a bigger blowup later — better to let it out in the moment than let it build up all week and come out worse somewhere else. It was a story he'd told himself before, always after the fact, always as an explanation rather than a plan.

Proverbs draws a plain line between two kinds of men here: in Proverbs 29:11, the fool lets every bit of his anger loose, while the wise man holds himself back and keeps it in check. The fool isn't the man who feels anger — Scripture doesn't ask a father to stop feeling things. The fool is the man who lets it all loose, straight from his gut to his voice with nothing in between to slow it down. The wise man feels the same anger and holds it back anyway, which takes more strength than letting it go, not less.

Enzo came back with the towel, got down on the floor to clean up the juice, and found Knox still sniffling by the table leg. "I yelled bigger than the mess," Enzo said, "and that's on me, not you. I'm sorry." Knox nodded, more interested in whether he'd get another cup than in the apology itself, which was fine. The words mattered for Enzo's own account of who he wanted to be as a father, whether or not a four-year-old registered them yet.

**Today:** The next time your child does something small and clumsy that catches you already on edge, pause three full seconds before you respond. Use them to ask yourself: "Is this reaction sized to what actually happened, or to everything else stacked up behind it?"

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I let today's pressure land on my son over something as small as spilled juice, and his face told me exactly how big it landed. Teach me to hold back what's mine to carry instead of handing it to my kids in a raised voice. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
