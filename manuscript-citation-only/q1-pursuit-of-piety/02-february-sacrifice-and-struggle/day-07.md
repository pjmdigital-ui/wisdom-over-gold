# Day 7 — Why Avoidance Never Actually Works

Sunday evening, Nate cleans out his truck before the week starts: fast-food bags, a stray glove, a stack of Post-it notes that have been sliding around the console for days. He almost throws them out without looking. Instead he lines them up on the dash: *Call the fence guy back. Tell Jake about the vendor thing. Ask Dad about the doctor's appointment he keeps rescheduling.* Five notes, five different corners of his life, and every single one is a task he wrote down meaning to do and then didn't.

He sits there a minute longer than he means to. Nothing blew up this week. Nothing fell apart. But looking at five separate notes side by side, he can finally see what each individual day made easy to miss: the same move, over and over, wearing a different costume each time. A hard call becomes "I'll get to it." A hard conversation becomes a vague text about coffee. A hard feeling becomes forty minutes of scrolling in the driveway. Different discomforts, identical response. He's been running the same play all week and calling each one a separate, understandable exception.

What strikes him hardest is that none of it actually worked. The fence is still a problem. Jake still doesn't know what Nate's been meaning to tell him. His dad's appointment is still unscheduled. Every dodge bought him a few hours of not feeling something, and none of it made the underlying thing smaller. If anything, the notes on the dash prove it's piling up: a week's worth of postponed costs, still fully owed.

St. Paul names exactly what a man forfeits when he skips the hard thing instead of walking through it. He tells the Romans that when suffering is met well, it builds a man's endurance, that endurance goes on to shape his character, and that shaped character is what finally gives him real hope (Romans 5:3-4). That chain starts with the suffering being met head-on, not with it disappearing. Something gets built in the process that can't be built any other way: endurance from the meeting, character from the enduring, hope from the character. Every one of Nate's five dodges this week was a missed link in that chain. Nothing got forged, because nothing was faced.

Avoidance was never actually free. It only looked free, because the bill doesn't come due in the moment you dodge it. It comes due later, bigger, usually at a worse time than the one you avoided. A man who spends a week routing around every hard thing ends it exactly where he started, not ahead, minus five days he could have spent building something.

**Today:** Look back over the last week and find your own version of Nate's Post-it notes, the calls, conversations, and tasks you've kept moving to tomorrow all week. Pick the one costing you the most and say it plainly: *Avoiding this hasn't made it smaller. Facing it is the only way it ever gets built into something.* Do it before you go to bed tonight.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, this week showed me how much of my life I've been routing around instead of walking through, and how little that dodging has gained me. Give me the honesty to see the pattern in myself and the courage to meet what's hard head-on, trusting that You build endurance and character in me through it, not around it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ROMANS 5:3-4
