# Day 28 — What This Month Was For

Twenty-eight days ago, you hit a specific moment of friction — a decision, a conversation, a plan — where the discomfort was real and your first move was to route around it. Maybe you took the easy call instead of the hard one, said yes to comfort and told yourself it was wisdom, found the smallest workaround available and took it before you'd even finished naming what you were avoiding. That instinct is old and strong, and Day 1 named the belief underneath it plainly: suffering means something's gone wrong, so my job is to get out of its way as fast as I can.

A month later, that instinct hasn't left you. You still felt the pull to avoid this week, probably more than once. What's changed is what you've learned to do with the pull — twenty-eight days of scenarios and Scripture and one uncomfortable choice after another, training you to recognize it and stay anyway.

St. James opened this month with a command that sounded almost absurd on Day 1: he told his readers to treat it as pure joy whenever they run into every kind of trial (James 1:2) — not endure trials, not survive them, count them joy. A month ago that line probably read like a nice idea you weren't sure you believed. You've since faced a fired employee, a hard conversation, maybe a body that hurt on purpose, maybe a bitterness you had to name out loud to get rid of. Joy in the middle of that is what showed up, sometimes, once you stopped treating the discomfort as the enemy and started asking what it was for — not a feeling you manufactured on command.

St. Paul, writing to the Romans, closes the loop James opened — a different letter, the same mechanism this whole month has been pointing at: he tells them that suffering, met well, builds endurance, endurance shapes character, and character produces hope, and that this hope doesn't leave a man empty-handed, because God has already poured His own love into the hearts of those who believe, through the Holy Spirit given to them (Romans 5:3-5). That's a chain — suffering to endurance, endurance to character, character to hope — and nearly every day this month handed you a link of it: a sacrifice at work, a costly word to your wife, a boundary you finally set with a brother who needed to carry his own load, a Thursday you kept showing up for with nothing to show for it yet.

Nobody completes that chain in twenty-eight days, but you started forging it in places you used to just avoid, and you now have a working answer, tested in your own life, to the question Day 1 left open: suffering is frequently proof that something real is finally being asked of you, not a sign something's gone wrong, and love gets proven by exactly what it costs and nothing less.

Piety was never going to be a subject you finish — February didn't complete what January started so much as give the same surrender a harder floor to stand on, because a man who has learned to offer God his discomfort has learned something a man who has only offered God his good moods never does.

**Today:** Go back to whatever you wrote or said on Day 1 — the thing you were avoiding. Write one new sentence beneath it: *I know now what that discomfort was for, and I'm not routing around the next one.* Then name, out loud, one hard thing still ahead of you this week, and choose it before it chooses you.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, twenty-eight days ago I was still running from anything that cost me something, and some of that instinct is still in me. Thank You for what this month's discomfort actually produced in me, and give me the endurance to keep offering You the hard things, not just the easy ones. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
