# Day 10 — The Cross Wasn't a Mistake

The funeral lunch was in the church hall, folding tables, coffee in styrofoam cups. Ray's father-in-law had died on a Tuesday after two hard years, and everyone was doing the thing families do at these lunches — repeating the good stories a little too loudly, avoiding the ones that would make someone cry. His fifteen-year-old, Caleb, found him refilling his coffee and asked the question in the flat, unguarded way teenagers ask the biggest ones, like it was nothing: "If God's really in charge, why'd He let Grandpa suffer for two years before this?"

Ray didn't have a fast answer. He gave the one most men give — something about a better place — and watched his son's face say it hadn't landed. Caleb wasn't looking for comfort. He was testing whether his father actually believed God was in control of something that looked, from where a fifteen-year-old stood, like it had gone badly wrong.

Ray drove home turning over what he should have said. Underneath his son's question was an assumption he realized he shared more than he wanted to admit: that real suffering — the kind that drags on, that doesn't heal, that ends in a folding-table funeral lunch — is evidence God either isn't in control or isn't paying attention. Good outcomes prove God. Bad ones raise doubt. That was math he'd been running for years without ever checking it.

Scripture doesn't run that math. Hebrews describes Jesus this way: "looking to Jesus the pioneer and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is seated at the right hand of the throne of God" (Hebrews 12:2, RSV-CE). God planned the cross before He planned to reverse it — the suffering came first, by design, and the resurrection fulfilled that design rather than repairing a mistake in it. The worst thing that ever happened to the best man who ever lived was the very thing God used to save everyone. If suffering that severe fit inside God's control instead of outside it, a father-in-law's two hard years fits there too, even when nobody at the lunch can explain how.

That's the answer Ray wished he'd given his son — not a reason his grandfather suffered, but the assurance that suffering entering a story isn't proof God stepped away from it. Jesus endured the cross because of the joy set before Him, not in spite of a plan gone sideways. A man who believes that can sit with hard things without needing them to resolve into something tidy before he'll trust the One holding them.

**Today:** Find a quiet ten minutes and say out loud to your kids, or just to yourself if there's no one to say it to yet: *Suffering doesn't mean God has lost control. The cross proves He can be fully in charge of the worst thing that happens.* Write down one hard thing in your life right now this truth needs to reach.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've measured whether You're in control by whether things go well, and I know that's backwards. Help me trust that You were as much in control at the cross as You are right now, in whatever I'm facing. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
