# Day 13 — Job Didn't Get an Answer

Pete had been praying for his son Danny by name every morning for four years — since the blowup after Danny dropped out of college, moved across the country, and stopped returning calls except at Christmas. Four years of roughly the same prayer: bring him home, soften his heart, show me what I did wrong so I can fix it. Nothing had changed. Danny still called twice a year. Pete still didn't know what, if anything, he'd done wrong.

He kept a small notebook where he wrote a line most mornings after praying. Looking back through it one Sunday, he noticed the same phrase recurring in different forms across four years: *Why won't You just tell me what's going on.* He wanted a mechanism — the specific mistake, the specific fix, a timeline. He'd been treating prayer like a request that should eventually get answered and closed out.

That's the belief worth naming plainly: *if God loved me, He'd at least explain what's happening.* Pete had made peace with not controlling the outcome; what he hadn't made peace with was never being told why. Four years without an explanation had started to feel less like waiting and more like being ignored.

Job asked for the same thing across forty chapters, in more dramatic language than Pete ever used, and got something other than an explanation. God never tells him why his children died, why he lost everything, why the suffering fell on him specifically. Instead God speaks out of the whirlwind about the created order, and Job's response at the end isn't "now I understand." He tells God that he had only heard about Him secondhand before, but now he has seen Him with his own eyes (Job 42:5). Job gets an encounter instead of an explanation. Something shifts anyway — not because the facts changed, but because hearing about God stopped being enough once he'd actually met Him in the middle of the suffering.

That's a strange comfort for a man who wants a mechanism, but it's the real one on offer. Pete's four years of unanswered mornings aren't proof God has gone silent. They might be exactly the space where hearing about God turns into meeting Him — not instead of an answer about Danny, but as something that matters more than that answer would have.

**Today:** Write one sentence in your own prayer journal — start one today if you don't have one: *I'm not waiting for an explanation anymore. I want to meet You in this, not just hear about You.* Pray it before you pray anything else tomorrow morning.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've wanted an explanation more than I've wanted You, and I'm asking You to change that in me. Let this waiting turn into knowing You, even if the situation itself never resolves the way I want. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
