# Day 16 — Sacrifice in Marriage

Two things happened at the same time: his phone buzzed with a group chat about Sunday's game, and his wife walked in holding a paint chart, asking — for the third time that week — whether he had twenty minutes to finally decide on a color for the hallway. It was a small decision, the kind that shouldn't have needed asking three times, except every time she'd raised it he'd been mid-something else and told her later. He said "one sec" to her and thumbed open the chat.

She didn't say anything. She just set the chart on the counter and walked back out, and the way she didn't push it told him more than an argument would have. He sat there with the phone in his hand, still warm from scrolling, and felt the specific shame of a man who'd chosen the group chat over his own house.

What was he actually operating on in that second? Not that the game mattered more than she did — he'd have denied that instantly if you'd asked him. It was smaller and worse than that: *my time is mine first, and what's left over is what she gets.* He'd never said it out loud. He didn't have to. The order of his attention said it for him.

St. Paul doesn't leave husbands much room to hedge on this: "Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her" (Ephesians 5:25, RSV-CE). Gave himself up — not gave his leftover time, his spare attention, whatever survived after his own interests were satisfied first. The standard is surrender, not generosity. Christ didn't love the Church out of what was convenient for Him.

A husband who's honest will admit most days he loves his wife out of the surplus — the energy, minutes, and attention that are left once he's taken his share. Real sacrifice in marriage looks like putting the phone down mid-sentence and picking the paint chart up instead, rarely anything dramatic, in a moment nobody's grading you on and nothing forces you to. Nobody writes a story about the husband who chose the paint chart. That's exactly why it counts — there's no audience to perform for, only a wife who's learning, one small moment at a time, whether she comes first or second.

**Today:** The next time something small pulls at you while your wife is asking for your attention, put it down first — not after you finish the sentence, not after the chat goes quiet. Say to yourself: "My time isn't mine first anymore." Then go finish whatever she asked you to help with, fully, without checking the phone again.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for the times I've given my wife what was left of me instead of giving myself up for her the way Christ did for His Church. Teach me to notice the small moments where I choose myself first, and give me the will to choose her instead. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
