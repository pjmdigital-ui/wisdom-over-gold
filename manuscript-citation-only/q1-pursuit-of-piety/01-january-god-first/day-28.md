# Day 28 — The Cost of Putting God First

The contract was worth more than your business had made in the past two years combined. All you had to do was sign off on marketing claims you knew weren't fully true. Numbers rounded up. A testimonial you were pretty sure was made up. A guarantee your product couldn't actually back up. Your business partner already had the pen out. "Everyone does this," he said, and he had a point. He wasn't lying to you either. He truly didn't see a problem with it.

You sat with it for two days. At some point a thought showed up that made it feel safe to say yes: *This one's small enough not to count. It's just rounding, not lying.* You ran the numbers again, like a different number might change your answer. You thought about what the deal would mean, the debt paid off, the pressure finally gone. Then you thought about your kids finding out someday how the money came in. You thought about having to look them in the eye afterward. You turned it down. Your partner was furious. The relief you were hoping for didn't show up right away. Mostly what you felt for a while was just how big the thing was that you'd said no to.

Jesus doesn't go easy on this trade-off anywhere in the Gospels. In Matthew 16:26 He asks a hard question. What good is it for a man to gain the whole world but lose his own soul? What could he possibly trade to get his soul back? No amount of money buys back a compromised conscience once you've spent it. The real struggle here was about the size of the lie you'd have to tell yourself to take it, more than the size of the deal itself. *Small enough not to count* is exactly the kind of thinking Jesus rules out a few chapters earlier. In Matthew 5:37, He tells His disciples what an honest man's word should sound like. Let your yes simply mean yes and your no simply mean no. Anything added on top of that comes from evil. No rounding. No "technically." A yes that needs footnotes and a no that needs excuses both come from the same place. Both come from believing your word can bend depending on what it costs you that day.

Putting God first is easy when it costs nothing, praying in the morning, being nicer to your wife. It gets expensive fast when it means turning down the deal, losing the client, or disappointing a partner who thinks you're being naive. Decide today, before the pressure hits. Write it down where future-you can find it. Not so future-you remembers the decision, but so future-you can't talk it down into something smaller and still call it honest.

**On St. Thomas More:** More was Chancellor of England, one of the most powerful men in the country. He lost everything, his position, his freedom, and eventually his life, rather than put his name on something he knew was a lie. He said no even when the king himself demanded it. On the scaffold, he called himself "the King's good servant, but God's first." His yes stayed yes, even at the cost of his life.

**Today:** Think of one place right now where doing the right thing would cost you something real. Write your decision down as a plain yes or no, no hedging. Say it out loud: *My word doesn't need footnotes.* Keep it somewhere you'll see it when the pressure comes.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, doing the right thing here is going to cost me something real. Part of me wants to find a way around that. Give me the courage to let my yes be yes, even when it's expensive. Remind me a clean conscience is worth more than any deal. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
