# Day 14 — The Man You're Becoming

You were sitting on the back steps watching your two boys play some game they'd made up in the yard. Half baseball, half wrestling, with rules that changed every thirty seconds and only made sense to them. Your older son, seven, was narrating the whole thing in a voice you recognized right away, because it was yours. The same short, impatient way you talk when you're frustrated. The same phrase, "come on, let's go, let's go," that you use when you're rushing everyone out the door in the morning. It sounded different now, coming out of a seven-year-old's mouth, with nothing forgivable about it.

You watched a little longer, and a question sat down next to you that you hadn't invited. When did you decide that the unguarded version of you didn't count? You had been assuming your kids were mostly shaped by what you told them. They weren't. They were being shaped by what you actually did with your Sundays, your evenings, your attention. The parts of you that ran on autopilot, where you weren't performing for anyone, where you'd quietly decided nobody was watching closely enough for it to matter.

Near the end of his life, Joshua gathered the people of Israel and gave them a choice. Serve the gods of the nations around them, the old gods of their ancestors, or serve the Lord. He didn't just ask them to decide. He told them where he stood. He and his household, he said, would serve the Lord, no matter what the rest of Israel chose. It's a family decision, made once, out loud, in front of everyone. Joshua understood something you'd been avoiding on the back steps. A man's choice about who sits at the center of his life doesn't stay private. It becomes the water his family swims in, whether he announces it or not. And it becomes that water especially in the moments he never planned on anyone watching.

Your kids are watching the Tuesday-night version of you. The one scrolling on the couch. The one who thinks the unguarded moments don't count. They count. You don't get to skip modeling something for your kids. The only choice left to you is what you model.

**On St. Thomas Aquinas:** Born into Italian nobility, Thomas was expected by his family to become a powerful abbot, a path that would have brought wealth and status to their name. Instead he insisted on joining the mendicant Dominicans, a choice so unwelcome that his own brothers kidnapped and locked him up for over a year trying to change his mind. He held firm. He set aside his family's ambitions and his own great intelligence for what he understood God was calling him toward. He became one of the greatest minds the Church has ever known.

**Today:** Watch your kids for ten unguarded minutes — playing, talking to each other, not performing for you. Notice one thing they're copying from you, good or bad. Then say out loud: "What I do when no one's watching still counts, because they're always watching." Name honestly what you'll do differently tonight because of it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, my kids are watching what I do, not what I say, and I don't like everything I just saw reflected back at me. Help me believe that the unguarded moments still count, and help me become in them the kind of man I actually want my sons to become. Let my Tuesday nights match my Sunday words. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

JOSHUA 24:15
