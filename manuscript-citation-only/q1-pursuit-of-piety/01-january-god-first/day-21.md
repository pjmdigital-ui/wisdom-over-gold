# Day 21 — God First as a Habit, Not a Mood

Wednesday morning you woke up flat. Not angry, not in crisis, just spiritually numb — the kind of morning where prayer feels like reading a script instead of talking to someone. You almost skipped it. Told yourself it would be hollow anyway, going through the motions, and God probably doesn't want fake words from a distracted man more than He wants nothing at all. That logic felt reasonable at 6 a.m. It's also, if you're honest, the exact logic that's talked you out of a hundred good habits over the years — the gym, the budget, the hard conversation you keep postponing. Wait until you feel ready, and you'll wait forever.

Underneath the logic is a belief you've never said out loud: if I don't feel it, the prayer doesn't count. You prayed anyway. Five flat, unremarkable minutes, no lightning bolt of feeling, nothing you'd call a breakthrough. Just showing up because it was the time you'd committed to showing up, whether or not your heart was in a cooperative mood that day.

St. Paul's instruction in 1 Thessalonians 5:17 comes down to two words, however you translate it: never stop praying. Not pray when moved. Not pray when the Spirit feels near. Pray constantly — a standard that has nothing to do with emotional weather and everything to do with consistency. Feelings are real and worth paying attention to, but they were never designed to be the engine of a spiritual life. They're too unreliable for that job. A habit built on feeling good will die the first week feeling good goes missing, and every man's feelings go missing eventually, usually on a random Wednesday for no reason he can name.

Consider the couple whose marriage still stands after thirty years. They didn't feel "in love" every single day of it — nobody does. They kept showing up: the dinners, the check-ins, the small kindnesses, on the good days and the flat ones alike, and the feeling followed the faithfulness more often than the other way around. Your relationship with God works the same way. Show up on the flat mornings, not because you've manufactured a feeling, but because faithfulness, not mood, is what actually builds a life.

**On St. Ignatius of Loyola:** Ignatius gave the Church a simple daily practice called the Examen — a few quiet minutes at day's end reviewing where God showed up, where you missed Him, and where you need His grace tomorrow. You can run it on your worst day exactly as well as your best, which is precisely why it's lasted five hundred years.

**Today:** Tonight before bed, say out loud: *Showing up counts even when I don't feel it.* Then spend five minutes on a simple Examen — walk back through your day and name one moment you sensed God's presence, one moment you turned away from Him, and one thing you'll ask His help with tomorrow. Do it whether or not you feel like it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I feel flat today and part of me wants to skip this because it seems like empty words. Help me show up anyway, trusting that showing up matters more than how I feel about it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 THESSALONIANS 5:17
