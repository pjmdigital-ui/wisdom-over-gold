# Day 21 — God First as a Habit, Not a Mood

Wednesday morning you woke up flat. Not angry, not in crisis, just spiritually numb. It was the kind of morning where prayer feels like reading a script instead of talking to someone. You almost skipped it. You told yourself it would be hollow anyway, just going through the motions, and that God probably doesn't want fake words from a distracted man any more than He wants nothing at all. That reasoning felt fair at 6 a.m. It's also, if you're honest, the exact reasoning that's talked you out of a hundred good habits over the years. The gym. The budget. The hard conversation you keep putting off. Wait until you feel ready, and you'll wait forever.

Underneath that reasoning is a belief you've never said out loud: if I don't feel it, the prayer doesn't count. You prayed anyway. Five flat, unremarkable minutes. No lightning bolt of feeling, nothing you'd call a breakthrough. You just showed up, because it was the time you'd promised to show up, whether or not your heart felt like cooperating that day.

St. Paul's instruction in 1 Thessalonians 5:17 comes down to two words, however you translate it: never stop praying. Not pray when you feel moved, or pray when God feels close. Pray constantly, no matter your mood. Feelings are real and worth paying attention to, but they were never meant to run a man's spiritual life. They're too unreliable for that job. A habit built on feeling good will die the first week the good feeling disappears. Every man's feelings disappear eventually, usually on a random Wednesday for no reason he can name.

Think about a couple whose marriage has lasted thirty years. They didn't feel "in love" every single day of it. Nobody does. They kept showing up: the dinners, the check-ins, the small kindnesses, on good days and flat ones alike. The feeling followed the faithfulness more often than the other way around. Your relationship with God works the same way. Show up on the flat mornings, not because you've manufactured a feeling, but because faithfulness, not mood, is what actually builds a life.

**On St. Ignatius of Loyola:** Ignatius gave the Church a simple daily practice called the Examen. It's a few quiet minutes at the end of the day, looking back at where God showed up, where you missed Him, and where you need His help tomorrow. You can do it on your worst day just as well as your best. That's exactly why it's lasted five hundred years.

**Today:** Tonight before bed, say out loud: *Showing up counts even when I don't feel it.* Then spend five minutes on a simple Examen — walk back through your day and name one moment you sensed God's presence, one moment you turned away from Him, and one thing you'll ask His help with tomorrow. Do it whether or not you feel like it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I feel flat today, and part of me wants to skip this because it feels like empty words. Help me show up anyway, trusting that showing up matters more than how I feel about it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 THESSALONIANS 5:17
