# Day 31 — The Rest of the Year

Thirty days ago, you sat in a truck, or a kitchen, or wherever your own version of that stillness happened. You felt the flat quiet of a goal that didn't deliver what it promised. Maybe it was a deal that closed and left you feeling empty. Maybe it was an ordinary Tuesday that finally cracked open a question you'd been avoiding for years: what are you actually building your life around? A month has passed since then. It wasn't a perfect month, if you're honest. You snapped at your wife more than once. You missed prayer some mornings. You caught yourself back on the throne of your own life more times than you'd like to count.

Somewhere in the last few days, that same flat quiet from Day 1 came back, wearing a different thought: *Thirty-one days should have fixed this by now.* You did the readings. You wrote the sentences. You prayed the prayers. And here you are on the last day, still capable of snapping at your wife over a dish in the sink. Part of you wanted this month to work like a light switch. Flip it once, stay lit forever. It didn't work that way. The voice telling you that means it failed is selling you the same finish-line lie the deal sold you back on Day 1.

St. Paul names what this month actually was. This whole book stands on Romans 12:2, where he calls for a mind that is renewed and transformed, not just patched up. This is renewal, not repair. A repair happens once and holds. A renewal is something you keep doing, the way you renew a lease, or a vow, or a commitment. You keep doing it because the thing it renews doesn't stay fixed on its own. Thirty-one days did exactly what renewal does. It happened, and it will need to happen again tomorrow. That was always the plan.

Matthew 6:33 opened this month. It's worth hearing again now that it's had thirty-one days to work on you. Seek God's kingdom and His righteousness first, and everything else you need will fall into place after that. You've tested that verse for a whole month, in your marriage, at work, with your money, and in the quiet moments you forced yourself into more than once. You've seen where putting Him first cost you something. You're still here, so you know it was worth the cost. You've also seen how fast the old order comes back the moment you stop paying attention. That's exactly why this has to be a practice you keep returning to, not a project you finish and file away.

Eleven months are still ahead. Everything still to come only means something if it's built on top of this one. A man chasing what's next for his own glory just builds a more impressive version of the same emptiness he started with. A man chasing it for God's glory builds on a foundation that doesn't shift, even when the win doesn't feel like enough.

**On St. Augustine:** He spent years chasing exactly what Day 1 described: status, pleasure, approval, sure that each one would finally be enough. Then he wrote the line that has followed him for sixteen centuries: "Our heart is restless until it rests in You." He didn't find rest once and stay there either. He spent the rest of his life as a bishop, still choosing every day to turn back toward the God he'd found. That's the same path this month has been tracing in you. A restless heart that keeps starting over, turning again and again toward the One who can actually hold it.

You are further from being the god of your own life than you were thirty-one days ago. Day 31 didn't finish what Day 1 started. You just learned the work never finishes. It renews, tomorrow morning, and the morning after that.

**Today:** Reread the one sentence you wrote on Day 1, what you were chasing. Underneath it, write one new sentence. Say it out loud first, then write it on the page: *This keeps renewing. Tomorrow, I'm choosing it again.* Keep both pages. Let February find you still choosing.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thirty-one days in, my heart is still restless. It still climbs back onto its own throne when I stop paying attention. Keep drawing me back to You, not just this month but every month after it. Help this restless heart finally learn to rest in You. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
