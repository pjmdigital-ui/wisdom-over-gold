# Day 24 — Fear Disguised as Planning

You've built a seventeen-tab spreadsheet that models your retirement under six different market scenarios. You check it more than you'd admit, some weeks every day. You've got a three-year runway calculated for the business, a college fund projection for each kid broken out by year, and a line item labeled "worst case" that you've lowered twice this year alone, just to feel like you're covering enough bad news. Your wife has started saying, half-joking, that you love that spreadsheet more than you love sleep. She's not entirely wrong.

You'd call this being careful, being responsible, taking care of your family the way a man should. A lot of it is exactly that. Nobody's saying you shouldn't plan, save, or think ahead. But at some point, planning stopped being good stewardship for you and became something else. It became a spreadsheet you keep opening because some part of you believes one more model, one more scenario, will finally make you feel safe. It hasn't yet. You keep opening the tab anyway.

You know the difference, if you're honest. Careful planning plans and then rests. Fear plans and then plans again, checking the same numbers for reassurance they were never able to give, because no spreadsheet has ever guaranteed a single future outcome. It just gives the feeling of a guarantee, and that's enough to keep a man opening the tab one more time. This tab, specifically, has become the thing you trust to keep your family safe, more than you'd ever say out loud.

Jesus speaks directly to this in Matthew 6:31-34. He tells His listeners not to worry, naming the exact worries a responsible man defaults to: what to eat, what to drink, what to wear. Their Father in heaven already knows they need all of it. Seek His kingdom and His goodness first, Jesus says, and the rest will be given to you. Jesus isn't telling you to stop planning. He's pointing you toward the real source of safety, a Father who feeds the birds outside your window and has never once opened a spreadsheet to do it.

The line that should stop you is the last one. Jesus tells them not to worry about tomorrow, because tomorrow will bring its own worries. Today already has enough trouble to fill it. You weren't built to carry tomorrow's trouble today, on top of today's. Over-planning tries to do exactly that. It pre-loads every future worry into the present moment so you can feel, falsely, like you've already handled it. You haven't handled it. You've just moved the worry earlier and made it live in your body longer than it needed to.

**Today:** Close the spreadsheet, the app, the account you keep checking, for the rest of today. Write this sentence somewhere you'll see it: *One more model won't make me safe. God already knows what I need.* Then hand your actual fear to God in prayer instead of to another projection.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I keep checking the numbers, looking for a guarantee they were never going to give me. Help me close the spreadsheet, name what I'm actually afraid of, and trust You with tomorrow instead of trying to control it today. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MATTHEW 6:31-34
