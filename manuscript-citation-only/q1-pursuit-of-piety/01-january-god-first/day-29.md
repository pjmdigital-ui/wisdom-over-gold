# Day 29 — Starting Over, Again

Twenty-eight days into trying to put God first, and you snapped at your wife over something that didn't deserve it. A dish left in the sink. A schedule mix-up. Something so small you can barely explain the tone you used. Afterward you sat in your truck in the garage, needing a minute before going back inside. The thought that really stung was this: *I should be past this by now.* Twenty-eight days of trying, and here you are again. Same old pattern. Same old you.

Say that thought out loud and you can hear what it's really claiming. It claims growth is a straight line. It claims twenty-eight real days should have made you permanently different, immune by now to the impatience that used to run you. When you're not, it tells you the whole month was fake. It tells you that you weren't actually changing. It tells you that you were just performing for a few weeks, and now the real you is leaking back through.

Scripture doesn't describe growth that way. It definitely doesn't describe mercy that way either. Jeremiah wrote Lamentations while watching his own city fall apart. Most of the book is one long, sad cry. And yet he still arrives here, in Lamentations 3:22-23. The Lord's love for us never runs out. His mercy toward us starts fresh every single morning. That faithfulness doesn't depend on how you did the day before. Jeremiah is telling us God's mercy resets every single morning, no matter what kind of man showed up yesterday. Today's setback doesn't decide anything by itself. Tomorrow morning does.

Nobody grows in a straight line. Not the men who seem to have it together. Not the saints, either, whose polished stories you read after their worst days were already edited out. Growth looks like falling back and getting up again, over and over. Getting up again is the growth itself. A man who falls back into an old pattern on day twenty-eight and gets back up on day twenty-nine is doing exactly what putting God first looks like over a whole lifetime. It means choosing that direction again and again, one reset morning at a time, instead of reaching some finish line and staying there.

You don't need a better twenty-eight days behind you. You need to be willing to start again this morning. That's exactly what's being offered to you.

**Today:** Go back to whoever you snapped at, wronged, or let down recently. Say it plainly: "I was wrong, and I'm sorry." No explanation, no excuses. If the old thought comes back tonight, *nothing's really changed*, open your list of truths and read it anyway. It won't argue with the thought. It will just remind you what's already true, no matter how today went.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I fell back into the same old pattern again, and I'm tired of thinking I should be past it by now. Thank You that Your mercy resets every morning, no matter what. Help me get back up today instead of staying down in the shame of it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

LAMENTATIONS 3:22-23
