# Day 15 — God First at Work

The contract was worth more than your firm had billed in the last two quarters combined. You'd been chasing it for months, and now it came down to one line in the proposal. A performance guarantee that, if you were honest, your team couldn't actually hit. Not by a lot. Close enough that you could talk yourself into it if you softened the wording just right. You could bury the catch somewhere the client's lawyer probably wouldn't notice until it was too late to matter.

Your business partner was already writing it that way when you looked over his shoulder. "We'll figure it out once we're in," he said. Underneath that sentence was the real belief, the one neither of you said out loud. This is small enough that it won't actually hurt anyone. You had the pen in your hand, literally about to initial the page, and you felt that familiar tug. You knew exactly what the right thing was, and you could also see, clear as day, what your bank account would look like if you didn't do it.

St. Paul writes to the Colossians, in 3:23-24. He first wrote this to servants working for masters who could take advantage of them. Whatever the task, do it with your whole heart, as if you're working for the Lord and not for people. It's the Lord who gives the real reward, and He's the one you're actually working for. That verse asks a sharper question than "did you work hard." It asks whose approval you're actually working for. The client's signature? Your partner's respect? This quarter's number? Or God's?

That question changes what the shortcut looks like. If the client is your ultimate audience, a small lie buried in the fine print really is small enough not to matter. Just a rounding error nobody will ever trace back to you. If God is your audience, there's no line small enough to hide in, because nothing is actually hidden from Him. You changed the guarantee to something true, a number your team could actually deliver. You lost some leverage in the negotiation because of it. The deal still closed, for less money. Your partner was annoyed for about a week. But you didn't spend the next year of the contract managing a lie, and you slept fine that night, which turned out to be worth more than the difference.

Nobody puts "God first" on a profit-and-loss sheet. It shows up instead in the sentence you're willing to cut from a proposal when nobody but you would ever have known it was there.

**Today:** Think of one place in your current work — a proposal, an invoice, a conversation you've been avoiding — where you've been shading the truth to protect an outcome. Write down: "This matters, even though it feels small, and I'm not the only one watching." Correct it this week, even if it costs you something.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, don't let me shade the truth to protect an outcome, even when it costs me. Help me remember that nothing I do is too small to matter to You. I want to work for Your approval, not just the client's signature or this quarter's number. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

COLOSSIANS 3:23-24
