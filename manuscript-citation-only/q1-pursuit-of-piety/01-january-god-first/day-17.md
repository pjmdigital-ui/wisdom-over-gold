# Day 17 — God First With Your Time

It's Sunday night. You're holding your phone, planning out the week on your calendar app. Work goes in first: the 8 a.m. call, the client lunch, and two hours on Thursday for the proposal due Friday. Next come the kids: Jake's practice, the orthodontist, and some school event you already forgot the name of. Then you pencil in the gym, if you're honest that you'll actually go. You scroll through all seven days. Then you notice something. Not one block of time, not even ten minutes, has your name on it for God.

Here's the belief that's really running your calendar, even if you'd never say it out loud: prayer doesn't need its own time slot. It'll just happen once things calm down. You've believed that for years. Things still haven't calmed down.

Ecclesiastes 3:1 says it simply. There is a right time for everything under heaven. People quote this verse at funerals and print it on kitchen signs. But read it plainly and it's blunt: everything gets a time, and whatever doesn't get a time doesn't happen. You already spend your time this way, the same way you spend money, whether or not you feel ready. Nothing on your calendar landed there because it "settled down" first. It landed there because you gave it a block.

Here's the hard truth: you'll find fifteen minutes for almost anything you decide matters. You'll find it for a scrolling session you don't even remember starting. You'll find it for a work email that could have waited until morning. Your calendar doesn't lie about your priorities. It tells the truth more plainly than you'd like. A man who says God comes first but never blocks time for Him is describing a hope, not a habit. And hopes don't survive a busy Tuesday.

Give God the same respect you give a client meeting. Set a fixed time and protect it from "I'll get to it later." Fifteen minutes with a real start time will survive a busy day. "Sometime today" never does. The men who pray every day aren't more disciplined than you overall. They just made one decision, on a calm night, that a busy Tuesday couldn't undo.

**Today:** Open your calendar right now. Write this sentence somewhere you'll see it tomorrow: *Prayer doesn't wait for a slow week.* Then block fifteen minutes tomorrow morning, before your first work task. Label it simply "Prayer." Don't move it. Don't put it off until "later."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I give You whatever time is left over instead of a real block on my calendar. Help me believe prayer doesn't have to wait for a slower season. Help me protect fifteen minutes tomorrow morning like it actually matters, because it does. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ECCLESIASTES 3:1
