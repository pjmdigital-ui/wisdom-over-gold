# Day 17 — God First With Your Time

Sunday night, phone in hand, you open the calendar app to plan the week. Work blocks go in first — the 8 a.m. call, the client lunch, the two hours you've protected for the proposal due Friday. Then the kids: Jake's practice, the orthodontist, the school thing you already forgot the name of. Then the gym, if you're honest with yourself about actually going. You scroll through all seven days before you notice it. Not one block, not one line, not one ten-minute gap has your name on it for God.

Here's the belief running the whole calendar, whether you'd say it out loud or not: prayer doesn't need a scheduled time to happen — it'll happen when things settle down. You've believed that for years. Things haven't settled down yet.

Ecclesiastes 3:1 says it plainly: there is a season for everything, a time appointed for every matter under heaven. The verse gets used at funerals and in framed kitchen art, but read plainly it's blunt: there is a time for everything, which means everything gets a time, and what doesn't get a time doesn't happen. You allocate time the same way you allocate dollars or attention, whether or not you ever feel ready to. Nothing on your calendar arrived there because it "settled down" first. It arrived because you gave it a block.

Here's the uncomfortable math: you will find fifteen minutes for almost anything you actually decide matters. You'll find it for a scrolling session you don't remember starting. You'll find it for a work email that could've waited until morning. The calendar doesn't lie about your priorities — it tells the truth more plainly than you'd like. A man who says God is first but has never blocked time for Him is describing a hope, not a practice, and hopes don't survive a busy Tuesday.

Give God the same respect you give a client meeting: a fixed time, protected from the drift of "I'll get to it." Fifteen minutes with a start time survives contact with a busy day in a way that "sometime today" never does. The men who pray consistently aren't more disciplined than you in general. They just made a decision once, on a calm night, that a busy Tuesday couldn't undo.

**Today:** Open your calendar right now and write the sentence *prayer doesn't wait for a slow week* somewhere you'll see it tomorrow. Then block fifteen minutes tomorrow morning, before your first work obligation, labeled simply "Prayer" — non-negotiable, no rescheduling it to "later."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I give You whatever time is left over instead of a real block on the calendar. Help me believe prayer doesn't have to wait for a slower season, and help me protect fifteen minutes tomorrow morning like it actually matters, because it does. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

ECCLESIASTES 3:1
