# Day 18 — God First With Your Money

The mortgage went up in April. Then the car needed brakes. Then your daughter's braces came due six months earlier than the orthodontist quoted. By the time the offering basket comes around this Sunday, you're doing the math before the usher reaches your pew — mortgage, brakes, braces, the credit card balance you haven't said out loud to your wife yet. You put in what you always put in, out of habit more than conviction, and a small resentment flickers that you'd never admit to anyone. *Not this month,* you tell yourself. *Ask me when things are less tight.*

Here's what that sentence is actually saying, if you push on it: I'll give once the budget feels safer. You already suspect the budget is never going to feel safe. There will always be a brake job. The month where giving feels comfortable and painless isn't coming, because comfort was never the point.

Matthew 6:21 says it in one plain line: wherever your treasure is, that's where your heart will end up too. Jesus is describing a mechanism, almost a law of nature about the human heart. Your heart doesn't lead your money — your money leads your heart. Put your money toward comfort and security and your heart orbits comfort and security, worrying about it, protecting it, measuring your peace by its balance. Put your money toward God's work and your heart starts to follow it there too.

This is why the tight month is the test that matters, not the flush one. Giving out of abundance costs you nothing — of course you'll give when you can't feel it leave. Giving when the number in the account makes you wince is the kind that reorders anything, because it requires trust instead of surplus. A man who only gives when it's easy has put his money first, and God gets whatever's left over — which, by definition, is never the treasure, only the crumbs.

Every dollar you release on purpose, especially the ones that hurt to release, is a small rehearsal for the truth that none of it was ever fully yours. You were a steward the whole time, not an owner. The tight budget didn't create that truth. It exposed how tightly you'd been gripping something you never owned.

**Today:** Before you pay a single other bill this week, write down the sentence *I don't need the budget to feel safe before I give* and set your giving aside first — even a smaller amount than you'd like, even if it makes the rest of the budget uncomfortable. Give it before the leftover math talks you out of it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, this month is tight and I want to hold onto every dollar until things feel safer. Help me give You the first part of what I have instead of whatever's left over, and trust You with the brakes and the braces and the rest of it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MATTHEW 6:21
