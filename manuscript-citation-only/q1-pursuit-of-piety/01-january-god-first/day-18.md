# Day 18 — God First With Your Money

The mortgage payment went up in April. Then the car needed new brakes. Then your daughter's braces came due six months earlier than the orthodontist said. By the time the offering basket comes around this Sunday, you're already doing the math before the usher reaches your pew. Mortgage. Brakes. Braces. The credit card balance you haven't told your wife about yet. You put in what you always put in, more out of habit than real desire. A small resentment flickers that you'd never admit to anyone. *Not this month,* you tell yourself. *Ask me when things are less tight.*

Push on that sentence and here's what it's really saying: I'll give once the budget feels safe. Deep down you already know the budget is never going to feel safe. There will always be another brake job. A month where giving feels easy and painless isn't coming, because comfort was never the point.

Matthew 6:21 says it in one plain line: wherever your treasure is, your heart will end up there too. Jesus is describing how the human heart actually works. Your heart doesn't lead your money. Your money leads your heart. Put your money toward comfort and safety, and your heart follows, worrying about it, protecting it, measuring your peace by what's in the account. Put your money toward God's work, and your heart starts to follow that instead.

This is why a tight month is the real test, not a flush one. Giving out of extra money costs you nothing. Of course you'll give when you don't even feel it leave. Giving when the number in your account makes you wince is the kind that actually changes you, because it takes trust instead of just spare cash. A man who only gives when it's easy has already put his money first. God gets whatever's left over — the crumbs, never the treasure.

Every dollar you give on purpose, especially the ones that hurt to give, is a small reminder that none of it was ever fully yours. You were always a steward, not an owner. The tight budget didn't create that truth. It just showed you how tightly you'd been holding onto something you never owned.

**Today:** Before you pay any other bill this week, write down this sentence: *I don't need the budget to feel safe before I give.* Then set your giving aside first, even a smaller amount than you'd like, even if it makes the rest of the budget tight. Give it before you talk yourself out of it with leftover math.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, this month is tight, and I want to hold onto every dollar until things feel safer. Help me give You the first part of what I have, not just whatever's left over. Help me trust You with the brakes and the braces and everything else. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MATTHEW 6:21
