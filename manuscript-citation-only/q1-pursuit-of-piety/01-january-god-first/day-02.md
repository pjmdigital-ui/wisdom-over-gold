# Day 2 — What Are You Actually Seeking?

Before your feet hit the floor, your hand already knows where the phone is. You don't even remember deciding to reach for it. It's just there, and your thumb starts moving before your eyes are fully open. Inbox. Texts. The score from last night. A headline that makes your stomach tighten before you've said a word to your wife or looked at your kids. By the time you actually stand up, your mind is already three inboxes deep in other people's demands and opinions.

Try counting the mornings this week you did this before you prayed, before you drank water, before you said good morning to the people in your own house. For most of us it's all seven. Nobody tells you that the first reach of the day is a kind of confession. It tells you, honestly, who you actually answer to before anyone's forced you to decide.

The psalmist writes something different into his own morning, in Psalm 63:1. He tells God plainly that he is seeking Him. His very soul is thirsty for Him, before anything else has had a chance to speak. Notice the order. He doesn't say, "I'll get to you once I've handled the inbox." He says first, before the day has had a chance to hand him its agenda. He also names his condition honestly: thirst. Not a mild preference. An actual bodily need, the kind that gets your attention because ignoring it has consequences.

Here's the uncomfortable question underneath the phone habit: what are you actually thirsty for? Information? Validation? The little rush of a notification badge going to zero? None of those things are evil on their own. But none of them were ever going to satisfy the thirst the psalmist is describing. You can drink from that well all morning and still walk into your kitchen feeling empty.

The phone is just the delivery method. What actually matters is what gets to speak into your soul first, before your defenses are up, before you've had a chance to remember who you want to be that day. Whatever goes first sets the tone for everything after it. A man who starts his day soaking up the world's noise will spend the rest of the day reacting to it. A man who starts his day seeking God, even for ninety seconds, starts the day already anchored.

**Today:** Put your phone in another room tonight, charging somewhere you can't reach from bed. Tomorrow morning, before you touch it, say one sentence out loud that names what you actually want first: *God, I want You before I want anything my phone can give me.* Then go get the phone.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, before I reach for my phone, I want to reach for You first. Meet me tomorrow morning before the inbox and the noise get a word in, and show me what I'm actually thirsty for. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PSALM 63:1
