# Day 6 — Who's on the Throne

It started over something small — whether he'd told her about the schedule change or not. Within two minutes the fight had turned into who was right. Dan could feel himself building the case in his head even while she was still talking. He was lining up the text message that would prove it, the date, the time stamp, the receipts. He stopped listening to his wife and started preparing a closing argument instead.

He won, technically. He found the text. He was right about the schedule. He stood in the kitchen holding his phone up like evidence, and he watched his wife's face do something worse than anger. She just looked tired, and a little bit alone, even though he was standing three feet away from her. Being right had cost him something that mattered more than being right ever could.

St. Paul had already diagnosed that exact kitchen two thousand years earlier. In Philippians 2:3-5, he tells believers to do nothing out of selfishness or pride, but to think of others as more important than themselves, and to have the same attitude Christ Jesus had. Selfishness. Pride. Those are the words for what Dan was doing with that phone held up like a trophy. They also name the belief he never would have said out loud: winning this argument mattered more right now than she did.

Needing to be right is control wearing a different costume — the same instinct as the white-knuckled contract, just aimed at your marriage instead of your business. It says: I can't stand being seen as wrong, because being wrong feels like losing ground I can't afford to lose. But a marriage doesn't work like a negotiation with a winner. The moment you start keeping score against your wife, you've made yourself her opponent instead of her husband. No one wins a war against the person they promised to love.

Christ had every right in the universe to insist on being right, being first, being served. He emptied Himself instead. That's the mindset Paul is asking for. Being correct still matters. But it matters far less than what a man is actually protecting when he lets it go: her, the marriage, the thing they both said "I do" to build together. Dan didn't need to be wrong about the schedule. He needed to put down the phone.

**Today:** Next time you feel yourself building a case against your wife mid-argument, stop and say silently: "Loving her matters more than winning this." Then ask her one question out loud instead: "What did that feel like for you?" Actually listen to the answer before you say anything else.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I want to be right more than I want to love my wife well, and I know that's backwards. Give me the humility to put the phone down, drop the case I'm building, and actually listen to her. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PHILIPPIANS 2:3-5
