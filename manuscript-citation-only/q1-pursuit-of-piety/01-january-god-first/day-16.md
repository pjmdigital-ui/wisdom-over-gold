# Day 16 — God First in Marriage

The argument was about something so small you can't even remember it clearly now. The school pickup schedule, maybe, or whose turn it was to call the insurance company. What you do remember is the shape of it. Your wife said something that landed wrong. You corrected her tone instead of her point. Within four minutes you were both standing in the hallway, refighting a fight you'd apparently had eleven times before. Neither of you was really listening. Both of you were building a case.

You had the winning move. You knew it the second it came to mind. A specific thing she'd said three weeks earlier that contradicted what she was saying now. A piece of evidence that would end the argument in your favor if you played it. You felt the pull to say it, the same way you feel the pull to hit send on a text you'll regret. Here's the belief driving that pull, plain as you'll ever see it. Winning this matters more, right now, than she does.

You didn't play it. Instead you said something closer to, "I don't want to win this, I want to figure it out with you." It felt weak coming out of your mouth, and it turned out to be the only thing that actually worked. Her shoulders dropped. The argument didn't get fully resolved in that hallway, but it stopped being a fight.

St. Paul writes to the Ephesians about marriage. He opens the whole passage with a line people often skip on their way to the more famous verses about husbands and wives. In 5:21, he calls both spouses to submit to one another out of respect for Christ. Before any instruction about roles, there's this: mutual submission, both spouses giving something up for the other, out of respect for someone bigger than either of them. That's the frame the rest of the passage has to be read through. It comes down to who is on the throne when the two of you disagree.

Here's the test. When God is genuinely first, being right stops being the point of an argument with your wife. Your loyalty belongs to Him now, and to what He's asked of you toward her, not to being correct. That change costs you something real. You'll walk away from arguments you could have won. It's worth the cost, because being right was never worth what winning costs your marriage.

**Today:** Next time you're in the middle of an argument with your wife and you feel the pull to play your best point, say to yourself first: "She matters more than winning this." Then ask her out loud: "What do you need from me right now?" Stop talking and listen to the answer.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, help me want peace with my wife more than I want to be right. When I feel the pull to play my best point, remind me she matters more than winning does. Teach me to ask what she needs and actually listen. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

EPHESIANS 5:21
