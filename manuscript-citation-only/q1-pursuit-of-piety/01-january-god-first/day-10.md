# Day 10 — Surrendering the Wheel

The email came in at 6:47 on a Sunday night. Your biggest client made up forty percent of your revenue. You'd put their logo on your website like a trophy. Now they were pulling out. No warning, no chance to negotiate, just a two-line notice from someone in their legal department. You read it standing in your kitchen with your coat still on. You felt the floor tilt under you.

For the next three days you did what you always did when something started slipping out of your hands. You gripped harder. You called everyone you knew. You drafted a counter-proposal at midnight. You ran the numbers seventeen different ways. You were looking for some angle that would make this not be happening. You barely slept, and when you did, you dreamed about spreadsheets.

It was your wife who finally said something. She had watched you pace the kitchen for four nights in a row. "You're acting like you're the one holding this whole company together." She didn't say it to hurt you. She said it because it was true, and because she loved you enough to say the hard thing. Ask yourself the question she didn't have to say out loud. When did you start believing that if you stopped pushing, everything you built would fall apart?

Proverbs 3:5-6 lays out the order plainly. Trust the Lord with your whole heart instead of leaning on your own understanding. In everything you do, look to Him, and He will make your path straight. Notice the order of that. Trust Him, not after you've used up your own ideas, but instead of relying on them. That means letting go of the wheel before the crash, not after it.

You didn't get the client back. But around day four, you stopped gripping the spreadsheet so hard and actually started praying about it. Not the kind of prayer you throw up when you want one specific outcome. The harder kind: *God, I don't know how this turns out, and I'm asking You to be enough either way.* The business, it turned out, could survive a bad quarter. What it couldn't survive was you running your whole life like God was a consultant you only called after your own plan failed.

**On St. Ignatius of Loyola:** Before his conversion, Ignatius was a soldier chasing glory and his own advancement. Then a cannonball shattered his leg, and with it, the life he had built around himself. His long recovery changed everything. He came to describe the goal of the spiritual life as "indifference" — a willingness to hold every outcome loosely. Wealth or poverty, health or sickness, success or failure. He still cared about outcomes. He just stopped needing them to go his way.

**Today:** Name one outcome you're currently gripping too tightly — a deal, a diagnosis, a decision someone else is making about your life. Say it out loud: "I trust You with this even if it doesn't go the way I want." Then do one thing today that proves you meant it. Make the call you've been avoiding, or just stop checking the number for the rest of the day.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've been gripping this instead of handing it to You, acting like everything falls apart if I stop pushing. I don't know how it turns out, but I trust You to be enough either way. Help me let go of the wheel before the crash, not after. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
