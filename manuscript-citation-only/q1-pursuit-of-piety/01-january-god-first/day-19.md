# Day 19 — God First in Silence

You set a timer for ten minutes. You sat down in the chair by the window before anyone else woke up and decided you'd just be quiet with God. No podcast, no phone, no to-do list running in the back of your mind. Just silence. It lasted about ninety seconds before your leg started bouncing.

Then the thoughts showed up, loud and uninvited. The email you forgot to send Friday. Whether you need to call the dentist. A conversation with your business partner you keep replaying. You caught yourself mentally writing a text message. You checked the timer, sure five minutes had passed. It had been two. By minute nine, you were pretty sure silence just wasn't for you.

Ask yourself honestly why nine quiet minutes felt harder than a nine-hour workday. What were you really protecting yourself from by staying busy instead of staying still? If you're honest, some part of you believes staying in motion, staying productive and useful, is safer than sitting still and finding out what's actually going on inside you.

Psalm 46:10 gives it to us as a command: be still, and know that He is God. It sounds gentle until you actually try to do it. Stillness turns out to be one of the hardest habits for a modern man, harder in some ways than fasting or waking up early. It removes every distraction you use to avoid what's underneath. Noise is a place to hide. Being busy is a place to hide. Silence takes that hiding place away, and what's left in the room is just you and God and whatever you've been running from.

The bouncing leg and the racing thoughts are just the noise finally becoming visible instead of drowned out. It's proof you've been outrunning this longer than you thought. Men who fill every open minute with golf, work, scrolling, or podcasts aren't more at peace than the man who sits still and feels restless. They've just never stopped long enough to notice the restlessness was already there.

You don't need a peaceful, empty mind on command, like flipping a switch. Showing up matters more than that. Sit in the discomfort and let God be present in it, not because you felt Him, but because He said He'd be there. Ten distracted, fidgety minutes offered to God are still ten minutes given to Him. Imperfect is the only way anyone actually does this.

**Today:** Sit in silence for ten minutes. No phone, no music, no agenda. Before you start, say out loud: *Staying busy isn't safer than sitting with You.* When your mind wanders, and it will, gently bring your attention back to God. Keep sitting until the timer goes off.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, my mind won't sit still, and the quiet makes me want to reach for anything else. Stay with me anyway, in the fidgeting and the racing thoughts. Help me keep coming back to You instead of walking away. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PSALM 46:10
