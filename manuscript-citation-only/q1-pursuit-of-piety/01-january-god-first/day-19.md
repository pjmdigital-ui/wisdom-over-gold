# Day 19 — God First in Silence

You set a timer for ten minutes, sat down in the chair by the window before anyone else woke up, and decided you were just going to be quiet with God. No podcast, no phone, no to-do list open in the other tab of your mind. Just silence. It lasted maybe ninety seconds before your leg started bouncing.

Then the thoughts arrived, uninvited and loud: the email you forgot to send Friday, whether you need to call the dentist, a conversation with your business partner you keep replaying and losing. You caught yourself mentally drafting a text message. You checked the timer, certain five minutes had passed. It had been two. By minute nine you were fairly sure silence was not for you.

Ask yourself honestly why nine minutes of quiet felt harder than a nine-hour workday. What exactly were you protecting yourself from by staying busy instead of staying still? If you're honest, some part of you believes that staying in motion — productive, useful, moving from one task to the next — is safer than sitting still and finding out what's actually going on inside you.

Psalm 46:10 says it as a command: be still, and know that He is God. It sounds gentle until you actually try to obey it. Stillness turns out to be one of the harder disciplines available to a modern man, harder in some ways than fasting or getting up early, because it removes every distraction you use to avoid what's underneath. Noise is a place to hide. Busyness is a place to hide. Silence takes the hiding place away, and what's left in the room is just you and God and whatever you've been outrunning.

The bouncing leg and the racing thoughts are the noise finally becoming visible instead of drowned out — proof you've been outrunning this longer than you thought. Men who golf, work, scroll, and podcast their way through every open minute of the day aren't more at peace than the man who sits still and feels agitated. They've just never stopped long enough to notice the agitation was already there.

You don't need a peaceful, empty mind on command, like flipping a switch. Showing up matters more — sitting in the discomfort and letting God be present in it, not because you felt Him, but because He said He'd be there. Ten distracted, fidgety minutes offered to God are still ten minutes given to Him, and imperfect is the only way anyone ever does this.

**Today:** Sit in silence for ten minutes with no phone, no music, no agenda. Before you start, say out loud: *Staying busy isn't safer than sitting with You.* When your mind wanders, and it will, gently return your attention to God and keep sitting until the timer goes off.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, my mind won't sit still and the quiet makes me want to reach for anything else. Stay with me in the fidgeting and the racing thoughts anyway, and help me keep coming back to You instead of walking away. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PSALM 46:10
