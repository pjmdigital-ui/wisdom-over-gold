# Day 1 — The God of Your Own Life

The email came in at 6:14 on a Tuesday morning. The deal had closed. Eighteen months of nights, weekends, and missed dinners were finally over. His marriage had been running on fumes the whole time. Mark sat in his truck in the driveway, engine off, phone lit up in his hand. He waited to feel something.

Nothing came. Not joy, not relief. Just a strange, flat quiet, like a room after the party ends and someone starts stacking the chairs. For a year and a half he'd told himself this was the finish line. Hit this number, land this deal, and everything else would sort itself out on the other side. His marriage, his kids, his own soul — all of it. He sat there five more minutes before he could make himself walk inside.

Most men don't build a shrine and call it a god. We build a career, a body, a reputation, a bank account instead. These are good things. They seem reasonable from the outside. But we make them the reason we get up in the morning. We become the god of our own life without ever deciding to. We just start living like the point of everything is our own name, our own comfort, our own version of winning. Nobody warns you that this works for a while. Then it stops working.

In Matthew 6:33, Jesus tells us to put God's kingdom first. Everything else — the deal, the paycheck, the whole life you're building — finds its place after that, not before it. Notice what He doesn't say. He doesn't say ambition is wrong. He doesn't say the deal shouldn't matter. He doesn't say a man shouldn't work hard for his family. He says seek God first. Order matters more than effort. A man can work just as hard chasing the wrong thing as the right thing, and from the outside the work looks the same either way. Only the center is different. And the center is everything.

That flat feeling in the truck was the truth finally getting a word in. Nothing you build for yourself was ever going to be big enough to carry that kind of weight. You were made for more than being your own end goal. You were made to belong to Someone bigger than the story you're writing about yourself.

**On St. Augustine:** Before he was a bishop and a saint, Augustine chased status, pleasure, and approval. He was smart and hungry, and he was sure each one would finally be enough. It never was. He wrote something that every man in that driveway already half-knows: "Our heart is restless until it rests in You." That restlessness is a signal pointing you home.

**Today:** Write down, in one sentence, what you're actually chasing right now — the thing you believe will finally make you feel like you've arrived. Underneath it, write the truth: *I already belong to God. Nothing else has to make me arrive.* Keep both sentences somewhere you'll see them tomorrow.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I can see what I've been chasing to make me feel like I've finally arrived, and I know it's not big enough to be my god. Quiet the restlessness in me tonight and remind me it was always meant to point back to You. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MATTHEW 6:33
