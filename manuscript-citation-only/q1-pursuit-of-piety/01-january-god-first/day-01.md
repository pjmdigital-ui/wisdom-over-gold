# Day 1 — The God of Your Own Life

The email came in at 6:14 on a Tuesday morning. The deal had closed. Eighteen months of nights, weekends, missed dinners, a marriage running on fumes — and it was finally done. Mark sat in his truck in the driveway, engine off, phone lit up in his hand, and waited to feel something.

Nothing came. Not joy, not relief. Just a strange, flat quiet, like a room after the party's over and someone's already stacking the chairs. He'd told himself for a year and a half that this was the finish line. Hit this number, land this deal, and everything else — the marriage, the kids, his own soul — would sort itself out on the other side. He sat there another five minutes before he could make himself walk inside.

Most men don't build a shrine and call it a god. We build a career, a body, a reputation, a bank account — good things, defensible things — and make them the reason we get up in the morning. We become the god of our own life without ever deciding to. We just start living like the point of our existence is our own name, our own comfort, our own version of winning. Nobody warns you that this works, for a while, and then it doesn't.

Jesus names the alternative plainly in Matthew 6:33: seek God's kingdom first, and everything else — the deal, the paycheck, the whole life you're building — finds its place after that, not before it. Notice what He doesn't say. He doesn't say ambition is wrong, or that the deal shouldn't matter, or that a man shouldn't work hard for his family. He says *seek first*. Order matters more than effort. A man can work exactly as hard chasing the wrong center as the right one — the labor looks identical from the outside. Only the center is different, and the center is everything.

That flat feeling in the truck was the truth finally getting a word in. Nothing you build for yourself was ever going to be big enough to hold the weight you were asking it to carry. You were made for more than being your own end goal — you were made to belong to Someone bigger than the story you're writing about yourself.

**On St. Augustine:** Before he was a bishop and a saint, Augustine chased status, pleasure, and approval with real intelligence and real hunger, certain each one would finally be enough. It never was. He wrote what every man in that driveway already half-knows: "Our heart is restless until it rests in You." That restlessness is a homing signal.

**Today:** Write down, in one sentence, what you're actually chasing right now — the thing you believe will finally make you feel like you've arrived. Underneath it, write the belief that's actually true: *I already belong to God. Nothing else has to make me arrive.* Keep both sentences somewhere you'll see them tomorrow.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I can see what I've been chasing to make me feel like I've finally arrived, and I know it's not big enough to be my god. Quiet the restlessness in me tonight and remind me it was always meant to point back to You. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MATTHEW 6:33
