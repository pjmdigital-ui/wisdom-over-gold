# Day 27 — Numbing Instead of Praying

The client fired you at 4:40 p.m., over email. Eight months of work, gone, with no phone call. Just one paragraph that ended the relationship and a chunk of your income at the same time. You closed the laptop. You sat in the driveway a minute, the way you do when you can't face walking in as yourself yet. Then you did what you always do on nights like this. You poured a drink before you'd even taken off your shoes. You told yourself it was just one. You know how that math tends to go on the worst nights. By nine, you weren't upset anymore. You also weren't really there. Your wife asked how your day was, and you said "fine." That was the first of several small lies, all aimed at not feeling what you were actually feeling.

It doesn't have to be a drink. For some men it's three hours of scrolling that leaves you feeling emptier than when you started. For others it's food, or burying a bad day under a bigger to-do list so there's no room left to feel it. The details change from man to man. What stays the same is the order. Something hard happens. You reach for God second, after you've already reached for something that promises to make the feeling smaller first.

St. Paul wrote to the Philippians from prison. He was in real chains, not sure if he'd live. He doesn't tell them to just feel better. In 4:6-7, he tells them what to actually do with their worry. Bring everything to God in prayer, and be thankful, instead of carrying the worry by yourself. Then a peace will come that's bigger than you can fully understand, and it will watch over your heart and mind in Christ. Notice the order here. Prayer comes first. The peace comes after. That order only makes sense if you truly believe you can hand God a feeling before you've dulled it. Without ever saying it out loud, you've been living by the opposite belief. You've believed a feeling this big has to be numbed before it's safe to sit with.

Numbing and praying are both ways of handling pain you don't want to carry alone. Numbing dulls the feeling but leaves the wound untouched underneath it. Prayer hands the wound to Someone strong enough to actually hold it, full and sharp, exactly as it is. This is the same choice this book keeps coming back to, picking the harder thing over the easy default, the same way Day 5 asked you to choose your family over the couch. Here it's just pointed at prayer instead. The drink works fast, for about three hours. Prayer works slower, but it means you're not carrying the pain by yourself.

You lost the client either way. The only real question that night was who you brought the loss to first.

**Today:** Next time you feel the pull toward the drink, the scroll, or the screen, pause for sixty seconds first. Say out loud, even if it feels awkward: "God, here's what happened today." Tell Him before you reach for anything else. Then decide if you still want it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I reached for something to numb the pain instead of bringing it to You first, and I'm sorry. Next time it hits this hard, help me tell You about it before I try to feel better on my own. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PHILIPPIANS 4:6-7
