# Day 5 — The Comfort Trap

By 8:30 the dishes were done, the kids were finally down, and the couch had that gravitational pull it gets after a fourteen-hour day. *It's just ten minutes* — I told myself I'd check the score, then go find my wife, who was folding laundry two rooms away and would have loved five minutes of actual conversation instead of a rerun on in the background. Ten minutes became the whole quarter. The quarter became the next game. She gave up waiting and went to bed before I'd even looked up.

Nothing about that night was a crisis. That's exactly what makes it dangerous. Nobody's marriage collapses over one night on the couch. It erodes over a thousand nights like it — small, defensible choices toward the thing that costs nothing and asks nothing of you, instead of the thing sitting two rooms away that actually needed you present. *It's just ten minutes* was the whole belief, right there, disguised as a small decision: comfort is free, and saying yes to it costs nothing.

St. Paul writes to the Colossians, in 3:2, telling them to fix their minds on the things above, not on the things of earth. He's not telling them to despise rest or pleasure — Paul isn't a Stoic. He's naming where the mind defaults to when nobody's steering it: down, toward the immediate and the easy, instead of up, toward what actually matters and will still matter in ten years.

That same pull runs deeper than the couch. The instinct that keeps a man sitting when his family is two rooms away is the same instinct that keeps him in bed when the alarm goes off for a workout, or lets him skip the hard set because nobody's watching and it costs nothing to skip it. Paul doesn't leave much room to treat the body as exempt from this. In Romans 12:1, he appeals to believers, by the mercies of God, to offer their bodies themselves as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God — calling that offering their true, reasonable worship. Worship, in Paul's mouth, isn't confined to a pew. Choosing the hard thing with your body — training when you don't want to, standing up from the couch when sitting is easier — is an offering, not a hobby.

A man who's putting God first doesn't have to give up rest. He has to stop letting comfort make his decisions for him by default, whether the decision is about who gets his presence tonight or whether his body gets trained tomorrow morning.

**Today:** Tonight, before you sit down, decide on purpose where the next hour goes — and if it's with your family, put the phone in another room first. Say out loud: "Comfort doesn't get the vote tonight." Tomorrow morning, when the alarm goes off for the workout you'd normally skip, apply the same rule.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for the nights I let comfort make the decision for me instead of choosing the people two rooms away who needed me present. Help me feel the pull toward the couch tonight and choose my family anyway, and give me the same resolve tomorrow when comfort asks for my body too. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
