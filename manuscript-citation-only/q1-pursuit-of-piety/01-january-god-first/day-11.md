# Day 11 — The Kingdom Before the Checklist

You had blocked six-thirty to six-fifty on your calendar. Twenty minutes, coffee still hot, before the house woke up. Just you, a Bible, and a few quiet minutes. You'd been doing it for almost three weeks, and it had actually started to feel like something. Not just another box to check.

Then Monday hit. You opened your laptop at six-thirty "just to check one thing" before you sat down to pray. The one thing turned into a fire. A vendor had missed a deadline. A client was already emailing you in all caps. By six-fifty you were four emails deep, your coffee was cold, and your Bible was still closed on the counter. Tuesday you told yourself you'd make it up at lunch. You didn't. By Thursday the block was still sitting on your calendar, but you had stopped even glancing at it. You just clicked past it, like a meeting you'd quietly decided not to go to anymore.

Nobody sat you down and told you prayer mattered less than the vendor problem. You never actually decided that either. What happened was quieter than that. Everything on your list felt equally urgent, so the newest fire always won. Prayer had no deadline. Nobody was emailing in caps about it. So it kept losing by default.

There's a story in Luke where Martha is doing exactly what needs doing — cooking, hosting, keeping the household running. Her sister Mary just sits at Jesus's feet, listening. Martha gets frustrated, understandably, and asks Jesus to make Mary help her. His answer isn't what she wants to hear. He tells her she's worried and troubled about many things, when only one thing is truly needed. Mary, sitting at His feet, has chosen the better part, and it won't be taken from her. Hold onto that idea — *only one thing is truly needed* — next to the belief you'd been running on all week. Not many things, equally weighted, fighting for whichever slot opens first. One thing, first, on purpose, before the rest gets a vote.

None of the items on your list were wrong. The vendor problem was real. The client's email needed an answer. But your work will always grow to fill whatever space you give it. It will keep growing until it eats the twenty minutes you set aside for the one thing that was supposed to order everything else. A checklist has no natural stopping point. It just has the next item. Only you can decide something goes before it, and mean that every day, not just the days it's convenient.

You put the block back on the calendar Friday morning. This time you moved it before you even opened the laptop, and you put your phone in another room. Not because the emails weren't urgent. You finally admitted they would always feel urgent, every single morning, for the rest of your life, unless you decided ahead of time what came first.

**Today:** Say it out loud before you touch your phone tomorrow: "This is the one thing that comes first — not because the rest doesn't matter, but because it doesn't get to compete." Then spend five minutes with a single verse of Scripture before you open your laptop. Put the phone in another room tonight if that's what it takes.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, my to-do list keeps eating the time I set aside for You, because I've been treating everything as equally urgent instead of putting You first. Help me choose the one thing that's needed before I open my laptop tomorrow. Give me the discipline to protect it, because it's the one thing that puts everything else in order. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

LUKE 10:41-42
