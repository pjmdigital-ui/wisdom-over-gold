# Day 12 — What You Worship, You Become

You'd known Peyton since college. A good guy, generous, the kind of friend who'd drive two hours to help you move. Over the last ten years you watched him build a real estate portfolio that made your own finances look small. Somewhere in there, something in him changed, slowly. Every conversation eventually turned to square footage, or comps, or the guy he'd just outbid on a deal. It was the new truck, then the newer truck. It was the way he'd check his watch during his daughter's dance recital. It was the way he introduced himself at parties now, with his net worth practically built into his job title.

You had dinner with him last month. Driving home, you realized you couldn't remember the last time he asked you a question about your life that wasn't secretly about his. He'd gotten smaller somehow. Sharper about the things that mattered to him, and dull to almost everything else. Underneath the mild judgment you felt about that was something plainer, something you hadn't even admitted to yourself. What Peyton has is actually worth wanting. You envied the truck. You envied the number. You wanted to feel bothered by what you saw in him, and instead, some part of you wanted his life.

The Psalmist describes idols this way, in Psalm 115. They have mouths that can't speak and eyes that can't see. He warns that the people who make them end up just like them, and so does everyone who trusts in them. Read that as a real warning, not just a story about ancient statues. Whatever you fix your attention and hope on, you start to become like it. Worship money and status long enough, and you get sharper elbows and a smaller soul. Not because money is evil, but because it was never built to carry the weight you're asking it to carry.

That's what makes watching a friend go this direction so uncomfortable. Every man has a Peyton-shaped temptation somewhere in his own ambition. The version of himself that would show up if he let the wrong thing sit on the throne long enough. Seeing it clearly in a friend can actually be a mercy, if you let it be one. It hands you the question early, before the number gets big enough to answer it for you. Are you becoming like him, or becoming like the God you say you actually worship?

**On St. Francis of Assisi:** Born into wealth as the son of a successful cloth merchant, Francis was expected to inherit both the family business and its status. Instead, he stripped off his fine clothes in the town square and gave them back to his father. He chose poverty and total dependence on God over the life laid out for him. He gave up what had been sitting first in his life, and became more himself because of it, not less.

**Today:** Name the person whose life bothers you because you envy it. Write down, specifically, what they've made an idol of. Then write one sentence underneath it: "That's not actually worth wanting more than God." Say it out loud once.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, show me honestly what I'm chasing when I envy someone else's life. I don't want what isn't actually worth wanting more than You. Keep me from letting anything but You sit on the throne. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PSALM 115:5, 8
