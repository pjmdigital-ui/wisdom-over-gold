# Day 26 — The Comparison Trap

You were only supposed to check the score. Ten seconds, phone in one hand, coffee in the other, standing at the kitchen counter before the kids were even up. But the app opened to your feed instead, and there he was — a guy you went to college with, bigger house, a boat now apparently, his family looking effortlessly happy on some beach you couldn't place. You knew, rationally, a photo isn't a life. It didn't matter. Something in your chest tightened anyway.

By the time your own kids came downstairs, you were short with them — over nothing they'd done. Your wife asked what was wrong and you said "nothing," because *I feel like a failure because of a boat I saw on Instagram* sounds as absurd out loud as it actually is. But underneath the irritation was a question you didn't say out loud either: what does he have that I don't? Is there something real there — some peace, some version of a life put together — that you're missing and he somehow found? You spent the drive to work running the math on it, comparing his one visible morning to your whole ordinary week, and losing every time.

St. Paul cuts straight through that question in Galatians 6:4: each man is called to examine his own work, and take pride in what he's actually done, measured against himself — not against his neighbor. Test your own work. Not his. Paul assumes you'll be tempted to measure yourself against a neighbor and tells you plainly that the only honest measurement is the one you take of your own calling, your own work, the actual life God gave you to steward.

Here's what the algorithm never shows you: the guy with the boat has 2 a.m. worries too. A marriage with its own quiet strain, a body failing him somewhere, a father he still hasn't made peace with. You weren't comparing your whole life to his whole life. You were comparing your whole life, the parts you actually live, to nine seconds of his that he chose to post. He doesn't have something you're missing. He has a highlight reel, same as the one you'd post if you were building his.

A man seeking God first stops asking how he's doing compared to the guy with the boat. He asks what God actually gave him to steward, and whether he's stewarding it well today.

**Today:** The next time you catch yourself measuring your life against someone else's feed, close the app and say out loud: *He doesn't have something I'm missing. I have what God gave me to steward.* Then name one specific thing in front of you — your kids, your work, your marriage — and thank God for it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for grading my life against a stranger's highlight reel and coming up bitter over it. Help me see what You've actually put in front of me today and thank You for it instead of resenting it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

GALATIANS 6:4
