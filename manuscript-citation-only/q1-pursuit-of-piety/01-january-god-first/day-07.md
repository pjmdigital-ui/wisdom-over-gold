# Day 7 — The Ache Underneath

It hit him on a Sunday afternoon, of all times, sitting in a lawn chair with a beer he didn't really want, watching his kids run through the sprinkler in a yard he owned outright. Good marriage. Healthy kids. A business that finally didn't keep him up at night the way it used to. By every measure he'd have used at twenty-five to define making it, he'd made it. And there it was anyway — that same low ache under his ribs. It didn't care what was going on in his life. It was like it had been waiting for a quiet moment to remind him it never actually left.

He almost felt guilty for it. What right did he have to feel unsettled with a life this good? But the ache wasn't asking permission, and it wasn't interested in his list of things to be thankful for. It just sat there, patient, the way it always had. Through the hard years and the good ones alike, it didn't change based on how things were going.

The psalmist knew this ache by name and didn't dress it up. In Psalm 42:1-2, he compares his own soul to a deer panting for streams of water. It's that same desperate, physical longing, except aimed at the living God Himself. This psalm was likely written during a season of real distance from the temple, real longing, even though his life had not actually fallen apart. The words he reaches for are about thirst, a bodily need that shows up whether or not anything around him is actually falling apart.

You can fix your marriage, your finances, and your health, one at a time, completely, and the ache stays exactly where it was. A man can spend years treating it as a symptom of whatever's currently going wrong in his life. He never notices that it survives every fix — the God-shaped space every man carries, rich or broke, married or single, thriving or barely holding on. Good things in your life can quiet the noise around it. They were never built to fill it. Only God fits there.

That Sunday afternoon was an invitation, arriving in the one moment he had nothing left to distract himself with. The ache is doing its job. It's just pointing you somewhere the sprinkler and the lawn chair were never going to be able to reach.

**Today:** The next time you notice that low ache — in a good moment, not just a hard one — don't reach for a distraction. Sit with it for sixty seconds and say simply, "God, I know this is You." Then open your List of Truths and read it slowly. Let it remind you the ache was never really about what's missing.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I feel that ache even in the good moments, and I know now it was never about my situation. Thank You for not letting me settle for anything smaller than You to fill it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

PSALM 42:1-2
