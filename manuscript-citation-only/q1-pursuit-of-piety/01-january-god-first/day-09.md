# Day 9 — Repentance Isn't Just for Sinners

You hadn't been to confession in eleven years. You knew the exact number because you'd done the math in the car more than once. Usually after a fight with your wife where you said things you didn't mean. Or after a week when you looked at your phone more than you looked at your kids. Eleven years is a long time. Long enough that you'd built a quiet case for yourself. You weren't robbing banks. You went to Mass most Sundays. By any normal measure, you were a decent man.

Then a friend mentioned he went to confession every month. Not because he was in some crisis. Just because it was Tuesday, and that's what he did. You laughed it off at the time. Then you thought about it the whole drive home. Here's what you had really been telling yourself, if you ever said it out loud: *I'm not bad enough for this. Confession is for men with something real to confess. Big sins. Mine don't count.*

You went on a Thursday, to a parish where nobody knew your name. You sat in the pew rehearsing what to say, like you were prepping for a hard client call. When you finally said it out loud — the pride, the shortcuts, the years of running your family like a business instead of a marriage — nothing dramatic happened. No lightning. Just a quiet man in a small room saying, "I absolve you." You walked out into the parking lot feeling lighter than you had in years.

John says it plainly in his first letter, chapter 1, verse 9. If we confess our sins, God is faithful and just. He forgives us and washes away everything wrong we've done. Read that again and look for the condition you'd been adding on your own. There isn't one. It doesn't say "if you've hit bottom." It doesn't say "if it's serious enough." It just says: if we confess. The verse asks for honesty, not a list of big crimes.

Men treat confession like a fire extinguisher. Something for emergencies, for the guy who really messed up. The proud man, the slightly selfish man, the man who looks fine on paper, decides he doesn't qualify. So he never goes. But selfishness keeps creeping back in, in small ways you could easily deny. The tone you take with your wife when you're tired. The corners you cut that you'd never admit to cutting. That's why a man has to keep coming back and putting God first again and again, on purpose, long before things turn into a real crisis.

You don't need a crisis to walk back through that door. You need to be honest about the eleven years, or the eleven days, since you last let anyone — including God — tell you the truth about yourself.

**Today:** Write one sentence and mean it: *I don't have to be bad enough. I only have to be honest.* Then find the confession times at a parish near you, even one you've never visited, and go this week. If it's really been years, don't overthink what to say. Just start with, "It's been a long time," and let the priest help you from there.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've been hiding behind being "decent" instead of being honest with You. Help me believe I don't have to be bad enough to need You. I only have to be honest. Give me the courage to walk through that door this week and say it out loud. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 JOHN 1:9
