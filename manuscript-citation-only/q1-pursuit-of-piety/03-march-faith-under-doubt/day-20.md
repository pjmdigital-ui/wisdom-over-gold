# Day 20 — Gratitude at Work

Our biggest client left for a competitor last month, and I've been rehearsing the same internal speech ever since. I built this company from a folding table in my garage. I answered calls at midnight for three years before we hired anyone. That account owed us its loyalty — I earned it. I've said some version of this to my wife, to my business partner, to the bathroom mirror, and every time it comes out a little more bitter.

What I haven't said out loud, because I've never quite put words to it, is the belief running underneath the bitterness: *what I have, I built, and it's owed to me because of what it cost me.* By that logic, losing the account feels less like a business setback and more like theft — something that was mine getting taken.

St. Paul asks the church at Corinth a question that lands harder the longer you sit with it: "For who sees anything different in you? What have you that you did not receive? If then you received it, why do you boast as if it were not a gift?" (1 Corinthians 4:7, RSV-CE). He's writing to people proud of their own gifts and achievements, and he doesn't argue that the gifts aren't real. He asks where they came from. The energy to work those midnight calls, the mind sharp enough to build something out of nothing, the health to keep going, the client relationships themselves — none of it originated with me. I was given the raw materials and I used them well. Using a gift well is real work. It still doesn't make the gift mine to begin with.

The effort still mattered. Losing the client still stings. But the account was never mine in the way I'd been treating it — as collateral I'd earned the right to keep. It was provision, on loan, for as long as it lasted. Providers don't owe extensions. They give what they give, and the honest response to a gift you didn't generate from nothing is thanks, not entitlement — whether the gift is still in your hand or it just walked out the door.

**Today:** Make a short list of three things at work you've been treating as owed to you — an account, a title, a level of respect, a raise you assumed was coming. Next to each one, write: *I received this. I didn't manufacture it alone.* Say it to your business partner, spouse, or a trusted friend today instead of keeping it in your own head.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've been keeping score like I built everything myself, and it's turned a real loss into resentment instead of grief I could actually process. Show me what was Yours to give all along. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
