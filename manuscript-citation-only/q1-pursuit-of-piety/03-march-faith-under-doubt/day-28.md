# Day 28 — The Faith That Gets Handed Down

Every Wednesday for two years, Cade dropped his two kids at the parish hall for religious education and drove to the coffee shop across the lot to answer emails for fifty-five minutes. It felt responsible. He'd signed them up, paid the registration fee, showed up on time, sat through the parent orientation night. In his head, the faith-formation column of his fatherhood was checked off — handled by people more qualified than him, on a night that ran itself without requiring anything further from him.

His wife asked at dinner one night what they'd learned that week. Both kids shrugged. The older one said something about Noah, maybe, or was that last month. Cade realized he didn't know either — he'd never once asked on the drive home, and neither had they volunteered it, because in two years nobody in the family had made a habit of talking about any of it outside that one hour in that one room.

Cade had been running on a belief he'd never said out loud, not even to himself: *I've provided the class, so the faith is being handed down.* Enrollment isn't formation. A fifty-five-minute hour led by a volunteer catechist, however good she is, was never going to do the job Cade had handed it without ever discussing the terms — raise his kids in the faith on his behalf while he waited in a coffee shop.

Moses gives Israel the actual job description, and it isn't a program. In Deuteronomy 6:6-7, he tells them to keep these commands on their own hearts first, then teach them to their children constantly — talking about them at home, on the road, before bed, and first thing in the morning. Sitting in the house. Walking on the road. Lying down. Rising up. That's every ordinary hour of an ordinary day, named on purpose, because the handoff was never meant to happen in a classroom. It happens in a car, at a dinner table, at a bedside, in the fifteen seconds between "goodnight" and the door closing.

Cade still drives his kids to religious ed on Wednesdays. That hasn't changed, and it shouldn't — good instruction is worth having. What changed is what he no longer believes it's carrying by itself. The class can teach the words. Only Cade can teach his kids that the words matter enough for their father to bring them up unprompted, on a random Tuesday, for no reason except that he wanted to.

**Today:** At dinner or in the car this week, bring up something about your faith without being asked — a verse, a saint, a question you're wrestling with. Say to your kids, plainly: *This matters enough that I want to talk about it with you, not just drop you off for it.* Then actually ask what they learned, and listen to the answer.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've handed off what was mine to carry and called it handled. Give me the words and the habit to teach my children Yourself, in the ordinary hours of an ordinary week, not just on the nights it's someone else's job. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

DEUTERONOMY 6:6-7
