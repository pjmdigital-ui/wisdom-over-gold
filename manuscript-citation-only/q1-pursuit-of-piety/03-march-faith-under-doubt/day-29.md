# Day 29 — Ninety Days In

Piers spent Saturday morning doing what he does every quarter close: pulling the numbers, building the deck, figuring out what actually happened to the business over the last ninety days versus what he'd told the team would happen. Revenue up eight percent. Two accounts lost he didn't see coming. A hire that worked out, a process that didn't. He's good at this kind of honest accounting — it's half of why the business runs.

On a whim, before he closed the laptop, he pulled out the notebook he'd been scribbling sentences into most mornings since New Year's — the one-line beliefs, the prayers, the pages this book had him fill in one at a time since January. He flipped back to the first entries expecting to find, if he's honest, evidence of a different man. Instead he found himself still writing some version of the same struggles in March that he'd written in January: still short with his wife more nights than he'd like, still skipping the morning prayer when the week got loud, still catching himself chasing the wrong things before he caught himself.

*What do I actually have to show for ninety days of this,* he thought, closing the notebook harder than he meant to. That question is the belief worth naming: *real change should be dramatic and finished by now, or it isn't real change at all.* Piers applies quarterly targets to his soul the same way he applies them to the business — expecting a clean number that proves the quarter worked.

But Piers's business took years to become what it is, built quarter over quarter, most of them unremarkable on their own. He'd never once judged a single quarter's numbers as the final verdict on the company. He extended it patience he wasn't extending himself. St. Paul writes to a church he loved with a confidence that has nothing to do with how far along they were. In Philippians 1:6, he tells them he's certain that God, who started the good work in them, will carry it all the way to completion by the time Christ returns. Notice who's doing the finishing. Not Piers, ninety days in, grading himself against a standard he invented. God — over a timeline that runs to the day of Christ's return, not to the end of a fiscal quarter.

The notebook was proof of exactly what ninety days of real work looks like from the inside — not a finished man, a man still being worked on, noticing the same patterns faster than he used to and turning back toward God more often than he did in January, even if not every day. That is the completion in progress, not the absence of it.

**Today:** Reread your own list of truths — the one you started keeping back in January. Add one new line: *God isn't finished with me, and ninety days was never the deadline.* Then keep going tomorrow like you mean it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, ninety days in, I want a finished man to show for it, and I know that was never the promise. Thank You for the work You've already done in me that I can't fully see yet, and for staying faithful to finish it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
