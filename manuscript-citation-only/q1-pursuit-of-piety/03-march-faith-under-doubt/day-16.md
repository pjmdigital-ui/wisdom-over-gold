# Day 16 — Counting What You Actually Have

Back in January you started running a five-minute Examen most nights — walking back through the day, naming where God showed up, where you turned from Him, what tomorrow needs. Some nights you've kept it. Plenty of nights you haven't, and last night was one of the misses — you scrolled instead, ran your usual inventory of what you don't have, and went to sleep without once naming what you do.

Tonight, sit down and run it, but with a specific edit: instead of just reviewing the day generally, count. Out loud or on paper, name ten things God has truly given you — not vague ones, specific ones. The coffee that was hot this morning. The kid who hugged you without being asked. The job that's stable even on the days it's boring. The knees that still let you kneel down at Mass. You'll find, if you're honest, that you can get to ten without much effort, and that most of them never once made your mental list of things worth noticing.

That's worth sitting with. Somewhere along the way you'd started operating as if gratitude were a mood that shows up on its own when life happens to feel good — something you'd feel if it came, not something you'd go looking for on purpose. It doesn't work that way, any more than the Examen worked on the nights you skipped it because you didn't feel like doing it. Gratitude, like faith itself, is a discipline you practice whether or not it's arrived on its own that day. The nights you don't count what you have are the nights what you have goes invisible — not because it's gone, but because nobody went looking.

The psalmist doesn't leave this to memory or mood. He commands his own soul: "Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits" (Psalm 103:2, RSV-CE). Forget not. That's an instruction aimed at a mind that forgets by default, the same mind that can recite a stranger's boat trip from three nights ago but can't name what it was given that same morning. The psalmist knew gratitude has to be spoken and counted on purpose, or it slips.

You've spent enough nights this month running the tally of what's missing. Tonight, run the other one — deliberately, out loud, specific — and let it be the last thing you do before you sleep instead of the thing you never get around to.

**Today:** Before bed tonight, name ten specific things God has given you — said out loud, not just thought. Write them down if you can. Then say: *Counting what I have is how I keep from forgetting it.* Make this part of your nightly Examen going forward, not a one-time exercise.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for how easily I forget what You've already given me while I chase what I don't have. Tonight I bless You and I choose to remember, specifically and on purpose, every good gift already in my hands. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
