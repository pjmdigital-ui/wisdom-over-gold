# Day 1 — The Doubt You're Not Allowed to Have

Tuesday night, church basement, folding chairs in a circle. Eight guys, coffee in styrofoam cups, going around for prayer requests and wins before the study starts. Rhys has done this every week for three years. He knows how it goes. Somebody's kid made the team. Somebody's dad had a rough scan. Somebody's marriage is better than it was in January.

When it got to him, Rhys said his job was good. He said his wife and kids were good. He asked for prayers for a coworker's mother. All of that was true. None of it was what was really sitting in his chest.

Eight months earlier, his uncle Nick died of pancreatic cancer. He was fifty-nine. He died just three months after his diagnosis, while half the parish prayed a novena for him the whole way through. Rhys prayed that novena himself, every night, and he meant it. Nick died anyway, fast and hard. Some nights since then, Rhys lies awake wondering if any of it does anything at all. The rosary. The Masses offered up. All the prayer. He wonders if he has just been talking into silence and calling it God. He has never said that sentence out loud. Not to his wife. Not to these seven men who would probably just tell him to pray harder.

*A man who really believes doesn't wonder if any of it's real.* That's the rule Rhys has been living by, even though nobody ever taught it to him in those words. He picked it up somewhere. Maybe from a confident uncle. Maybe from a youth minister who never once looked unsure. Somewhere along the way he decided doubt is what happens to men whose faith wasn't strong enough to begin with. So he locks his doubt away and calls the rest of himself a believer.

Scripture doesn't back up his rule. Mark tells the story of a father whose son is very sick. The boy convulses and gets thrown into fire and water, and the father has run out of strength to pretend everything is fine. In Mark 9:24, he cries out to Jesus that he believes, and in the same breath he admits part of him still doesn't. He begs Jesus to help him with that part too. Jesus doesn't ask him to sort out the contradiction first. He heals the boy right there, on the strength of a faith that's still arguing with itself.

That's the part Rhys's rule leaves out. Belief and doubt sat together in the same sentence that day, spoken by the same worn-out father. Jesus didn't wait for the sentence to settle before He acted. That father is the model, not the exception. A faith that has to feel certain before it counts isn't the faith the Gospels describe.

**Today:** Write down the specific doubt you've been keeping out of the go-around. Write the real sentence, not a softened version. Underneath it, write: *God can hold my doubt and my belief in the same sentence. I don't have to resolve one before I bring Him the other.* Say both sentences out loud once, alone, before you go to bed tonight.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I have been carrying a doubt I haven't let You near because I was afraid of what it meant about my faith. Meet me in it the way You met the father in Mark's Gospel, and let my believing and my doubting both belong to You tonight. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

MARK 9:24
