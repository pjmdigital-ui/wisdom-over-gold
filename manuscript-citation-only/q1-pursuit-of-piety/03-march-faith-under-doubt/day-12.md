# Day 12 — Doubt in the Company of Believers

Wednesday mornings, six of us sit around a folding table before work and go around the circle with prayer requests. Mostly it's safe stuff: a sick parent, a job interview, patience with the kids. Two weeks ago it was my turn, and what I really wanted to say was that I'd been lying awake wondering if God hears any of this, or if I've just gotten good at talking to a silence I've decided to call prayer.

I got two words into it — "honestly, lately" — and stopped. I felt five sets of eyes shift toward me, and something in my chest slammed the door shut. I finished the sentence with, "lately work's been busy, pray I get some rest this week." Safe. True enough. Not the real thing.

Driving to the office afterward, I could hear the sentence I hadn't said, still fully formed, still sitting there. Underneath it was another sentence I'd never once said out loud to myself: *if I say this, they'll think I'm the one falling apart, not the one holding it together.* I'd built a reputation in that group as steady, dependable, the guy others lean on. Some part of me had decided that reputation couldn't survive an honest sentence about doubt. So I carried it alone instead. Keep the reputation, keep the weight, tell no one. That's a strange trade.

St. James doesn't leave room for that trade. Writing to believers, he tells them in James 5:16 to confess their sins to each other and pray for each other so they can be healed. He adds that the prayer of a good man carries real power. Notice the order. Healing comes after confessing to one another, not after working it out alone in the car. James assumes struggle brought into the room among believers is where the power is. Not struggle managed privately and presented to the group only once it's resolved and safe to admit.

I'd had it backwards. I thought bringing the doubt to the table would make me the weak one. James says the opposite. The doubt kept in silence is the one that grows teeth. The doubt spoken among men who pray for each other in earnest is the one that starts losing its grip.

**Today:** Go back to the person or group you held something back from — this week if you can, today if you're able — and say the real sentence you swallowed last time. Not the polished version. The unguarded one. Then write and say: *Bringing this into the room is how it gets healed, not how I get exposed.* Let someone pray for you out loud about it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've been carrying something alone that I was afraid to bring into the room with men I trust, thinking silence would protect me. Give me the courage to speak the honest sentence instead of the safe one, and let their prayer do what my silence never could. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

JAMES 5:16
