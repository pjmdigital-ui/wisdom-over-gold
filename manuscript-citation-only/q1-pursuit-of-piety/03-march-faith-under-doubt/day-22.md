# Day 22 — What Monica Prayed For

I keep a page in the back of my prayer journal with my brother's name on it. I started it eleven years ago, after he stopped coming to Mass and stopped taking my calls about it. I've prayed for him almost every night since — for his marriage, for his kids, for whatever it is that keeps him circling the Church without ever walking back through the door. Flipping back through those pages recently, I noticed the entries have gotten shorter. Less detail. Fewer new things to say. If I'm honest, some nights I'm not sure I still believe the prayer is doing anything.

That's the quiet conclusion I've landed on without ever quite deciding it: *if eleven years of praying for him hasn't produced a visible change, either the prayer isn't working or I'm not the one God's going to use to answer it.* Either way, the logic says, stop expecting a result and just keep it as a private habit, low-stakes, low-hope.

Jesus tells a parable in Luke 18:1 meant to teach that His followers should keep praying and never give up — a widow who keeps showing up before an unjust judge, refusing to quit, until persistence alone moves him. Jesus doesn't promise the judge will rule quickly. He promises the widow keeps showing up anyway, and that persistence is itself the point of the story, not a footnote to it. Not losing heart is a decision, made over and over, long after the feeling that used to fuel it has run out.

**On St. Monica:** Monica prayed for her son's soul for close to two decades — through his years chasing status and pleasure, through a heretical sect he joined and left, through a mother's tears a bishop once told her God would not ignore. She didn't get to see most of it resolved in her own lifetime the way she'd hoped. She died not long after her son's baptism, having spent almost her whole adult life on a prayer that looked, for most of those years, like it wasn't working. Monica is a patron for this kind of prayer precisely because her answer took so long — her son became St. Augustine, one of the towering figures of the Church, and none of it happened on a timeline she could see from where she was standing.

**Today:** Write your brother's, your son's, your friend's name — whoever's soul you've been praying for without visible progress — at the top of a page, the way you did the first year, with real detail instead of a shorthand plea. Pray for him tonight like it's still year one, because it is still a story God is writing, whether or not you're allowed to see the ending.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let a decade of silence talk me into praying smaller and expecting less. Give me Monica's patience — keep me showing up for the person I love even when nothing visible changes. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

LUKE 18:1
