# Day 24 — The Prayers You'll Never See Answered

I pray for my kids every night before bed — short prayers, mostly the same words, asking God to keep their faith strong long after they've left my house. Last week I caught myself mid-sentence asking God to let me *see* it — see them raising their own kids in the Church, see the payoff of every rosary said in the car and every dinner-table conversation about God I forced when nobody wanted to have it. I stopped. That word, "see," had slipped in from somewhere else entirely.

It's the language I use at work — track the input, measure the output, confirm the return before you call the investment a success. I'd started praying for my children's faith the same way I run a project: expecting to be there for the reveal, expecting the numbers to close out in my column before I'm willing to call the effort worthwhile. Underneath the prayer was a belief I'd never said out loud: *faithfulness only counts if I get to watch it pay off.*

St. Paul describes his own ministry differently to a church he helped start. In 1 Corinthians 3:6, he says he did the planting, Apollos did the watering, and God was the one who made anything actually grow. Notice what's missing from that sentence. Paul doesn't say he stayed to watch the harvest. He planted. Someone else watered. The growth — the part that actually matters — belonged to God on a timeline neither of them controlled or witnessed in full. Paul's job ended at the planting. He didn't need the reveal to know the work was real.

I may not be alive to see my grandchildren's confirmations. I may not be around for the conversation, twenty years from now, where one of my kids tells their own child why faith matters to them. The actual deal was always just the planting — the dinner-table conversations, the rosary in the car, the example of a man who went to confession and meant it. Someone else waters what I plant. God grows what gets watered. My name doesn't have to be on the harvest for the planting to have mattered.

**Today:** Write one blessing, by hand, for a child or grandchild you may not live to see grown — a specific hope for their faith, not a vague wish. Tuck it into a Bible or a box they'll find someday. Pray over it once, then let it go: *I planted this. The growth is God's to give, on His timeline, not mine.*

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've been praying for a harvest I expect to see with my own eyes, and I need to let that go. Let me plant faithfully and trust You with everything that grows after I'm gone. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

1 CORINTHIANS 3:6
