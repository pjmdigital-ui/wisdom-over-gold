# Day 25 — Gratitude for the Faith You Didn't Earn

Merrick keeps a spreadsheet. Not for money — for everything. Workouts logged, sales calls tracked, even a column for "prayer" with a checkbox he fills in most mornings. He built the business that way, one careful row at a time, and it worked. So when his daughter asked him at dinner why he believed in God, his first instinct was to answer like it was a line item: because I show up, because I put in the work, because I've earned it.

He caught himself mid-sentence and didn't finish it. He wasn't sure the sentence was true.

Merrick runs his faith the way he runs his business: as an output of effort. Skip enough mornings and the account feels overdrawn. String together a good streak and he feels, however he'd never say it out loud, a little more entitled to God's attention than the guy who doesn't check the box. It's the same belief that built his business, aimed at something a spreadsheet was never built to hold: *my standing with God is a number I'm keeping current.*

St. Paul spent the first half of his life certain his own record was exactly that: a ledger he could point to. Then he met the risen Christ on the road to Damascus and spent the rest of his life unlearning that math. What he landed on, writing to a church he founded, cuts the legs out from under every version of Merrick's spreadsheet. In Ephesians 2:8-9, Paul tells them plainly that they were saved by grace, through faith, and that none of it came from themselves. It's a gift from God, not a reward for anything they did, precisely so nobody can take credit for it. Paul says grace covers all of it, not part of it. Nobody gets a discount for being disciplined, and nobody earns extra credit for a good streak. It is gift, all the way down, so that nobody — not the man with the perfect streak, not the man who missed a month — gets to point at his own effort as the reason he's standing there.

That should be a relief, not a letdown. Merrick's faith was never a debt he's paying off with better behavior. It was never owed to him either, which means it was never his to lose by missing a checkbox, and never his to boast about by hitting one. The discipline still matters, but somebody handed him the room he's standing in, and being thankful is the only honest response to a gift you didn't earn. Not pride in your own effort, not worry about your streak.

**Today:** Say out loud, before you open the spreadsheet or the to-do list: *This faith is a gift, not a wage I earned. I didn't buy my way into it, and I can't buy my way out of needing it.* Then thank God for it specifically — not for what you did this week, for the fact that you believe at all.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've treated my faith like something I earn one disciplined morning at a time, and I know that's not what You gave me. Thank You for a gift I didn't buy and can't lose by missing a day. Keep me grateful instead of proud, and grateful instead of anxious. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

EPHESIANS 2:8-9
