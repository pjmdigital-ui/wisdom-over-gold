Why I Wrote This Book

I was cynical about religion for most of my life. I made a lot of stupid decisions instead — the kind that landed me in federal prison at twenty years old.

I technically prayed my whole life. But the God I believed in was an idea I'd built myself, distant and impersonal. It took almost dying three separate times, watching ordinary faithful men live out a version of Christianity I'd never actually seen, and finally admitting we're living inside real spiritual warfare, whether anyone acknowledges it or not — before that changed.

Since I let that faith take root for real, I've watched God work in my life in ways I can't explain away. And I kept coming back to one truth: living out my faith well comes down to four pursuits I'm never finished with — I'm just always in them. That's what this book is built around.

— Paul Mascetta

Most Devotionals Weren't Built for Guys Like Us

— Checking your bank balance at 2am, running the numbers again, hoping they add up differently this time.
— Losing your temper with your kid and telling yourself it's just how much you care.
— Meaning to call your closest friend for months, and never actually picking up the phone.
— Tying your worth to a number on a scoreboard — revenue, title, deal size — that never sits still long enough to satisfy you.
— Wanting to lead your household well and having no idea, most days, what that actually looks like.

Everything Runs Through Four Pursuits

Pursuit I

Piety

Seeking God first — ordering your life around Him instead of yourself.

Pursuit II

Protection

Guarding your wife, your children, and the people who trust you.

Pursuit III

Provision

Providing real value and real security for the people who depend on you.

Pursuit IV

Posterity

Building something that outlasts you and leaves the world better for it.

Questions

Do I need to read it in order?

No. Each day stands on its own, though the four pursuits build on each other across the year if you read straight through.

Is this only for Catholic men?

It's written from a Catholic perspective, with the Church's saints and sacraments as reference points — but the four pursuits apply to any man trying to lead his family and keep his word.
