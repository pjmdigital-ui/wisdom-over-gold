# Instagram captions

One caption per post, meant to go in the caption field under the image (not on the card itself). Standard CTA line stays consistent across every post — same move as the reference account, which reuses its CTA nearly word-for-word every time so it becomes a habit for followers rather than something they have to notice fresh each post.

**Standard CTA (use as the last line on every post):**
> Comment "Amen" if this hit home, and send it to a man who needs to hear it today.

## The Ache Underneath

*(`instagram-post-hook-lesson.html`)*

There's a kind of ache that shows up even on your best days — good marriage, healthy kids, business finally steady — and it doesn't make sense until you realize it was never meant to be solved by any of those things. You can fix your whole life, one piece at a time, and it'll still be there. It's not pointing to a problem. It's pointing to God. Only He fits in the space it's pointing at.

## A Covenant With His Eyes

*(`instagram-post-covenant-eyes.html`)*

Willpower always seems to fail at the worst possible moment — because that's the one moment you're least equipped to decide anything well. The men who actually hold the line under real pressure aren't relying on willpower in the moment of testing. They already decided, days or weeks earlier, on a boring afternoon with nothing on the line. That's what a covenant is: a decision made in advance, so it's already settled by the time the test shows up.

## Anxious About Money

*(`instagram-post-anxious-about-money.html`)*

Money anxiety has a way of hitting hardest at 2am — running the same numbers over and over, like enough repetition might finally change what they add up to. It won't, and it was never supposed to be on you to make it add up alone. Do the honest work in daylight: the budget, the plan, the next step. Tonight, let God carry the part that was always His to carry.

## The Anxious Search

*(`instagram-post-anxious-search.html`)*

You think that edge in your voice after a scare means you're failing as a father. But God isn't asking you to feel less. St. Joseph wasn't calm for three days searching Jerusalem for a missing twelve-year-old Jesus — he was terrified, and God didn't ask him to stop feeling it. He asked him to keep searching until he found his son. Let your own fear sound like love instead of an edge, and tell your kid plainly what finding them means to you.
