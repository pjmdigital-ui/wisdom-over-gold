Example — hook, lesson, hope

A blunt headline earns the thumb-stop, the gold line names the real teaching, and the close resolves it forward instead of just naming the problem — same energy as the testimony-style closers that do well in faith content, kept in our own black/gold voice instead of copying that post's white/yellow "notes app" look.

Ever feel an ache under your ribs on a good day?

You can solve your marriage, your finances, your health, one at a time, completely — and it stays exactly where it was.

Only God fits there.

That's not a hole to fill. That's a compass — pointing you somewhere only God can take you.

Amen.

What changed, and why

  • The close is more forward-looking now — "an invitation, already open" was quiet; "a compass, pointing you somewhere only God can take you" gives the reader real forward motion, the way a good testimony does.
  • "Amen." closes it like the book does — every day in Seek First ends its prayer the same way; it's a small, quiet, on-brand sign-off instead of a shouted CTA.
  • Light at the end of the tunnel, literally — a soft warm glow sits behind that closing line, the one bit of light on an otherwise flat black card.
  • No name, no day, no citation — footer just reads "Seek First." A verse reference or "Day 7" tag means nothing to someone who hasn't read the book.
  • A blunt headline replaces the quote-card — heavy sans-serif, left-aligned, sized to fill the frame, in the flat "stop-scrolling" register people actually see in a feed.
  • Two typefaces, two jobs — sans for the hook (immediate, digital), serif for the lesson (unhurried, book-voiced) — so the shift in register from grab to teaching is felt, not just read.
