Example — hook + lesson post

A blunt, modern headline earns the thumb-stop; the gold serif payoff underneath is the actual teaching, so a scroller still walks away with something even if they never open the caption.

Ever feel an ache under your ribs on a good day?

You can solve your marriage, your finances, your health, one at a time, completely — and it stays exactly where it was.

Only God fits there.

Seek First

What changed, and why

  • No name, no day, no citation — footer now reads just "Seek First." A verse reference or "Day 7" tag means nothing to someone who hasn't read the book.
  • A blunt headline replaces the quote-card — heavy sans-serif, left-aligned, sized to fill the frame, in the flat "stop-scrolling" register people actually see in a feed.
  • The question mark became the art — an oversized ghosted glyph gives the card a focal point without a photo or stock graphic.
  • The lesson still lands even without a caption — the gold serif block underneath is a complete two-beat teaching: the setup, then the line worth screenshotting.
  • Two typefaces, two jobs — sans for the hook (immediate, digital), serif for the lesson (unhurried, book-voiced) — so the shift in register from grab to teaching is felt, not just read.
