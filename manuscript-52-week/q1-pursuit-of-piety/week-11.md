# Week 11 — Thankful in the Waiting

Gratitude is easy when the thing you wanted finally happens.

The call comes.

The numbers improve.

The test result is good.

The prayer is answered the way you asked.

Then gratitude feels almost automatic.

The harder question is whether thanksgiving has any place before the situation resolves.

Habakkuk gives us one of Scripture's strongest pictures of that kind of gratitude. He imagines the visible supports of life failing—the crops, the harvest, the animals—and still chooses to rejoice in God.

He does not deny the losses first.

That is what makes the passage useful.

Biblical gratitude is not pretending you are less disappointed than you are.

It is refusing to let one unresolved thing make every remaining gift invisible.

That happens easily.

A man is worried about work, and suddenly the good marriage becomes background.

A child is struggling, and everything else in the household begins feeling dark.

Money is tight, and the fact that everyone is healthy becomes difficult to notice.

One painful reality expands until it fills the entire field of view.

Gratitude narrows it back to truth.

This is hard **and** God has still given me good things.

I am waiting **and** I am not abandoned.

I am disappointed **and** I can still thank Him.

Both can be true.

That is different from forced positivity.

Forced positivity says the loss should not matter.

Gratitude says the loss does not get to erase everything else that matters too.

Psalm 103 tells the soul not to forget God's benefits. That command assumes forgetting is possible.

It is.

Especially when you are hurting.

Gratitude therefore has to become deliberate before it becomes emotional.

You may not feel particularly thankful when you begin naming what remains good.

Name it anyway.

Your wife.

Your children.

A friend who answered the phone.

A roof.

Work you still have.

A body still capable of carrying you.

The sacraments.

Another morning.

The fact that God is still God while the problem remains unresolved.

The practice is not designed to make the problem disappear.

It prevents the problem from becoming the only story you can tell.

That matters spiritually because waiting can become a kind of tunnel vision.

The thing you lack receives all your attention while what you already have becomes ordinary.

Gratitude breaks that pattern.

It reminds you that God's goodness is not measured only by whether He gives the next thing you want.

Sometimes the most mature thanksgiving is offered while you are still asking Him for something else.

### This Week

Set aside ten minutes once this week.

Name the unresolved thing plainly first.

Then write down ten specific gifts that are still true while you wait.

Thank God for each one without adding a condition.

If one of those gifts is a person, consider telling that person you are grateful for him or her.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, I let the thing I am waiting for become so large that I stop seeing what You have already given me. Teach me to be grateful without pretending disappointment is not real. Keep my losses from erasing Your goodness in my mind, and make thanksgiving part of the way I wait. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**HABAKKUK 3:17–18**
