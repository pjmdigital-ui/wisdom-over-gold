# Week 2 — What Are You Actually Seeking?

One of the easiest ways to discover what you are seeking is to look at what gets your attention before you have time to explain yourself.

Think about the first few minutes after you wake up.

What do you reach for?

For a lot of men, the answer is the phone.

Before prayer, before Scripture, sometimes before speaking to the person lying beside us, we check messages, email, news, numbers, notifications, schedules, and whatever happened while we were asleep.

The habit seems small.

It is still revealing.

Psalm 63 describes a soul that seeks God early because it thirsts for Him. The point is not that holiness can be measured by whether you touch a phone before 7:00 a.m. The point is that desire leaves evidence.

We move toward what we want.

And most men have competing wants.

We want God.

We also want control.

We want peace, but we also want to know what everyone is saying about us.

We want to trust, but we also want to check the account again.

We want prayer, but we also want the quick stimulation of whatever the screen is offering.

This is why the question is not simply, **“Do I believe in God?”**

The more useful question is:

**“What am I actually seeking?”**

Your attention is one of the clearest answers.

Where does your mind go when nobody directs it?

What do you think about while driving?

What do you check repeatedly?

What outcome occupies your imagination?

What loss are you afraid of?

What would make you feel as though you had finally arrived?

Those questions do not automatically reveal sin. They reveal desire.

And desire needs to be ordered just as much as behavior does.

A man can do many outwardly correct things while his interior life is chasing something else entirely.

He can go to Mass while spending the entire hour thinking about work.

He can pray for God's will while already having decided which outcome he will accept.

He can say money is not everything while checking the number constantly because the number has become a verdict on whether he is winning.

That is why Piety begins inside before it becomes visible outside.

God is not only interested in whether you can produce Christian behavior.

He wants the heart.

That sounds obvious until you notice how much easier it is to manage behavior than desire.

You can force yourself to put the phone down.

The deeper work is asking why you feel compelled to pick it up in the first place.

Maybe you are afraid something important happened without you.

Maybe work has become the place you get your worth.

Maybe the silence of the morning makes you uncomfortable.

Maybe checking everything creates a temporary feeling of control.

Whatever the reason, it is worth naming.

The goal is not to become suspicious of every desire.

It is to stop living as though your strongest desires are automatically trustworthy.

The heart can be trained.

Attention can be redirected.

A man who repeatedly turns toward God begins changing what he reaches for naturally.

Not overnight.

Through repetition.

That is one reason the morning matters. The first movement of the day does not determine the whole day, but it establishes an order.

Before the world tells you what needs your attention, you remember who deserves it first.

Before the email tells you what is urgent, you remember what is important.

Before the numbers tell you how secure you are, you remember where your security actually rests.

It may take sixty seconds.

That is enough to begin.

### This Week

Put your phone somewhere you cannot reach it automatically from bed.

Before checking email, messages, news, or social media each morning, say one short prayer:

**“Lord, I want You before I want anything else.”**

Then sit with that sentence long enough to mean it.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, show me what I am actually seeking beneath the things I say I value. Redirect my attention when it begins attaching itself to control, approval, money, distraction, or anything else that tries to become first. Teach my heart to thirst for You and make my first movement toward You more natural through faithful repetition. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**PSALM 63:1**
