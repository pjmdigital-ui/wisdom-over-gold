# Week 3 — God Before the Noise

Prayer usually does not disappear because a man decided it no longer matters.

It disappears because everything else feels louder.

There is an email waiting.

A child needs something.

The calendar is full.

Work starts early.

You are already behind.

And prayer has one disadvantage in that competition: it rarely screams.

It does not arrive with a notification.

It does not usually have a deadline attached.

Nobody from work calls because you failed to sit with God that morning.

So the urgent things keep winning.

Luke gives us the familiar scene of Martha and Mary. Martha is busy serving. Mary is sitting at the feet of Jesus. Martha's work is not pointless. She is doing something useful, even hospitable.

The problem is not service.

The problem is that legitimate activity has become agitation, and the agitation is making it difficult to recognize what matters most in the room.

Jesus does not praise laziness.

He restores order.

That is the challenge for a modern man because we can build entire identities around being useful.

We solve.

We answer.

We produce.

We handle things.

Then we bring that same instinct into the spiritual life and feel uncomfortable when prayer does not seem productive.

Nothing got checked off.

No problem got solved.

No visible result appeared.

But relationship with God is not a task you complete so that you can move on to the real work.

It is the place from which the rest of the work is supposed to be done.

If prayer gets only what remains after the day's demands have taken everything else, it will eventually receive almost nothing.

That is why making time for God often requires giving Him time before you know whether you can “afford” it.

You will rarely reach a point in a busy season where the calendar announces that you now have abundant extra space for prayer.

You have to protect it.

That can feel irresponsible at first.

There are things to do.

But ten or fifteen minutes given to God before the day accelerates is not neglecting your responsibilities. It can be the very thing that helps you enter them as the right man.

Prayer changes the order before the pressure starts.

It reminds you that the business is not God.

The problem is not God.

Your anxiety is not God.

The opinion waiting in your inbox is not God.

And you are not God either.

That last one matters.

A great deal of what we call busyness is tied to the belief that everything depends on us.

If I stop, something falls apart.

If I do not answer now, everything backs up.

If I am not constantly available, I am failing.

Prayer interrupts that belief.

You sit down and, by sitting down, admit that the world continues for a few minutes without your management.

That is not laziness.

It is humility.

And if your mind runs the entire time, pray distracted.

If the to-do list keeps appearing, return.

If you spend half the time pulling your attention back, that still counts as time given to God.

The goal is not to become instantly serene.

The goal is to put yourself in His presence before everything else starts making claims on you.

### This Week

Choose one specific period of time each day this week that belongs to God before your phone, email, social media, or work.

Keep it realistic enough that you will actually do it.

Ten faithful minutes are better than an elaborate plan you abandon after two days.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, I let urgency crowd You out too easily. Teach me to give You time before the day's demands decide what matters. Help me sit in Your presence even when my mind is busy and prayer feels unproductive. Put my responsibilities in the right order and remind me that everything does not depend on me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**LUKE 10:41–42**
