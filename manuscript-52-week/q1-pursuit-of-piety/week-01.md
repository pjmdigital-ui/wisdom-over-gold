# Week 1 — Seek First

Matthew 6:33 is the verse this entire book is built around.

Jesus tells us to seek God's Kingdom first. The word **first** matters. It does not mean eventually, after the work is finished, after the bills are paid, after the kids are settled, after the phone stops buzzing, or after life becomes easier to manage.

First means first.

That is simple to understand and difficult to live.

A man can believe in God, attend Mass, pray, love his family, work hard, and still build a life in which God is functionally somewhere in the middle. He may never deny God. He simply keeps allowing everything else to get there first.

Work gets the first attention.

Money gets the first worry.

The phone gets the first look in the morning.

The problem gets the first mental energy.

The ambition gets the first sacrifice.

Then, once everything else has taken what it wants, God receives what is left.

None of those other things has to be bad for the order to be wrong.

That is important because most of the things competing with God in a man's life are not obvious sins. They are often good things: providing for your family, building a business, staying healthy, caring about your reputation, wanting your children to succeed, wanting financial security.

The danger is not that you care about them.

The danger is that they become the center around which everything else begins to orbit.

Seeking first does not mean becoming irresponsible toward the rest of your life. Jesus is not telling you to stop working, stop planning, stop providing, or stop caring about the people entrusted to you.

He is telling you to put those things in the right order.

That is what Piety means in this book.

Not simply performing religious practices. Not looking spiritual. Not knowing enough Catholic terminology to sound convincing.

Piety is ordering the whole life toward God.

That changes the question you bring to ordinary decisions.

Instead of asking only, **“Will this work?”** you also ask, **“Does this honor God?”**

Instead of asking only, **“Can I afford this?”** you ask, **“Is this good stewardship?”**

Instead of asking only, **“Will this make me successful?”** you ask, **“What kind of man will this require me to become?”**

Instead of asking only, **“What do I want?”** you begin asking, **“What is God asking of me?”**

That does not make decisions easier every time.

It gives them a center.

And a man needs a center because life will keep pulling him in different directions.

The Four Pursuits in this book—Piety, Protection, Provision, and Posterity—are all good. You should protect. You should provide. You should build something worth handing down.

But none of those things can carry the weight of becoming your god.

Provision without Piety can become greed or obsession.

Protection without Piety can become control.

Posterity without Piety can become vanity: a man trying to make his own name survive him.

Piety puts all three under God.

It reminds you that your family is His before it is yours. Your money is His before it is yours. Your abilities are His before they are yours. Your life itself is something you received before it became something you could manage.

That is why this pursuit comes first.

And it is why the practice is not something you complete once.

You will put God first and then drift.

You will recognize the drift and return.

You will have a good week of prayer and then wake up one morning with your mind already at work before your feet hit the floor.

You will remember what matters, forget, and remember again.

That is not permission to stay careless.

It is a reason not to confuse the Christian life with one dramatic decision that permanently fixes the order of everything.

Seeking first is a direction.

You keep choosing it.

### This Week

Take an honest inventory of what receives the most attention, energy, worry, and sacrifice in your life right now.

Do not answer with what you believe should be first. Look at the evidence.

Then identify one concrete change that would put God ahead of something that has been getting there first.

Make that change this week.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, I have allowed good things to become disordered more often than I want to admit. Teach me to seek You first—not eventually, not after everything else has taken its share, but first. Put my work, my family, my ambitions, my money, and my plans in their proper place beneath You. When I drift, make me quick to notice and willing to return. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**MATTHEW 6:33**
