# Week 4 — The God of Your Own Life

There is a version of responsibility that is not really responsibility anymore.

It is control.

The difference can be difficult to see because responsible men are supposed to plan, work, provide, make decisions, and think ahead. Scripture does not call us to passivity.

But somewhere in the process, a man can begin acting as though wise effort entitles him to a guaranteed outcome.

He plans the year.

He builds the budget.

He chooses the strategy.

He decides what the family should do.

And without saying it directly, he begins expecting life to cooperate.

Then something refuses.

The deal falls through.

A child makes a choice he did not approve.

The health problem appears.

The market changes.

Another person says no.

Suddenly the difference between responsibility and sovereignty becomes obvious.

Proverbs says that a man can plan his course while God establishes his steps. Another passage tells us to trust the Lord rather than leaning completely on our own understanding.

Those verses do not make planning useless.

They put planning in its proper place.

You can make the best decision available to you and still not control what follows.

That is uncomfortable for men who have built their lives around solving problems.

We like cause and effect.

Do the right work, get the right result.

Train correctly, improve.

Spend less, save more.

Make the call, close the deal.

Sometimes life works that way.

Sometimes it does not.

Faith begins to matter in a different way when the equation stops behaving.

There is a temptation in those moments to tighten your grip.

More research.

More checking.

More pressure.

More attempts to manage other people's reactions.

At some point, what began as diligence becomes the refusal to accept that part of the situation belongs to God.

St. Ignatius wrote about spiritual freedom: the ability to receive or release created things according to whether they help us toward God. That kind of freedom is hard to practice when you have already decided which outcome you need in order to be okay.

A man who cannot release an outcome will eventually be ruled by it.

That does not mean he stops caring.

Detachment is not indifference.

You can want the business to work, the child to choose wisely, the test to come back clean, and the marriage to heal.

You can pray specifically for all of those things.

The question is whether your faith survives if God does not give you the exact answer on the exact timeline you chose.

You have responsibilities.

You do not have sovereignty.

That sentence is not meant to make you smaller than you are.

It is meant to free you from carrying a job you were never given.

Your job is to discern, act, repent when necessary, adjust, and remain faithful.

God's job is everything you cannot control after that.

The more clearly you separate those two, the less energy you waste trying to force open doors that are not yours to open.

### This Week

Choose one situation you are currently trying to control.

Write two columns:

**What is mine to do?**  
**What is not mine to control?**

Act on the first column.

Then pray over the second without trying to turn prayer into another form of management.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, I confuse responsibility with control and carry outcomes You never asked me to carry. Give me wisdom to do what belongs to me and humility to release what belongs to You. Keep me diligent without making me controlling, and teach me to trust Your direction when my plan does not unfold the way I expected. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
