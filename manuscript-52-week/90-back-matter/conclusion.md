# A Final Word — Seek First

If you have made it through all fifty-two weeks, there is a temptation to treat this moment like a finish line.

It isn't one.

You have spent a year looking at Piety, Protection, Provision, and Posterity. You have examined what sits at the center of your life, how you respond under pressure, what you do with your work and money, how you love your wife, how you father your children, and what kind of man you are becoming.

You may have changed in some of those areas.

You may also recognize some of the same struggles you had when you began.

That does not mean the year failed.

It means you are still a man being formed.

St. Paul tells the Philippians that the God who began a good work in them would remain faithful to complete it. His confidence was not that they had finally become disciplined enough to finish themselves. His confidence was in the One doing the work.

That is an important way to end this book.

You are not finished.

Neither am I.

There are things we understand and still fail to live consistently. There are lessons we learn, forget, and have to learn again. There are mornings when God is clearly first and others when we realize halfway through the day that we have put ourselves right back at the center.

The goal was never perfection after fifty-two weeks.

The goal was direction.

A man can drift for years without noticing where he is headed. Then something gets his attention, and he begins turning back.

That turning matters.

Maybe you apologize faster than you used to.

Maybe you recognize anger before it controls the room.

Maybe you understand that providing for your family means more than earning money.

Maybe you pray when you once would have tried to carry everything yourself.

Maybe you notice when work, money, reputation, comfort, or control begins becoming more important than it should.

Maybe you simply come back to God faster.

That is not a small thing.

St. Augustine understood that the human heart remains restless when it tries to make created things carry the weight that belongs to God. Success can be good. Money can be good. Marriage, fatherhood, work, ambition, and achievement can all be good.

They become disordered when we ask them to tell us who we are.

That is why Matthew 6:33 has been underneath this entire book.

Seek God first.

Not only when life falls apart.

Not only when you are afraid.

Not only on Sunday.

First.

Then let everything else find its proper place.

That is Piety.

And when God is first, Protection becomes more than physical strength. It becomes integrity, patience, self-command, brotherhood, and the character that makes the people around you safer.

Provision becomes more than income. It becomes stewardship: your work, your money, your household, your time, your responsibilities, and the things God has placed in your hands.

Posterity becomes more than leaving behind possessions or a name. It becomes the faith, character, love, and example that continue working in people after you are gone.

The four pursuits were never meant to compete.

They belong together.

And none of them stays permanently in order.

That is why you will have to keep returning to the beginning.

There will be another difficult season.

Another temptation.

Another financial decision.

Another argument with your wife.

Another moment when your child needs patience and you do not feel particularly patient.

Another opportunity to tell the truth when a lie would be easier.

Another morning when prayer feels less urgent than everything waiting on your phone.

And in each of those moments, the question remains the same:

**What am I seeking first right now?**

Not what do I say matters most.

Not what did I decide six months ago.

What is first **now**?

Your calendar will answer.

Your attention will answer.

Your spending will answer.

Your reactions will answer.

The way you treat people when you're tired will answer.

And when you discover that the answer is not God, you do not need to declare the whole year a failure.

Return.

That may be one of the central habits of the Christian life.

Return when pride takes over.

Return when ambition becomes worship.

Return when anger wins.

Return when prayer disappears.

Return when money becomes security.

Return when your marriage becomes management.

Return when fatherhood becomes control.

Return when you become more concerned about your name than God's Kingdom.

Repentance is not evidence that the pursuit failed.

It is part of the pursuit.

You will never reach a point in this life where you no longer need grace.

That is not discouraging news.

It means your hope was never supposed to rest entirely on your ability to hold everything together.

The work God has begun in you is larger than one year.

So when you close this book, do not think of it as completing something.

Think of it as returning to ordinary life with a clearer question.

Tomorrow will not have a chapter waiting for you.

It will simply be another day.

Work.

Family.

Money.

Distraction.

Prayer.

Responsibility.

Opportunity.

Temptation.

Love.

And somewhere inside all of it, you will make decisions about what comes first.

Keep choosing.

When you get it wrong, choose again.

When you become restless, return to the One the restlessness was pointing toward all along.

When something good begins asking to become your god, put it back in its proper place.

And when you are unsure where to begin, begin exactly where this book began.

Seek first.

## Before You Close This Book

Write down one sentence answering this question:

**“What am I seeking first in my life right now?”**

Be honest.

Then write one more:

**“What would change if God were truly first here?”**

Keep those two sentences somewhere you can find them again.

Not so you can grade yourself.

So you can return.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, thank You for every way You have challenged, corrected, strengthened, and carried me through this year. I know I am not finished, and I know I cannot finish this work in myself. Keep doing what You have begun in me. When I drift, turn me back. When pride takes over, humble me. When fear takes over, remind me who holds my life. Teach me to seek Your Kingdom first in my faith, my character, my work, my marriage, my fatherhood, and everything I leave behind. Keep drawing my restless heart home for every day You give me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**MATTHEW 6:33**
