# Week 24 — The Brotherhood That Costs Something

Friendship can remain shallow for years if nobody ever asks it to cost anything.

You talk sports.

Business.

Politics.

Training.

You send jokes, see each other at events, and genuinely enjoy the relationship.

There is nothing wrong with that.

But brotherhood begins to reveal itself when inconvenience enters the picture.

A man needs you at eleven at night.

His marriage is in trouble.

His father dies.

He loses the job.

He tells you something about himself that is hard to hear.

Now friendship has a price.

Time.

Attention.

Confidentiality.

The willingness to sit in a room where you cannot solve anything.

Jesus describes the greatest form of love as laying down one's life for another.

Most friendships will not ask for literal martyrdom.

They will ask for pieces of your life.

An evening.

A drive.

A phone call you did not have time for.

A conversation where you listen instead of waiting to speak.

A willingness to become uncomfortable because the man in front of you matters more than your schedule for an hour.

That is why deep friendship cannot be built entirely on convenience.

Convenience produces contact.

Sacrifice produces trust.

There is another cost too: allowing the other man to matter enough that his good can inconvenience your preference.

If he is drifting, you speak.

If he is hurting, you go.

If he needs help you can reasonably give, you give it.

And if you are the one in trouble, you stop forcing the relationship to remain one-directional because being needed feels safer than needing.

Some men are excellent at helping and terrible at receiving help.

That is still a barrier to brotherhood.

If you never let anyone carry you, you deny him the same form of love you pride yourself on offering others.

Brotherhood costs both men something.

That is part of why it becomes valuable.

### This Week

Think of one man in your life who may need more than a casual check-in.

Do something concrete that costs you time or convenience.

Call.

Show up.

Schedule the breakfast.

Offer the help.

Do not wait until he has crafted the perfect request.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, I like friendship when it fits neatly into the life I already planned. Teach me brotherhood that costs something. Give me the generosity to show up before I am asked, the humility to receive help when I need it, and the faithfulness to let another man's good interrupt my convenience. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**JOHN 15:13**
