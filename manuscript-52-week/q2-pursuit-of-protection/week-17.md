# Week 17 — Who You Are When Honesty Costs You

Honesty is easy to admire when it is free.

Tell the truth and nothing bad happens.

Own the mistake and everyone respects you for it.

Keep the promise and the cost remains manageable.

The real test arrives after the price becomes clear.

Tell the truth and you may lose the deal.

Own the mistake and your reputation takes a hit.

Keep your word and it costs more than you expected.

Speak plainly and someone may think less of you.

Psalm 15 describes the kind of man fit to live in God's presence, including the man who keeps his word even when doing so hurts him.

That is integrity after the invoice arrives.

The temptation then is not usually an obvious lie.

It is rationalization.

Maybe you simply leave out the inconvenient part.

Maybe you wait until somebody asks the exact right question.

Maybe you tell yourself that the truth would create unnecessary trouble.

Fear of man can make dishonesty sound prudent.

Proverbs calls that fear a trap.

You begin managing other people's opinion instead of your own character.

Scripture gives us examples of men who refused to make obedience depend on the outcome. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego trusted that God could deliver them from the furnace, but they did not make obedience conditional on guaranteed rescue.

That is the part worth carrying into ordinary life.

The moral decision does not become right or wrong after you discover whether it worked out.

Sometimes honesty will cost you.

It may also save you from building a future on a lie that has to keep being managed.

One reason men avoid telling the truth is that they imagine the moment after it.

The angry client.

The disappointed wife.

The lost opportunity.

The embarrassment.

Those costs may be real.

But there is another cost that remains hidden at first: becoming the man who protects his image by bending truth whenever the image is threatened.

That cost is larger.

You do not control what honesty costs you.

You control whether you are honest.

### This Week

Identify one truth you have been avoiding because you are afraid of the cost.

Name the feared cost plainly.

Then say:

**“I do not control what honesty costs me. I control whether I am honest.”**

Tell the truth this week.

### Prayer

Give me courage to tell the truth when it threatens money, approval, opportunity, or pride, and teach me to leave the outcome in Your hands. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
