# Week 21 — What Happens After You Lose Your Temper

Even a man committed to self-control will fail sometimes.

You will say the thing.

Raise the voice.

Become sharper than the situation required.

The question then changes.

What do you do next?

One of the worst habits around anger is acting as though the passage of time repairs what the outburst damaged.

Everyone cools off.

Dinner happens.

The next morning feels normal enough.

So you move on.

But normal is not the same as repaired.

St. Paul tells believers not to allow anger to become a place where evil gains a foothold. One way that happens is through unresolved damage.

The angry moment ends, but resentment remains.

A child learns to brace.

A wife remembers the sentence even after the tone disappears.

A coworker stops bringing bad news early because he now knows what happens when you hear it.

Repair begins with ownership.

Not:

**“I'm sorry, but you pushed me.”**

Not:

**“You know how I get when I'm tired.”**

Not:

**“I shouldn't have yelled, but what you did was wrong.”**

If the other person's behavior needs correction, deal with it separately.

Your reaction belongs to you.

A clean apology names what you did without using the apology to re-prosecute the other person.

**“I raised my voice and spoke to you disrespectfully. That was wrong. I'm sorry.”**

Then repair what can be repaired.

If trust was damaged, understand that one apology does not instantly restore it.

If your children have seen the pattern repeatedly, they may need to see change repeatedly too.

That is not punishment.

It is consequence.

There is also value in learning from the outburst before shame buries it.

What happened two minutes before you lost your temper?

What had you been carrying that day?

What sentence were you telling yourself about the other person's intention?

What boundary should have been spoken earlier before resentment built?

The goal is not to obsess over failure.

It is to use the failure as information.

Repentance is not only feeling bad about what happened.

It is turning.

### This Week

If you lose your temper this week, do not wait for the moment to fade.

Go back within twenty-four hours.

Name specifically what you did, apologize without a **but**, and ask what repair is needed.

Then write down one trigger you noticed so the failure teaches you something before you move on.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, when I fail in anger, keep pride from making the damage last longer than it has to. Give me humility to apologize plainly, courage to repair what I can, and wisdom to learn from the moment instead of merely regretting it. Teach me to turn, not just to feel bad. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**EPHESIANS 4:26–27**
