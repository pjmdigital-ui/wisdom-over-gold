# Week 25 — Correction From a Brother

Most men say they want honest friends.

The test is what happens when one becomes honest.

A brother tells you that you interrupted your wife three times at dinner.

He says you have become arrogant at work.

He notices that your drinking has changed.

He asks why you keep making the same excuse about Mass.

Before you have even decided whether he is right, your mind begins building the defense.

Context.

Nuance.

What he does not understand.

Why this situation is different.

Defensiveness often sounds intelligent because it comes dressed as explanation.

That does not make it honest.

Proverbs treats the wound delivered by a faithful friend differently from the pleasant words of someone who does not love you enough to tell the truth.

Correction can hurt and still be a gift.

The first discipline is therefore not agreeing with every criticism.

Your friend can be wrong.

The discipline is refusing to defend yourself before you have actually considered whether he is right.

That requires enough security to let a sentence land.

Ask:

**“What part of this is true?”**

That question is harder than:

**“How do I explain why I did it?”**

A man who explains himself quickly can preserve his image while losing the opportunity to change.

Brotherhood without correction eventually becomes mutual permission.

Everyone stays comfortable.

Nobody gets sharper.

That is not what iron does to iron.

Sharpening requires contact and resistance.

You should want at least one or two men in your life who have permission to tell you what they see.

Not men who enjoy criticizing you.

Men who have earned trust, want your good, and are willing to risk your annoyance because the truth matters more than being liked for five minutes.

Then become that kind of friend in return.

Correction should be private when possible, specific, and aimed at restoration rather than superiority.

You are not exposing a brother to prove you are more righteous.

You are helping him see what he may not see alone.

And when he does the same for you, listen before you litigate.

### This Week

Ask one trusted man:

**“Is there something you see in me lately that I may not be seeing clearly?”**

Then do not interrupt his answer.

When he finishes, thank him before you explain anything.

Give yourself a day to consider what is true before deciding what needs to change.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, give me friends who love me enough to tell me the truth and enough humility to hear them without immediately defending myself. Protect me from correction given in pride and from the pride that rejects needed correction. Make me a brother who sharpens and is willing to be sharpened. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
