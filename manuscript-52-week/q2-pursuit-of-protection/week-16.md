# Week 16 — Your Word Has to Mean Something

A man's word rarely becomes worthless all at once.

It loses value through small promises that keep expiring without being kept.

You tell your wife you will handle something and forget.

You tell a client the work will be finished by Friday when you already know Friday is optimistic.

You tell your child, **“After this call, I promise,”** because the promise solves the immediate interruption.

You mean it when you say it.

That is part of the problem.

Good intentions can make careless promises feel honest.

Jesus teaches that a man's yes should mean yes and his no should mean no.

The simplicity is demanding.

Do not use words to borrow relief from the present if you have no plan to pay them back later.

A promise is not only a statement about the future.

It is something another person may organize his expectations around.

Your child waits for the card trick.

Your wife stops reminding you because you said you would handle it.

Your client makes a decision based on the date you gave.

The other person may have no leverage over you at all.

That makes your word more revealing, not less.

A promise made to a child who cannot enforce it still matters.

A commitment nobody is tracking still matters.

Ecclesiastes warns against making vows lightly and then failing to keep them. The better path is not to become afraid of every commitment.

It is to say less carelessly.

Sometimes integrity looks like refusing to say yes too quickly.

**“Let me check before I promise.”**

**“I can probably do that, but I don't want to give you a date until I know.”**

**“Not tonight, but I can do it Saturday morning.”**

Those answers sound less impressive in the moment.

They create more trust over time.

Trust is accumulated evidence.

People learn whether your words correspond to what happens next.

That is especially true in the home, where promises often carry no formal consequence.

Your children are learning whether words mean something partly by watching what happens to yours.

When you cannot keep a commitment, integrity does not require pretending circumstances never change.

Go back.

Own it.

Update honestly.

Do not make the other person chase you for the truth.

A man whose word means something does not need to sound certain all the time.

He needs to become reliable.

### This Week

Identify one open promise or commitment you have not completed.

Either keep it this week or go back to the person and honestly change the expectation.

Then pause before your next meaningful **yes** and ask:

**“Can I actually pay this promise?”**

If the answer is no, do not give your word cheaply.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, forgive me for the words I have used carelessly because they solved the moment in front of me. Teach me to guard my yes and my no so that the people who depend on me can trust what I say. Give me the humility to correct a commitment I cannot keep and the discipline to keep the ones I make. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**MATTHEW 5:37**
