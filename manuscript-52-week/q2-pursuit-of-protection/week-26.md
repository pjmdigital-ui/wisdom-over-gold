# Week 26 — Show Up

There are moments when words are useful.

There are other moments when your presence is the thing being asked of you.

A man loses someone he loves.

His marriage falls apart.

He receives a diagnosis.

He loses the business.

You want to say something that helps.

The pressure to find the right sentence can become so strong that you forget the simpler responsibility:

Go.

Job's friends are remembered later for saying things they should not have said. Before the talking went wrong, they did something deeply right.

They heard about his suffering, came to him, sat with him, and remained there in silence.

They showed up.

That matters because suffering often makes men withdraw at exactly the time they need other people most.

And friends can withdraw too, not from lack of love but from uncertainty.

**I don't know what to say.**

**I don't want to make it worse.**

**I'll give him space.**

Space may be appropriate for a time.

It can also become an excuse for absence.

You do not need a speech to sit beside someone.

You do not need an answer to bring food, make the drive, attend the funeral, send the message, or stay on the phone.

Most men are better at the coming than the staying.

Showing up once is a gesture.

Staying through the second week, when the attention fades and the crisis becomes ordinary life, costs more.

That is often when friendship matters most.

This applies outside tragedy too.

Brotherhood is built by repeated presence.

The standing breakfast.

The training session.

The men's group after the original crisis has passed.

Hebrews tells Christians not to abandon gathering together but to keep encouraging one another toward love and good works.

You do not outgrow the room because you are doing better now.

The relationships that helped form you still need your presence, and other men may now need from you what you once received.

Show up when you need help.

Show up when you do not.

Show up when there is something useful to say.

Show up when there is not.

Consistency turns acquaintance into brotherhood.

### This Week

Think of the first man who comes to mind when you read this.

Do not merely think about contacting him.

Call or text with a specific day and time.

If he is carrying something difficult, do not arrive with an agenda to fix it.

Arrive to be there.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, forgive me for the times good intentions have stood in for actual presence. Give me the discipline to show up for the men You have placed in my life, especially when I do not know what to say or when the crisis has lasted longer than my attention. Make my friendship dependable. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
