# Week 43 — A Covenant With Your Eyes

Most men do not wake up one morning intending to betray their marriage.

The drift usually starts much smaller than that. A look you allow to linger. A conversation you enjoy a little too much. A message that becomes more personal than it needed to be. An account you keep following because you like what it puts in front of you.

Nothing dramatic has happened, so you tell yourself nothing is wrong.

That is usually where the danger begins.

Job describes a different approach. He had made a covenant concerning his eyes. He decided in advance that there were places he would not allow his attention to go.

That matters because temptation is a terrible time to start deciding what your standards are.

If you wait until the moment you want something, you are already negotiating from a weakened position. You begin asking how far is too far, what technically counts, whether anyone would really consider this wrong, and whether you can handle it without crossing the final line.

A covenant works differently.

The decision has already been made.

That is what gives Job's statement its strength. He isn't saying he hopes to exercise enough willpower when temptation appears. He has already established what kind of man he intends to be before the situation arrives.

A married man needs some decisions like that.

Not because every woman is a threat. Not because attraction disappears when you get married. Not because you should move through the world afraid to speak to women.

Because you are human.

You can be happily married and still notice another woman is attractive. You can love your wife and still enjoy attention from someone else. You can be faithful for twenty years and still be vulnerable to something you never expected to become important to you.

Pretending otherwise doesn't make you stronger.

Knowing yourself does.

The problem is rarely the first moment of attraction.

It is what you do next.

Do you feed it?

Do you go looking again?

Do you create opportunities to be around that person?

Do you keep the conversation going after the legitimate reason for it ended?

Do you start sharing things with another woman that properly belong inside your marriage?

That is where attention becomes direction.

Your eyes are not passive.

What you repeatedly look at shapes what you desire. What you give attention to becomes more significant in your mind. Something that begins as a passing image can become something you seek deliberately if you continue returning to it.

Modern life makes that remarkably easy.

You don't have to leave your house to place yourself in front of temptation anymore. The phone can bring it into your hand in seconds.

Social media feeds can become whatever you train them to become. The algorithm notices what you stop on, what you watch again, what you click, what you search, and what keeps your attention.

Eventually the screen starts handing you more of it.

That means a man can gradually build an environment around himself that constantly invites him to look, compare, fantasize, and desire—and then act surprised that his mind keeps going there.

The wiser question is not simply:

**“Have I technically done anything wrong?”**

It is:

**“What am I training my attention to want?”**

That gets much closer to the issue.

Pornography makes this obvious, but the principle goes beyond pornography. A man can stay outside what he considers the obvious line while still giving large amounts of sexual or emotional attention to women who are not his wife.

He tells himself it doesn't count because there has been no physical contact.

But fidelity begins before physical contact.

Your marriage has a claim on your attention too.

That becomes especially important with private communication.

A conversation can begin completely innocently. Work. A shared interest. A joke. A quick message about something practical.

Then the tone changes slightly.

She begins telling you things she doesn't tell everyone else. You start looking forward to seeing her name on your phone. You become more animated in those conversations than you have been at home lately.

Nothing has happened.

But something has started.

One useful test is very simple:

**Would I be completely comfortable if my wife read this conversation from beginning to end?**

Not whether you could explain it.

Not whether each individual message is defensible.

Whether you would be comfortable handing her the phone without deleting anything, minimizing anything, or preparing an argument first.

If the answer is no, pay attention.

Secrecy is often the first signal that a relationship has moved somewhere it should not be.

You may not have committed adultery.

That doesn't mean the situation deserves to keep growing.

A man who takes fidelity seriously does not wait until something becomes a disaster before establishing boundaries. He notices drift while it is still drift.

That might mean ending a private conversation that has become too personal. It might mean unfollowing an account. It might mean changing how you use your phone when you travel. It might mean refusing situations where you already know your motives are becoming mixed.

That can feel extreme when nothing has happened yet.

But that is exactly the point.

You are protecting something before it is damaged.

We understand this everywhere else.

You don't wait until the house is burning to decide whether smoke near the wall matters. You don't wait until the account is empty to start paying attention to spending.

Marriage deserves the same foresight.

A covenant with your eyes is not merely about saying no to another woman.

It is also about saying yes to your wife.

Every time you refuse to feed a fantasy, you protect your ability to see the woman you actually married.

Comparison can quietly destroy gratitude.

If you constantly expose yourself to carefully selected images of other women, eventually ordinary married life has to compete with something that isn't ordinary and often isn't even real.

Your wife cannot compete with an endless stream of novelty.

She shouldn't have to.

You married a real woman.

A woman who gets tired.

Who ages.

Who has bad days.

Who wakes up without perfect lighting.

Who has carried real life beside you.

Fidelity means continuing to direct your attention toward the woman you actually gave yourself to instead of training yourself to crave endless alternatives.

That does not happen automatically.

Marriage itself does not guard your eyes for you.

You do.

And the strongest time to make that decision is not at 11:00 at night when temptation is already sitting in your hand.

Make it before then.

Decide what your lines are while nothing is happening.

Decide what you will not search for.

Decide which conversations will remain public and appropriate.

Decide what you will do if attraction toward someone begins becoming something more.

Decide what access your wife should have to the parts of your life most capable of becoming hidden.

Then when the moment comes, you are not inventing your character under pressure.

You are simply living a decision you already made.

That is what a covenant does.

### This Week

Identify **one area where your attention has begun drifting somewhere it should not go**.

It may be an account you follow, something you search for, a private conversation, a habit when you are alone, or a relationship that has started becoming too personal.

Don't wait until something bigger happens.

Draw one specific line now.

Delete it. Unfollow it. End the conversation. Change the habit. Put a boundary in place before the next test arrives.

If the situation has already become something you would hide from your wife, bring it into the light rather than managing the secrecy.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, guard my eyes, my imagination, and my attention. Give me the honesty to recognize temptation before I have turned it into something larger, and the courage to draw boundaries before I am forced to deal with consequences. Keep me from using technical innocence to excuse a heart that is drifting. Teach me to direct my attention faithfully toward the wife I have given myself to and to protect our covenant in the private moments nobody else sees. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**JOB 31:1**
