# Week 44 — The Wife of Your Youth

There is a danger in becoming so familiar with your wife that you stop seeing her.

You still live together. You talk every day. You make decisions, pay bills, handle schedules, solve problems, and keep the household moving. From the outside, the marriage may look completely stable.

But somewhere along the way, the woman you once pursued can slowly become the person you manage life beside.

That shift rarely happens all at once.

It happens because familiarity changes attention.

Early in a relationship, you notice everything. The way she laughs. The way she looks at you. The things she cares about. The stories she tells. You remember details because you want to know her.

Years later, you already know the stories.

You've seen the expressions.

You've heard the opinions.

You know which restaurant she'll probably choose and how long she'll take to get ready.

Familiarity can become a gift because it creates intimacy.

It can also become an excuse to stop paying attention.

Proverbs gives married men an interesting command. It tells a man to rejoice in the wife of his youth.

Notice what it does not say.

It does not tell him simply to remember how much he once enjoyed her.

It does not speak only to newlyweds.

The wife of his youth is still his wife now.

The command is present.

**Delight in her now.**

That means delight in marriage is not supposed to be treated as something that naturally belongs to the beginning and then disappears as maturity takes over.

Marriage becomes different with time.

But different does not have to mean less alive.

One of the easiest lies to accept in a long marriage is that the excitement belonged to an earlier stage and what remains now is simply the serious version.

You had romance then.

Now you have responsibilities.

You had long conversations then.

Now you have logistics.

You pursued each other then.

Now you run a household.

Some of that change is inevitable. Life becomes fuller. Children, work, illness, aging parents, money, and responsibilities all place demands on a marriage that dating never did.

But responsibility does not have to erase delight.

The problem is that delight requires attention, and attention is easy to redirect.

You can spend more energy planning a renovation than planning time with your wife. You can spend twenty minutes comparing televisions online and barely look up when she walks into the room. You can learn everything about a new hobby while assuming there is nothing new left to learn about the woman you've been married to for twenty years.

That is how a marriage can become efficient and emotionally thin at the same time.

Everything gets handled.

Nothing feels particularly wrong.

You simply stop enjoying each other on purpose.

Sometimes a man does not notice the difference until he remembers how he used to look at his wife.

Not merely physically, although that matters too.

He admired her.

He was curious about her.

He wanted her attention.

He looked forward to telling her things.

He noticed what she wore, what made her laugh, what bothered her, what excited her.

Then somewhere along the way he stopped needing to win her attention, and he stopped giving as much of his own.

That is worth correcting.

A wedding vow does not end pursuit.

It changes what pursuit looks like.

You may not be twenty-five anymore.

You may not have the freedom, money, energy, or circumstances you had when you first met.

That's not the point.

The question is whether the woman beside you still experiences herself as someone you delight in, or mainly someone you depend on.

Those are not the same thing.

A man can appreciate what his wife does while rarely expressing delight in who she is.

He thanks her for handling the schedule.

He appreciates that she takes care of things.

He knows she is dependable.

But when was the last time he told her something he genuinely admires about her?

Not what she accomplished.

Her.

Her humor.

Her strength.

Her patience.

Her intelligence.

Her beauty.

The way she loves your children.

The way she carries herself.

Something about her that still makes you glad she is the woman you married.

Those things need to be spoken.

There is also something important about memory.

A long marriage has a history.

You have versions of each other stored in your mind that nobody else has access to. You remember what she was like before the children, before the house, before some of the losses and responsibilities that changed both of you.

Those memories should not become evidence that the best years are over.

They can become reminders of what is still present.

Maybe she has changed.

Of course she has.

So have you.

The point is not to force your wife to become the woman she was at twenty-four.

It is to recognize the woman she has become and remember that she is still the same person you once considered extraordinary enough to build your life around.

That requires seeing her in the present, not simply remembering her in the past.

There is another reason this matters after a week spent talking about guarding your eyes.

A man who stops delighting in his own wife can become especially vulnerable to the false excitement of novelty.

Someone new has no history attached.

You don't know her irritating habits.

You haven't argued about money with her.

You haven't watched her deal with the same responsibilities for fifteen years.

You encounter only a small, selected part of her.

That can make the familiar feel dull by comparison.

But novelty and intimacy are not the same thing.

Your wife carries something another woman cannot: the life you've actually built together.

She knows your history.

She has seen you fail.

She has probably seen you at your least impressive and remained beside you anyway.

She has carried things with you that no stranger could understand from a few conversations.

There is depth there that novelty cannot imitate.

But depth still needs attention.

A man should not expect his marriage to remain warm merely because it has survived.

The absence of a major problem is not the same thing as delight.

You can go months without a serious fight and still be drifting apart because almost every conversation has become functional.

Who is picking up the kids?

Did you pay the bill?

What time are we leaving?

What's happening this weekend?

Those conversations are necessary.

They just cannot be the whole marriage.

At some point, you have to look at the woman across from you and remember:

**I actually like you.**

I enjoy you.

I chose you.

I still want to know you.

Sometimes that requires doing something you stopped doing because it seemed unnecessary.

Ask her out.

Put the phones away.

Ask a question that has nothing to do with logistics.

Tell her something you've been thinking about.

Bring up a memory that still makes you laugh.

Do something together that neither of you needs to accomplish.

Marriage needs room where nobody is managing anything.

And if the relationship has been strained lately, this may feel unnatural at first.

That's okay.

Delight can be practiced before it becomes effortless again.

You don't have to manufacture a feeling you don't have.

You can begin by giving attention.

Notice.

Speak.

Remember.

Pursue.

Enjoy.

Sometimes affection follows the attention you were waiting for affection to create.

That is part of the wisdom in Proverbs.

It does not tell the man to wait until he happens to feel delighted.

It points him toward delight in the wife already beside him.

The wife of his youth.

Not because she is unchanged.

Because the covenant has carried both of them far enough that she deserves to be seen again, not merely relied upon.

### This Week

Think of **one specific thing you loved about your wife early in your relationship that you have stopped noticing or saying out loud.**

Then tell her.

Not as nostalgia for a version of her that no longer exists.

Tell her how you still see that quality in her now.

Then make time for one conversation or activity together this week that has nothing to do with solving a problem, managing the house, or checking something off a list.

Enjoy your wife on purpose.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, thank You for the wife You have given me and for the years of life we have been allowed to share. Forgive me for the times familiarity has made me careless and I have stopped noticing what once came naturally to me. Teach me to delight in my wife as she is now, to speak the admiration I too often leave unspoken, and to keep pursuing the woman I promised to love. Protect our marriage from becoming nothing more than responsibilities handled side by side, and restore in me a grateful attention toward the wife of my youth. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
