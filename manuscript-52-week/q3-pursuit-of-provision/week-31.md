# Week 31 — Whose Money Is This?

The question changes everything before you spend a dollar.

**Whose money is this?**

The natural answer is obvious.

Mine.

I earned it.

I took the risk.

I worked the hours.

I paid the taxes.

I made the decisions that produced it.

All of that may be true.

Psalm 24 starts the ledger one level higher: the earth and everything in it belongs to God.

That means your capacity to work belongs inside a world you did not create, using a body you did not design, gifts you did not manufacture, relationships you did not fully control, and opportunities you could not have guaranteed for yourself.

Stewardship does not erase effort.

It places effort inside gift.

You really did work.

You are still not the ultimate owner.

That distinction changes the way a man holds money.

An owner asks:

**“What do I want to do with what is mine?”**

A steward also asks:

**“What would faithfulness look like with what was entrusted to me?”**

That question reaches into spending, saving, giving, debt, investing, taxes, generosity, and the household.

It does not produce one identical budget for every Catholic man.

It produces a different posture.

Open hands.

If money is yours absolutely, giving always feels like losing ownership.

If money is entrusted, giving can become part of the assignment.

If money is yours absolutely, a market drop can feel like something has been stolen from your identity.

If money is entrusted, loss still hurts, but your security does not have to collapse with the account.

If money is yours absolutely, your wife may begin feeling like someone asking permission to access your achievement.

If it belongs to God and the household is one, the conversation becomes stewardship together.

This is especially challenging for self-made men.

The more you remember how hard you worked, the easier it becomes to believe effort created ownership in the deepest sense.

It did not.

King David had more visible claim to wealth and authority than most men will ever have, yet he could still recognize that what he offered to God had first come from God.

That humility is worth learning before money becomes large enough to make it difficult.

Stewardship is not anti-wealth.

A steward can manage a great deal.

The amount does not determine the posture.

The question is whether you can hold it without believing it belongs to you in a way that excludes God from the decision.

### This Week

Look at one financial asset you feel especially attached to: the account, the house, the business, the investment, the paid-off car.

Say aloud:

**“This is Yours before it is mine. I am responsible for it, but I am not its ultimate owner.”**

Then choose one practical stewardship action connected to it this week.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, everything I have ultimately exists inside what You created and allowed me to receive. Forgive me for confusing effort with absolute ownership. Teach me to hold money as a steward—responsibly, gratefully, generously, and without making it my security. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**PSALM 24:1**
