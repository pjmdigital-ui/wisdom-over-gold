# Week 28 — Tend What God Put in Your Hands

Provision begins with attention.

Not ambition.

Not income.

Attention.

A man cannot steward what he refuses to look at closely.

That is why the biblical image of tending is so useful.

A field, flock, household, business, craft, or responsibility does not remain healthy because the owner has good intentions toward it. Someone has to notice what is happening.

What needs repair?

What needs pruning?

What is growing?

What is being neglected?

What did God actually put in my hands, as opposed to the thing I keep wishing He had put there instead?

That last question matters.

Men waste enormous energy comparing assignments.

Someone else's business is larger.

Someone else's career moved faster.

Someone else's gifts receive more attention.

Someone else appears to have been given better material to work with.

Meanwhile, the actual responsibility in front of you goes untended.

Jesus repeatedly teaches through the language of stewardship. Servants are entrusted with something, and the question is what they do with what they were actually given.

Not what they would have done with somebody else's portion.

Not what they plan to do when the circumstances become more impressive.

What did they do with this?

That is a liberating standard because comparison becomes largely irrelevant.

Your task is not to produce another man's harvest.

It is to be faithful with your field.

Maybe your field right now is a small business.

Maybe it is learning a trade.

Maybe it is rebuilding finances after a bad season.

Maybe it is a household that needs more of your attention than your career currently does.

Maybe it is a skill you have been given that you keep treating as ordinary because it comes naturally to you.

Gifts become stewardship questions too.

Your ability to communicate.

Teach.

Build.

Sell.

Lead.

Repair.

Organize.

Create.

Those are not merely pieces of leverage in a marketplace.

They are capacities you received.

You can monetize them. There is nothing wrong with that.

But the fact that a gift can make money does not tell you its entire purpose.

The Christian asks a wider question:

**“Who can this serve?”**

Tending therefore means developing what has been entrusted to you rather than wasting it through neglect or entitlement.

You practice.

You learn.

You become better.

You keep the equipment maintained.

You keep the promises.

You look at the numbers.

You pay attention to the people.

You do not call something stewardship merely because your name appears on the ownership document.

Ownership and stewardship can be opposites if ownership makes you careless.

You can own a business and neglect it.

Own a house and fail to maintain it.

Have a gift and never train it.

Have a family and assume love will tend itself.

Tending is active.

And because everything is ultimately God's, it is also an act of worship.

You are saying through your care:

**“What You placed in my hands matters enough to handle well.”**

### This Week

Choose one thing God has placed in your hands that has been under-tended.

A skill.

A responsibility.

A relationship connected to your work.

A piece of your business.

A household obligation.

Give it one focused block of attention this week and make one concrete improvement.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, keep me from ignoring what You have already entrusted to me while I compare myself with what another man received. Teach me to tend my actual field, develop my gifts, and handle responsibility as something on loan from You. Make me faithful with what is in my hands now. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**MATTHEW 25:21**
