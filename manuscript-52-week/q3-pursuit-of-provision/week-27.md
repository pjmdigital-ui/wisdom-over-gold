# Week 27 — Work Was Always Part of the Plan

A lot of men think of work as the thing standing between them and real life.

Get through the week.

Make enough money.

Reach the number.

Retire.

Then life can finally begin.

Scripture gives work a much older and better place than that.

Genesis shows Adam working before sin enters the world. Before the curse, before the thorns, before the sweat and frustration, God places the man in the garden and gives him something to tend.

Work was already there.

That matters because the Fall did not create work.

It made work harder.

The frustration, futility, exhaustion, and conflict we experience around labor belong to a world that is not yet what it should be. But the act of cultivating, building, serving, organizing, repairing, creating, and providing was part of human life before anything had gone wrong.

That changes the way a man looks at Monday.

Your job may not be your dream job.

It may not be your permanent job.

There may be legitimate reasons to change it.

But work itself is not a sentence you serve while waiting for the rest of your life.

It is one of the places where your life is happening.

A man can spend twenty years resenting that fact and lose twenty years that could have formed him.

Work teaches responsibility because something has to be done whether you feel inspired or not.

It teaches patience because projects rarely move at the speed you want.

It teaches service because useful work solves a problem for somebody else.

It exposes pride because you will eventually be wrong, corrected, overlooked, or forced to do something beneath the level you think you have earned.

And it provides the material support that allows a household to stand.

That is why vocation is bigger than career.

Career is one expression of work.

Vocation asks what God has actually placed in front of you to tend.

For one season that may be a company.

For another it may be a job you did not plan to keep.

It includes the home, the marriage, the children, the parish, the people who depend on your skill, and the responsibilities nobody pays you to carry.

The Christian question is not only:

**“Do I like my job?”**

It is:

**“What has God placed in my hands here, and am I tending it faithfully?”**

That does not mean staying forever in work that is immoral, destructive, or clearly no longer where you should be.

Sometimes faithful work includes preparing to leave.

But there is a difference between discerning a change and treating the present like dead time until the change arrives.

You can honor God in work you hope eventually to leave.

You can do a good job for an employer you do not plan to serve forever.

You can treat customers honestly even when nobody is checking.

You can teach someone beneath you rather than guarding what you know.

You can bring order to the thing in front of you because order is good.

Genesis places man in the garden to cultivate and guard it.

The garden is not yours in the absolute sense.

You received it.

That is how provision begins.

Not with ownership.

With stewardship.

### This Week

Identify one part of your work you have been treating as something to endure until “real life” starts.

Ask:

**“What has God actually given me to tend here?”**

Then do one piece of that work with full attention and gratitude rather than resentment.

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, forgive me for the times I have treated work only as an obstacle between me and the life I want. Teach me to see labor as part of the calling You placed into human life from the beginning. Give me wisdom to know when change is needed and faithfulness to tend what is in my hands while it is still mine to tend. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**GENESIS 2:15**
