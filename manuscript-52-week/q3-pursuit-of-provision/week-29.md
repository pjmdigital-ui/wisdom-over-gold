# Week 29 — Faithful in the Work Nobody Sees

Some of the most revealing parts of your work are the parts nobody checks.

The homeowner will never climb onto the roof.

The client will never read the underlying file.

The boss has no way to know whether you spent another twenty minutes making the thing right or stopped when it looked good enough from the outside.

Those moments feel small because there is no audience.

That is why they matter.

St. Paul tells the Colossians to work wholeheartedly as though the real person they are serving is the Lord rather than the human supervisor.

That standard follows you into the unobserved part of the job.

The work does not become less real because nobody will grade it.

A man who works only to the level of visible accountability is not practicing excellence.

He is practicing performance.

There is a difference.

Performance asks what other people can see.

Faithfulness asks what is right.

Sometimes the difference is invisible to everyone except you and God.

You correct the small error.

You tell the customer what he actually needs rather than what makes you the larger sale.

You finish the part beneath the surface.

You write the accurate report instead of the convenient one.

You give the person who works for you the training you promised even though nobody is forcing you to do it.

That is ordinary holiness.

We can make vocation sound grand and miss the fact that most work is repetitive.

Emails.

Calls.

Measurements.

Invoices.

Cleaning.

Follow-up.

Quality control.

The same skill again.

The same route.

The same kind of problem.

Repetition is where standards are tested because nobody feels heroic doing the same thing for the four-hundredth time.

This is why work can form character if you let it.

You are practicing what kind of man you are when enthusiasm is absent.

And the habits travel.

A man who cuts corners where nobody sees can become comfortable cutting them elsewhere.

A man who learns to do hidden work well builds an interior standard he carries beyond the job.

Your children may never know the extra twenty minutes you spent making something right.

They may still inherit the kind of father those repetitions helped form.

That is enough reason to care.

### This Week

Find one part of your work that nobody checks, grades, or would ever notice if you shorted it.

Do that part exactly right anyway.

Tell yourself:

**“This work is real even when no one sees it.”**

### Prayer

Father in Heaven, make my standard higher than the amount of supervision around me. Teach me to work faithfully when nobody is watching and to serve You in the hidden details other people may never notice. Build in me the kind of character that does the thing right because it is right. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.

**COLOSSIANS 3:23–24**
