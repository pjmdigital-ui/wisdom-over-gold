# Day 2 — The Domestic Church

Ben's daughter climbed into bed one night and asked why God let her grandmother get sick if He could have stopped it. Ben sat on the edge of the mattress and felt the question land somewhere he wasn't prepared for. He gave her something short and vague about mystery and faith, kissed her forehead, and told her Sister Catherine could probably explain it better on Wednesday. She seemed satisfied. He wasn't.

Driving to work the next morning, the exchange kept surfacing. Ben paid the tuition for religious ed without complaint. He drove the carpool. He signed every form. In his mind that had always counted as handling his kids' faith formation — outsourced to people more qualified than him, the same way he outsourced the oil changes and the taxes. *Isn't that what CCD is for?* The question had never once occurred to him as a question until his daughter asked him something real and he handed her off to someone else to answer.

Moses told the Israelites something different as they stood at the edge of the promised land, and he didn't frame it as optional or delegable. "These words which I command you this day shall be upon your heart; and you shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise" (Deuteronomy 6:6-7, RSV-CE). Not once does that instruction mention a teacher, a program, or a building. It's addressed to the man sitting in his own house, walking his own road, putting his own kids to bed.

The Church has always called the home a domestic church for a reason — it's the first and smallest parish most kids will ever belong to, and the man of the house is its only priest by default, whether he's prepared for the role or not. Sunday school can hand a child vocabulary and structure. It can't hand a child the sight of her own father praying, wrestling honestly with a hard question, or admitting he doesn't have every answer but believes anyway. That transmission only happens at home, and only a father or mother actually living there can do it.

Ben hadn't been failing his daughter by paying for religious ed. He'd been failing her by treating it as the whole job instead of the support for a job that was always his first to carry. The tuition check was never the hard part. Sitting back down on the edge of her bed and finishing the conversation he'd handed off — that took something else.

**Today:** The next time one of your kids asks a real question about God, suffering, or faith, don't defer it to Sunday school, a priest, or "we'll talk about it later." Sit down right then and answer as honestly as you can, even if your answer is "I don't fully know, but here's what I believe and why."

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've handed off the hardest questions in my own house to people who see my kids one hour a week, while I see them every day. Forgive me for treating this home as anything less than the first church my children will ever know. Give me the words and the nerve to teach them Yourself, through me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
