# Day 28 — Closing Week Four

Micah manages a team of twelve and spent this week seeing his own job in a light he didn't love. A new manager tempted to lead by title instead of service. A man who cut a corner in a deal because no one would ever check the math. A business owner deciding what to do with a good quarter's surplus. Another chasing a promotion for a feeling it was never going to deliver. A tradesman who couldn't make himself stop working even after the day's work was actually done. A business partner staying silent about a problem everyone else could see. Micah recognized himself in at least three of the six, and not the flattering parts.

The thread running under all of it wasn't really about leadership, or honesty, or generosity as separate skills to improve on. It was about whose the authority, the money, the skill, and the position actually belong to. A man who believes his title is his own will use it to be served. A man who believes his skill is his own will hide it rather than pass it on, or bend it when hiding a mistake seems safer than owning it. A man who believes his profit is his own will keep it. Every failure this week traced back to the same quiet substitution — treating something entrusted as something owned.

St. Peter names the correction directly, in a single sentence built for exactly this pattern: "As each has received a gift, employ it for one another, as good stewards of God's varied grace" (1 Peter 4:10, RSV-CE). Received. Employed for one another. Stewards, not owners. That word does the real work in the verse — a steward manages what belongs to someone else, on that someone else's terms, and answers for how it was used. Micah's team, his authority to lead them, the skill that got him the position in the first place — none of it originated with him, and all of it is on loan.

That reframes what this whole week was actually testing. Not whether Micah is a good enough manager, salesman, or business owner by the world's measure, but whether he can hold his position, his money, and his skill the way a steward holds someone else's property — carefully, honestly, and ready to give an account of it whenever it's asked for.

Nobody closes a week like this having become a perfect steward. The bar was whether Micah started managing his position, his honesty, and his money like something entrusted to him rather than something he'd earned the right to hoard, hide, or wield however he wanted — never perfection.

**Today:** Go back through this week's entries and name the one that described you most accurately — the corner you've cut, the number you've chased, the conversation you've avoided. Say it plainly: *What I've been given at work is mine to steward well and hand back an honest account of, not mine to keep or wield.* Then take the one action from that day you skipped.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, show me where I've been treating what You've entrusted to me like something I earned the right to own outright. Make me a good steward of the authority, skill, and provision You've placed in my hands this week. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
