# Day 10 — The Work Nobody Sees

By two in the afternoon the roof deck was hot enough that Fletcher could feel it through his boots, and the crew still had four squares of underlayment to lay before they could start dropping shingles. The foreman had left for a supply run an hour earlier. Nobody on the ground could see whether Fletcher ran the extra row of fasteners along the eave the way the manufacturer's spec actually called for, or whether he saved himself twenty minutes and ran the minimum. Once the shingles went down, that decision would disappear under three layers of roofing and never surface again — not in an inspection, not in a complaint, not for twenty years, if ever.

He ran the extra row. It cost him twenty minutes and a fresh round of sweat he didn't have in reserve, and not one person on that job site, that street, or that company would ever know the difference.

Underneath the shortcut he didn't take was a belief plenty of men do act on without ever saying it plainly: *work only really counts when somebody's watching it get done.* That belief turns quality into a performance for an audience, and the moment the audience leaves, so does the standard.

Proverbs draws a straight line between actual labor and actual reward: "In all toil there is profit, but mere talk tends only to want" (Proverbs 14:23, RSV-CE). Toil is the sweat, the extra row of fasteners, the part of the job that happens under a roof deck where no inspector will ever crawl. Mere talk is everything that only resembles work — the version that looks finished from the ground, sounds finished in a status update, and isn't actually finished underneath. The verse promises the toil is where the profit actually lives, seen or not.

Fletcher's twenty minutes were owed to the work itself, not to the foreman who never asked or the homeowner who'll never climb up to check — to doing the thing right because it's right, not because someone's grading it. That standard has to survive being alone on a roof in July heat, or it was only ever a costume worn for other people.

**Today:** Find the one part of your work today that nobody checks, grades, or would ever notice if you shorted it. Do that part exactly right anyway, and tell yourself plainly: "This work is real even when no one sees it — the toil is the profit, not the applause."

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, thank You for seeing the hours I put in when no one else does. Strip out of me the habit of working harder for an audience than I do alone. Let today's hidden effort be as honest as anything I do in front of a crowd. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
