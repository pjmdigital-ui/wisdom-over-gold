# Day 25 — Once I Hit This Number

Sean blew past his quarterly quota in June with three weeks to spare. Most reps would have coasted into July. Sean opened a new deal instead, because Regional Director was one signature away and he'd promised himself he'd hit it before his thirty-eighth birthday. He missed his son's last two baseball games chasing that signature. He told his wife it was temporary, just this stretch, just this number.

He checked the dashboard four times during dinner Tuesday night. Refreshed it again before he fell asleep. The number hadn't moved either time, and some part of him already knew what would happen if it did: relief for about a day, and then a new number waiting behind it. He kept telling himself the sentence anyway, because it was the only thing that made the missed games feel worth it: *Once I hit this number, I'll finally feel like I've made it.*

Nobody has run that experiment more thoroughly than Solomon, and the results are in Scripture, not speculation. He had wealth, projects, pleasure, every want fulfilled the moment he wanted it: "Whatever my eyes desired I did not keep from them; I kept my heart from no pleasure, for my heart found pleasure in all my toil, and this was my reward for all my toil." Then he looked back at it. "I considered all that my hands had done and the toil I had spent in doing it, and behold, all was vanity and a striving after wind, and there was nothing to be gained under the sun" (Ecclesiastes 2:10-11, RSV-CE). Solomon reached every number available to him and reports back, in his own words, that the arrival didn't do what he'd promised himself it would.

Sean's ambition isn't the problem. Wanting to provide well, to be excellent at his work, to lead a team — none of that needs apologizing for. The problem is what he's asking the title to give him, which is a settled sense of having made it, a feeling no promotion has ever produced for anyone who chased it for that reason. The number will always move, because the number was never actually what he was after. What he was after — being enough, having proven something, resting in his own worth — was never for sale at that price to begin with.

The son whose games he missed doesn't need a Regional Director. He needs a father who shows up believing he's already enough without the title, because that's the man who'll actually be present when the title does or doesn't come.

**Today:** Write down the actual number you've been chasing for identity, not just provision, and cross it out. Underneath it write the true sentence: *I am already enough in God's eyes with or without this number, and my worth was settled before I closed a single deal.* Then do one thing today the old belief would have sacrificed — leave on time, make the call, show up to the thing.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for the nights I've measured my worth by a dashboard instead of by You. Free me from the number I keep chasing for a peace it was never going to give me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
