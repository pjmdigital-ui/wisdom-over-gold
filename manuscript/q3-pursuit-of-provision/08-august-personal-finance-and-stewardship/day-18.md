# Day 18 — Closing His Heart

Brendan noticed the man before he noticed anything else about the parking lot that morning — sitting against the wall of the gas station with a hand-lettered sign, a duffel bag, and a dog on a length of rope. Brendan had cash in his wallet, twenty minutes before his next meeting, and a straight line from his car to the door that would take him right past the man if he chose it, or around the far side of the lot if he didn't.

He went around the far side. He told himself he was in a hurry, which was true in the way that most excuses are true just enough to survive a quick glance. He'd made this same choice a dozen times over the years, at a dozen intersections and gas stations, always with a reasonable-sounding reason attached, and he'd mostly stopped noticing he was making a choice at all. It had become a reflex: see the need, calculate the cost of engaging with it, route around.

What made this morning different was that Brendan caught himself doing it — watched his own feet carry him the long way around a man he'd already decided, before fully deciding, not to help. He sat in his car afterward for a full minute before starting the engine, running the math on twenty dollars and twenty minutes, both of which he clearly had, against a resistance he couldn't fully name.

The sentence underneath the resistance, if he'd been honest with himself in that minute, was blunt: *helping him would cost me something I'd rather keep.* Not the twenty dollars specifically — Brendan spent more than that on coffee some weeks without thinking twice. What he didn't want to spend was the discomfort of eye contact with a man whose life had gone somewhere his hadn't, the two minutes of not knowing what to say, the small tear in the illusion that his morning belonged entirely to him.

Scripture names this exact scene with uncomfortable precision: "But if any one has the world's goods and sees his brother in need, yet closes his heart against him, how does God's love abide in him?" (1 John 3:17, RSV-CE). Not a man who lacks the goods to give — a man who has them, sees the need clearly, and closes his heart anyway. The verse doesn't ask whether Brendan is generous in general. It asks what happened in that specific minute, at that specific gas station, when he had exactly what the moment required and chose the long way around instead.

**On St. Vincent de Paul:** Vincent de Paul spent his life organizing real, sustained care for the poor of seventeenth-century France — not just collecting money from a distance, but insisting the men and women he recruited visit the poor in person, sit with them, look them in the eye. He believed charity that never costs the giver anything real isn't the kind Christ had in mind, and he built entire religious communities around that conviction.

**Today:** The next time you see a real need today — a person, not a cause — do not calculate the cost first. Walk toward it instead of around it, and give something that actually costs you: time, eye contact, a real conversation, not just spare change dropped without breaking stride.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for the times I've seen a need clearly and closed my heart against it because helping would cost me something. Give me the courage to walk toward the next one instead of routing around it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
