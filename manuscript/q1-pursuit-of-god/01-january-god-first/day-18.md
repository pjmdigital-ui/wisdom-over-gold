# Day 18 — God First With Your Money

The mortgage went up in April. Then the car needed brakes. Then your daughter's braces came due six months earlier than the orthodontist originally quoted. By the time the offering basket comes around this Sunday, you're doing the math in your head before the usher even reaches your pew — mortgage, brakes, braces, the credit card balance you haven't said out loud to your wife yet. You put in what you always put in, out of habit more than conviction, and you feel a small flicker of resentment you'd never admit to anyone. Not now. Not this month. Ask me when things are less tight.

Here's the thing you already suspect, even as you write the check: things are never going to feel less tight. There will always be a brake job. The month where giving finally feels comfortable and painless is not coming, because comfort isn't how faith works and it was never the point.

Matthew 6:21 says it in nine words: "For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also." Jesus isn't describing a rule about percentages. He's describing a mechanism, almost a law of nature about the human heart. Your heart doesn't lead your money — your money leads your heart. Put your money toward comfort and security and your heart will orbit comfort and security, worrying about them, protecting them, measuring your peace by their balance. Put your money toward God's work and, strangely, your heart starts to follow it there too, caring about things it never used to notice.

This is why the tight month is actually the test that matters, not the flush one. Giving out of abundance costs you nothing and proves nothing — of course you'll give when you can't feel it leave. Giving when the number in the account makes you wince is the only kind that actually reorders anything, because it's the only kind that requires trust instead of surplus. A man who only gives when it's easy hasn't put God first with his money. He's put his money first, and God gets whatever's left over — which, by definition, is never the treasure, only the crumbs.

This isn't a formula for buying blessings or guaranteeing an easier month. It's a discipline for un-gripping your hand. Every dollar you release on purpose, especially the ones that hurt to release, is a small rehearsal for the truth that none of it was ever fully yours to begin with. You were a steward the whole time, not an owner. The tight budget didn't create that truth. It just exposed how tightly you'd been gripping something you never owned.

**Today:** Before you pay a single other bill this week, set aside your giving first — even if it's a smaller amount than you'd like, even if it makes the rest of the budget uncomfortable. Give it before the leftover math has a chance to talk you out of it.

**Prayer:** God, this month is tight and I want to hold onto every dollar until things feel safer. Help me give you the first part of what I have instead of whatever's left over, and trust you with the brakes and the braces and the rest of it.
