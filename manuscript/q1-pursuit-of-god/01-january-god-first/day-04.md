# Day 4 — When Success Isn't Enough

The house closed on a Thursday. Four bedrooms, the yard he'd promised his wife for a decade, a driveway wide enough for both cars and the boat he'd also finally bought himself that spring. Tom stood in the empty living room after the movers left, sunlight coming through windows nobody had hung curtains in yet, and felt the strangest thing: nothing new. Just the same low hum underneath everything that had been there before the house, before the promotion, before the number in his 401(k) that used to feel like it would fix this exact feeling.

He didn't tell anyone. What was there to say? He'd hit every marker he'd set for himself at twenty-five, and the marker had moved, silently, the moment he crossed it. There was already a next number in his head. A bigger house than this one, somewhere down the line. He recognized the pattern even as it was happening to him again and couldn't seem to stop it.

The writer of Ecclesiastes got there before Tom did, by about three thousand years: "I denied myself nothing my eyes desired; I refused my heart no pleasure... Yet when I surveyed all that my hands had done and what I had toiled to achieve, everything was meaningless, a chasing after the wind; nothing was gained under the sun" (Ecclesiastes 2:10-11). This is a man with more resources than almost anyone in history, who built and acquired without limit, telling you flatly: it didn't work. Not because he did it wrong. Because it can't work. It was never designed to.

That's the part worth sitting with. This isn't a story about a guy who failed to achieve enough. It's the opposite — a story about a guy who achieved everything and still hit the wall. The wall isn't a flaw in your effort. It's built into the nature of created things. Houses, promotions, bank balances — they're good gifts, but they were never built with the capacity to be your final answer. Ask a good thing to be your ultimate thing and it will always, eventually, come up short. Not because it's bad. Because it's not God, and only God was ever sized to fill that particular space.

The empty feeling in that living room wasn't a sign Tom needed a bigger house. It was a sign he'd been asking the house to do a job no house can do.

**Today:** Name one achievement you're currently chasing as though it will finally be enough. Say honestly: "This is a good thing, not my ultimate thing." Then thank God specifically for one gift you already have that you've stopped noticing.
