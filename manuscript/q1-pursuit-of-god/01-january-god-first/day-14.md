# Day 14 — The Man You're Becoming

You were sitting on the back steps watching your two boys play some invented game in the yard — half baseball, half wrestling, rules that changed every thirty seconds and applied only to them. Your older one, seven, was narrating the whole thing in a voice you recognized immediately, because it was yours. The same clipped, impatient way you talk when you're frustrated. The same phrase — "come on, let's go, let's go" — you use when you're rushing everyone out the door in the morning, stripped of whatever context made it forgivable in you and now just sitting there, bare, in a seven-year-old's mouth.

You watched a little longer. He wasn't copying your words. He was copying your posture toward the world — hurried, a little impatient, more focused on getting to the next thing than on the thing in front of him. You realized, sitting on those steps, that you'd been assuming your kids were mostly shaped by what you told them. They weren't. They were being shaped by what you actually did with your Sundays, your evenings, your attention — the parts of you that ran on autopilot, where you weren't performing for anyone.

Near the end of his life, Joshua gathered the people of Israel and gave them a choice — serve the gods of the surrounding nations, the old gods of their ancestors, or serve the Lord. He didn't just ask them to decide. He told them where he stood: "As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD" (Joshua 24:15). It's a household declaration, not just a personal one. Joshua understood that a man's choice about who sits at the center of his life was never going to stay private. It was going to become the water his household swam in, whether he announced it or not.

That's the weight of it. Your kids aren't watching for your sermons. They're watching for what you reach for when you're tired, what you prioritize when nobody's making you, whether the God-first talk on Sunday matches the Tuesday-night version of you scrolling on the couch. You don't get to opt out of modeling something. The only choice is what.

**On St. Thomas Aquinas:** Born into Italian nobility, Thomas was expected by his family to become a powerful abbot, a path that would have brought wealth and prestige to their name. Instead he insisted on joining the mendicant Dominicans — a choice so unwelcome that his own brothers kidnapped and imprisoned him for over a year trying to break his resolve. He held firm, subordinating both his family's ambitions and his own considerable brilliance to what he understood God was calling him toward, and became one of the greatest minds the Church has ever produced anyway.

**Today:** Watch your kids for ten unguarded minutes — playing, talking to each other, not performing for you. Notice one thing they're modeling that they picked up from you, good or bad, and name it honestly to yourself.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I give You thanks and praise. Thank You for opening my eyes to another day in this beautiful world You have created for me. I pray You send Your Holy Spirit down upon me today, to help me be the best father, neighbor, husband, friend, businessman, and Christian I can be. Let everything I do please and glorify You. My kids are watching what I do, not what I say, and I don't like everything I just saw reflected back at me. Help me become, in the unguarded moments, the kind of man I actually want them to become. Let my Tuesday nights match my Sunday words. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
