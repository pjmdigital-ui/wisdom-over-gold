# Day 8 — Seek First

He'd heard the verse a hundred times. Grew up hearing it, probably could have recited it half-asleep since he was twelve. Matthew 6:33 — seek first the kingdom of God, everything else gets added. It was on a plaque in his grandmother's hallway, stitched into a throw pillow somewhere in his mother's house, the kind of verse that gets so familiar it stops landing anywhere at all. Church background noise. A nice sentiment for a needlepoint.

Then one ordinary Tuesday, sitting in his car in a parking lot before a meeting he didn't want to walk into, he heard it read out loud on the radio and it stopped him cold, the way a word you've read a thousand times can suddenly reorganize itself and mean something you'd never actually let it mean. *Seek first.* Not seek eventually, once the deal closes, once the kids are older, once the mortgage is paid off, once he's finally earned the right to slow down and get spiritual. First. Before. As the actual operating order of an actual life, not a value he'd get around to once the urgent stuff was handled.

That's the whole weight of this verse, and it's worth reading in full: "But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you" (Matthew 6:33). Jesus says this after telling a crowd of working people not to worry about food, clothing, or tomorrow — the exact things a responsible man feels obligated to worry about most. He's not telling them those needs don't matter. He's telling them where those needs belong in the order of things: after, not first. Downstream of trusting God, not upstream of it.

This is the verse this entire book stands on. Not because it's a nice sentiment, but because it's a diagnosis and a promise in the same breath. The diagnosis: most of us have quietly built our lives around ourselves, seeking our own comfort, security, and reputation first, and calling whatever's left over for God devotion. The promise: reverse the order — God first, actually first, not eventually — and the things you've been white-knuckling to secure on your own get added, not because you stopped working for them, but because you stopped needing them to be your god.

That parking lot moment didn't fix his Tuesday. The meeting still happened. But something in him had reordered, quietly, in the space of ninety seconds, and he couldn't unhear the verse the way he'd been not-hearing it for thirty years.

**On St. Paul:** He was riding to Damascus with letters authorizing him to arrest and imprison Christians, utterly convinced his life was already ordered rightly — around his own zeal, his own righteousness. A light on the road stopped him cold, and the man who woke up three days later wasn't reformed at the edges. He was reordered at the root, from the ground up, around a Kingdom he'd been persecuting the day before. Nothing in his resume changed. Everything about what it was for did.

**Today:** Say Matthew 6:33 out loud, slowly, twice, before you do anything else today — before the phone, before the coffee. Let the word "first" actually mean first, just for today, and notice one place you're tempted to put something else there instead.

**Prayer:** Lord, I've heard "seek first" my whole life without ever letting it reorder anything. Reorder me starting today — not once the deal closes or the mortgage is paid, but first.
