# Day 28 — The Cost of Putting God First

The contract was worth more than your business had made in the previous two years combined. All you had to do was sign off on marketing claims you knew weren't fully true — numbers rounded up, a testimonial you were pretty sure was fabricated, a guarantee your own product couldn't actually back. Your business partner already had the pen out. "Everyone does this," he said, and he wasn't wrong about that part. It's practically the industry standard. He wasn't lying to you either — he genuinely didn't see what the problem was.

You sat with it for two days. Ran the numbers again like a different number might change the answer. Thought about what the deal would mean — the debt paid off, the college accounts funded, the pressure finally, finally easing. Then you thought about your kids finding out someday how the money came in, and about the version of yourself that would have to look them in the eye afterward. You turned it down. Your partner was furious. The relief you'd been hoping to feel by walking away didn't show up right away — mostly what you felt for a while was just the size of what you'd said no to.

Jesus doesn't soften this trade-off anywhere in the Gospels: "For what will it profit a man if he gains the whole world and forfeits his soul? Or what shall a man give in return for his soul?" (Matthew 16:26). It's not a rhetorical flourish. It's a real question with a real answer, and the answer is nothing — there is no amount of money that buys back a compromised conscience once you've spent it. The world will always have a next contract. It will not always have a next chance to be the man who kept his word when it cost him something real.

This is where "God first" stops being a phrase you say and becomes a decision that actually costs you. It's easy to put God first when it's free — when it just means praying in the morning or being nicer to your wife. It gets expensive fast when putting Him first means turning down the deal, losing the client, disappointing the partner who thinks you're being naive. That expense is the whole test. Cheap faith never gets tested. Only the kind that costs you something proves it was real.

**On St. Thomas More:** More was Chancellor of England, one of the most powerful men in the country, and he lost everything — his position, his freedom, eventually his head — rather than sign his name to something he knew was a lie, even when the king himself demanded it. On the scaffold he called himself "the King's good servant, but God's first." He served well. He just never confused who he ultimately answered to.

**Today:** Think of one place right now where doing the right thing would cost you something real — money, a relationship, a reputation. Decide today, before the pressure is on, which way you're going to go. Write it down so future-you can't quietly renegotiate.

**Prayer:** Lord, doing the right thing here is going to cost me something real, and part of me wants to find a way around that. Give me the courage to keep my word anyway, and remind me that a clean conscience is worth more than the deal.
