# Day 13 — Small Surrenders

The rule had always been no phones at dinner. You'd said it to your kids a hundred times, usually while glancing at your own phone face-down on the table beside your plate — close enough to feel the buzz, far enough to pretend you weren't waiting for it. One night your ten-year-old asked, mid-sentence about something that happened at school, "Are you even listening?" You weren't, not really. You were three-quarters present, the other quarter somewhere in your inbox.

It bothered you enough that you tried something small the next night — you left the phone in the other room entirely, on the charger, ringer off. Twenty-two minutes of dinner. That's all it was. And it felt, honestly, a little ridiculous to make a big deal out of something so minor. Nobody was going to write a book about the courage it took to eat dinner without a phone nearby. But you noticed something: your daughter told a longer story than usual. Your wife caught your eye across the table twice, like she noticed the difference too. And you noticed your own hand, twice, reaching toward where the phone used to sit, finding nothing there.

Jesus says, almost in passing, in a parable about stewardship: "One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much, and one who is dishonest in a very little is also dishonest in much" (Luke 16:10). He's not talking about phones at dinner. He's talking about money, about trust, about the character a man reveals in the tiny unwatched moments nobody's grading him on. But the principle cuts exactly here. The size of the surrender was never the point. Twenty-two minutes without a phone isn't a spiritual achievement. What matters is that it's a rehearsal — a small, low-stakes proof to yourself that you can put something down when it's not asked of you in some dramatic, life-altering way.

That's the thing about putting God first: it rarely shows up as one enormous, cinematic decision. Mostly it shows up as a string of small ones — the phone in the other room, the extra ten minutes given instead of taken, the word held back instead of said. Get those wrong consistently and no grand gesture later will make up for it. Get those right consistently, in the small unwatched moments, and you become the kind of man who's actually capable of the big surrender when it's finally required of him.

**Today:** At your next meal with your family, put your phone in a different room — not on silent, not face-down, gone. Notice, without judging yourself, how many times you reach for where it used to be.
