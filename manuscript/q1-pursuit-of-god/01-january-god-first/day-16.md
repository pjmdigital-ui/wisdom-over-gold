# Day 16 — God First in Marriage

The argument was about something small enough that you can't even remember it clearly now — the school pickup schedule, maybe, or whose turn it was to call the insurance company. What you remember is the shape of it: your wife said something that landed wrong, you corrected her tone instead of her point, and within four minutes you were both standing in the hallway relitigating a fight you'd apparently had eleven times before, neither of you actually listening, both of you building a case.

You had the winning move. You knew it the second it occurred to you — a specific thing she'd said three weeks earlier that contradicted what she was saying now, a piece of evidence that would end the argument in your favor if you played it. You felt the pull to say it the way you feel the pull to hit send on a text you'll regret. And for a second you actually wanted that more than you wanted things to be okay between you — wanted to be right more than you wanted to be close to her.

You didn't play it. You said, instead, something closer to "I don't want to win this, I want to figure it out with you," which felt weak coming out of your mouth and turned out to be the only thing that actually worked. Her shoulders dropped. The argument didn't get resolved in that hallway, not fully, but it stopped being a fight.

Paul, writing to the Ephesians about marriage, opens the whole passage with a line that gets skipped past on the way to the more famous verses about husbands and wives: "Submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ" (Ephesians 5:21). Before any instruction about roles, there's this — mutual submission, both spouses laying something down for the other, out of reverence for someone bigger than either of them. It's not about who's the head of the household. It's about who's actually on the throne when the two of you disagree.

Here's the test: when God is genuinely first, being right stops being the point of an argument with your wife, because your allegiance isn't to your own correctness anymore — it's to Him, and to what He's asked of you toward her. That reordering doesn't make you a pushover. It makes winning the argument stop being worth what it costs you.

**Today:** Next time you're mid-argument with your wife and you feel the pull to play your best point, ask instead: "What do you need from me right now?" Then actually stop talking and listen to the answer.
