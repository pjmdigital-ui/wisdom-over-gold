# Day 22 — Too Busy for God

By Wednesday the week had already collapsed on you. Two client fires, one of them threatening to walk. A school pickup you swapped with your wife twice in three days because your calendars kept colliding. Your dad called about a procedure he's having next month and you had to cut it short because you were already ten minutes late to the next thing. You fell into bed each night having eaten dinner standing up more than once, and somewhere around Thursday you noticed you hadn't prayed — not once, not even the quick kind — since Monday.

It wasn't a decision. Nobody sat down and chose to cut God out of the week. It's just that prayer doesn't send a follow-up email when you ignore it. The client does. Your kid's teacher does. Your wife, eventually and rightly, does. God waits, quietly, at the back of a line that never seems to run out of people ahead of Him — and because He never forces His way to the front, He's always the easiest thing to lose in a week like this one.

Mark 1:35 records something easy to skip past: "And rising very early in the morning, while it was still dark, he went out and departed to a desolate place, and there he prayed." This is Jesus — with actual crowds waiting, actual sick people needing healing, actual disciples about to come looking for Him because His time was in such demand it was making headlines in the region. If anyone had a legitimate excuse to skip prayer for the sake of urgent, good, important work, it was Him that week. He didn't take the excuse. He got up before the demands could start and went first to the Father, and only then to the crowd.

That order is the whole lesson. Jesus didn't pray because His schedule was light. He prayed because He knew a full schedule is exactly when a man most needs to be anchored before it starts pulling at him. A busy week isn't a reasonable excuse to skip God — it's the specific condition prayer was built for. The men who keep this habit through jammed weeks aren't the ones with more free time than you. They're the ones who moved prayer earlier in the day than the chaos can reach, so the choice gets made before they're too depleted to make it well.

You don't need an extra hour you don't have. You need five minutes positioned before the first fire of the day gets lit, not squeezed in after, hoping there's anything left of you by then.

**Today:** Tomorrow, before you check your phone or open your email, pray for five minutes — even just the Our Father, said slowly, out loud. Do it before the day gets a vote.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I give You thanks and praise. Thank You for opening my eyes to another day in this beautiful world You have created for me. I pray You send Your Holy Spirit down upon me today, to help me be the best father, neighbor, husband, friend, businessman, and Christian I can be. Let everything I do please and glorify You. This week ran me over and You were the first thing I dropped without even deciding to. Help me get to You before the fires start tomorrow, not squeezed in after there's nothing left of me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
