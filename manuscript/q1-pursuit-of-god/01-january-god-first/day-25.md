# Day 25 — When God Says Wait

You applied for the promotion in March. It's August now, and the position is still technically "under review," which is corporate for *we haven't decided and we're not going to tell you why*. You've prayed about it more times than you can count — in the car, in the shower, at 2 a.m. when your mind won't quit running the numbers on what the raise would mean for the mortgage. Nothing has moved. No sign, no door opening, no answer that feels like an answer. Just the same silence you started with, five months deep now.

Or maybe it's not a job. Maybe it's the biopsy results that came back "we'll monitor it." Maybe it's the marriage that's better but not healed, the son who still won't call, the business that hasn't turned the corner you were promised it would turn by now. You did the faithful thing — you prayed, you worked, you waited — and the waiting just kept going. Nobody warns you how much of the Christian life is spent in a room with no clock and no exit sign.

David knew this room. He wrote Psalm 27 in the middle of real threat, surrounded by enemies, not in some tidy season of reflection, and what he lands on isn't a resolved problem — it's an instruction to himself: "Wait for the Lord; be strong, and let your heart take courage; wait for the Lord!" (Psalm 27:14). Read it closely. He says it twice, like a man talking himself down from the ledge of giving up. He doesn't say the waiting will be short. He doesn't say he understands why it's taking this long. He just tells his own heart to stay.

That's the piece we skip past. Waiting on God isn't passive — it's not the absence of a plan while you sit around hoping. It's an active posture, a decision renewed daily, to keep trusting the Giver when the gift hasn't come. A man who's white-knuckling his way through an unanswered prayer isn't failing at faith. He's living it, in the hardest place it gets lived — the gap between the asking and the answer, where you find out whether you actually believe God is good or just believed it when things were going your way.

Here's what's easy to miss in the delay: God is rarely doing nothing. He's usually doing something in you that He can't do any other way — patience you didn't have, trust you hadn't earned, a dependence on Him instead of your own timeline. The wait isn't wasted time. It's formation you can't see the shape of yet, the way you can't see a foundation once the house is built on top of it.

You don't have to feel strong to obey "be strong." You just have to stay in the room one more day.

**Today:** Name the thing you've been waiting on longest. Write today's date next to it, and one sentence: *I don't know when, but I'm staying.* Pray that sentence out loud once before bed tonight.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I give You thanks and praise. Thank You for opening my eyes to another day in this beautiful world You have created for me. I pray You send Your Holy Spirit down upon me today, to help me be the best father, neighbor, husband, friend, businessman, and Christian I can be. Let everything I do please and glorify You. I don't understand why this is taking so long, and I'm tired of the silence. Give me the strength to stay in this waiting instead of running from it, and help me trust You haven't forgotten me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
