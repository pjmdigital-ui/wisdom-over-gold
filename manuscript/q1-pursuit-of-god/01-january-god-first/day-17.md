# Day 17 — God First With Your Time

Sunday night, phone in hand, you open the calendar app to plan the week. Work blocks go in first — the 8 a.m. call, the client lunch, the two hours you've protected for the proposal that's due Friday. Then the kids: Jake's practice, the orthodontist, the school thing you already forgot the name of. Then the gym, if you're honest with yourself about actually going. You scroll through all seven days before you notice it. Not one block, not one line, not one ten-minute gap has your name on it for God. You didn't decide that. It just happened, the way a room gets dusty — nobody chose the dust, it just accumulates while you're busy with other things.

You tell yourself you'll pray "when things settle down." You've been telling yourself that for years. Things don't settle down. They just get replaced by the next full week.

Ecclesiastes 3:1 says there is "a time for every matter under heaven." The verse gets used at funerals and in framed kitchen art, but read it plainly and it's blunt: there is a time for everything, which means everything gets a time, which means what doesn't get a time doesn't happen. Time isn't a feeling you wait to have. It's a resource you allocate, the same way you allocate dollars or attention. If God has no allocated time, it's not because you love Him less than your work or your kids. It's because you never scheduled Him at all, and unscheduled things lose, every time, to things with a deadline attached.

Here's the uncomfortable math: you will find fifteen minutes for almost anything you actually decide matters. You'll find it for a scrolling session you don't even remember starting. You'll find it for a work email that could've waited till morning. The calendar doesn't lie about your priorities — it just tells the truth more plainly than you'd like. A man who says God is first but has never once blocked time for Him is describing a hope, not a practice. And hopes don't survive a busy Tuesday. Practices do.

This isn't about becoming a monk or adding guilt to an already full week. It's about giving God the same respect you give a client meeting — a fixed time, protected from the drift of "I'll get to it." Fifteen minutes with a start time survives contact with a busy day in a way that "sometime today" never does. The men who actually pray consistently aren't more disciplined than you in some general sense. They just made a decision once, on a calm night, that a busy Tuesday couldn't undo.

**Today:** Open your calendar right now and block fifteen minutes tomorrow morning, before your first work obligation, labeled simply "Prayer." Treat it like you would a call from your biggest client — non-negotiable, no rescheduling it to "later."

**Prayer:** Lord, I give you whatever time is left over instead of giving you a real block on the calendar. Help me schedule fifteen minutes for you tomorrow morning and treat it like it actually matters, because it does.
