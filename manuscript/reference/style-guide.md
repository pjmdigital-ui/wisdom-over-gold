# Style Guide — Seek First: The Four Pursuits of the Modern Catholic Man

Use this as the shared reference for every daily entry so voice and format stay consistent across the whole book.

## Core Thesis
The most important lesson of a man's life: to live to glorify and please God instead of himself — to make God the center of his world rather than being the god of his own life. Rooted in Matthew 6:33 — "But seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things shall be yours as well" (RSV-CE). Every other pursuit (Protection, Provision, Posterity) only works rightly once this one (Piety) is in place.

## The Four Pursuits (book-wide, deliberately alliterative)
1. **Piety** — Q1 (Jan/Feb/Mar). Seeking God first; ordering your life around Him rather than yourself. The foundation everything else stands on.
2. **Protection** — Q2 (Apr/May/Jun). Becoming the guardian your family, marriage, and character need.
3. **Provision** — Q3 (Jul/Aug/Sep). Stewarding work and money as calling and stewardship, not just hustle or duty.
4. **Posterity** — Q4 (Oct/Nov/Dec). Legacy in the true sense — the man who leads his household and leaves something behind.

Use these exact names (capitalized, as above) whenever referring to a pursuit by name in front matter, quarter/section headers, or cross-references. Individual month themes (e.g. January's "God First") are more specific than the quarter's pursuit name and don't need to restate it.

## Audience
Christian, specifically Catholic, fathers and family men trying to be the best father, husband, neighbor, friend, and businessman they can be. They are busy, tired some days, trying hard, and not looking for a lecture.

## Voice
- Standard devotional/reflective tone — NOT first-person "God speaking" (this is not Jesus Calling).
- Written like a man who is actually in the trenches of fatherhood and business, not a monastery. Earned wisdom, not lecture.
- Direct, warm, unsentimental. Short sentences carry weight. No academic throat-clearing, no seminary jargon.
- Address the reader as "you" in the reflection and action step. The opening scenario can be first-person ("I") or a close third-person composite ("Mark stood in his kitchen...") — vary it, don't make every single day the author's own story.
- Avoid clichés ("at the end of the day," "journey," "authentic," "intentional" used as filler). Be concrete instead of abstract.
- **Avoid the "it's not X, it's Y" / "this isn't really about X, it's about Y" rhetorical contrast construction.** This is one of the most recognizable AI-writing tells, and the first full January draft leaned on it constantly ("That's not a discipline problem. It's that..."). State things plainly and declaratively instead. Vary sentence construction day to day — don't let any single rhetorical move (including the belief-naming move below) become a repeated formula the reader can spot by day 5.
- **Don't name-drop secular sources or authors inline in the reflection prose** (no "as James Clear writes in Atomic Habits..."). Saint references are the one genre-native exception — the book's voice can name a saint the way it can't name a psychologist or a business author. Draw techniques FROM the sources in the outline/style-guide without citing them on the page; the reflection should sound like earned personal wisdom, not a book report.
- **Watch for repeated pet words across a month, not just within a single day.** "Quietly" appeared 15 times across 13 of January's 31 days — invisible day-to-day, obvious once you read the month straight through. After drafting a batch, grep the whole month for words used more than 3-4 times and vary them. This applies to any word, not just "quietly" specifically — re-check each month once it's drafted.
- **Saint/biblical-figure naming convention:** the first time a canonized saint or apostle is named within a given day's entry, use the full title — "St. Paul," "St. Augustine," "St. Thomas More." Every subsequent mention of that same person within that same entry drops the title — just "Paul," "Augustine," "More." Reset to the full title at the start of the next day's entry (each day stands alone). This applies to apostles and canonized saints (Paul, Augustine, Ignatius, Francis, Aquinas, Thomas More, etc.). It does NOT apply to Old Testament figures who aren't canonized in the usual Catholic sense (David, Joshua, Moses) — those stay as plain names or with their biblical title ("King David," "Joshua") throughout, per standard Catholic devotional convention. Flag it if a day's saint-naming situation is ambiguous rather than guessing.

## Daily Entry Format (every day, in this order)
1. **Real-world scenario** (2-4 short paragraphs) — a specific, non-generic moment: a fatherhood failure, a business decision, marriage friction, a 2am worry, a text he shouldn't have sent. Concrete details (what was said, what he was doing) beat generic description. No two entries should use the same scenario setup (job loss, kid's soccer game, etc. — vary contexts: work, marriage, parenting, friendship, solitude, money, health, faith practice itself).
2. **Name the specific belief driving the scenario** — not just the feeling or the behavior, the actual sentence the man is unconsciously operating on (e.g. *"Once I hit this number, I'll finally feel like I've made it"*). This is the book's core mechanism (see "Mindset-First Structure" below) but MUST be surfaced differently every day — sometimes stated outright, sometimes shown through what the character says to himself, sometimes asked as a question, sometimes only implied until the scripture names it. A rigid, identically-worded "here's the belief running underneath this" move repeated 31 times in a row is its own tell — vary it as much as the scenario varies.
3. **Scripture as the reframe** — one verse (cited, e.g. Matthew 6:33), woven into the reflection as the truth that replaces the false belief, not just dropped in as a floating quote block. Vary the books used across the month — don't lean only on the Gospels every day. **Every quoted verse MUST be the exact, verbatim RSV-CE (Revised Standard Version, Catholic Edition) text, verified against a live online source at drafting time — never quoted from memory.** This book's own manuscript initially had this wrong across nearly every January entry (text that blended NIV/ESV/KJV phrasing rather than actual RSV-CE wording), caught only when the author stress-tested one quote. Before drafting a day, look up the verse (e.g. biblestudytools.com/rsv/[book]/[chapter]-[verse].html, or a source explicitly labeled RSV-CE such as biblia.com resourceName=rsvce or coursebible.com's RSVCE pages) and copy the wording exactly, including its archaic diction ("thou/thee," "yea," etc. — RSV-CE does not modernize these). Cite every quote with its translation: "(Matthew 6:33, RSV-CE)" for parenthetical citations, or "(RSV-CE)" appended right after an inline verse reference (e.g. "Mark 1:35 (RSV-CE) records..."). If the reflection prose leans on a specific word or phrase from the quote, double-check that word actually survives in the verified RSV-CE wording — several January fixes required also adjusting a nearby sentence that had quietly assumed NIV phrasing. Romans 12:1-2 is the book's standing anchor pair ("present your bodies as a living sacrifice... be transformed by the renewal of your mind") — it doesn't need to be quoted every day, but the body-offered/mind-renewed mechanism it describes is the DNA of every day's structure.
4. **Short reflection** (2-4 short paragraphs) — connects the scenario and scripture to the day's theme. This is where the "so what" lives. Should feel earned, not preachy. It's fine to be blunt about the reader's likely resistance to the point.
5. **Saint reference** — ONLY where it fits naturally (not forced into every entry). Follow the day-by-day outline's saint assignments — they're placed where the fit is real, roughly a quarter to a third of days in a month, not evenly distributed. One or two sentences connecting the saint's own life to the day's point.
6. **The new belief, stated and spoken** — the action step names the specific replacement belief (in the reader's own words, written and/or said out loud), not just a behavior to perform. The small physical action (write it, say it, block the calendar, make the call) follows FROM the new belief — it's evidence of it, not a substitute for it.
7. **One concrete action step** — a specific "do this today" line, built on the belief-replacement above. Must be doable in one day, specific enough to actually act on (not "spend more time in prayer" but "before you open your phone tomorrow morning, say the Our Father slowly, out loud, once").

## Recurring Threads (book-wide, not confined to one month — weave in where a day genuinely fits, never forced)
These are the author's own core convictions, meant to surface periodically across all 12 months, most concentrated in the quarter noted but not exclusive to it:
- **Do hard/uncomfortable things, especially physically, every day.** Primary home: Protection (Q2), but recurs everywhere. Anchors: Romans 12:1 ("present your bodies as a living sacrifice... your spiritual worship"), 1 Corinthians 9:27 ("I pommel my body and subdue it"). Sources (don't name inline): David Goggins, Jocko Willink.
- **Keep your promises and commitments.** Primary home: April (Integrity). Anchor: Matthew 5:37, verified — "Let what you say be simply 'Yes' or 'No'; anything more than this comes from evil" (RSV-CE). Source: Stephen Covey.
- **Communication — internal (self-talk) and external.** Internal ties to the book's own mind-renewal mechanism (Romans 12:2, Philippians 4:8). External lives mainly in June (Brotherhood) and October (Marriage). Source: Marshall Rosenberg (Nonviolent Communication) — technique only, not named inline.
- **Master your body language / presence.** Primary home: Protection and Fatherhood. Anchor cautiously in Romans 12:1 (the body offered, not decorated) — avoid citing "power posing" research (Amy Cuddy), which has real replication problems; lean on more solid nonverbal-communication research (Paul Ekman) if a secular anchor is needed at all.
- **Master one thing at a time.** Already present in January (Day 11, Luke 10:41-42, "one thing is necessary"). Primary home going forward: July (Vocation). Anchor: Philippians 3:13. Sources: Cal Newport, Greg McKeown.
- **Pride is devastating; humility is the antidote, not weakness.** A core personal conviction of the author's, scripturally well-anchored. Already present in January (Day 23, James 4:6 — "God opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble" — and lightly in Day 6). Primary home going forward: April (Integrity), but recurs everywhere, since pride is the belief-distortion underneath most of the other threads (control, comparison, defensiveness). Additional anchors: Proverbs 16:18 ("pride goes before destruction"), 1 Peter 5:5-6, Luke 18:9-14 (the Pharisee and the tax collector), Daniel 4 (Nebuchadnezzar's fall) as a dramatic case study. Verify exact RSV-CE wording live before using any of these beyond James 4:6, which is already verified.
- **Systems and processes over end goals.** Primary home: July/August (Provision). Anchor: Proverbs 21:5, verified — "The plans of the diligent lead surely to abundance, but every one who is hasty comes only to want" (RSV-CE). Source: James Clear's "you fall to the level of your systems" thesis — technique only, not named inline.

## Your List of Truths (recurring reference, not a one-time page)
`00-front-matter/your-list-of-truths.md` teaches a specific practice (a personal list of positive, true, God-evidencing statements to return to when discouraged — anchored in 1 Samuel 17:37 and Philippians 4:8's "true AND lovely" filter) and includes the author's own list as the worked example. Days that land on discouragement, waiting, or a man feeling like he hasn't made progress should point back to this page as a concrete tool rather than re-explaining the practice from scratch. In January that's Days 7, 25, and 29.
6. **A short prayer.** The book's front matter includes a standalone "A Daily Prayer" page (the fixed thanksgiving/Holy Spirit prayer, prayed once each morning before that day's entry) — do NOT repeat that fixed block inside daily entries; it would be repetitive across 365 days. Each day's own prayer is short and follows this structure instead:
   - **Address:** open by addressing God as either "Lord God Almighty," or "Father in Heaven," — alternate between the two across consecutive days (odd days use "Lord God Almighty," even days use "Father in Heaven,").
   - **Day-specific petition (immediately after the address, same sentence — lowercase the first word unless it's "I" or a proper noun, since it continues the sentence after the vocative comma):** one to three sentences, in the reader's own voice, tied directly to that day's specific scenario/struggle — not a generic line reusable across days. Capitalize every "You/Your/Yours/Yourself" that refers to God. Plain, direct, prayed-not-performed language; avoid flowery or archaic phrasing ("thee/thou" forbidden).
   - **Fixed closing (verbatim every day):** "I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen."

## Length
Target ~500-650 words per entry (~4-5 minute read).

## Formatting per file
```
# Day [N] — [Short Title]

[Scenario paragraphs]

[Reflection paragraphs, scripture woven in, cite as (Matthew 6:33)]

[Saint paragraph, if used — start with **"On [Saint Name]:"** in bold as a lead-in]

**Today:** [One concrete action step, one or two sentences.]

**Prayer:** [Lord God Almighty, / Father in Heaven,] [day-specific petition, 1-3 sentences, lowercase first word unless "I"/proper noun]. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
```

The fixed thanksgiving/Holy Spirit prayer that used to open every daily prayer now lives once, in front matter, as its own page — `00-front-matter/a-daily-prayer.md`. Don't reintroduce it into individual days.

## Theological guardrails
- Catholic in tenor: comfortable referencing the sacraments (confession, the Eucharist, Mass), saints, and Church teaching as lived practice, not as apologetics lectures.
- Not liturgical-calendar-bound — don't tie entries to specific feast days or seasons (no "today is the feast of..." framing), though saints can still be referenced anytime.
- Scripture must be both accurate in substance AND exact in wording — see the RSV-CE verification requirement above. A verse that says roughly the right thing in the wrong translation's words is not acceptable; it must be the real RSV-CE text, verified live, every time.
- The book's translation is RSV-CE throughout. See `00-front-matter/scripture-notice.md` for the citation convention and the print-permissions note (securing actual written permission from the copyright holder is a real pre-print requirement, not just a courtesy credit).

## Standing pre-commit audit (every month, before committing)
When a month is drafted in parallel batches, run all of these before commit — not just when something looks off:
1. **RSV-CE citation check** — every quoted verse cites "(Book Chapter:Verse, RSV-CE)"; `grep -L "RSV-CE" day-*.md` should return nothing.
2. **Pet-word sweep** — `grep -c "quietly" day-*.md` and a scan for other AI-writing tells (delve, tapestry, underscore, testament, myriad, vibrant, realm, navigate, profound, unwavering, boundaries, journey, authentic, intentional). A handful of natural, varied uses across a month is fine; a repeated tic (10+ uses of one word) is not.
3. **"It's not X, it's Y" sweep** — `grep -niE "isn't (really )?(a|about|an)[a-z ]+\. It'?s" day-*.md`. Every hit needs a rewrite to a plain declarative statement.
4. **Saint/apostle naming** — for every saint or apostle referenced in the month, confirm the first mention per entry carries the full title and every subsequent mention in that same entry is bare. Biblical-but-not-canonized figures (David, Joshua, Moses) never get "St." **Check body text only — a saint's name in a day's title (e.g. "Day 11 — The Man Who Formed Augustine") is a header, not prose, and correctly stays untitled there; don't flag it as a violation.** This applies especially to entries that reference a saint in passing without a dedicated "**On St. X:**" callout that day — the narrative's own first mention has to carry the full title itself, since there's no callout label to do it.
5. **Character-name collision scan.** Parallel agent batches routinely reuse the same protagonist first name without knowing it — this has happened in every multi-batch month drafted so far. Run a script like this after all batches land, before commit:
   ```python
   import re, glob
   from collections import defaultdict, Counter
   STOPWORDS = {...}  # common words, book names, days of week, saint name parts, pronouns
   name_files = defaultdict(set)
   for f in sorted(glob.glob("<month folder>/day-*.md")):
       text = open(f, encoding="utf-8").read()
       c = Counter(re.findall(r"\b[A-Z][a-z]{2,}\b", text))
       for w, n in c.items():
           if w not in STOPWORDS and n >= 2:
               name_files[w].add(f)
   for name, files in name_files.items():
       if len(files) > 1:
           print(name, "->", sorted(files))
   ```
   Fix by keeping the earliest occurrence of a name and renaming the later one(s) — verify the replacement name isn't used ANYWHERE ELSE in the book already (not just the current month), since a fix in one month has collided with a different month's character before. Re-run the scan after fixing to catch second-order collisions (a fix that accidentally matches a different existing name).
