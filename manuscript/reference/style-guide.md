# Style Guide — Seek First: The Four Pursuits of the Modern Catholic Man

Use this as the shared reference for every daily entry so voice and format stay consistent across the whole book.

## Core Thesis
The most important lesson of a man's life: to live to glorify and please God instead of himself — to make God the center of his world rather than being the god of his own life. Rooted in Matthew 6:33 — "But seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you." Every other pursuit (protecting, providing, leading) only works rightly once this one is in place.

## Audience
Christian, specifically Catholic, fathers and family men trying to be the best father, husband, neighbor, friend, and businessman they can be. They are busy, tired some days, trying hard, and not looking for a lecture.

## Voice
- Standard devotional/reflective tone — NOT first-person "God speaking" (this is not Jesus Calling).
- Written like a man who is actually in the trenches of fatherhood and business, not a monastery. Earned wisdom, not lecture.
- Direct, warm, unsentimental. Short sentences carry weight. No academic throat-clearing, no seminary jargon.
- Address the reader as "you" in the reflection and action step. The opening scenario can be first-person ("I") or a close third-person composite ("Mark stood in his kitchen...") — vary it, don't make every single day the author's own story.
- Avoid clichés ("at the end of the day," "journey," "authentic," "intentional" used as filler). Be concrete instead of abstract.

## Daily Entry Format (every day, in this order)
1. **Real-world scenario** (2-4 short paragraphs) — a specific, non-generic moment: a fatherhood failure, a business decision, marriage friction, a 2am worry, a text he shouldn't have sent. Concrete details (what was said, what he was doing) beat generic description. No two entries should use the same scenario setup (job loss, kid's soccer game, etc. — vary contexts: work, marriage, parenting, friendship, solitude, money, health, faith practice itself).
2. **Scripture tie-in** — one verse (cited, e.g. Matthew 6:33), woven into the reflection, not just dropped in as a floating quote block. Vary the books used across the month — don't lean only on the Gospels every day.
3. **Short reflection** (2-4 short paragraphs) — connects the scenario and scripture to the day's theme. This is where the "so what" lives. Should feel earned, not preachy. It's fine to be blunt about the reader's likely resistance to the point.
4. **Saint reference** — ONLY where it fits naturally (not forced into every entry). Follow the day-by-day outline's saint assignments — they're placed where the fit is real, roughly a quarter to a third of days in a month, not evenly distributed. One or two sentences connecting the saint's own life to the day's point.
5. **One concrete action step** — a specific "do this today" line. Must be doable in one day, specific enough to actually act on (not "spend more time in prayer" but "before you open your phone tomorrow morning, say the Our Father slowly, out loud, once").

## Length
Target ~500-650 words per entry (~4-5 minute read).

## Formatting per file
```
# Day [N] — [Short Title]

[Scenario paragraphs]

[Reflection paragraphs, scripture woven in, cite as (Matthew 6:33)]

[Saint paragraph, if used — start with **"On [Saint Name]:"** in bold as a lead-in]

**Today:** [One concrete action step, one or two sentences.]
```

## Theological guardrails
- Catholic in tenor: comfortable referencing the sacraments (confession, the Eucharist, Mass), saints, and Church teaching as lived practice, not as apologetics lectures.
- Not liturgical-calendar-bound — don't tie entries to specific feast days or seasons (no "today is the feast of..." framing), though saints can still be referenced anytime.
- Scripture should be accurate — use real verses that actually say what's being claimed. When in doubt, choose a well-known, clearly-fitting verse over an obscure one force-fit to the theme.
