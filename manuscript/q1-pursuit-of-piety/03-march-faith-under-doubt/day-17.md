# Day 17 — Gratitude Isn't Denial

I'm standing in the hallway outside my father's hospice room, holding a coffee I haven't drunk, when my sister texts: *Try to focus on the good — we still have time with him.* I read it twice and feel something close to anger. She isn't wrong. But right now, being told to be grateful sounds like being asked to lie — to stand next to a man who is dying and pretend this is somehow fine.

That's the trap. Somewhere along the way I picked up the idea that gratitude and grief can't share a room — that thanking God means telling Him everything is okay, and since it isn't, the honest move is to skip the thanking part until the pain passes. So I skip it. I go quiet on gratitude the moment life gets hard, which is exactly the moment I need it most.

The prophet Habakkuk had every reason to skip it too. He's writing into real devastation — crops failed, herds gone, the whole economy of survival collapsed — and he doesn't soften a single detail. "Though the fig tree do not blossom, nor fruit be on the vines, the produce of the olive fail and the fields yield no food, the flock be cut off from the fold and there be no herd in the stalls, yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation" (Habakkuk 3:17-18, RSV-CE). Read that again slowly. He names every loss by category before he says one word about rejoicing. Nothing is minimized. Nothing is spun.

That order matters. Habakkuk doesn't rejoice because the fig tree bloomed after all, or because he talked himself into seeing the famine differently. He rejoices while the fig tree is still bare. Gratitude here isn't a report on circumstances — it's a decision about where he's still standing. The losses stay real. God's goodness stays real. Both, at once, without either one canceling the other out.

I've been treating gratitude like an assessment: things are either good enough to thank God for, or they're not. But it was never a scorecard. It's a posture toward a Person, and a person can hold your hand at a hospital bed while still meaning it when they say thank you for the years they got. Denial says the illness isn't really happening. Gratitude says it is happening, and God is still God in the middle of it.

**Today:** Write two sentences, one under the other, and read them out loud. First, name the actual loss you're facing — plainly, without softening it. Then write: *I can call this exactly what it is and still choose to rejoice in the Lord.* Say both out loud, even the second one, even if you don't feel it yet.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I don't want to pretend this hurt isn't real, and I don't want the hurt to talk me out of thanking You either. Hold both of those in me at once — my grief and my gratitude — and let neither one crowd out the other. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
