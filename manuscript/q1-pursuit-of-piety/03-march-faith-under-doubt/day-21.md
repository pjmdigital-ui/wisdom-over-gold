# Day 21 — The Discipline of Saying Thank You Out Loud

Miguel's wife catches him off guard on a Tuesday night, drying a pan she'd already dried. "You never actually say it," she tells him. He asks what she means. "Thank you. You never say it. You show up, you work, you pay for things — but I can't remember the last time you just said the words." He wants to defend himself: he shows his gratitude, he provides, isn't that louder than words? But he can't remember the last time either.

Miguel is not a stingy man or an ungrateful one. Ask him privately and he'll tell you he's thankful for his wife's patience, his son's laugh, the health that lets him coach Little League on weekends. All of it is real to him. None of it has ever left his mouth. Somewhere he picked up the assumption that felt gratitude is finished gratitude — that if the thanks is genuine on the inside, it doesn't especially matter whether anyone ever hears it.

St. Paul closes out a section of instructions to the Colossians with a line easy to read past: "And whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father through him" (Colossians 3:17, RSV-CE). Word *and* deed — he doesn't let one stand in for the other. A life can be full of grateful deeds and still be missing grateful words, and Paul doesn't treat that as close enough. The thanks is supposed to leave the chest and become something spoken, something the people around you can actually hear and be built up by.

This is the last day of a week spent training your eyes to see what you already have — the fig tree still bare and the God still worth rejoicing in, the comparison habit, the small unnoticed faithfulness, the provision you didn't manufacture alone. All of it can stay a private conviction forever if you never say a word of it out loud. Gratitude that stays internal is real, but it's incomplete. It was never meant to just sit in you. It was meant to reach somebody.

**Today:** Say thank you out loud to one specific person for one specific thing — not "thanks for everything," but the actual detail: *thank you for staying up with me last night when I couldn't sleep. Thank you for the way you handled that phone call with your mother.* Say it to their face or over the phone, not in a text. Let them hear it land.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've kept so much thanks locked up inside me that the people who deserve to hear it never have. Loosen my tongue to say what I actually feel. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
