# Day 30 — The Restless Heart, Revisited

You crossed the finish line at 4:47, watch beeping, a medal someone hung around your neck without you quite registering the weight of it. Five months of 5 a.m. alarms, a training plan taped inside a kitchen cabinet, a hundred mornings of talking yourself out of bed before your body agreed to go. You'd told yourself for most of those mornings that finishing would feel like arrival — that the medal would be proof, finally, of something you'd been trying to prove for years.

You stood there in the finisher's chute, legs shaking, and waited to feel it. What came instead was a strange, flat quiet, the kind that shows up after a party when someone's already stacking the chairs. Familiar, somehow, though you couldn't place why at first. It took until the drive home to place it: you'd felt this exact quiet before, ninety days ago, on a morning that had nothing to do with running.

That's the pattern worth naming honestly, now that a whole quarter has given you the distance to see it: the deal, the medal, the number on the scale, the promotion — the object changes, but the belief underneath doesn't. *This one will finally be enough to make me feel like I've arrived.* You've now tested that belief twice in ninety days, in two completely different arenas, and gotten the same flat answer both times. That's not bad luck. That's the belief being wrong.

Jesus named the alternative on Day 1 of this book, and it's worth hearing again now that a full quarter has had a chance to work on you: "But seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things shall be yours as well" (Matthew 6:33, RSV-CE). Ninety days ago that verse was a claim you hadn't tested yet. It isn't anymore. You've watched achievement fail to fill the hole twice now — once in a truck in a driveway, once in a finisher's chute — and you've also watched, across January, February, and March, what it looks like to reorder the center instead of chasing a bigger version of the same prize. Some mornings you did it. Some mornings you didn't. Both kinds of morning taught you something a medal never could.

St. Augustine named this restlessness sixteen centuries ago, after spending years chasing status and approval with the same certainty you brought to that finish line: "Our heart is restless until it rests in You." He wasn't describing a problem you solve once. He was describing the actual shape of a human heart — built with a hole only God fits, so that anything else you pour into it, however good, however hard-won, sits there and eventually goes flat. The medal isn't the problem. Expecting the medal to do God's job is.

Here's what's different about hearing this on Day 90 instead of Day 1: you're not guessing anymore. You've run the experiment. Piety — the pursuit this whole quarter has been building — was never about feeling holy or having your doubts resolved. It was about relocating the center, over and over, every time you catch it drifting back to your own name. That relocation is the whole practice, and it doesn't graduate. It just gets more familiar.

**Today:** Write one sentence: what are you chasing right now, ninety days after you first asked yourself that question in January? Underneath it, write: *I already know how this ends if I make it my god. I'm choosing to seek Him first instead — not because the restlessness is gone, but because I finally recognize it when it shows up.* Keep it where January's sentence still sits.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, ninety days in, my heart still goes looking for something to fill it besides You, and now I recognize the shape of that search when it starts. Thank You for a quarter that taught me to see it faster. Keep drawing this restless heart back to Yours. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
