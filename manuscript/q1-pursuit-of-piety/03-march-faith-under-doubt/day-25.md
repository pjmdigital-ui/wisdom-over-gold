# Day 25 — Gratitude for the Faith You Didn't Earn

Ray keeps a spreadsheet. Not for money — for everything. Workouts logged, sales calls tracked, even a column for "prayer" with a checkbox he fills in most mornings. He built the business that way, one disciplined row at a time, and it worked. So when his daughter asked him at dinner why he believed in God, his first instinct was to answer like it was a line item: because I show up, because I put in the work, because I've earned it.

He caught himself mid-sentence and didn't finish it. He wasn't sure the sentence was true.

Ray runs his faith the way he runs his business — as an output of effort. Skip enough mornings and the account feels overdrawn; string together a good streak and he feels, however he'd never say it out loud, a little more entitled to God's attention than the guy who doesn't check the box. It's the same belief that built his business, aimed at something a spreadsheet was never built to hold: *my standing with God is a number I'm keeping current.*

St. Paul spent the first half of his life certain his own record was exactly that — a ledger he could point to. Then he met the risen Christ on the road to Damascus and spent the rest of his life unwriting the math. What he landed on, writing to a church he founded, cuts the legs out from under every version of Ray's spreadsheet: "For by grace you have been saved through faith; and this is not your own doing, it is the gift of God — not because of works, lest any man should boast" (Ephesians 2:8-9, RSV-CE). Not a mostly-grace, partly-earned arrangement. Not a discount for the disciplined. Gift, all the way down, so that nobody — not the man with the perfect streak, not the man who missed a month — gets to point at his own effort as the reason he's standing there.

That should be relief, not deflation. Ray's faith was never a debt he's servicing with better behavior, and it was never owed to him either — which means it was never his to lose by missing a checkbox, and never his to boast about by hitting one. The discipline still matters, but somebody handed him the room he's standing in, and gratitude is the only honest response to a gift you didn't earn — not pride in your own diligence, not anxiety about your streak.

**Today:** Say out loud, before you open the spreadsheet or the to-do list: *This faith is a gift, not a wage I earned. I didn't buy my way into it, and I can't buy my way out of needing it.* Then thank God for it specifically — not for what you did this week, for the fact that you believe at all.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've treated my faith like something I earn one disciplined morning at a time, and I know that's not what You gave me. Thank You for a gift I didn't buy and can't lose by missing a day. Keep me grateful instead of proud, and grateful instead of anxious. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
