# March Outline — Faith Under Doubt / Gratitude & Legacy

31 days. Closing month of Q1 (Piety) — sets up the transition into Q2 (Protection) starting in April. Two braided sub-themes per the original brief: honest doubt examined (not suppressed or hidden), and gratitude/legacy as what grounds a man once the doubt has been faced rather than avoided.

Belief being dismantled, doubt half: *"Having doubts means my faith isn't real — a good Christian doesn't doubt."* Reframe: *"Faith examined honestly grows stronger; doubt brought to God is different from doubt nursed alone in silence."*

Belief being dismantled, gratitude/legacy half: *"What I don't have yet defines my life more than what God has already given."* Reframe: *"Gratitude reorders what I actually see; the legacy that matters is the faith and character handed down, not what's achieved."*

Arc: Week 1 names the instinct to hide or suppress doubt. Week 2 works through doubt using real figures (Thomas, Newman) who show examined doubt strengthening rather than destroying faith. Week 3 turns to gratitude as a discipline, not a mood. Week 4 turns gratitude forward into legacy — what a man hands down, prayers he'll never see answered, the faith passed to his children. Days 29-31 close the entire first quarter (Piety) and explicitly bridge into April/Protection.

Every day follows the mindset-first structure in the style guide exactly: scenario → name the specific false belief → scripture as the reframe (verify exact RSV-CE wording live, never from memory) → saint where assigned → new belief written/spoken → small action → prayer (alternating address, fixed closing). No "it's not X, it's Y" constructions. Vary belief-reveal technique across your batch. St. [Name] full title on first mention per entry, bare name after. Watch for repeated pet words across the whole month.

Two verses below are already verified elsewhere in this book and can be reused verbatim without re-searching (but do not alter a word):
- Psalm 103:2 (RSV-CE): "Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits."
- Matthew 6:33 (RSV-CE): "But seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things shall be yours as well."

Note on Day 27 (Romans 8:28): this verse is one of the most-misused in casual Christian speech, often deployed as a glib "everything happens for a reason." Handle it honestly — acknowledge the real tension of applying it to genuine loss or unresolved suffering, in the spirit of how February handled Job and unanswered "why." Don't let it read as a platitude.
