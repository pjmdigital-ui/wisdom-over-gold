# Day 9 — Repentance Isn't Just for Sinners

You hadn't been to confession in eleven years. You knew the number because you'd done the math in the car more than once, usually after some argument with your wife that left you saying things you didn't mean, or after a week where you'd looked at your phone more than you'd looked at your kids. Eleven years. Long enough that you'd half-convinced yourself you didn't need it — you weren't robbing banks, you went to Mass most Sundays, you were, by any reasonable measure, a decent man.

Then a friend mentioned he went monthly. Not because he was in crisis. Just because it was Tuesday and that's what he did. You laughed it off in the moment, then thought about it the whole drive home. Decent wasn't the bar. You knew that. You'd just built a life where nobody was checking.

You went on a Thursday, at a parish where nobody knew your name. Sat in the pew rehearsing what to say like you were prepping for a difficult client call. And when you finally said it out loud — the pride, the shortcuts, the years of running your family like a business you were CEO of instead of a covenant you were part of — nothing dramatic happened. No lightning. Just a quiet man in a small room saying, "I absolve you," and you walking out into the parking lot feeling lighter than you had in years, embarrassed at how long you'd avoided something so small.

John writes it plainly: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, and will forgive our sins and cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9, RSV-CE). Not "if we're bad enough." Not "if we've hit bottom." If we confess. The verse doesn't require a rock-bottom story — it requires honesty.

That's the part men miss. We treat confession like a fire extinguisher — something for emergencies, for the guy who really messed up. So the quietly proud man, the moderately selfish man, the man who's fine on paper, never goes. He doesn't think he qualifies. But putting God first isn't a one-time reordering you do at rock bottom and then coast on. It's a discipline you return to, again and again, because self keeps creeping back onto the throne in small, deniable ways — the way you talk to your wife when you're tired, the way you cut corners you'd never admit to cutting. Repentance isn't the emergency exit. It's the maintenance schedule.

You don't need a crisis to walk back through that door. You need honesty about the eleven years, or the eleven days, since you last let anyone — God included — tell you the truth about yourself.

**Today:** Find the confession times at a parish near you — even one you've never been to — and go this week. If it's genuinely been years, don't overthink the wording. Start with, "It's been a long time," and let the priest help you from there.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, It's been too long since I let You tell me the truth about myself. I've been hiding behind "decent" instead of being honest with You. Give me the courage to walk through that door this week and say it out loud. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
