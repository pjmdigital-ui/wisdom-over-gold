# Day 9 — Repentance Isn't Just for Sinners

You hadn't been to confession in eleven years. You knew the number because you'd done the math in the car more than once, usually after some argument with your wife that left you saying things you didn't mean, or after a week where you'd looked at your phone more than you'd looked at your kids. Eleven years. Long enough that you'd built a quiet case for yourself: you weren't robbing banks, you went to Mass most Sundays, you were, by any reasonable measure, a decent man.

Then a friend mentioned he went monthly. Not because he was in crisis. Just because it was Tuesday and that's what he did. You laughed it off in the moment, then thought about it the whole drive home. Here's what you'd actually been telling yourself, if you ever said it out loud: *I'm not bad enough for this. Confession is for men with something to confess — real sins, headline sins. Mine don't qualify.*

You went on a Thursday, at a parish where nobody knew your name. Sat in the pew rehearsing what to say like you were prepping for a difficult client call. And when you finally said it out loud — the pride, the shortcuts, the years of running your family like a business you were CEO of instead of a covenant you were part of — nothing dramatic happened. No lightning. Just a quiet man in a small room saying, "I absolve you," and you walking out into the parking lot feeling lighter than you had in years.

John writes it plainly: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, and will forgive our sins and cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9, RSV-CE). Read that again for the qualifier you'd been adding on your own. There isn't one. Not "if we've hit bottom." Not "if it's serious enough." If we confess. The verse asks for honesty, not a body count.

Men treat confession like a fire extinguisher — something for emergencies, for the guy who really messed up. The quietly proud man, the moderately selfish man, the man who's fine on paper, decides he doesn't qualify and never goes. Self keeps creeping back onto the throne in small, deniable ways — the tone you take with your wife when you're tired, the corners you cut that you'd never admit to cutting — which is why a man returns to reordering his life around God again and again, on purpose, long before anything qualifies as a crisis.

You don't need a crisis to walk back through that door. You need honesty about the eleven years, or the eleven days, since you last let anyone — God included — tell you the truth about yourself.

**Today:** Write one sentence and mean it: *I don't have to be bad enough. I only have to be honest.* Then find the confession times at a parish near you — even one you've never been to — and go this week. If it's genuinely been years, don't overthink the wording. Start with, "It's been a long time," and let the priest help you from there.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've been hiding behind "decent" instead of being honest with You. Help me believe I don't have to be bad enough to need You — I only have to be honest. Give me the courage to walk through that door this week and say it out loud. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
