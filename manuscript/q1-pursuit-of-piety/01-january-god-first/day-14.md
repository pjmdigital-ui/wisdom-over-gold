# Day 14 — The Man You're Becoming

You were sitting on the back steps watching your two boys play some invented game in the yard — half baseball, half wrestling, rules that changed every thirty seconds and applied only to them. Your older one, seven, was narrating the whole thing in a voice you recognized immediately, because it was yours. The same clipped, impatient way you talk when you're frustrated. The same phrase — "come on, let's go, let's go" — you use when you're rushing everyone out the door in the morning, stripped of whatever context made it forgivable in you and now just sitting there, bare, in a seven-year-old's mouth.

You watched a little longer, and a question sat down next to you that you hadn't invited: when did you decide the unguarded version of you didn't count? You'd been assuming your kids were mostly shaped by what you told them. They weren't. They were being shaped by what you actually did with your Sundays, your evenings, your attention — the parts of you that ran on autopilot, where you weren't performing for anyone, where you'd decided, without ever quite meaning to, that nobody was grading you closely enough for it to matter.

Near the end of his life, Joshua gathered the people of Israel and gave them a choice — serve the gods of the surrounding nations, the old gods of their ancestors, or serve the Lord. He didn't just ask them to decide. He told them where he stood: "As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD" (Joshua 24:15, RSV-CE). It's a household declaration, made once, out loud, in front of everyone. Joshua understood something you'd been avoiding on the back steps: a man's choice about who sits at the center of his life doesn't stay private. It becomes the water his household swims in, whether he announces it or not — and it becomes that water especially in the moments he never planned on anyone watching.

Your kids are grading the Tuesday-night version of you, the one scrolling on the couch, the one who thinks the unguarded moments are off the record. They aren't off the record. You don't get to opt out of modeling something. The only choice left to you is what.

**On St. Thomas Aquinas:** Born into Italian nobility, Thomas was expected by his family to become a powerful abbot, a path that would have brought wealth and prestige to their name. Instead he insisted on joining the mendicant Dominicans — a choice so unwelcome that his own brothers kidnapped and imprisoned him for over a year trying to break his resolve. He held firm, subordinating both his family's ambitions and his own considerable brilliance to what he understood God was calling him toward, and became one of the greatest minds the Church has ever produced.

**Today:** Watch your kids for ten unguarded minutes — playing, talking to each other, not performing for you. Notice one thing they're modeling that they picked up from you, good or bad. Then say out loud: "What I do when no one's grading me still counts — because they're always watching." Name honestly what you'll do differently tonight because of it.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, my kids are watching what I do, not what I say, and I don't like everything I just saw reflected back at me. Help me believe that the unguarded moments still count, and become in them the kind of man I actually want my sons to become. Let my Tuesday nights match my Sunday words. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
