# Day 29 — Starting Over, Again

Twenty-eight days into trying to put God first, and you snapped at your wife over something that didn't deserve it — a dish left in the sink, a schedule mix-up, something so small you can barely justify the tone you used. Sitting in your truck in the garage afterward, because you needed a minute before going back inside, the thought that actually stung was this: *I should be past this by now.* Twenty-eight days of trying, and here you are again, same old pattern, same old you.

Say that thought out loud and you can hear what it's actually claiming: that growth is a straight line, that twenty-eight real days should have made you measurably, permanently different — immune by now to the impatience that used to run you. When you're not, it tells you the whole month was fake. You weren't actually changing. You were performing for a few weeks and the real you is leaking back through.

Scripture doesn't describe growth that way, and it definitely doesn't describe mercy that way. Jeremiah, writing in the middle of watching his own city fall apart, in a book that's largely one long lament, still lands here: "The steadfast love of the Lord never ceases, his mercies never come to an end; they are new every morning; great is thy faithfulness" (Lamentations 3:22-23, RSV-CE). Jeremiah is reporting that God's mercy resets every single morning regardless of what kind of man showed up the day before. Today's relapse doesn't decide anything by itself. Tomorrow morning does.

Nobody grows in a straight line — not the men who seem to have it together, not the saints whose polished stories you read after their worst days had already been edited out. Growth is relapse and return, relapse and return — the return itself is the growth, not evidence it was fake. A man who falls back into an old pattern on day twenty-eight and gets back up on day twenty-nine is doing exactly what putting God first looks like over a lifetime — not a state you reach and hold, but a direction you keep choosing, one reset morning at a time.

You don't need a better twenty-eight days behind you. You need a willingness to start again this morning, which is precisely what's on offer.

**Today:** Go back to whoever you snapped at, wronged, or let down recently and say it plainly: "I was wrong, and I'm sorry." No explanation, no justification. Then, if the old thought comes back tonight — *nothing's really changed* — open your list of truths and read it anyway. It won't argue with the thought. It'll just remind you what's already true regardless of today's stumble.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I fell back into the same old pattern again, and I'm tired of thinking I should be past it by now. Thank You that Your mercy resets every morning regardless — help me get back up today instead of staying down in the shame of it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
