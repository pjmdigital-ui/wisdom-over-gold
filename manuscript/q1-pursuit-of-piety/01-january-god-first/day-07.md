# Day 7 — The Ache Underneath

It hit him on a Sunday afternoon, of all times, sitting in a lawn chair with a beer he didn't really want, watching his kids run through the sprinkler in a yard he owned outright. Good marriage. Healthy kids. A business that finally didn't keep him up at night the way it used to. By every measure he'd have used at twenty-five to define making it, he'd made it. And there it was anyway — that same low ache under his ribs, present and unbothered by any of his circumstances, like it had been waiting for a quiet moment to remind him it never actually left.

He almost felt guilty for it. What right did he have to feel unsettled with a life this good? But the ache wasn't asking permission, and it wasn't interested in his gratitude list. It just sat there, patient, the way it always had — through the lean years and the good ones alike, unmoved by whether things were going well.

The psalmist knew this ache by name and didn't dress it up: "As a hart longs for flowing streams, so longs my soul for thee, O God. My soul thirsts for God, for the living God" (Psalm 42:1-2, RSV-CE). What's remarkable about that psalm isn't the language of thirst — it's where it shows up. Not in a lament about circumstances falling apart. It's a soul-deep hunger that exists independent of how life is currently going, because it was never actually about circumstances in the first place.

That's the piece most men miss, chasing the next fix for a feeling that isn't circumstantial. You can solve your marriage, your finances, your health, and the ache doesn't move, because the ache was never a symptom of any of those things being wrong. It's a symptom of something deeper being unaddressed — the God-shaped space every man carries whether he's rich or broke, married or single, thriving or barely holding on. Good circumstances can quiet the noise around the ache. They cannot fill the space itself. Only God fits there.

That Sunday afternoon wasn't a malfunction in an otherwise good life. It was an invitation, arriving at the one moment he had nothing left to distract himself with. The ache is doing its job. It's just pointing you somewhere the sprinkler and the lawn chair were never going to be able to reach.

**Today:** The next time you notice that low ache — in a good moment, not just a hard one — don't reach for a distraction. Sit with it for sixty seconds and say simply, "God, I know this is You." Let that be the whole prayer.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I feel that ache even in the good moments, and I know now it was never about my circumstances. Thank You for not letting me settle for anything smaller than You to fill it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
