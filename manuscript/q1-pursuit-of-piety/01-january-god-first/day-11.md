# Day 11 — The Kingdom Before the Checklist

You'd blocked six-thirty to six-fifty on your calendar. Twenty minutes, coffee still hot, before the house woke up — just you, a Bible, and a few minutes of quiet. You'd been doing it for almost three weeks, and it had actually started to feel like something, not just another box.

Then Monday hit. You opened your laptop at six-thirty "just to check one thing" before you sat down to pray, and the one thing was a fire — a vendor had missed a deadline, a client was already emailing in caps. By six-fifty you were four emails deep, coffee cold, Bible still closed on the counter. Tuesday you told yourself you'd make it up at lunch. You didn't. By Thursday the block was still on the calendar, but you'd stopped even glancing at it — just clicked past it like a meeting you'd silently decided not to attend anymore.

Nobody sat you down and told you prayer mattered less than the vendor problem. You never actually decided that. What happened is quieter and more common: everything on the list felt equally urgent, so the newest fire always won, and prayer — having no deadline, no one emailing in caps about it — kept losing by default.

There's a scene in Luke where Martha is doing exactly what needed doing — cooking, hosting, keeping the household running — while her sister Mary just sits at Jesus's feet, listening. Martha, understandably, gets frustrated and asks Jesus to make Mary help. His answer isn't what she wants to hear: "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things; one thing is needful. Mary has chosen the good portion, which shall not be taken away from her" (Luke 10:41-42, RSV-CE). Sit with that phrase — *one thing is needful* — against the belief you'd been running on all week. Not many things, equally weighted, fighting for whichever slot opens first. One thing, first, on purpose, before the rest gets a vote.

None of the items on your list were wrong. The vendor problem was real. The client email needed an answer. But your work will always expand to fill whatever space you give it, and it will keep expanding until it's eaten the twenty minutes you'd set aside for the one thing that was supposed to order everything else. A checklist has no natural stopping point. It just has the next item. Only you can decide something goes before it — permanently, not just on the days it's convenient.

You put the block back on the calendar Friday morning, but this time you moved it to before you opened the laptop at all, phone in another room, not because the emails weren't urgent, but because you finally admitted they'd always feel urgent, every single morning, for the rest of your life, unless you decided in advance what came first.

**Today:** Say it out loud before you touch your phone tomorrow: "This is the one thing that comes first — not because the rest doesn't matter, but because it doesn't get to compete." Then spend five minutes with a single verse of Scripture before you open your laptop. Put the phone in another room tonight if that's what it takes.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, my to-do list keeps eating the time I set aside for You, because I've been treating everything as equally urgent instead of putting You first. Help me choose the one thing that's needful before I open my laptop tomorrow. Give me the discipline to protect it, because it's the one that orders everything else. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
