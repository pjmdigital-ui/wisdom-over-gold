# Day 10 — Surrendering the Wheel

The email came in at 6:47 on a Sunday night. Your biggest client — forty percent of your revenue, the one whose logo you'd put on your website like a trophy — was pulling out. No warning, no negotiation window, just a two-line notice from someone in their legal department. You read it standing in your kitchen with your coat still on, and you felt the floor tilt.

For the next three days you did what you always did when something threatened to slip out of your hands: you gripped harder. You called everyone you knew. You drafted a counter-proposal at midnight. You ran the numbers seventeen different ways looking for an angle that would make this not be happening. You barely slept, and when you did, you dreamed about spreadsheets.

It was your wife, watching you pace the kitchen a fourth night in a row, who finally said it: "You're acting like you're the one holding this company together." She didn't say it to wound you. She said it because it was true, and because she loved you enough to say the thing you didn't want to hear. Ask yourself the question she didn't have to ask out loud: when did you start believing that if you stopped pushing, everything you'd built would fall apart?

"Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not rely on your own insight. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make straight your paths" (Proverbs 3:5-6, RSV-CE). Read the order of that sentence carefully. Trust him — not after you've exhausted your own insight, but instead of relying on it. That's letting go of the wheel before the crash, not after it.

You didn't get the client back. But somewhere around day four, you stopped white-knuckling the spreadsheet and started actually praying about it — not the foxhole kind of prayer you throw up when you want a specific outcome, but the harder kind: *God, I don't know how this turns out, and I'm asking you to be enough either way.* The business, it turned out, could survive a bad quarter. What it couldn't survive was you continuing to run your life like God was a consultant you'd call in only after your own plan failed.

**On St. Ignatius of Loyola:** Before his conversion, Ignatius was a soldier obsessed with glory, ambition, and his own advancement — until a cannonball shattered his leg and, with it, the life he'd built around himself. His recovery reordered everything: he came to describe the goal of the spiritual life as "indifference," a radical willingness to hold every outcome — wealth or poverty, health or sickness, success or failure — loosely, so that only God's will mattered. He kept caring about outcomes; he simply let go of needing them to go his way.

**Today:** Name one outcome you're currently white-knuckling — a deal, a diagnosis, a decision someone else is making about your life. Say out loud: "I trust You with this even if it doesn't go the way I want." Then do one thing today that proves you meant it — make the call you've been avoiding, or simply stop checking the number for the rest of the day.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've been gripping this instead of handing it to You, acting like it all falls apart if I stop pushing. I don't know how it turns out, but I trust You to be enough either way. Help me let go of the wheel before the crash, not after. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
