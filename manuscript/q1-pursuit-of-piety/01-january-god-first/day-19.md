# Day 19 — God First in Silence

You set a timer for ten minutes, sat down in the chair by the window before anyone else woke up, and decided you were just going to be quiet with God. No podcast, no phone, no to-do list open in the other tab of your mind. Just silence. It lasted maybe ninety seconds before your leg started bouncing.

Then the thoughts arrived, uninvited and loud: the email you forgot to send Friday, whether you need to call the dentist, a conversation with your business partner you keep replaying and losing. You caught yourself mentally drafting a text message. You checked the timer, certain five minutes had passed. It had been two. By minute six you were negotiating with yourself — this counts, right, even if my mind wandered the whole time? — and by minute nine you were fairly sure silence was not for you. Some men are wired for this. You are apparently not one of them.

Psalm 46:10 (RSV-CE) says, "Be still, and know that I am God." It sounds gentle until you actually try to obey it. Stillness turns out to be one of the harder disciplines available to a modern man, harder in some ways than fasting or getting up early, because it removes every distraction you use to avoid facing what's actually going on inside you. Noise is a place to hide. Busyness is a place to hide. Silence takes the hiding place away, and what's left in the room is just you and God and whatever you've been outrunning.

That discomfort isn't a sign you're doing it wrong. It's a sign you've been running from stillness for a long time, and your mind is finally getting the bill for it. Men who golf, work, scroll, and podcast their way through every open minute of the day aren't more at peace than the man who sits still and feels agitated — they've just never stopped long enough to notice the agitation was already there. The bouncing leg and the racing thoughts are the noise finally becoming visible instead of being drowned out. It was always there. Silence just turns the lights on.

The goal was never to achieve a peaceful, empty mind on command, like flipping a switch. The goal was to show up, sit in the discomfort, and let God be present in it anyway — not because you felt Him, but because He said He'd be there. Ten distracted, fidgety minutes offered to God are still ten minutes given to Him. That's not failure. That's the actual practice, done imperfectly, which is the only way anyone ever does it.

**Today:** Sit in silence for ten minutes with no phone, no music, no agenda — just presence. When your mind wanders, and it will, don't quit. Gently return your attention to God and keep sitting until the timer goes off.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, my mind won't sit still and the quiet makes me want to reach for anything else. Stay with me in the fidgeting and the racing thoughts anyway, and help me keep coming back to You instead of walking away. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
