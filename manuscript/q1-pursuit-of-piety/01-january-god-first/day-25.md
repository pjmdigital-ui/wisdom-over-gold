# Day 25 — When God Says Wait

You applied for the promotion in March. It's August now, and the position is still technically "under review" — corporate for *we haven't decided and we're not telling you why*. You've prayed about it in the car, in the shower, at 2 a.m. when your mind won't quit running the mortgage numbers. Nothing has moved. No sign, no open door, no answer that feels like an answer. Just the same silence you started with, five months deep now.

Or maybe it's not a job. Maybe it's the biopsy results that came back "we'll monitor it." Maybe it's the marriage that's better but not healed, the son who still won't call, the business that hasn't turned the corner you were promised it would turn by now. Somewhere around month four, a sentence starts forming that you'd never say out loud to another believer but let yourself think anyway: *If God actually loved me, this would already be resolved.* You don't announce it. It just sits underneath everything else, quietly rewriting how you read the silence.

David knew this room. He wrote Psalm 27 surrounded by real threat, not in some tidy season of reflection, and the problem he's facing never actually resolves within the psalm. What he lands on instead is an instruction to himself: "Wait for the Lord; be strong, and let your heart take courage; yea, wait for the Lord!" (Psalm 27:14, RSV-CE). He says it twice, like a man talking himself down from a ledge. He doesn't say the waiting will be short, or that he understands why it's taking this long. He tells his own heart to stay, which only makes sense if the length of the delay says nothing about how loved he is.

Love was never the thing in question. A father who loved his son less would resolve the wait sooner just to be rid of him. God's timeline doesn't run on how much He loves you — it runs on what He's building in you that can only get built in a room with no clock and no exit sign. Patience you didn't have. Trust you hadn't earned. A dependence on Him instead of on your own schedule for how this was supposed to go.

You don't have to feel strong to obey "be strong." You just have to stay one more day, and let the waiting mean something other than what your worst 2 a.m. thought keeps telling you it means.

**Today:** Name the thing you've been waiting on longest. Then open your list of truths — the one you built in the front pages of this book — and read it slowly. Let it remind you what God has already proven true about how He treats you, before today's silence tries to convince you of something else. Write today's date next to the thing you're waiting on and one sentence: *I don't know when, but I'm staying.*

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I don't understand why this is taking so long, and some days it makes me doubt You love me at all. Remind me what You've already proven true, and give me the strength to stay in this waiting instead of running from it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
