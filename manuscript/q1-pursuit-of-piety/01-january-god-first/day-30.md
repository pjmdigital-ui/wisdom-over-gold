# Day 30 — Building the Life Around Him

You finally sat down and looked at your family's actual calendar — not the version you'd describe to someone if they asked, but the real one, the shared app with every practice and shift and dentist appointment logged in color-coded blocks. Soccer, three nights. Your work travel, two more this month. Date night, penciled in, moved twice, quietly gone. Mass, when it fit. Prayer, nowhere — not because you'd decided against it, but because you'd never once put it on the calendar in the first place, and a thing that's never on the calendar loses to everything that is.

That was the part that stopped you. You hadn't rejected God-first living. You'd just never built a schedule that made room for it. You'd been assuming it would happen the way spontaneous things happen — if there's a quiet moment, you'll pray; if the week isn't too busy, you'll get to Adoration. But there is never a quiet moment nobody else has already claimed. A life doesn't organize itself around what matters most by accident. It organizes itself around whatever you actually scheduled, and everything else gets the leftovers, including, apparently, God.

Jesus ends the Sermon on the Mount — the same sermon that gives you "seek first the kingdom" — with a warning about exactly this gap between believing something and building on it: "Every one then who hears these words of mine and does them will be like a wise man who built his house upon the rock... And every one who hears these words of mine and does not do them will be like a foolish man who built his house upon the sand" (Matthew 7:24, 26, RSV-CE). Both men, notice, heard the same words. The difference wasn't belief. It was construction. One man let what he heard shape what he actually built. The other let it stay a sentiment he agreed with while building on whatever was easiest.

A rule of life sounds like a monastery word, but it just means this: deciding in advance, before the week's chaos hits, where God actually sits in your calendar — not as a hope but as a block of time as real and protected as the client call you'd never casually skip. Morning prayer before the phone. A specific night that's family and nothing else. Confession on the calendar, not just in the someday pile. None of it complicated. All of it deliberate, which is the entire point — the flood doesn't care how sincerely you meant to build on rock. It only reveals what you actually built on.

**Today:** Open your family calendar right now and put three things on it for the next two weeks: a fixed time for personal prayer, a protected family night with no screens, and one specific date for Mass or confession beyond your normal Sunday. Treat all three like they're non-negotiable, because they are.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let everything else claim my calendar and given You whatever was left over, which was usually nothing. Help me actually build my week around You instead of just hoping You'll fit into whatever space happens to be free. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
