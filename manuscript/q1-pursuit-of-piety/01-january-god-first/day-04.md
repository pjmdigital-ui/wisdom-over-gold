# Day 4 — When Success Isn't Enough

The house closed on a Thursday. Four bedrooms, the yard he'd promised his wife for a decade, a driveway wide enough for both cars and the boat he'd also finally bought himself that spring. Tom stood in the empty living room after the movers left, sunlight coming through windows nobody had hung curtains in yet, and felt the strangest thing: nothing new. Just the same low hum underneath everything that had been there before the house, before the promotion, before the number in his 401(k) that used to feel like it would fix this exact feeling.

He didn't tell anyone. What was there to say? *This one will finally be enough* — that sentence had been quietly rewritten every time he crossed a line he'd once believed was the finish line. There was already a next number in his head. A bigger house than this one, somewhere down the line. He recognized the pattern even as it was happening to him again and couldn't seem to stop it.

The writer of Ecclesiastes got there before Tom did, by about three thousand years: "And whatever my eyes desired I did not keep from them; I kept my heart from no pleasure... Then I considered all that my hands had done and the toil I had spent in doing it, and behold, all was vanity and a striving after wind, and there was nothing to be gained under the sun" (Ecclesiastes 2:10-11, RSV-CE). This is a man with more resources than almost anyone in history, who built and acquired without limit, telling you flatly: it didn't work. It was never designed to. No amount of doing it right would have changed the outcome.

That's the part worth sitting with. Tom achieved everything and still hit the wall. The wall belongs to the nature of created things — houses, promotions, bank balances. They're good gifts, but no effort was ever going to route around the fact that they weren't built to be a final answer. Ask a good thing to be your ultimate thing and it will always, eventually, come up short; good things were never built to carry that weight. Only God was ever sized to fill that particular space.

The empty feeling in that living room was the proof: he'd been asking the house to do a job no house can do.

**Today:** Name one achievement you're currently chasing as though it will finally be enough. Say the belief that's actually true out loud: "This is a good thing. It was never built to be my ultimate thing." Then thank God specifically for one gift you already have that you've stopped noticing.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I keep asking good things — the house, the number, the next promotion — to fill a space only You were ever sized for. Thank You for what I already have and haven't stopped to notice, and help me stop moving the finish line. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
