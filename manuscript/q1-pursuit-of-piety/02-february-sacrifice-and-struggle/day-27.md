# Day 27 — The Cost That Doesn't Make Sense

Emery has driven forty minutes each way to pick up his nephew Tyler every Thursday for three years — dinner, sometimes a job-site visit, sometimes just sitting in a parking lot while Tyler talked or didn't talk. Tyler's still failing classes. Tyler still disappeared for six weeks last spring and came back defensive. Emery has never once heard him say thank you, and last Thursday, waiting in Tyler's driveway for twenty minutes while the kid decided whether to even come out, Emery asked himself the honest question: what has three years of this actually produced?

He couldn't point to anything. No turnaround, no grades improved, no relationship visibly repaired. Just Thursdays, and gas money, and a nephew who still might be headed somewhere bad. The math looked like a bad investment by every measure Emery knew how to run.

That's the specific weight of a sacrifice with no visible return — not the difficulty of it, but the silence afterward, the absence of any evidence it mattered. A man can endure real cost if he can see what it's buying. It's harder to keep paying when the ledger stays blank.

St. Paul had run out of visible returns more than once by the time he wrote to the Corinthians — beaten, shipwrecked, imprisoned, still with no clean proof any of it was working. What he says is a claim about where the actual accounting happens, not a denial of the cost: "this slight momentary affliction is preparing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison" (2 Corinthians 4:17, RSV-CE). "Slight" and "momentary" measure how three years of Thursdays look from inside a ledger that runs far longer than a lifetime, not how they feel to Emery in the middle of them — a ledger he doesn't get to see the balance of yet.

**On St. Isaac Jogues:** Isaac Jogues was a French Jesuit missionary among the Mohawk in the 1640s — captured, tortured, and mutilated, his fingers cut and chewed until he could barely hold the host to say Mass. He escaped, returned to France, and then did the thing that makes no sense by any visible accounting: he went back to the same people who had tortured him. He saw almost no fruit before they killed him in 1646. The fruit came generations later, in a young Mohawk woman named Kateri Tekakwitha, born the year he died, raised in the faith his mission had planted, who became the first Native American canonized a saint. Jogues never saw a single sign of it.

Emery doesn't get to see Tyler's ending yet either. Thursdays are still the assignment.

**Today:** Show up for the cost today whether or not you can see what it's buying. Say it out loud before you do: *This isn't wasted just because I can't see what it's for yet.* Do the one thing the sacrifice requires of you today — the drive, the call, the hour — without demanding proof first.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I want to see the return before I keep paying the cost, and You're asking me to pay it without seeing anything yet. Give me the endurance to keep showing up for what looks, right now, like it isn't working. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
