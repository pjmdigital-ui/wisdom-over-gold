# Day 23 — Comparing Crosses

I stood at the fence at my son's baseball game last week next to Greg, another dad from the team. His construction company took off two years ago almost by accident — a referral, then another, then he couldn't keep up with the calls. Mine has taken eleven years of grinding, and this spring alone I've had two guys quit and a lawsuit threat over a job that wasn't even our fault. I heard myself say, "must be nice," and I meant it as a joke, but it wasn't really a joke.

I've said some version of that sentence about a dozen different men over the years — the guy whose marriage looks easier than mine, the guy whose kid doesn't have the health stuff mine does, the guy who inherited the money I've spent two decades trying to earn. Standing at that fence I wasn't watching the game. I was doing math on whose road was harder, and coming up with reasons why mine should count for more.

Why does he get an easier road than I do? It's Peter's exact question in John's Gospel, asked about the apostle John himself: "Lord, what about this man?" (John 21:21, RSV-CE). Peter had just heard Jesus predict his own death, and instead of sitting with that, he pointed at the other guy and asked what his fate would be. Jesus doesn't explain John's road at all. He says, "If it is my will that he remain until I come, what is that to you? Follow me!" (John 21:22, RSV-CE).

That answer sounds almost dismissive until you sit with it. Jesus refuses Peter an explanation because the explanation isn't the point, not because there isn't one. Peter's job was to follow, not to audit John's cross against his own. The belief under my question at that fence was that my sacrifice only counts if it's harder than the next man's, that fairness is the measure God is using. Following is the measure instead.

Comparing crosses does something specific to a man: it turns his own calling into a grievance instead of a path. Greg's easier road, if it really is easier, is just his road — not a verdict on how much God loves him more than me. Mine is mine, and the only question that actually belongs to me is whether I'm walking it.

**Today:** Name the person whose road you've been comparing against your own — out loud, specifically. Then say: *His road isn't mine to carry or judge. Mine is enough to follow today.* The next time comparison shows up this week, in your head or out of your mouth, catch it and say that sentence again before you finish the thought.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I keep grading my sacrifices against other men's instead of just carrying mine. Settle that comparison in me and keep my eyes on the road actually in front of me. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
