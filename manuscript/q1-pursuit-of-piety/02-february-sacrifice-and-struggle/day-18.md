# Day 18 — The Sacrifice of Money

The parish was raising money for a family whose house had burned down two towns over — nothing left, five kids, insurance that covered a fraction of what they'd need to start over. Rich saw the sign-up sheet at the end of Mass and did what he always did: pulled a twenty from his wallet, folded it into the basket, and felt the small warm feeling of having done something.

Driving home, he did a number he doesn't usually let himself do. He thought about what he'd spent that same week — the upgraded seats for the flight next month, the round of golf with clients that was really just a round of golf, the standing order for a service he'd forgotten he still paid for. He didn't even try to tally the total; he already knew it would embarrass him. None of it was wrong. All of it was more than the twenty.

Rich would bristle at being called cheap, and fairly, though the real sentence running underneath it was quieter, more respectable, and harder to catch: *I'll give once the important things are covered.* Generosity lived in whatever was left over after his own comfort had already taken its share, which made it surplus with better PR than genuine sacrifice.

St. Paul describes a very different kind of giving in the churches of Macedonia — people who had nothing, giving anyway: "for in a severe test of affliction, their abundance of joy and their extreme poverty have overflowed in a wealth of liberality on their part" (2 Corinthians 8:2, RSV-CE). Their poverty was the soil their generosity grew out of. They gave before the ledger balanced, not after.

**On St. John Vianney:** Vianney, the parish priest of Ars, kept nothing for himself. He wore a threadbare cassock long after his parishioners begged him to replace it, ate almost nothing, and gave away every coin that came to him — most of it funding an orphanage he founded for abandoned girls, La Providence, which he kept afloat for years on gifts he had no real margin to give. Visitors who came expecting a comfortable rectory found a man sleeping on a bare frame, having sent whatever he might have spent on himself out the door before it could accumulate. He didn't wait to have enough. He gave out of what he had, which was almost nothing, and somehow it was always enough.

**Today:** Before your next act of giving — the collection basket, a friend in need, a cause you believe in — give an amount that actually costs you something to write, not the amount that's comfortable. State it to yourself first: "This isn't coming from what's left over." Then give it before you can talk yourself down to the smaller number.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, forgive me for calling it generosity when it only ever cost me what I wouldn't miss. Teach me to give in a way that actually costs me, trusting You to provide what I give away. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
