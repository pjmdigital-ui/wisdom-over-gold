# Day 14 — Closing Week Two

Bastian sat at the kitchen table Sunday night after the kids were down, looking back over the week the way a man checks a scoreboard he didn't know he was keeping. Monday he'd snapped at his son Otto over a broken lamp before he'd even asked what happened, correcting out of his own bad mood more than anything Otto had actually done. Wednesday, driving to practice, Otto had asked him something honest about heaven, and Bastian had actually stopped and answered it instead of putting it off. Friday, he'd caught himself enforcing the screen-time rule on one kid and letting it slide for another, and said so out loud at dinner instead of pretending he hadn't noticed. Saturday morning, he'd gotten up and run in the cold with Otto timing him from the driveway, and it had felt like the most honest hour of the week.

Four days out of seven, he'd gotten something right that used to trip him up regularly. Two days he'd gotten it visibly wrong, in ways his kids noticed even when he tried to cover it. That was the real shape of a week of fatherhood — not a clean run of good decisions, but a mix, examined honestly instead of glossed over on the way to Monday.

Proverbs doesn't romanticize what it takes to raise a kid well: "Folly is bound up in the heart of a child, but the rod of discipline drives it far from him" (Proverbs 22:15, RSV-CE). The verse assumes something a lot of fathers would rather skip past — that a child left entirely to himself doesn't drift toward wisdom on his own, and that correction, applied consistently and for the child's actual good, is part of how folly gets driven out rather than left to grow. Monday's failure and Friday's fix boil down to the same distinction: correction aimed at his own irritation doesn't drive out folly the way this verse describes. Correction applied evenly, for the child's actual good, does.

None of it required a dramatic turnaround. It required paying attention to Tuesday and Thursday the same way he'd paid attention to Monday and Friday — noticing where he was teaching well without meaning to, and where he was only reacting. Bastian wrote both down before he went up to bed, one line each, the kind of record he'd never kept before this month and suspected he should have been keeping for years.

**Today:** Look back at your own past week the way Bastian did. Name one day you got it right as a father and one day you got it wrong. Tell your kids both, out loud, tonight.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, this week showed me both what a patient father looks like and what a reactive one looks like, and I know which one I want more of. Thank You for the moments I got right, and give me the honesty to keep working on the ones I didn't. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
