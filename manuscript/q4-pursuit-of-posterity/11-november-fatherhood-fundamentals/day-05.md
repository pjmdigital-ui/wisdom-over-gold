# Day 5 — Rejoicing Over You

Truett's sixteen-year-old son August had spent three weeks building a water-filtration project for the regional science fair, and he won second place in his category, beating kids from two magnet schools. He found his dad in the crowd afterward, ribbon still in hand, grinning in a way sixteen-year-olds mostly stop letting themselves do in public. Truett shook his hand, told him "nice job," and asked whether he'd remembered to email his teacher a thank-you for the extra lab time. August's grin folded up fast, and he said "yeah, I'll do it" in the flat voice he used for homework reminders, not victories.

Truett didn't clock it in the moment. He clocked it three days later, replaying the ribbon and the handshake and the reminder about the email, and noticing that in sixteen years of fathering this kid, he couldn't remember the last time August had seen him simply happy about him — not proud in the managing sense, not satisfied that a task had been executed correctly, but happy the way you're happy watching someone you love do something they love.

Was there a difference between being pleased with his son's performance and delighting in his son? Truett had never separated the two questions before, and once he did, he suspected August had been getting a steady diet of the first and almost none of the second.

Zephaniah gives Israel a picture of God that has nothing to do with performance review: "The LORD, your God, is in your midst, a warrior who gives victory; he will rejoice over you with gladness, he will renew you in his love; he will exult over you with loud singing" (Zephaniah 3:17, RSV-CE). Loud singing — not a nod of approval, not a quiet satisfaction kept to Himself. The prophet describes a God who exults audibly over His people, the way a father might if he let himself. Nothing in the verse ties that exulting to a performance metric first. It's simply what God does over the people He loves.

Truett had built a father-son relationship almost entirely out of the authority side of the job — setting the standard, checking the follow-through, keeping August moving in the right direction. All of that mattered. None of it had ever once sounded like loud singing. A son can know his father is proud of his output and still wonder, underneath it, whether his father actually likes him.

**Today:** Tell your kid, specifically and out loud, one thing about them that has nothing to do with achievement or output — just something you genuinely enjoy about who they are. Let your voice show it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've let being pleased with what my kids accomplish stand in for actually delighting in who they are. Thank You for a Word that shows You rejoicing over Your people with loud singing, not quiet approval held in reserve. Teach me to let my son hear that same gladness from me today, plainly, with nothing attached to it. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
