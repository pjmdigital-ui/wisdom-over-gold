# Day 29 — A Fruitful Vine

Lawrence stood at the edge of the reception hall watching his daughter's first dance, working through half a glass of champagne he wasn't sure he'd actually wanted. Between songs, his new son-in-law found him by the bar and said something Lawrence hadn't expected: that watching Lawrence and Lucille argue and make up and keep choosing each other over thirty-some years had taught him more about how to be married than anything he'd read or been told outright, including what he'd just promised a few hours earlier at the altar.

Lawrence hadn't set out to teach anyone anything. He and Lucille had just kept doing the ordinary work of thirty-some years — the apologies, the budget arguments, the Sunday Mass they didn't always feel like attending, the long stretch in their thirties when the marriage had felt more like maintenance than love. None of it had felt like it was building toward anything beyond itself.

He'd assumed, without ever stating it, that whatever happened inside a marriage stayed inside it — a private matter between two people, its effects contained to the house they shared. Watching his daughter step into her own marriage, patterned already in ways he half-recognized from his own, told a different story.

Psalm 128 makes the same point as a promise, not a warning: "Blessed is every one who fears the LORD, who walks in his ways! ... Your wife will be like a fruitful vine within your house; your children will be like olive shoots around your table" (Psalm 128:1, 3, RSV-CE). The psalm doesn't measure the blessing by how the marriage feels from the inside on any given Tuesday. It measures it by what grows up around the table over years — a vine, olive shoots, a household that keeps producing fruit long after the two people at its center stop noticing they're doing anything remarkable.

**On Sts. Joachim and Anne:** Joachim and Anne were, by tradition, an ordinary married couple who spent years childless and unremarkable by any worldly measure, faithful to each other and to God with no evidence yet that their marriage was building toward anything larger than itself. Their daughter was Mary. Their grandson was Jesus. Joachim and Anne never saw the full shape of what their faithfulness became — it unfolded generations past anything they could have measured from inside their own household.

A marriage doesn't have to look remarkable to be building something that outlasts it. It has to be faithful, ordinarily, for long enough.

**Today:** Name one person outside your household — a son-in-law, a nephew, a younger man at work — who's watching how you and your wife treat each other, whether you've noticed or not. Do one thing today, in front of them, that you'd want them to learn from.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, thank You for a marriage that's building something wider than I can see from inside it. Let what my wife and I do today be worth someone else learning from, whether we ever find out or not. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
