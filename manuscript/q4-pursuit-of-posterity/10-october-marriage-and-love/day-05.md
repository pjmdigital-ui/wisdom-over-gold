# Day 5 — Joint Heirs

Vincent's company offered him a promotion that meant relocating the family for the third time in six years. He said yes before he'd finished the phone call, then told Renata about it that night the way he'd tell her about a schedule change — as a decision already made, with logistics attached. Renata had spent two years rebuilding a client base after the last move. She asked him, evenly, whether it had occurred to him to ask her first instead of informing her after.

It hadn't. Vincent had assumed, without examining the assumption, that his career was the load-bearing wall of the family's future and hers was something built around it — worth protecting when convenient, movable when it wasn't. He'd never said that sentence to himself in those words. He'd just never once, across three moves, treated her work or her exhaustion as carrying the same weight as his own.

*My career is what actually provides for this family. Her job, her needs, her limits — those flex around mine because mine is the one that can't.* Vincent had built years of decisions on that hierarchy without ever putting it into words he'd have to defend out loud.

St. Peter addresses husbands directly on exactly this point, and the reasoning he gives has nothing to do with whose income is larger: "Likewise you husbands, live considerately with your wives, bestowing honor on the woman as the weaker sex, since you are joint heirs of the grace of life, in order that your prayers may not be hindered" (1 Peter 3:7, RSV-CE). Peter's phrase — joint heirs — puts Renata on equal footing with Vincent before God, inheriting the same grace, holding the same dignity, regardless of whose paycheck happened to be larger that year. He doesn't tell husbands to defer to their wives on everything. He tells them to live with consideration, to actually weigh what she's carrying before deciding what he's owed.

The verse's warning is the part Vincent had never noticed: a husband who won't honor his wife as a joint heir finds his own prayers hindered. God ties a man's relationship with Him to how he treats the woman he's supposedly leading. Vincent's third relocation went wrong in how the call got made — like a decision about a resource, when it needed to be a decision made alongside someone who inherits everything he inherits.

Vincent didn't need to stop pursuing the promotion. He needed to go back to Renata and actually ask the question this time — not inform her, ask her — and mean it enough to hear a no.

**Today:** Identify one decision where you treated your wife's job, needs, or limits as secondary to your own without saying so out loud. Go to her today, name it honestly, and ask — not tell — what she actually needs from you regarding it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've treated my wife's needs as something that flexes around mine instead of honoring her as an equal heir of Your grace. Forgive me for the decisions I made for both of us as if they were only mine to make. Teach me to live with her the way Peter describes — with real consideration, not convenience. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
