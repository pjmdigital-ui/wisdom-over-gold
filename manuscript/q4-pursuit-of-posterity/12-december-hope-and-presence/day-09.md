# Day 9 — One Thing Is Needful

Marlon had the whole Saturday mapped by nine a.m.: soccer at ten, a work call at noon he couldn't move, groceries, and June's dance recital at four. He made every stop. He stood on the sideline in the cold and clapped. He took the call in the car with the engine running so nobody could hear him talking numbers. He pushed the cart while June debated cereal brands. He sat in the third row at four o'clock and watched his daughter spin through eight minutes of choreography she'd practiced for two months.

He also checked his phone during the recital — twice, quick, under the program in his lap — because a client had emailed and he didn't want Monday to start with a fire he could have put out Saturday. June found him afterward, buzzing, wanting to know if he saw the part where she didn't mess up the turn. "I saw it," he said, and he had, mostly. He'd been there for all four stops. He'd started believing that was the whole job — show up to everything, and the presence takes care of itself.

It doesn't. A man can be in every room on the calendar and absent from every one of them, because attendance and attention aren't the same currency, and he'd been spending the first while telling himself it covered the second.

This book has stood on one verse before to break apart exactly this belief. Back in January, on a morning when a prayer time kept losing to whatever email came in first, this same passage did the work. It's worth returning to on purpose here, not by accident, because the thing that ate that January morning and the thing that ate four minutes of June's recital are the same thing wearing a different calendar. In Luke's Gospel, Martha is pulled in a dozen directions getting a house ready while her sister Mary sits at Jesus's feet, giving Him her whole attention. Martha wants help. Jesus doesn't send Mary back to the kitchen: "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things; one thing is needful. Mary has chosen the good portion, which shall not be taken away from her" (Luke 10:41-42, RSV-CE). Not many things weighed evenly. One thing, chosen, ahead of the rest.

Marlon's Saturday didn't need fewer stops. It needed him actually at whichever one he was standing in. A calendar with four things on it and a man split four ways produces motion that resembles presence closely enough to fool the man doing it, though rarely the daughter watching him check his phone mid-recital. The soccer field gets him for an hour. The recital gets him for eight minutes. Either one is short enough that giving it his whole attention should have been the easy part, and it wasn't, because he'd trained himself to treat every stop as background for whatever came next.

**Today:** Pick the one commitment already on today's calendar that matters most to someone else — a game, a recital, a dinner, a call from your kid. Put your phone in another room for the whole of it, no exceptions, and give that one thing what Mary gave hers.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've been mistaking showing up for actually being there, and my daughter noticed the difference even when I told myself she wouldn't. Help me choose one thing today and give it my full attention instead of splitting myself across everything at once. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
