# Day 4 — Sufficient for the Day

Stanley woke at 2:14 and didn't get back to sleep. He lay there running numbers that don't actually resolve at 2 a.m. or any other hour: what three college tuitions will cost in real dollars a decade from now, whether the retirement account is on pace for a date he can't quite picture, what happens if his father's health takes the turn everyone's been avoiding naming. He worked the arithmetic forward year by year until the years ran out ahead of him faster than the math could keep up, and he was still awake.

He never quite finished the arithmetic, because there's no version of 2 a.m. long enough to solve for every year still ahead of a man. What he was actually doing, without naming it that way, was trying to earn tomorrow's peace tonight — treating every future contingency as something he had to account for before he was allowed to rest, as if provision fifteen years out depended on Stanley solving it in the dark tonight instead of God supplying it when it actually arrived.

Jesus addresses this exact posture directly, and He doesn't soften it: "Therefore do not be anxious about tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Let the day's own trouble be sufficient for the day" (Matthew 6:34, RSV-CE).

This verse is worth pausing on longer than most, because of where it sits. It's the sentence that comes immediately after the one this whole book was built on. January's first entry stood on Matthew 6:33 — "seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things shall be yours as well." Jesus doesn't stop there and let the disciples move on to something else. He continues in the very next breath with the line above, and the two were never meant to be separated: seek the kingdom first, and let today's trouble be enough for today. Ordering a life around God is about how much of tomorrow a man is allowed to carry tonight, as much as it's about the direction he aims over decades. This book will return to 6:33 itself one final time, on its very last day — this verse is a deliberate down payment on that ending, not a coincidence of the calendar.

Stanley's 2 a.m. arithmetic assumes God's provision for year fifteen depends on Stanley finishing the equation tonight. It doesn't. Sufficiency gets measured a day at a time, the way manna did in the desert — enough for today, gathered today, trusted to show up again tomorrow whether or not the whole future got solved in advance.

**Today:** If you wake at 2 a.m. running the numbers again tonight, say this out loud: "Today's trouble was enough for today. I'm giving tomorrow back to God until it's actually tomorrow." Tomorrow morning, write today's date on a sticky note and put it somewhere you'll see it — a plain reminder that today is the only day you're actually responsible for.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've been trying to solve fifteen years of worry in the middle of the night instead of trusting You with tomorrow when tomorrow actually comes. Thank You for making today's trouble sufficient, not the whole future's. Help me hand back what isn't mine to carry yet, and let me rest tonight in what You've already promised to supply. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
