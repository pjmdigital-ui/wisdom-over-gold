# Day 16 — Not Crushed

Jason found out about the promotion the way most bad news arrives at a mid-size company: buried in a forwarded email, three sentences long, congratulating a junior colleague on stepping into the operations director role Jason had been told for over a year was basically his. He read it twice in the parking lot before he made himself walk back inside and finish the afternoon like a man who hadn't just watched fourteen months of extra hours amount to nothing.

He didn't say much on the drive home. He didn't say much at dinner either, and when his daughter asked him three times to check her math homework before he finally looked up from his plate, he heard himself snap at her in a voice that had nothing to do with long division. He caught it, apologized, and did something close to the same thing the next night, and the night after that — three days running where his family got the leftover version of him, because as far as Jason could tell, that was simply what a man had left to give once the wind got knocked out of him like that.

*Whatever happens to me during the day is going to happen to everyone I come home to. That's just how it works.*

St. Paul had far more standing to make that claim than Jason did, and he doesn't make it. Writing out of a life that included beatings, shipwreck, and repeated imprisonment, he says: "We are afflicted in every way, but not crushed; perplexed, but not driven to despair; persecuted, but not forsaken; struck down, but not destroyed" (2 Corinthians 4:8-9, RSV-CE). Paul isn't pretending the affliction wasn't real — every verb in that sentence names something that actually happened to him. He's naming a second thing, right alongside the first, that didn't automatically follow from it. Afflicted is a fact. Crushed is closer to a choice than a man wants to admit on the night he loses the promotion.

That's the gap Jason's three bad nights lived inside. Losing the role was the affliction — real, disappointing, entirely outside his control. Coming home and letting his daughter eat the fallout of it was the crushed part, and that part had his fingerprints on it. Paul's list describes a man whose circumstances don't get to file the final report on his interior life, available to Jason on his worst Tuesday, not just to an apostle who already had decades of practice.

**Today:** Name, out loud tonight, the thing that's afflicting you right now — the job, the diagnosis, the argument you can't fix. Then walk into whatever room your family is in and give them the version of you that isn't crushed, on purpose, for one evening.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've let a bad day decide how I treat the people I love most, more than once this week. Teach me the difference between what's happened to me and what I hand to my family afterward. Where I've been short with them out of my own disappointment, give me the grace to go back and make it right tonight. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
