# Introduction

I used to think I was the exception.

Not out loud — I wasn't stupid enough to say it that way — but it was there, underneath everything. Other men needed discipline, needed help, needed God. I needed a better plan. I could out-work the problem, out-think the problem, out-hustle the problem. Whatever the problem was.

For a long time, that worked well enough to be dangerous. [I built a business from nothing / I climbed further than anyone in my family had climbed], and every time it worked, it confirmed the thing I already believed: I was in charge of my own life, and I was doing a decent job of it. God was somewhere in the background — a thing I believed in, technically, the way you believe in a country you've never visited. He was real. He just wasn't relevant to the actual decisions I was making on a Tuesday.

I'd tell you I was a skeptic, but that's too generous. A skeptic is at least asking questions. I wasn't asking anything. I had decided, without ever quite deciding, that faith was for people who needed a crutch, and I didn't need one. I was self-sufficient. That was the whole identity: the guy who handles it. The guy who doesn't need help. The guy who is, functionally, the god of his own life — even if I would have laughed if you'd said it to my face.

Then [the year everything I was sure of stopped working] happened.

I won't walk through every detail of it here — some of it belongs later in this book, in the months where it's actually useful to you instead of just being my story. But the short version is this: something broke that hustle couldn't fix. [The business decision that blew up. The relationship that almost didn't survive me. The night I sat in my car in my own driveway because I couldn't make myself go inside.] Whatever the specific shape of it was for you, you probably know the feeling already — the moment you realize the guy who handles it doesn't, actually, handle it. Not this.

What I remember most clearly isn't the crisis. It's what came after — the slow, humiliating process of admitting that the life I'd built around myself couldn't hold the weight I was putting on it. I had made myself the center, and the center wasn't strong enough. It was never going to be strong enough. No man's is.

I didn't come to faith in a single dramatic moment, and if that's what you're expecting from this book, I want to be honest with you now so you're not waiting for it later. There was no voice, no vision, nothing you could put in a movie. There was a slow, grinding realization — read one page at a time, prayed one clumsy prayer at a time — that I had the whole thing backward. I thought seeking God was something you did once you'd gotten everything else in order: the business steady, the marriage good, the kids raised right, and then, sure, add some faith on top as a kind of finishing touch. What I learned, late and the hard way, is that it doesn't work in that order. It can't. You don't get the business, the marriage, and the kids right and then add God. You seek God first, and the rest gets built on something that can actually hold it.

That's Matthew 6:33, and it took me most of my adult life to actually believe it instead of just being able to quote it: "Seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness, and all these things will be added to you." I'd heard that verse a hundred times before it meant anything to me. It sounds like a nice sentiment on a wall somewhere. It is not a nice sentiment. It is an order of operations, and I had mine completely inverted.

Here's what I want to be clear about, because I think a lot of men pick up a book like this expecting either a lecture or a highlight reel, and I'm trying to write neither. I am not writing to you from a mountaintop. I still catch myself, most weeks, quietly re-appointing myself god of my own life — of my schedule, my business, my house, my marriage — and having to notice it and hand it back. That noticing, and that handing back, is not a thing you finish. It's not a box you check once and move on from. It's the whole game, every day, for the rest of your life. That's why this book isn't structured as twelve lessons you learn and graduate from. It's 365 days, because seeking first isn't an achievement. It's a direction you keep choosing to walk in, one morning at a time, for as long as you're breathing.

I wrote this book for men who are tired of two things. Tired of the version of faith that feels like it was written for a monastery instead of a mortgage — all theory, no traction on the actual mess of running a business, raising kids, and staying married to someone who sees you at your worst. And tired, too, of being handed encouragement with no substance to it — devotionals that could apply to anyone, that never actually get specific about the pressure you're under at 6 a.m. with a sick kid and a client waiting and a marriage that needs more than you've got left to give it.

This book is my attempt at the thing I went looking for and couldn't find: something built specifically for a Catholic man trying to be faithful in the middle of an ordinary, demanding, unglamorous life — not despite the business and the mortgage and the marriage, but right in the middle of them.

The four pursuits that structure this book — God, protection, provision, legacy — aren't four separate projects you're supposed to run at once. They're four expressions of one decision, made and remade daily: whether you are going to be the god of your own life, or whether you are going to seek His Kingdom first and let everything else take its proper place underneath that. Protection, provision, and legacy don't work — not really, not for long — if the first pursuit isn't in place. I know that because I spent years trying to get the other three right while skipping the first one, and I can tell you exactly how far that gets you. It gets you far enough to look successful and be quietly hollow. It got me a life that looked finished from the outside and felt unfinished everywhere it actually mattered.

So that's where we start. Not with protection, provision, or legacy — as important as those are, and as much space as this book gives them later. We start with the one pursuit underneath all the others: seeking Him first. Everything in the following pages, starting with January, is my attempt to walk that out with you, one ordinary day at a time.

I'm not ahead of you on this. I'm just a little further down the same road, still getting it wrong some days, still having to hand the throne back. Let's walk it together.
