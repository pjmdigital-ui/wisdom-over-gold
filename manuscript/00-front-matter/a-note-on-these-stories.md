# A Note on These Stories

The introduction you just read is my own story, told as accurately as I can tell it.

The daily entries that follow are different. Most of the scenarios that open each day — the argument in the kitchen, the contract on the desk, the phone at the dinner table — are illustrative. They're composites, built from the kinds of pressure I've faced, watched other men face, or can easily imagine a husband, father, and businessman facing. Some are written in the first person and some are written about a man I've named, but none of them are reported as literal, factual accounts of specific events unless I say so directly, the way I did in the introduction.

I wanted that distinction on the record. Stories are a good way to make a point land — Christ used them constantly — but I don't want you trusting a scenario as something that actually happened to me when it didn't. Where this book is telling you what actually happened in my life, you'll know it, because I'll say so plainly, the way I did above.
