# Day 21 — Closing Week Three

Sunday night, the house finally settled, you sat down with the week behind you and made yourself actually look at it instead of just closing the tab on it. Monday, you'd shaded a number on your tax spreadsheet because the money had come in cash. Wednesday, you'd typed something under a half-anonymous account that you'd never have said at the mailbox. Thursday, you'd told your wife "fine" at dinner while every muscle in your body argued otherwise, until your own son called it. Somewhere in there, a promise to your daughter about a bike ride got quietly swallowed by a work call that ran long.

Four different rooms. Four different people on the other end — the IRS, a neighbor, your wife, your daughter. None of them related to each other. And yet lined up next to each other on a Sunday night, they don't look like four separate lapses. They look like one habit wearing four different outfits.

The habit is this: you keep deciding, case by case, whether a given audience is the kind that "counts." The government's watching, so be careful there. Your wife's watching, so manage the tone even if not the truth. The stranger online isn't watching your actual face, so let the guard down. Your daughter's seven, so surely the bike ride can slide. Integrity handled that way isn't a trait you have — it's a setting you adjust per audience, generous with some people, careless with others, and never actually stable.

Scripture doesn't describe integrity as situational. "The integrity of the upright guides them, but the crookedness of the treacherous destroys them" (Proverbs 11:3, RSV-CE). Notice the verb — integrity *guides*. It's not a badge you wear when convenient; it's meant to function like a compass, giving the same reading in every room regardless of who's watching or what it costs you. A compass that only points true north when someone's checking has already stopped being a compass.

That's the pattern worth naming plainly after a week like this one: *my integrity is only as strong as whichever audience I've decided matters most today.* Written out like that, it's obviously not something you'd choose on purpose. Nobody sits down and decides to be honest with his wife but not with the IRS. It happens by inches, one unexamined exception at a time, until a Sunday night review is the first time you've seen all four exceptions in the same light.

The good news carried inside a week like this is that none of the four moments were fatal on their own. A shaded number, a sharp comment, a swallowed "fine," a broken bike-ride promise — every one of them is fixable by Monday morning. The only real danger is never lining them up long enough to see the shape they make together.

**Today:** Take ten minutes and write down every moment this week your integrity flexed depending on who was watching. Don't judge them yet — just list them. Then pick the one you can still repair by tomorrow, and repair it.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for treating my integrity as something that changes size depending on the audience. Make my word and my actions the same in every room this week, whether You're the only one who'll ever know. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
