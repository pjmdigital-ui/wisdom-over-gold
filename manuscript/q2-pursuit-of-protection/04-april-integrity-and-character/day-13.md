# Day 13 — The Double Life

At the nine o'clock Mass, Jordan reads the second reading from the ambo in a steady voice, robes his son for altar serving, and shakes hands with three people on his way out who thank him for how well he reads. He's patient in that hour. Warm. The kind of man other fathers in the parish ask for advice.

Monday night, eleven-forty, Jordan is still awake under a screen name in a fantasy football forum, tearing into a stranger's take with a cruelty he'd never let himself use with his own name attached. Two nights later it's a coworker's LinkedIn post — a minor project mistake made public — and Jordan leaves a comment under a different pseudonym that's technically true and deliberately humiliating. Nobody in either room knows about the other room. Jordan barely notices the distance between them himself; both versions feel like him.

Jordan wants, honestly, to be the man reading at the ambo. He also enjoys the sharper edges he gets to keep on the anonymous account. He lets the room decide which version of him shows up, and calls that flexibility instead of what it actually is.

James writes about a man whose loyalty is split between two masters, asking God for wisdom with one hand while trusting himself with the other: "that person must not suppose that a double-minded man, unstable in all his ways, will receive anything from the Lord" (James 1:7-8, RSV-CE). The Greek word underneath "double-minded" literally means two-souled — not confused, but divided, carrying two separate operating systems and switching between them depending on the room.

A man doesn't need an anonymous account to be double-minded. He needs two audiences and two different standards for how he treats them. The uncomfortable part is that Jordan's Sunday self is entirely real, and so is his Monday-night self — the man reading the second reading and the man writing that comment are the same person, no mask involved.

Integrity, in the plainest sense, means the room doesn't get a vote. One man, one standard, whether his name is attached or not.

**Today:** Look at how you've spoken to someone — online, anonymously, or where no one from your parish or family was watching — in the last week. Ask whether the man in the ambo would recognize himself in it. Where he wouldn't, delete the account, close the tab, or change how you show up there, starting today.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've let different rooms bring out different versions of me, and I've told myself that was just adapting. Make me the same man everywhere, whether or not anyone knows my name. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
