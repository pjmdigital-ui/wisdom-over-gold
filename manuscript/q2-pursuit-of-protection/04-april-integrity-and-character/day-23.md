# Day 23 — The Sunk Cost of a Lie

Three weeks ago, on a call you hadn't prepared for, a client asked how the website redesign was coming. Your developer had quit the week before and you hadn't replaced him yet. "About eighty percent done," you said, a number you invented on the spot to end an uncomfortable silence. The client seemed relieved. You hung up and told yourself you'd catch up the actual work before it mattered.

It's Wednesday now, and the client just emailed asking for a screenshot to show his board on Friday. There is no screenshot. There's a folder with almost nothing in it.

You have two moves available. You can call him and say the truth — the project stalled, you're behind, here's the real timeline. Or you can spend Thursday night building a mockup that looks far enough along to survive a glance from a board member, and buy yourself another few weeks to actually catch up before anyone checks closely.

Notice what's actually driving you toward the second option. It isn't that the mockup is easier — building a fake screenshot that holds up under scrutiny is real work, probably harder than just calling him. What's driving you is the first lie. You've already spent it. Correcting the record now would mean admitting the eighty percent was invented, which feels like it would cost you more than the original lie did — as if the first lie were an investment you now have to protect by adding a second one, and eventually a third, each one purchased to keep the last one from being discovered. That's the whole logic, stated plainly: *I've already lied once, so the only way forward is to keep making it true rather than admit it never was.*

That logic never actually stops the bleeding. It just moves the reckoning later and makes it bigger when it comes. Scripture is direct about which of the two roads actually leads somewhere survivable: "He who conceals his transgressions will not prosper, but he who confesses and forsakes them will obtain mercy" (Proverbs 28:13, RSV-CE). Concealment is a road with a real destination: continued exposure, a lie that keeps needing new lies to hold it up. Confession, by contrast, is the only move on the board that actually ends the debt instead of refinancing it.

Here's what the sunk-cost logic hides from you in the moment: the first lie already happened. Nothing you do Thursday night gets it back or makes it not have happened. The only real choice left is whether you pay for one lie now or a compounding pile of them later, each with interest.

A client who hears "I overstated our progress three weeks ago, here's where we actually stand and how we'll catch up" is dealing with a man he can trust going forward. A client who eventually discovers a doctored screenshot is dealing with a man he can never trust again.

**Today:** Find the lie you're currently protecting with a second one — the story you're maintaining instead of correcting. Make the call or send the message today: state the truth plainly, and say to yourself first, "The debt gets smaller today, not later."

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, forgive me for treating an old lie as something worth protecting instead of something worth confessing. Give me the courage to pay the real cost of the truth today rather than let the debt grow larger by morning. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
