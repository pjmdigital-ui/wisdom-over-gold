# Day 10 — A Soft Answer

The warehouse manager was already shouting before Preston got his safety glasses off. A shipment had gone out three days late, a client was threatening to walk, and the manager wanted someone to answer for it in front of the whole floor crew. Preston was that someone — not because the delay was his fault, but because he was standing closest when the man needed a target.

He felt the old pattern load up fast: match the volume, match the accusation, stand his ground in front of the crew so nobody thought he could be talked to like that. His jaw was already set for it. What stopped him wasn't calm. It was a single question he made himself ask before he opened his mouth: *what does this man actually need from me in the next ten seconds — to be answered, or to be heard?*

He kept his voice level and said the delay had happened, he understood why it mattered, and he'd have the full timeline in the manager's inbox within the hour. Nothing he said backed down from the truth. It just didn't add fuel that wasn't necessary. The shouting dropped by half almost immediately, not because Preston won an argument, but because he didn't hand the man anything to fight.

Most men read a raised voice as a challenge that has to be met at the same pitch, or it counts as losing. That's the belief hiding inside the instinct to match volume with volume: *staying calm while someone's yelling at me means I'm letting them win.* Proverbs says the opposite plainly: "A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger" (Proverbs 15:1, RSV-CE). The verse doesn't call softness weakness. It calls it the thing that actually works — a harsh word doesn't just fail to fix the conflict, it manufactures more of exactly what started it. A soft answer isn't surrender. It's the only response in the room capable of ending the escalation instead of extending it.

That distinction matters because a lot of men were taught, somewhere along the way, that matching heat is what strength looks like. It isn't. Strength is being able to stay level when everything in you wants to match the pitch, and staying level is a decision, not a personality trait — which means it's available to a man who's never once felt naturally calm under pressure.

**Today:** In your next tense conversation, notice the moment you want to match the other person's volume. Say to yourself instead: "Staying level isn't losing — it's the thing that actually ends this." Answer one degree cooler than the temperature you were handed.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, I've mistaken volume for strength more times than I've admitted, and made hard moments harder by matching them. Give me the soft answer instead of the harsh one, especially when I feel cornered into proving something. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
