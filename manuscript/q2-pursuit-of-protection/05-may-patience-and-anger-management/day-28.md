# Day 28 — Closing Week Four

Felix sat down Sunday night with the week laid out behind him: the resentment toward a business partner he'd been nursing for months without saying a word about it out loud. The temple-cleansing story he'd read midweek and used, if he was honest, to justify a blow-up at a referee during his daughter's soccer game. The dressing-down he'd given a young employee in front of a client. The two silent days he'd handed his wife over something that hadn't even been that serious. The apology he'd offered his daughter that he now realized hadn't actually repaired anything. A hard, necessary conversation with his son about why the boy hadn't told him something that mattered.

Five very different mornings. One thread running under all of them, if he was willing to see it: every single time, some part of him had been looking for the version of anger that gets to be exempt — righteous instead of reactive, understandable instead of damaging, deserved instead of chosen.

Scripture doesn't leave that exemption anywhere to hide. Writing to men scattered and under real pressure, St. Peter doesn't say be at peace except when you're right. He says: "Finally, all of you, have unity of spirit, sympathy, love of the brethren, a tender heart and a humble mind. Do not return evil for evil or reviling for reviling; but on the contrary bless, for to this you have been called, that you may obtain a blessing" (1 Peter 3:8-9, RSV-CE). A tender heart and a humble mind aren't reserved for the people who deserve them. They're required precisely toward the people who provoke you — the partner who's frustrating, the referee who's blind, the employee who made the mistake, the wife mid-argument, the kid who spilled the juice.

This week didn't ask Felix to stop feeling anger. It asked him to stop finding places to hide it — in a slow burn he never named, in a Bible story he misused, in a raised voice, in cold silence, in an apology that skipped the actual repair. Every hiding place felt justified in the moment. None of them survived a tender heart and a humble mind held up next to them.

That's not a discouraging way to close a hard week. A man who can finally see the pattern is a man in a position to actually change it.

**Today:** Reread whichever day this week hit hardest, and do the one thing it asked of you that you haven't done yet. If nothing comes to mind, that's worth being honest with yourself about too.

**Prayer:** Father in Heaven, this week showed me how many hiding places my anger has — resentment, excuse, silence, a bad apology. Give me the humility to stop defending them and the tenderness to bless instead of return evil for evil. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
