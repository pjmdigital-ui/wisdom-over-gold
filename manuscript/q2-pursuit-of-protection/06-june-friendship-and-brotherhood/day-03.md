# Day 3 — Contacts Aren't Friends

After my wife and I had the kind of argument that leaves both people too raw to keep talking, I went out to sit in the truck in the driveway, the way I do when I need somewhere to put the leftover adrenaline. I opened my phone and started scrolling contacts, looking for someone to call. Three hundred and forty names. Vendors, old coworkers, guys from two different churches, a dozen men whose weddings or funerals I'd stood in the room for.

I got to the bottom of the list without finding one man I could actually call at eleven at night and say "I need to talk." Not because those men were bad men. Most of them would have picked up. But somewhere between "I know him" and "I could call him tonight" there was a gap I hadn't noticed until I needed to cross it, and standing at the edge of that gap in my own driveway was the loneliest I'd felt in years.

I thought of Gideon, my college roommate, who I used to talk to about everything that actually mattered. We hadn't spoken in maybe four years. Not from any falling-out — just the slow erosion of two men who let "we should catch up" become a thing we said instead of a thing we did. He was probably still the man I'd have called first, ten years ago. Ten years of accumulating contacts and losing him had somehow felt like progress.

The belief hiding under all three hundred and forty names was that a wide network is its own kind of security — that if I know enough people, I don't need to invest deeply in any one of them. Scripture draws the line I'd been ignoring: "There are friends who pretend to be friends, but there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother" (Proverbs 18:24, RSV-CE). Pretend friends and sticking friends are not different quantities of the same thing. They're different categories entirely, and no amount of the first converts into the second.

A contact list is a record of people who would recognize your name. A friend is someone who would drop what he's doing at eleven at night. Those are not the same asset, and treating them as interchangeable is how a man ends up surrounded by acquaintances and genuinely alone at the exact moment he needs to not be. Building the second kind takes something the first kind doesn't — time spent with nothing to gain from it, trouble shared before it was resolved, showing up before you were asked.

I didn't call anyone that night. I sat in the truck until the adrenaline burned off on its own, which is its own kind of answer.

**Today:** Look through your contacts and find the name of a man like Gideon — someone you used to be close with before life thinned it out. Text him something specific: not "we should catch up" but an actual invitation, a day and a place. Tell yourself plainly: a long list of names isn't the same as one man who'd stick closer than a brother, and I'm going to build the second one, starting with him.

**Prayer:** Lord God Almighty, I've mistaken a full contact list for a full life, and let real friendships thin out while I collected people who barely know me. Bring back to mind the men You've placed in my life for something deeper than small talk. Give me the will to reach for one of them today instead of scrolling past his name again. I make this prayer in the almighty name of Jesus Christ, our Lord and Savior. Amen.
